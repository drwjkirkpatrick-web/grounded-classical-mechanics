# The Grounded Materials Guide

Every demonstration and build in this course uses objects that already exist in Kenyan
homes, markets, farms and jua kali workshops. This guide is the shopping-and-scavenging
companion: what each object is, what it teaches, and how to get it cheaply or free.

## How to Use This Guide

- **Borrow before you buy.** Most items live in the school kitchen, the nearest
  homestead, or a fundi's scrap pile.
- **Buy once, use for years.** The starred (★) items serve dozens of lessons.
- **Substitutes are always listed** in each lesson's Parts list.

## Scavenging Calendar

- **Weekly:** PET bottles, bottle tops, tins, rubber offcuts.
- **Term start:** jerricans (retired cooking-oil ones, washed), mabati offcuts, broken
  bicycle parts (hubs with bearings are gold).
- **Ask the jua kali fundi:** worn bearings, springs, gears, chain — perfect for
  Units 6, 10 and 13, and he may visit as a guest expert (Community Science Day).

## Safety-Critical Sourcing

- **No glass** in student-built pressure systems (PET only).
- **Inner-tube rubber** only from road tyres — never from medical or chemical tubing.
- **Wash oil jerricans** with ash/soap and rinse thrice before classroom water use.
- **Panga/jembe demos** are teacher-handled; students use blunt wedges and sticks.

## Storage

One labelled debe per unit (16 debes). Parts return to their debe at the end of each
lesson — the habit is part of the engineering curriculum.
