# How to Solve a Physics Problem

**A simple four-step method that works for every lesson in this course.**

---

## Step 1 — Read the Question Carefully

Read the problem twice. The first time, read for the story — what is
happening? A ball is falling, a jerrican is being lifted, a pendulum is
swinging. The second time, read for the numbers and the question mark.

Ask yourself: **What is the problem asking me to find?**
That unknown is your target. Hold it in your mind before you touch any
equation.

## Step 2 — List Every Variable

Draw a small table on your page. In the left column write the **name** of
each variable you can spot in the problem. In the right column write its
**value with units** — or a question mark if it is the unknown.

### Example

> *A 2 kg stone is dropped from a cliff. It hits the ground after
> 3 s. How fast is it moving when it lands? (g ≈ 10 m/s²)*

| Variable | Value |
|----------|-------|
| mass (m) | 2 kg |
| time (t) | 3 s |
| acceleration (a) | 10 m/s² |
| initial velocity (v₀) | 0 m/s (dropped, not thrown) |
| final velocity (v) | **?** ← this is what we want |

Notice that mass was given but we do not always need it. Listing it
anyway keeps you from forgetting a variable that might matter.

## Step 3 — Select the Relevant Equation

Look at your variable table. You know t, a, and v₀. You want v. The
equation that connects these four — and only these four — is:

```
v = v₀ + a × t
```

You do not need mass, distance, or force for this question. The right
equation is the one that contains your **knowns** and your **unknown**,
and nothing you do not have.

### Common Equations in This Course

| You know… | You want… | Use this |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| v₀, a, t | v | v = v₀ + at |
| v₀, a, t | s | s = v₀t + ½at² |
| v, a, s | v₀ | v² = v₀² + 2as |
| F, m | a | F = ma |
| m, g, h | PE | PE = mgh |
| m, v | KE | KE = ½mv² |
| F, s | W | W = Fs |
| W, t | P | P = W/t |
| P, A | — | P = F/A |
| ρ, g, h | P | P = ρgh |
| ρ, V | m | m = ρV |
| T, L, g | — | T = 2π√(L/g) |
| f, λ | v | v = fλ |
| m₁, m₂, v₁, v₂ | — | p = mv (momentum) |

## Step 4 — Solve for the Answer

Substitute your known values into the equation and solve for the
unknown. **Always write the units with every number** and carry them
through the calculation. If the units come out right, you are almost
certainly right. If the units come out wrong, something is wrong.

### Worked Example (continued)

```
v = v₀ + a × t
v = 0 + (10 m/s²) × (3 s)
v = 30 m/s
```

**Check the units:** m/s² × s = m/s. That is a velocity unit. ✓

**Sanity check:** 30 m/s is about 108 km/h. A stone dropped for 3
seconds reaching that speed is believable. ✓

## Quick Checklist

```
☑  Read the question twice
☑  List every variable and its value (with units)
☑  Circle the unknown — that is your target
☑  Pick the equation that has your knowns and your unknown
☑  Substitute, solve, and check the units
☑  Ask: does the answer make sense in the real world?
```

---

*The best way to solve physics problems is to read the question
carefully, write out every variable, select the relevant equation, and
solve for the answer — usually the one variable you did not know when
you started.*
