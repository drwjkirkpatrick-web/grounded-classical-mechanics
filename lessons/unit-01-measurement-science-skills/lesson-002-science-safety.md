# Lesson 002: Science Safety and Workshop Rules

**Unit:** 1 — Measurement & Scientific Skills  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.1; UP-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** safety-charter

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain why safety rules are agreed *before* any experiment begins.
2. Identify hazards of sharps (panga, nails), heat (jiko), water and glass, and state a control for each.
3. Distinguish a hazard from a risk and choose sensible controls.
4. Draft a class safety charter that the whole class signs.

## Materials
- Chart paper (2–3 sheets) and marker pens
- Masking tape to display the charter
- Demo items for hazard spotting: a nail, a razor blade or panga (sheathed), a jiko or candle (unlit until demo), a glass bottle, a basin of water
- Exercise books for individual notes

## Vocabulary
- **hazard** (hatari) — something that can cause harm
- **risk** — the chance that the hazard actually harms someone
- **control** (kinga) — an action that reduces the risk
- **charter** (mkataba) — an agreement the whole class signs and keeps

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. Why do we agree on safety rules before starting experiments?
2. Name one hazard in a home workshop and how you would protect yourself.
3. True or false: water on the floor is only a problem if it is hot. Explain your answer.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. So everyone knows the dangers and how to avoid them before anyone can be hurt; accept any "prevention" reasoning.
2. Accept any pair, e.g. panga — cut away from the body / store sheathed; jiko — use a dry cloth for hot pots.
3. False — any water makes floors slippery; falls cause injuries regardless of temperature.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — administer pre-test while demo items stay covered at the front.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — tell a short story: a fundi in a jua kali shed who lost a fingertip to a grinding disc, and how his workshop changed afterwards. Ask: *was he careless, or was there simply no agreed rule?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — hazard safari and charter build (see Hands-On Build). Uncover the demo items one at a time; for each, groups name the hazard, the risk, and a control. Groups then draft charter rules on chart paper; the class merges them into one charter.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — board the pattern: hazard → risk → control. Show that a hazard stays (a panga is always sharp) but the *risk* can be made small by controls. Confront the misconception: "rules are for young children" — point out that professional workshops, hospitals and matatu Saccos all run on written safety rules.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *we control risk before we touch equipment.* Homework: spot two hazards at home and write the control your family already uses.

## Hands-On Build: Class Safety Charter
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; charter stays on the wall all year.*

### Parts
- Chart paper — 2–3 sheets — school supply or market stationery shop
- Marker pens — 2 colours — one for rules, one for signatures
- Masking tape — for wall display
- Demo hazard items — nail, sheathed panga or blade, unlit jiko or candle, glass bottle, water basin — from home/kitchen (teacher handles sharps)

### Workflow
1. Uncover one demo item at a time; groups write: hazard, who could be harmed and how, and one control.
2. Groups share their best control; the class votes the wording of a rule ("Cut away from the body", "Dry hands before electricity work").
3. Write the agreed rules on the charter in marker. **Checkpoint:** every rule names an *action*, not just "be careful" — reject vague rules and redraft them.
4. Every learner and the teacher sign the charter; tape it beside the workspace door.
5. Final test: point at any demo item; a learner must state hazard and matching control from the charter.

### Common Mistakes
- **Vague rules** ("be careful with fire") — redraft as an action ("tie back hair and keep water nearby when the jiko is lit").
- **Too many rules** — more than ten and nobody remembers them; merge overlapping rules.
- **Rules without owners** — assign one safety monitor per group each lesson so the charter is lived, not decoration.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. Why do we agree safety rules before experiments? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Name one workshop hazard and its control. (mirrors pre-test)
3. Fill in the chain: a broken bottle is the ______; a cut foot is the harm; wearing shoes and sweeping glass is the ______.
4. Write one good charter rule for using a jiko to heat water in a lesson.
5. Challenge: a rule says "never touch glass." Explain why this is a poor rule and improve it.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. So hazards are known and controlled before anyone is exposed; prevention, not reaction.
2. Any hazard + control pair, e.g. nail — sweep the floor and store nails in a tin; hot sufuria — dry cloth/gloves.
3. hazard; control.
4. Accept e.g. "Only the teacher lights the jiko; keep a sand tin and water nearby; tie back loose clothing."
5. It is impossible to follow — we use glass (bottles, windows). Better: "Handle glass with dry hands, over a basin; report and sweep breakages immediately."

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Write a risk assessment (hazard, risk, control) for three builds planned for this course: a beam balance with string and pans, a water-displacement can, and a stick sundial.
   **Worked Answer:** Beam balance — hazard: falling pivot stone/pans; risk: toe injury or splinters, moderate; control: work seated, pans low over a table, check knots before loading. Displacement can — hazard: spilled water and cut tin edges; risk: slips and cuts; control: fold/file cut edges, mop spills at once, work over a basin. Sundial — hazard: sun exposure and looking at the sun; risk: heat strain/eye damage; control: hats, short sessions, never look directly at the sun — read the shadow only.
2. **Q:** A workshop has a choice: ban all sharp tools, or train everyone and enforce sheathing. Using hazard vs risk language, argue which is better science teaching.
   **Worked Answer:** The hazard (sharpness) is unchanged in both cases. Banning removes the risk by removing the tool — but it also removes the learning. Training plus sheathing reduces the *risk* (chance of harm) to an acceptable level while keeping the hazard present, which is how real fundis work and what learners must master. Controls manage risk; they need not eliminate every hazard.
3. **Q:** Statistically, most home injuries are falls on wet floors, not cuts. Design a charter rule set that reflects this reality rather than the most dramatic hazards.
   **Worked Answer:** Include at least: "spills are mopped within one minute", "cables and pipes routed along walls, never across walkways", "report broken tiles". Good controls target frequency × severity of harm, not just fear. The class should rank hazards by how likely harm is, then control the top risks first.

## Safety Notes
- The teacher alone handles the panga/blade demo; it stays sheathed except for a held display, point down, arm's length from learners.
- If the jiko is lit for demonstration, light it outside or by an open window, keep a sand tin and water at hand, and never leave it attended by learners alone.
- Glass bottle demo: hold over the basin so any drop is contained; sweep and bin any breakage immediately in newspaper.
- Water basin stays on the floor, not on desks, so a knock cannot soak books or create a slip patch under benches.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lesson 001 has run; this charter governs every hands-on lesson in the course — refer back to it whenever a rule is needed.
- Ages 10–13: hazard → control pairing is enough. Ages 14–17: add the risk-assessment format (hazard / risk / control) as a written exercise; it reappears in project lessons.
- Local tips: ask a local fundi or a nurse to describe a real workshop/clinic accident — first-hand stories fix rules in memory far better than lists.
- Keep the signed charter; photograph it for the school records.
- CBE learner record: charter participation and the pre/post growth are evidence for the safety and responsibility competencies in practical work.
