# Lesson 009: Measuring Mass: Build a Beam Balance

**Unit:** 1 — Measurement & Scientific Skills  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; G10-PHY-1.5  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** beam-balance-mk0

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State that mass is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms.
2. Build a working beam balance from a ruler, a pivot and two pans.
3. Compare the masses of two stones and order three objects by mass.
4. Explain in words why a level beam means the two masses are equal.

## Materials
- Wooden ruler or flat, straight stick (about 30–50 cm), one per pair
- Pivot: a pencil, a round stone or a narrow piece of firewood
- String or strong thread (sisal string works), four short pieces per pair
- Two bottle-cap or small tin pans per pair (debe lids or soda-bottle caps)
- Cellotape or masking tape
- Three stones of different sizes per pair, plus a known 1 kg bag of sugar for reference

## Vocabulary
- **mass** (uzani) — the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms
- **balance** (mizani) — a tool that compares masses
- **beam** — the rigid bar (our ruler) that tips on the pivot
- **pivot** (egemezi) — the point the beam turns on
- **level** (sawa) — horizontal; neither side dipping

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. Which has more matter in it: a 1 kg bag of sugar or a 1 kg stone? Explain.
2. What unit do we use for mass in science?
3. A see-saw is level with one child on each side. What does that tell you about the two children?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. They have the same amount of matter — 1 kg each. Material does not matter, only the kilograms.
2. The kilogram (kg); grams (g) also accepted.
3. Their masses (or weights) are equal — the see-saw balances when both sides match.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out pre-test slips; collect before any apparatus is touched.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — tell the story of the market trader at Marikiti weighing sukuma wiki with a hanging balance. Ask: *how does she know your half kilo is really a half kilo?* Board the word **mass**.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs build the beam balance (see Hands-On Build). Once balanced, they compare two stones (which is heavier?), then order three stones lightest to heaviest, recording each decision in their books.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate on the board: mass = amount of matter, unit kilogram. When the beam rests level, the two sides pull down equally, so the masses are equal. Confront the misconception that "big means heavier" — a big pumice stone can lose to a small dense stone; only the balance decides.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark if time allows.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *a level beam means equal masses*. Homework: find a balance at home or in the market and write one sentence on what it compares.

## Hands-On Build: Beam Balance Mk 0
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; gather pans and string the day before.*

### Parts
- Ruler or flat stick — 1 per pair — desk or carpenter's offcut
- Pivot pencil or stone — 1 per pair — must be round and firm
- String — 4 pieces, about 20 cm each — sisal string or thread
- Bottle-cap pans — 2 per pair — soda caps or debe lids; must be the same type
- Cellotape — 1 roll to share — holds strings to caps

### Workflow
1. Tape two strings to each pan, opposite each other, and tie the free ends together so the pan hangs level.
2. Balance the bare ruler on the pivot and mark the balance point with chalk. **Checkpoint:** the ruler alone should rest almost level, not crash to one side; slide it until it does.
3. Hang one pan at each end of the ruler, at equal distances from the balance mark.
4. **Checkpoint:** with empty pans the beam should sit level or tip gently either way; if one side always wins, shift that pan slightly toward the pivot.
5. Place a stone in each pan. The lower side holds the heavier stone. Swap to confirm.
6. Order the three stones lightest to heaviest using only the balance, and record the order.

### Common Mistakes
- **Pans hung at unequal distances** — the nearer pan wins even with a lighter load; measure equal distances from the pivot.
- **Strings of very different lengths** — one pan swings and spills; trim them equal.
- **Ruler not balanced first** — a heavy-ended ruler fakes a result; always find the balance point with empty pans.
- **Judging by speed of tip** — only the final resting position matters, not which side dropped first.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. A 1 kg bag of feathers and a 1 kg lump of iron: which has more mass? (mirrors pre-test)
2. A beam balance rests level with a stone on the left pan and a 500 g mass on the right. What is the stone's mass? (mirrors pre-test)
3. Explain how you would use only your beam balance to find the heavier of two potatoes.
4. Why must the pans hang at equal distances from the pivot for a fair comparison?
5. Challenge: your beam tips left even with both pans empty. Give one likely cause and the fix.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Neither — both are 1 kg; mass is the same even though the feathers are bulkier.
2. 500 g (0.5 kg) — a level beam means equal masses.
3. Put one potato in each pan; the side that sinks holds the heavier potato. Swap them to confirm.
4. Because distance from the pivot changes the turning effect; equal distances make the turning effects equal only when the masses are equal (moments cancel).
5. The ruler's own balance point was not found (or one pan/string is heavier); re-balance the empty beam and shift the lighter side's pan outward, or equalise the pans.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Why must the pans hang at equal distances from the pivot? Link your answer to moments.
   **Worked Answer:** A hanging mass exerts a moment about the pivot: moment = force × distance from pivot = mgd. If the left pan hangs at 15 cm and the right at 10 cm, a 100 g mass on the left gives moment 0.100 kg × 10 N/kg × 0.15 m = 0.15 N·m, balanced by 150 g on the right (0.150 × 10 × 0.10 = 0.15 N·m) — the scale would lie by 50%. Only when d is equal on both sides does a level beam prove m₁ = m₂.
2. **Q:** A stone balances against 250 g placed 12 cm from the pivot while the stone sits 10 cm from the pivot on the other side. Find the stone's mass.
   **Worked Answer:** Balance moments: m × 10 N/kg × 0.10 m = 0.250 kg × 10 N/kg × 0.12 m. So m × 0.10 = 0.250 × 0.12 = 0.030, giving m = 0.30 kg = 300 g.
3. **Q:** A trader claims his balance is fair, but the beam's own balance point is 2 cm off-centre toward the customer pan. Who gains, and why?
   **Worked Answer:** The trader gains. The heavy end is on the trader's side, so the customer's pan must carry extra mass to level the beam; the customer pays for more goods than the marked mass. This is why honest balances are zeroed before use.

## Safety Notes
- Stones can drop onto bare feet — learners stand back when loading pans and place stones gently, never throw.
- Cut string with scissors or a blade under teacher supervision; store pangas and blades away during the build.
- Do not over-load bottle-cap pans — a snapping string flicks knots and stones toward faces; keep loads under about 1 kg.
- Keep the pivot stone stable on the desk; a rolling round stone can sweep apparatus to the floor.
