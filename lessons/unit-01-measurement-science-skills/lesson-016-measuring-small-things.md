# Lesson 016: Measuring Very Small Things

**Unit:** 1 — Measurement & Scientific Skills  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.1; JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain why a ruler cannot measure one page and a balance cannot weigh one grain.
2. Use the measure-many-then-divide method for tiny lengths, masses and volumes.
3. Measure the thickness of one book page, the mass of one maize grain and the volume of one water drop.
4. State one reason this method gives a smaller percentage error than measuring one object.

## Materials
- Exercise book (any, the thicker the better), one per pair
- About 100 dry maize grains per group, in a cup
- Beam balance from Lesson 009 (or any sensitive balance)
- Dropper or a straw used as a dropper
- Measuring tube or small calibrated bottle
- Ruler, water, exercise books

## Vocabulary
- **average** (wastani) — total shared equally among the count
- **sample** (sampuli) — the many you measure to learn about the one
- **percentage error** — error as a fraction of the true value, × 100%
- **drop** (tone) — the small unit of water from a dropper
- **grain** (punje) — one seed, e.g. of maize

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. Try to measure the thickness of one page of your exercise book with a ruler. What goes wrong?
2. A cup holds 100 maize grains and the cup of grains weighs 25 g on the balance. Estimate the mass of one grain.
3. Count how many drops of water you think fill a teaspoon. Just guess.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. One page is far thinner than the smallest ruler division — the reading is meaningless.
2. 25/100 = 0.25 g per grain (accept the division idea at this stage).
3. Accept any guess (a teaspoon holds roughly 60–100 drops).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — slips out; pairs collect materials.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — challenge the class: *measure one page. You have a ruler. Go.* Let them fail for a minute, then offer the trick: *what if the ruler is allowed to see 200 pages at once?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — three mini-labs in pairs (see Hands-On Build): (a) page thickness — measure a thick stack of pages, divide by the page count; (b) grain mass — weigh 100 grains, divide by 100; (c) drop volume — count drops to fill a small marked volume, divide. Each result is recorded as "one page ≈ … mm", "one grain ≈ … g", "one drop ≈ … cm³".
4. **Explain (8 min)** — board the method: **measure many, divide by the count**. The ruler's error (about 0.5 mm) is now spread over hundreds of pages, so the error per page is tiny. Confront the misconception that dividing "cheats" — every member of the stack is identical enough that the average IS the individual, to good accuracy.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *measure many, divide by the count*. Homework: find the thickness of one sheet of newspaper at home by the stack method; bring the number.

## Hands-On Build: The Measure-100-Divide Station
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; count maize grains the day before.*

### Parts
- Exercise book — 1 per pair — the class's own books are fine
- Maize grains — about 100 per group — from any kitchen; count into cups beforehand
- Beam balance — 1 per group — zeroed before use
- Dropper or straw — 1 per group — a straw with a finger on top meters drops well
- Small measuring tube or calibrated bottle — 1 per group — marked in cm³

### Workflow
1. Page thickness: measure the height of the whole book's page block (say 200 pages) in mm with the ruler pressed firmly. **Checkpoint:** press out the air gaps — a loose stack reads too thick.
2. Divide by the number of pages (count the numbered pages — note one sheet has two pages).
3. Grain mass: weigh the empty cup, then the cup with 100 grains; subtract. Divide by 100. **Checkpoint:** re-weigh once; the two answers should agree closely.
4. Drop volume: count drops to fill the tube to a marked level, e.g. 5 cm³. Divide volume by drop count.
5. Record all three "one-thing" answers with units in the exercise book.

### Common Mistakes
- **Counting sheets instead of pages** — a 100-sheet book has 200 pages; the error doubles the thickness.
- **Loose page stack** — air between pages inflates the reading; press firmly.
- **Forgetting the empty cup's mass** — always subtract the container (tare).
- **Drops of mixed sizes** — squeeze gently and steadily so each drop is the same size.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. Why can a ruler not measure the thickness of one page directly? (mirrors pre-test)
2. 100 maize grains (without the cup) weigh 28 g. Find the mass of one grain. (mirrors pre-test)
3. 80 drops of water fill a tube to the 4 cm³ mark. Find the volume of one drop.
4. Explain in one or two sentences why measuring 100 things and dividing is better than measuring one thing.
5. Challenge: your page-thickness answer is 0.5 mm — thicker than card. What most likely went wrong?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. One page (~0.1 mm) is far smaller than the ruler's smallest division (1 mm); the reading cannot resolve it.
2. 28/100 = 0.28 g.
3. 4/80 = 0.05 cm³ per drop.
4. The instrument's fixed error is shared across the whole count, so the error per item is divided by the count too — a much smaller percentage error.
5. Probably measured sheets as pages (or a loose stack): e.g. 100 sheets treated as 100 "pages" instead of 200 — answer doubles; or the stack was not pressed flat.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Page thickness comes out as 0.09 ± 0.02 mm. Explain how the many-at-once method reduces the percentage error compared with measuring one page.
   **Worked Answer:** A ruler's absolute error is fixed (≈ ±0.5 mm). On one page of 0.09 mm that is an absurd ±500%. Measure 200 pages: the stack is 18 mm, and ±0.5 mm on 18 mm is ±2.8%. Dividing by 200 shares the error across all pages: 0.09 mm ± 2.8% ≈ 0.09 ± 0.003 mm — comparable to the quoted ±0.02 mm once pressing differences between trials are included. The error stays fixed; the measured quantity grows; the percentage shrinks.
2. **Q:** A learner counts 95 drops to reach 5 cm³, her partner counts 104. Their answers for one drop differ. Compute both and give one procedural cause.
   **Worked Answer:** 5/95 ≈ 0.053 cm³ and 5/104 ≈ 0.048 cm³. Cause: inconsistent drop size — squeezing harder makes bigger drops, so fewer fill the mark. Fix: same dropper, same gentle pressure, same height.
3. **Q:** You need the mass of one grain of rice, but your balance only reads to the nearest 1 g. Rice grains average 0.02 g. How many grains must you weigh at once to keep the balance's error under 5%?
   **Worked Answer:** Error 0.5 g must be ≤ 5% of the weighed mass M: 0.5 ≤ 0.05M → M ≥ 10 g. Number of grains = 10/0.02 = 500 grains.

## Safety Notes
- Dry maize grains are a choking hazard and roll underfoot — no eating in the lab session; sweep spills at once.
- Droppers and straws touch water only — never share mouth contact; use the squeeze type, not mouth suction.
- Water spills on floors: wipe immediately, as always.
- Rulers used for pressing are not for flicking; collect them before free time.
