# Lesson 023: Unit Conversions Mastery

**Unit:** 1 — Measurement & Scientific Skills  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; UP-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Convert between mm, cm, m and km; g and kg; mL and L; s and min.
2. Use the conversion chain method — multiply by fractions equal to 1.
3. Preview the km/h to m/s conversion used all through physics.
4. Check an answer by asking whether it should be bigger or smaller.

## Materials
- Conversion cards: small card squares each bearing one fact (1000 mm = 1 m, 100 cm = 1 m, 1000 m = 1 km, 1000 g = 1 kg, 1000 mL = 1 L, 60 s = 1 min) — make from old cartons
- Board and markers
- Exercise books
- A 1 kg sugar packet, a 500 mL water bottle and a metre stick as physical anchors
- Optional: matatu speed limit sign photo drawn on the board (80 km/h)

## Vocabulary
- **conversion** (ubadilishaji) — changing a measurement from one unit to another
- **unit** (kipimo) — the standard a quantity is measured in
- **chain method** (njia ya mnyororo) — multiplying by fractions equal to 1 until units cancel
- **prefix** (kiambishi) — kilo- means ×1000, milli- means ÷1000, centi- means ÷100

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. How many centimetres are in 1 metre? And how many metres in 1 kilometre?
2. A packet holds 500 g of unga. How many packets make 1 kg?
3. A boda travels at 36 km/h. Do you think that is more or less than 10 m/s? Guess.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. 100 cm = 1 m; 1000 m = 1 km.
2. 1000 g ÷ 500 g = 2 packets.
3. Accept either guess now; 36 km/h = 10 m/s exactly, shown in the lesson.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out pre-test slips; collect before the card game.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — hold up the 1 kg sugar packet and ask: *the wholesaler's invoice says 0.001 tonnes, the packet says 1 kg, the mama mboga's scale reads 1000 g — who is cheating whom?* Answer: nobody — same mass, three units. Today we learn to move between units without being cheated.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — introduce the chain method on the board: write 2.5 m, multiply by (100 cm / 1 m), cancel the m, get 250 cm. The fraction equals 1, so the value never changes — only the unit. Pairs then draw conversion cards and solve each on mini-boards: 3500 m to km; 750 g to kg; 2 L to mL; 5 min to s; 25 mm to cm. Each pair must also write the "sanity check" sentence: *my number got bigger because centimetres are smaller than metres.* Teacher circulates asking: *is your answer bigger or smaller, and must it be?*
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate the prefixes table: kilo ×1000, centi ÷100, milli ÷1000. Preview speed: 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s, so km/h × (1000/3600) = m/s — that is, divide by 3.6. Demonstrate: 36 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s. Confront the misconception that "to convert you just move the decimal point anywhere" — the chain shows *why* the point moves.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark in class if time allows.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *multiply by 1 in a clever disguise and the units cancel themselves*. Homework: find one label at home (flour, soda, milk) and write it in two different units.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. How many centimetres in 1 metre, and how many metres in 1 kilometre? (mirrors pre-test)
2. How many 500 g packets make 1 kg? (mirrors pre-test)
3. Convert by the chain method: 4.2 km to m; 2500 mL to L.
4. Convert 54 km/h to m/s.
5. Challenge: a jerrican holds 20 L. A drum holds 0.21 m³. Which holds more, and by how many litres?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. 100 cm = 1 m; 1000 m = 1 km.
2. 2 packets.
3. 4.2 km × (1000 m / 1 km) = 4200 m. 2500 mL × (1 L / 1000 mL) = 2.5 L.
4. 54 ÷ 3.6 = 15 m/s.
5. 0.21 m³ = 0.21 × 1000 = 210 L. The drum holds more by 210 − 20 = 190 L.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Convert 72 km/h to m/s by the chain method, and explain why multiplying by 1000/3600 works.
   **Worked Answer:** 72 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 72 × 1000 / 3600 = 72 000 / 3600 = 20 m/s. Each bracket equals 1, so the value is unchanged — the km and h units cancel, leaving m/s. Dividing by 3.6 is the same operation compressed.
2. **Q:** A sprinter runs 100 m in 12.5 s. Find her speed in m/s, then convert it to km/h.
   **Worked Answer:** v = 100 m ÷ 12.5 s = 8 m/s. To km/h: 8 m/s × (1 km / 1000 m) × (3600 s / 1 h) = 8 × 3.6 = 28.8 km/h.
3. **Q:** A fundi cuts mabati sheets 2.4 m long from a roll. The order says "240 cm pieces". A learner claims the sheets are wrong. Settle it with the chain method.
   **Worked Answer:** 2.4 m × (100 cm / 1 m) = 240 cm. The sheets are exactly right — the order and the cut are the same length in different units. Lesson: always convert before accusing the fundi.

## Safety Notes
- This is a desk-based lesson; the only physical items are labelled shop goods — no hazards beyond normal classroom care.
- If the metre stick is used for pointing at the board, keep it away from faces.
- Card squares are small — keep them off the floor to avoid slips and away from very young siblings at home.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lessons 017–022 (length, mass, volume, area) have run; this lesson unifies all their units.
- Younger learners (10–13): master mm↔cm↔m↔km and g↔kg only; leave the ÷3.6 speed rule to the older group. Older learners: full chain method including two-step speed conversions.
- The sanity-check sentence (bigger or smaller?) is the highest-value habit in the whole unit — insist on it in every answer.
- Local anchors help: a soda is 300 mL, unga comes in 2 kg packets, matatu speed limit is 80 km/h (= 22.2 m/s, a nice extension).
- CBE assessment note: the chain-method workings in exercise books are direct evidence for the numeracy-in-science competency; record pre/post growth per learner.
