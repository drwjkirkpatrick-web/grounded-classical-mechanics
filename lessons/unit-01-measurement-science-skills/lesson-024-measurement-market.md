# Lesson 024: Measurement in the Market: Standards and Trade

**Unit:** 1 — Measurement & Scientific Skills  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.1; UP-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe the debe system and how dry goods are sold by volume in Kenyan markets.
2. Explain why trade needs agreed standards (kilogram, litre) and the role of KEBS and county inspectors.
3. Check a labelled "1 kg" packet against a balance and record the result honestly.
4. Discuss honest trade: what measurement error does to buyer and seller.

## Materials
- Debe tin (the rectangular 18 kg unga tin, reused everywhere) — clean and dry
- 1 kg sugar packet (unopened, labelled)
- Simple balance (class beam balance from Lesson 018, or a see-saw plank on a stone with known masses)
- Water and a jug to fill the debe
- Dry maize or beans for pouring practice (a small basin)
- Board and markers

## Vocabulary
- **debe** (debe) — the standard rectangular tin used to measure maize, beans, unga
- **standard** (kizio) — an agreed measurement everyone trusts
- **calibration** (urekebishaji) — checking an instrument against a known true value
- **KEBS** — Kenya Bureau of Standards, the body that guards measurement honesty
- **tare** (uzito wa chombo) — the weight of the empty container, subtracted from the total

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. In the market, how is maize usually measured when you buy it?
2. Why might a trader's "full debe" not always be the same amount?
3. What would you do if you suspected a shopkeeper's scale reads high?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. By volume, using a debe tin (or by weight at a scale).
2. Accept sensible ideas: heaping vs level tin, dented tins, wet vs dry grain, dishonest shaving of the tin's base.
3. Accept: check with a known weight, ask the county inspector, compare with another trader's scale.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out pre-test slips; collect before the market story.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — tell the story: two mamas buy "one debe" of beans from two traders; one feeds her family four days, the other six. *Same words, different amounts — who lost, and how do we fix it?* Board the word **standard**.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — three table investigations in groups. (a) Debe volume: fill the debe level with water, pour into 1 L jugs, count the litres; the class discovers a debe holds about 18 L — it is a *volume* standard, not a weight standard. (b) True kilo: place the labelled 1 kg sugar on the class balance against known masses; record the true mass and compare with the label. (c) Honest debe: measure beans with a heaped tin vs a struck (levelled) tin and pour both into jugs; record the difference. Groups post all numbers on the board.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate: a standard is an agreed amount; trade works only when both sides trust it. Volume measures (debe) are honest only if levelled; weight is safer because it ignores heaping and settling. KEBS and county weights-and-measures inspectors stamp scales; a stamped scale has been checked against true masses. Confront the misconception that "a big tin always means more" — a dented or thick-bottomed tin can hold less.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark in class if time allows.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *fair trade rests on standards everyone can check*. Homework: ask at home what one debe of maize costs this week, and watch how the trader levels it.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. How is maize usually measured in the market, and what is the tin called? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Give two reasons one trader's "full debe" may differ from another's. (mirrors pre-test)
3. Our class found a level debe holds about 18 L of water. A trader sells a debe of beans as "18 kg". Is that necessarily right? Explain.
4. A 1 kg sugar packet balances only 950 g of known masses. Write one honest sentence for your lab book about this result.
5. Challenge: why is buying by weight fairer than buying by heaped volume for a product that settles, like flour?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. By volume in a debe tin (or weighed on a scale).
2. Heaped vs level filling; dented or altered tins; settling of grain; wet grain weighs more.
3. Not necessarily. 18 L is a volume; the mass depends on the density of the beans. Only if the beans' bulk density is 1 kg/L (like water) would 18 L be 18 kg.
4. Accept honest reporting such as: "The packet labelled 1 kg measured 950 g on our balance, 50 g short; our balance may also have error, so we would recheck with a second instrument."
5. Heaped volume varies with how high it is piled and how much the product has settled; weight is the same however it is piled, so both buyer and seller get exactly what was agreed.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Design an inspection: you have one known 5 kg mass and must check 10 market scales in one morning. Describe the procedure and what you record.
   **Worked Answer:** At each stall: (1) note the scale reading with nothing on it (zero check); (2) place the 5 kg mass, record the reading; (3) repeat three times to test repeatability. A scale is honest if it reads 0 kg empty and 5.0 kg (within the allowed tolerance) loaded, three times running. Record stall, readings, time; report any scale reading high (cheats the buyer) to the county weights-and-measures office. One mass works everywhere because the kilogram is a shared standard.
2. **Q:** A debe of dry maize has a level volume of 18 L. Shelled maize has a bulk density of about 0.72 kg/L. What mass of maize is in one level debe? If maize sells at 60 KES per kg, what is the debe worth?
   **Worked Answer:** Mass = 18 L × 0.72 kg/L = 12.96 kg ≈ 13 kg. Value = 12.96 × 60 ≈ 778 KES (about 780 KES).
3. **Q:** A trader wets beans overnight so they swell and weigh more. From a physics and honesty standpoint, explain what the buyer loses, and one test the buyer could do.
   **Worked Answer:** Water adds mass but no food: the buyer pays bean price for water, and wet beans spoil faster in storage. Test: compare a sample against a known dry measure — a level tin of the suspect beans poured into jugs shows less grain volume (swollen beans pack differently), or weigh a fixed volume and compare with the known bulk density of dry beans (~0.72–0.8 kg/L); unusually heavy-for-volume beans suggest added water.

## Safety Notes
- Pouring grain and water: sweep spills immediately — scattered beans are a slipping hazard and attract pests.
- Do not eat the demonstration grain afterwards; it has been handled by many hands and poured through old tins.
- Debe tins can have sharp rim edges and internal rust — inspect before class; sand or tape any sharp lip.
- The balance plank on a stone must be low and stable; keep fingers clear of the pivot when loading masses.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lessons 017–018 (length and mass measurement, the class balance) have run.
- Younger learners (10–13): focus on the level-vs-heaped discovery and the story of honest trade. Older learners: run the density calculation and design the scale-inspection procedure.
- A real visit matters: if possible invite a market trader or county inspector to speak for ten minutes, or walk the class to observe (never confront) traders' scales after school.
- Local resource tip: debes are sold cheaply in every open-air market; a scale-repair fundi can explain how false scales are made and detected — a powerful honesty lesson.
- This lesson builds toward G10-PHY-1.1 ideas of physical quantities and standards, and links to density (Lesson 019) and pressure later.
- CBE assessment note: the honest-reporting sentence in Q4 practises the integrity strand of scientific investigation; file it in the learner record.
