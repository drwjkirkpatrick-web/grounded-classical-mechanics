# Lesson 026: Unit Review: Measurement Olympics

**Unit:** 1 — Measurement & Scientific Skills  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.1; JS-FE; UP-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Apply the whole unit — length, mass, volume, time, density and estimation — at timed stations.
2. Report measurements honestly, including uncertainty, even when the truth is embarrassing.
3. Convert units fluently under mild time pressure.
4. Review Unit 1 and identify personal gaps before Unit 2 (Forces).

## Materials
- All Unit 1 kit, laid out at six stations: metre stick and tape; balance and known masses; measuring jug, debe and bottles; stopwatch or phone; a stone and a bottle of water for density; estimation jar of beans
- Score sheets (one per team) and a results board
- Station cards with the task written on each
- Unit quiz papers (the post-test) for the final block
- Whistle or a tin to tap for rotations

## Vocabulary
- **accuracy** (usahihi) — how close a measurement is to the true value
- **estimation** (makadirio) — a reasoned guess made before measuring
- **uncertainty** (shaka ya kipimo) — the ± range you honestly attach to a result
- **review** (marudio) — going back over learned work to fix it in memory

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. List the six quantities we measured in this unit and one instrument for each.
2. A team measures a table as 1.48 m; the true value is 1.50 m. What is their error?
3. Why is it better science to write 1.48 m ± 0.02 m than just 1.5 m?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Length (metre stick), mass (balance), volume (jug/debe), time (stopwatch), density (mass ÷ volume), estimation (counting sample). Accept reasonable alternatives.
2. Error = 1.50 − 1.48 = 0.02 m (2 cm too small).
3. The ± shows how much you trust the value; it is honest about the instrument's limits.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out pre-test slips; explain the Olympics rules: teams rotate on the whistle; points for accuracy *and* for honest uncertainty; a team that guesses close but reports false precision loses points.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — announce the medal table is open. *Today the whole unit comes back in one competition — and the judges can spot a liar, because every station has a known true value.*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — six stations, about 2 minutes each with rotation on the whistle. **Length:** measure the marked line on the floor (true value hidden on the card's back). **Mass:** find the mass of the wrapped stone on the balance. **Volume:** how much water is in the unlabelled bottle? **Time:** call "stop" at exactly 30 s without looking at the clock. **Density:** compute the stone's density from the balance and displacement bottle. **Estimation:** how many beans in the jar — estimate first, then count a handful and scale up. Teams record estimate, measurement, and honest ± at every station.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — score together on the board: error = |measured − true|; percentage error = error ÷ true × 100%. Celebrate the most accurate teams *and* the most honest reporting. Quick-fire revision on the board: unit conversions (÷3.6 for km/h to m/s), V = πr²h, 1 m³ = 1000 L, slope = speed. Address the standing misconception that a small error means a bad scientist — error admitted and measured is good science; error hidden is not.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — the unit quiz, individual, collected for the learner record.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — award medals (drawn on the board). Takeaway: *you now own the tools of measurement; Unit 2 puts them to work on forces*. Homework: none — rest before the new unit.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. Name four quantities from this unit, one instrument for each, and its SI unit. (mirrors pre-test)
2. A team measures a jug as 480 mL; the true value is 500 mL. Find the error and the percentage error. (mirrors pre-test)
3. Convert: 3.5 km to m; 90 km/h to m/s.
4. A tank is a cylinder with r = 0.5 m and h = 1.0 m. Find its volume in litres (π = 3.14).
5. Challenge: at the estimation station, team A guessed 900 beans and counted 40 in a handful from a jar holding about 20 handfuls. Whose method is better science — the guesser or the sampler — and why?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any four: length — metre stick — m; mass — balance — kg; volume — jug — m³ (or L); time — stopwatch — s; density — balance + jug — kg/m³.
2. Error = 500 − 480 = 20 mL. Percentage error = 20/500 × 100% = 4%.
3. 3.5 km × 1000 = 3500 m. 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s.
4. V = 3.14 × 0.5² × 1.0 = 0.785 m³ = 785 L.
5. The sampler: 40 × 20 = 800 beans from a repeatable method that can be checked; the bare guess of 900 cannot be defended or improved. Estimation by sampling scales up evidence.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Tank volume comes from V = πr²h with both r and h measured to ±3%. Estimate the worst-case percentage error in V.
   **Worked Answer:** V depends on r² and h. A 3% high r makes r² about (1.03)² ≈ 1.061, i.e. +6.1%; a 3% high h adds +3%. Worst case both high: 1.061 × 1.03 ≈ 1.093, so V can be up to about 9% high (or 9% low if both read low). Errors compound — squared quantities double their share.
2. **Q:** At the time station, a learner calls "stop" at 33 s when the target was 30 s. At the density station her team's stone reads 780 g with volume 300 mL. Find the density and the time-call percentage error.
   **Worked Answer:** Density = m/V = 780 g / 300 mL = 2.6 g/mL = 2600 kg/m³ (a typical stone). Time error = (33 − 30)/30 × 100% = 10% late.
3. **Q:** Design the fairest scoring rule for the Olympics so that lucky guesses cannot beat careful measurement. Give the rule and one example.
   **Worked Answer:** Score each station on two parts: percentage error (accuracy) and whether the true value lies inside the team's stated ± range (honesty). Example: team X reports 1.50 m ± 0.05 m, true 1.52 m — inside range: full honesty points, small error points. Team Y reports 1.520 m with no ±, true 1.52 m — right by luck but no stated uncertainty, so reduced points. Rule: no uncertainty stated, no accuracy medal.

## Safety Notes
- Rotations are the main risk: teams walk, do not run, on the whistle; keep station walkways clear of bags and basins.
- Water at the volume and density stations — mop spills at once; keep water away from the balance pans' pivots.
- The stone at the mass/density stations: pass hand to hand, never throw; keep away from bare feet.
- Beans are counted, not eaten (many hands), and swept up fully — they roll underfoot.
- Stopwatch phones stay at the time station, not in pockets, until the lesson ends.

## Teacher Notes
- This is the Unit 1 capstone review; it assumes Lessons 017–025 have all run. Unit 2 (Forces in Everyday Life) begins next.
- Younger learners (10–13): run four stations (length, mass, volume, time) and drop the density/error algebra. Older learners: all six stations plus the advanced error-compounding question.
- Preparation is everything: write the true values on the backs of the station cards the night before, and pre-check every instrument's zero.
- Teams of 4–5 work best; mix ages so an older learner anchors the calculations at the density station.
- Local resource tip: a posho-mill scale or shop spring balance borrowed for the day adds a real-world mass station and a talking point from Lesson 024.
- CBE assessment note: the collected quiz and the team score sheets together give a full unit record — accuracy, honest reporting and pre/post growth per learner.
