# Lesson 035: Friction II: When We Need It

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** UP-FE; JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Give at least four everyday situations where friction is useful or essential.
2. Explain how tyre treads and shoe grips use friction, including in mud.
3. Describe what would go wrong in a frictionless world (walking, holding, braking, writing).
4. Link friction at brakes to the heat produced.

## Materials
- Chalk (writing depends on friction)
- A bicycle or boda tyre with tread — or a cut tread sample from a fundi
- A length of sisal rope for the grip test
- A smooth stick and a rough jembe handle to compare grips
- (Optional) wet patch of ground for the mud discussion

## Vocabulary
- **grip** (kushika) — friction holding things in place
- **tread** (mino ya tairi) — the patterned grooves in a tyre or shoe sole
- **brake** (breki) — a device that uses friction to slow a wheel
- **skid** (kuteleza) — sliding with little grip, wheels locked
- **traction** — the friction that lets wheels and feet push backwards to go forwards

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. Name one thing you did this morning that would be impossible without friction.
2. Why do boda tyres have grooves cut into the rubber?
3. Would life be easier or harder with no friction at all? Give one reason.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Walking, holding a cup, dressing, writing — accept any honest answer.
2. For grip on the road (accept "so as not to slide").
3. Harder — we would slip and drop everything. Accept any reasoned answer.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — slips out; tyre sample at the front.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — the one-minute skit: teacher "wakes up in a frictionless world" — feet shoot out, cup slides off the table, chalk will not write. Learners supply the disasters. Laughter, then the question: *so is friction our enemy or our friend?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — carousel of four mini-demos. (a) Writing: chalk writes on the rough board but not on a polished tile — friction scrapes chalk dust off. (b) Grip test: learners hold the smooth stick then the rough jembe handle while a partner gently twists — rough grips win. (c) Tyre tread: pass the tread sample around; pour a little water on it and show the channels; connect to murram roads in rain. (d) Walking physics: push a foot backwards against the floor (without slipping) — the floor pushes you forwards; that push IS friction.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate on the board, two columns: *friction helps* (walking, braking, holding, writing, knots, nails) vs *friction costs* (wears soles, heats machines, wastes fuel). Confront the misconception from Lesson 034: friction is not just a nuisance tax on motion — remove it and transport, sport and cooking all collapse.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *we spend fuel fighting friction, and we would spend our lives helpless without it.* Homework: list three more "friction helps" examples at home and one "friction costs".

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. Name two things that would be impossible without friction. (mirrors pre-test)
2. Why do boda tyres have tread grooves? Answer fully. (mirrors pre-test)
3. When you walk, which way does your foot push the ground, and which way does friction push you?
4. Why does chalk write on the rough board but not on a polished tile?
5. Challenge: boda brakes glow hot after a long hill. Where does the heat come from?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any two: walking, holding objects, writing, braking, tying knots, clothes staying on.
2. Tread edges bite into the road for grip, and the channels push water (and mud) aside so rubber touches the road instead of skating on water.
3. Foot pushes the ground backwards; friction pushes the foot (and you) forwards.
4. Writing needs friction to scrape chalk particles off onto the surface; the polished tile gives almost no friction, so nothing rubs off.
5. Brake pads rub the wheel/drum; friction converts the boda's kinetic energy into heat, slowing it down.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Explain why wet murram roads reduce friction, and how tyre tread reduces the danger.
   **Worked Answer:** On a dry road, rubber micro-welds to grit for high grip. A water layer separates the surfaces, removing most contact welds, so friction falls sharply and wheels skid or spin. Tread grooves give the water an escape path — the leading edge of each block squeezes water into the channels, letting the rubber blocks regain road contact behind it. A smooth tyre on water just planes over the film (aquaplaning).
2. **Q:** A braking boda of mass 150 kg slows from 20 m/s to rest. Its kinetic energy was ½mv² = ½ × 150 × 400 = 30 000 J. If 70% of that energy heats the brakes, how much heat do the brakes absorb?
   **Worked Answer:** Heat = 0.70 × 30 000 = 21 000 J = 21 kJ. This is why brakes fade on long descents — the metal cannot dump that heat fast enough, which is also why matatu drivers use low gears downhill.
3. **Q:** A learner says: "Friction is useless because it wastes energy in machines." Write a two-sentence reply that a fundi would agree with.
   **Worked Answer:** In bearings and engines we do fight friction with oil, because there it wastes fuel and wears parts. But the same physics gives us walking, gripping, braking and fastening — a machine world without friction could not be built, held or stopped at all.

## Safety Notes
- Skit and grip tests: no running "to prove slipping"; the comedy is acted, not performed at speed. Clear the demo floor of bags.
- Twisting-stick grip test: partners twist gently; stop at any sign of pain — the point is comparison, not strength.
- Water for the tread demo: a cup is enough; mop spills immediately to avoid real slips on cement.
- Tyre sample from a fundi may have exposed wire bead ends — wrap cut edges in tape before it circulates.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lesson 034 (friction opposes sliding); today's job is to overturn "friction = bad" before Lesson 036 shows where we *do* reduce it.
- Younger learners (10–13): the skit and the four demos carry the lesson; aim for three named examples each. Older learners: add the walking free-body diagram and the brake energy calculation.
- A boda stage or bicycle fundi will usually give a worn tyre for free; the more worn, the better the discussion about bald tyres in rain.
- Kenyan athletics hook: sprinters' spikes and Kipchoge's shoe grip on wet tarmac — same physics.
- CBE assessment note: listen for correct direction language ("foot pushes back, friction pushes forward") — record it as communication-competency evidence.
