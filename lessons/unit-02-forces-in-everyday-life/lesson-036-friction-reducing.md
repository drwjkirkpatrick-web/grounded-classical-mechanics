# Lesson 036: Friction III: Reducing It with Oil, Rollers and Bearings

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; G10-PHY-1.6  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate three ways to reduce friction: lubrication, rollers, and ball bearings.
2. Measure and compare pull forces for sliding vs rolling a loaded debe.
3. Explain why rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction.
4. Name machines around them that use each method (hinges, mkokoteni axles, boda hubs).

## Materials
- Cooking oil or used motor oil (a small tin; fundi shops give used oil free)
- 4–6 round sticks as rollers (broom handles, smooth poles, or thick dowels)
- A debe or heavy jerrican, plus stones for load
- An old ball bearing from a boda wheel hub (jua kali)
- A plank or smooth board as a runway
- String and the spring balance from Lesson 029

## Vocabulary
- **lubricant** (mafuta) — oil or grease that separates rubbing surfaces
- **roller** — a round stick that turns sliding into rolling
- **ball bearing** (bea) — steel balls in a ring that let hubs spin with tiny friction
- **rolling friction** — the small resistance when a round object rolls
- **sliding friction** — the larger resistance when surfaces rub

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. A door hinge squeals. What simple home cure do people use, and what do you think it does?
2. Predict: is it easier to drag a loaded debe across the floor, or to roll it over round sticks?
3. Where in a bicycle do you think ball bearings sit? Guess if unsure.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Oil/grease; it makes the rubbing parts slippery (separates the surfaces). Accept everyday answers.
2. Rolling over sticks should be easier. Accept any prediction with a reason.
3. In the wheel hubs (and pedals, chain wheel). Accept guesses.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — slips out; rollers and debe staged at the runway.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — pass the ball bearing around: spin it on a finger. Ask: *a boda wheel spins thousands of times per trip — what stops its hub from grinding to dust?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — build and run the roller test (see Hands-On Build). Teams measure pull force in three conditions on the same plank: debe sliding dry, debe sliding with oil wiped on the plank, debe on rollers. Record pulls on the board table. Then disassemble the ball bearing if possible (or roll the loose balls between two lids) to feel how little it resists.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate the three methods: oil puts a slippery film between surfaces so the micro-welds never form; rollers replace rubbing with rolling, so contact points keep renewing instead of dragging; bearings are captured rollers for spinning shafts. Confront the misconception: "oil makes things lighter" — the load is unchanged; only the resistance to sliding is removed.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *replace rubbing with rolling, or separate the surfaces with oil.* Homework: find one oiled or greased point at home (hinge, jiko damper, padlock) and name it tomorrow.

## Hands-On Build: Roller Debe Test Rig
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; gather rollers and oil the day before.*

### Parts
- Debe or jerrican — 1, loaded with stones to a fixed mass — keep the SAME load for all trials
- Round sticks — 4–6, similar diameter — broom handles or smooth poles; no square sticks
- Plank runway — about 1.5 m — any smooth board; cement veranda works
- String + spring balance — 1 set — the rubber-strip meter works if calibrated
- Oil — a spoonful on a rag — cooking oil; used engine oil stains, warn learners
- Ball bearing — 1 — from a boda hub; a fundi will open one for you

### Workflow
1. Load the debe and weigh the load once; write the mass on the board.
2. Tie the string to the debe handle, lay it on the dry plank. **Checkpoint:** pulling with the spring balance, the debe slides at steady speed when you hold the pull reading steady — practise reading while moving.
3. Record the steady pull force for dry sliding (trial 1, three repeats, take the middle value).
4. Wipe a thin oil film on the plank; repeat the pull and record (trial 2).
5. Lay 4 rollers across the plank, set the debe on top, and pull steadily so the debe advances over the rollers; a partner moves freed rollers from back to front. Record the pull (trial 3). **Checkpoint:** rollers must roll, not skid — if they skid, the reading is sliding friction again; clean and retry.
6. Compare the three forces on the board and compute the reduction.

### Common Mistakes
- **Changing the load between trials** — unfair test; one load, one debe, one plank.
- **Reading the balance at the jerk of starting** — starting friction is higher; read during steady motion.
- **Too much oil** — a dripping puddle is a slip hazard and wastes the demo; a thin film is the physics.
- **Rollers too few** — fewer than three and the debe tips between gaps; use at least four.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. What does oil do to a squeaky hinge, in surface terms? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Which needed less pull in our test — sliding the debe or rolling it? By how much (use class results)? (mirrors pre-test)
3. Why is rolling friction smaller than sliding friction?
4. Name the three friction-reducing methods and one machine using each.
5. Challenge: why must you keep re-oiling a hinge, but a ball bearing hub runs for months on grease?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. It coats both surfaces with a slippery film so they no longer touch and weld directly.
2. Rolling needed far less (use the class numbers, e.g. from 18 N to 3 N).
3. Rolling contact points are always fresh and nothing drags across; in sliding, surface welds are continuously formed and torn.
4. Lubricant — hinge/jiko damper; rollers — moving heavy loads on poles, conveyor; ball bearings — boda and bicycle hubs.
5. Oil in an open hinge is squeezed out, washed by rain and collects dust; a bearing is enclosed, so its grease stays put and the balls keep rolling on smooth races.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Our class measured an 18 N pull sliding the debe dry, and 3 N on rollers, same load. Compute the percentage reduction in friction force and say where the "saved" effort was going before.
   **Worked Answer:** Reduction = (18 − 3)/18 × 100% = 83%. Before, 15 N of every pull was being converted by sliding friction into heat, sound and surface wear at the debe–plank contact; on rollers only 3 N is lost that way.
2. **Q:** A 40 kg load (weight 400 N) slides with friction 0.30 of its weight. A worker wants to keep it moving at steady speed. What pull is needed? What pull if rollers cut friction by 80%?
   **Worked Answer:** Sliding friction = 0.30 × 400 = 120 N, so steady pull = 120 N. With 80% reduction, friction = 0.20 × 120 = 24 N — one fifth the effort, which is why one person can move a drum that three could not drag.
3. **Q:** Bearings still fail eventually. Give two physical reasons and one maintenance habit that extends their life.
   **Worked Answer:** Dust/grit enters and grinds the balls (abrasive wear); grease dries or washes out so metal welds return (pitting); water causes rust. Habit: keep them greased and covered — that is why fundis pack hubs with grease and fit dust caps.

## Safety Notes
- Oil on the floor is a serious slip hazard: oil only the plank, wipe excess, and assign one learner as "spill marshal" with a rag.
- The loaded debe is heavy — toes clear of its path; lower, never drop, when loading stones.
- Rollers can shoot out from under the load — keep fingers off the top of rollers and hands away from the debe's leading edge.
- Used engine oil is an irritant and possibly contaminated; prefer cooking oil, and everyone washes hands after.
- Degrease and wrap any sharp race edges of the opened bearing before it circulates.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lessons 034–035; this completes the friction trio before the lab (Lesson 037) quantifies it.
- Younger learners (10–13): the three measured pulls tell the whole story — get them to say "rolling is easier because…" in full sentences. Older learners: run the percentage and coefficient calculations with real class data.
- Ball bearings: any boda repair corner has dead hubs; ask the fundi to cut one open with a grinder the day before (his job, not the class's).
- Kijito windmills and posho mill shafts are local bearing showcases if a field walk is possible.
- CBE assessment note: the repeated-measures table (three trials, middle value) is evidence for the data-handling competency; record who controlled variables properly.
