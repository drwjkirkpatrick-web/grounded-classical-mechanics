# Lesson 037: Friction Lab: Pull Tests on Surfaces

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; G10-PHY-1.3  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Run a fair pull test: measure the force to slide a loaded block across different surfaces.
2. Record results in a table and plot friction force against load.
3. Conclude that friction grows with load and depends on the surface pair.
4. Read a gradient from their graph as the surfaces' "grip number" (older learners: coefficient of friction).

## Materials
- Wood block (about 10 × 10 × 4 cm) with a screw eye or string loop
- Spring balance (0–20 N) — or the calibrated rubber-strip meter
- Surfaces: bare wood plank, sandpaper sheet, grass/dust patch, oiled wood strip
- Stones or known masses for loading the block (mark them 0.5 kg, 1 kg)
- Graph paper or squared exercise-book pages, rulers, pencils
- Board table prepared before class

## Vocabulary
- **fair test** (jaribio la haki) — change one thing, keep the rest the same
- **load** (mzigo) — the weight pressing the surfaces together
- **gradient** (mwelekeo) — rise ÷ run of a straight-line graph
- **coefficient of friction** (μ) — friction force ÷ load; the grip number of a surface pair
- **variable** (kigeuzi) — a quantity that can change in an experiment

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. In a test of "which surface grips most", name two things you must keep the same.
2. Predict the order, most friction to least: sandpaper, oiled wood, grass, bare wood.
3. If you double the load on a block, what do you predict happens to the pull force needed?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Same block, same load, same pull speed/height, same balance — any two.
2. Any sensible order; the real test will decide (typical: grass/sandpaper > bare wood > oiled wood).
3. It increases — likely doubles. Accept predictions.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — slips out; surfaces laid in four stations.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — show the block and ask: *a road engineer must choose surfacing for a steep hill — how would she measure "grip" with nothing but our kit?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — run the lab (see Hands-On Build). Groups rotate: pull the block at steady speed on each surface with one fixed load, three repeats, middle value recorded. Then, on their assigned surface, repeat with double load. Results go to the board table as they come in.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — plot the class graph live: friction (y) vs load (x) for two surfaces. The points make two straight lines through the origin of different steepness. Steeper = grippier; gradient = friction ÷ load = μ. Confront the misconception: friction does NOT depend on the contact area (slide the block on its edge — same reading) — it depends on load and surface pair.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect with the graphs.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *grip has a number, and we measured it.* Homework: write the μ ranking of our four surfaces from the class table.

## Hands-On Build: The Pull-Test Rig
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; cut blocks and collect surfaces the day before.*

### Parts
- Wood block with screw eye — 1 per group — smooth one face with sandpaper, keep it dry
- Spring balance — 1 per group or shared — check its zero before class
- Surface set — 4 — plank, sandpaper pinned flat, grass/dust tray, oiled strip (label it, warn about slips)
- Load stones — 2–3 per group, marked with mass — or water bottles: 1 L = 1 kg
- String — short length to join balance to block
- Table slips — pre-drawn results tables per group

### Workflow
1. Zero the spring balance with the block hanging (or tare the strip meter). **Checkpoint:** balance reads the block's weight when still — sanity-check the instrument before trusting pulls.
2. Attach the string low on the block so it pulls level, not tipped.
3. Pull slowly along surface 1 until the block slides at steady speed; partner reads the steady force. Repeat three times; record the middle value.
4. Repeat on the other three surfaces, same load.
5. Add the second stone (double load) and repeat on your group's assigned surface.
6. Report all values to the board table. **Checkpoint:** each row has surface, load, three pulls, middle value — no orphans.

### Common Mistakes
- **Pulling at an angle** — lifting while pulling reduces the load's effect; keep the string level.
- **Reading the starting jerk** — static friction at breakaway is higher; read only during steady sliding.
- **Letting sandpaper curl** — pin or tape all four corners or the block climbs the wrinkles.
- **Mixing units** — balance marked in kg must be converted: pull (N) = reading (kg) × 10.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. Name two things we kept the same in every pull test. (mirrors pre-test)
2. Order our four surfaces from most to least friction, using the class results. (mirrors pre-test)
3. We doubled the load and the pull force roughly doubled. Write that conclusion in one sentence.
4. Why did we take three pulls and use the middle value?
5. Challenge: block on bare wood: pull 6 N at 2 kg load, 12 N at 4 kg. What is μ for this pair?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Same block, same balance, same pull style, same surfaces set — any two.
2. Per class data (typically: grass ≈ sandpaper > bare wood > oiled wood).
3. Friction force is proportional to the load pressing the surfaces together.
4. Single readings scatter (hand wobble, bumps); the middle value rejects flukes without hiding real grip.
5. Load 2 kg → 20 N, μ = 6/20 = 0.3; load 4 kg → 40 N, μ = 12/40 = 0.3. Constant μ = 0.3.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** From the class graph of friction vs load, explain what the gradient means and find μ for sandpaper if the line passes through (30 N, 9 N).
   **Worked Answer:** Gradient = rise/run = friction/load = μ, the coefficient of friction for that surface pair. μ = 9 N ÷ 30 N = 0.3. (The load axis is in newtons: 30 N = 3 kg × 10 N/kg.)
2. **Q:** A 5 kg crate on a floor with μ = 0.4 needs what steady pull? The owner then oils the floor, halving μ. New pull?
   **Worked Answer:** Weight = 5 × 10 = 50 N. Friction = μ × load = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N, so steady pull = 20 N. Oiled: μ = 0.2 → friction = 0.2 × 50 = 10 N.
3. **Q:** Your group's grass readings were 8 N, 15 N, 9 N. Identify the outlier, justify rejecting it, and state the value you report.
   **Worked Answer:** 15 N is the outlier — nearly double the other two, most likely a snag or misread, not a property of grass (nothing changed between trials). Report the middle/median value 9 N, and note that repeating the suspect trial is better than averaging all three (mean would give a misleading 10.7 N).

## Safety Notes
- Oiled-wood station: label it, keep it in a tray, and wipe spills instantly — oiled cement is invisible and dangerous.
- Spring balances stretched past range are ruined and can snap back — pull within the scale and release gently.
- The grass/dust tray stays on the ground; no throwing of soil; learners with dust sensitivity stand back during that pull.
- Stone loads: place, do not drop — bare toes and dropped stones do not mix.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lessons 034–036; this is the unit's first true quantitative lab — protect time for the graph.
- Younger learners (10–13): they can own the table and the ranking; teacher draws the graph with them. Older learners: they plot, find gradients, and carry μ forward to problem sets.
- If only one balance exists, run the lab as teacher-led demos with rotating reader pairs — still collect full class data.
- Sandpaper from the hardware fundi: medium grit grips best; fine grit is closer to wood and confuses the ranking.
- CBE assessment note: this lab is prime evidence for the "scientific investigation" and "data handling" competencies — file the graphs in learner records.
