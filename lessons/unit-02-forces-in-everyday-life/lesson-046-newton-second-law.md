# Lesson 046: Newton's Second Law: F = ma

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate that the same push accelerates an empty debe more than a full debe.
2. State Newton's second law as F = ma and say what each symbol means.
3. Build a falling-mass trolley (toy car and hanger pulley) and measure how acceleration changes with force.
4. Calculate a force, mass, or acceleration given the other two (g = 10 N/kg).

## Materials
- One debe (20-litre jerrican), plus water or sand to fill it
- Toy car (jua kali wire-and-tin car or small shop toy) with free-rolling wheels
- Strong thin string, about 2 m (fishing line or smooth sisal)
- 6–8 similar stones, 50–100 g each, tied or bagged as hookable masses
- Wire coat hanger (the pulley) — ask a jua kali fundi; one per group
- Tape measure or metre stick; stopwatch or phone timer
- Smooth table or a flat mabati sheet as the runway

## Vocabulary
- **acceleration** (mwendo kasi) — how quickly velocity changes, in m/s²
- **net force** (nguvu kamili) — the total unbalanced force on a body, in newtons (N)
- **mass** (uzani) — amount of matter, in kg
- **newton** — the force unit; roughly the weight of 100 g
- **friction** (msuguano) — the rubbing force that steals some of our applied force

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book. Purpose: baseline for measuring growth against the post-test.*

1. You push an empty debe and a full debe with the same effort. Which gains speed faster?
2. In F = ma, what do F, m and a stand for?
3. A force of 10 N acts on a mass of 2 kg. What is the acceleration?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The empty debe — less mass to set moving.
2. F = force (N), m = mass (kg), a = acceleration (m/s²).
3. a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s².

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — pre-test slips; collect before the demo.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — push the empty debe across the floor with one finger-push, then the full debe with the same push. The contrast is comic and obvious. Ask: *same force, different result — what rule is hiding here?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — groups build the falling-mass trolley (see Hands-On Build). First run: 2 stones hanging. Time the car over a 1 m runway. Then 4 stones, then 6. Record times on the board table; faster fall of time = greater acceleration. Groups with time add stones *to the car* instead and see acceleration drop for the same hanging mass.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate on the board: F = ma. More force → more acceleration (our times fell as stones increased); more mass → less acceleration (full debe, loaded car). Units: force in newtons, mass in kg, acceleration in m/s². Work one class example: 1 L of water = 1 kg, weight = mg = 1 × 10 = 10 N pulls the trolley. Confront the misconception that a *constant* force makes constant *speed* — it makes constant *acceleration*; speed keeps growing.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *big push or small load — either way, more acceleration.* Homework: estimate the force your foot applies to a football (use F = ma with any guessed numbers) and bring it tomorrow.

## Hands-On Build: Falling-Mass Trolley
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; cut string and bag stones the day before.*

### Parts
- Toy car — 1 per group — wheels must spin freely (test by blowing)
- Wire hanger — 1 per group — bent into an L-shaped hook that clamps the table edge; the string runs over the smooth wire bend as a pulley
- String — about 2 m — tie a loop at each end
- Stones — 6–8, bagged in small cloth or paper bags with loops — weigh on a kitchen scale if available, else compare by hand
- Tape measure or metre stick — to mark a 1 m runway

### Workflow
1. Clamp the bent hanger to the end of the table so the wire curve overhangs the edge — this is the pulley. Tape it firmly.
2. Tie one string end to the car, pass the string over the hanger bend, and hang the looped stones off the other end.
3. Mark a 1 m runway on the table with chalk; the car starts at the back mark, string taut, stones just below the pulley.
4. **Checkpoint:** hang 2 stones and release the car once — it should roll straight to the pulley without the string slipping off the wire. Re-align the hanger if it wanders.
5. Time the car over the 1 m with 2 hanging stones; record. Repeat with 4 stones, then 6.
6. Final measurement: compare the three times — more hanging stones (more force) should give shorter times (more acceleration).

### Common Mistakes
- **String rubbing the table edge** — it must run over the hanger wire, not the wood, or friction swallows the force.
- **Car not starting straight** — the car twists and scrubs speed; start it pointing exactly at the pulley.
- **Letting stones hit the floor early** — hang enough string that stones stay airborne for the whole 1 m.
- **Timing from the wrong moment** — start the watch at release, not when the car "gets going".

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test (growth measure); 3–5 probe the day's new idea.*

1. Same push, empty debe vs full debe: which accelerates more? State the law that explains it. (mirrors pre-test)
2. A force of 10 N acts on 2 kg. Find the acceleration. (mirrors pre-test)
3. In our trolley, hanging stones went 2, 4, 6 — and the 1 m times fell. Use F = ma to explain.
4. A mkokoteni of mass 60 kg is pushed with a net force of 30 N. Find its acceleration.
5. Challenge: we added stones *to the car* and acceleration fell even though the hanging force stayed the same. Explain.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The empty debe; Newton's second law, F = ma — same F, smaller m, bigger a.
2. a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s².
3. More stones = more weight = more force on the same car, so greater acceleration and a shorter time over 1 m.
4. a = F/m = 30/60 = 0.5 m/s².
5. The hanging force now accelerates car + added stones: same F, larger total m, so smaller a.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Quantitative or multi-step reasoning. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** A 2 kg trolley accelerates at 1.5 m/s² under a hanging mass. Compute the net force on the trolley, then discuss friction's role if the hanging mass actually weighs 5 N.
   **Worked Answer:** Net force F = ma = 2 × 1.5 = 3 N. But the hanging weight supplies 5 N, so 5 − 3 = 2 N is being lost to friction in the wheels and pulley. Friction is why real trolleys under-accelerate versus the ideal F = ma prediction; oiling axles or using better wheels closes the gap.
2. **Q:** What hanging mass (in grams) would give the ideal 2 kg trolley an acceleration of 2 m/s², ignoring friction? (g = 10 N/kg)
   **Worked Answer:** Required force F = ma = 2 × 2 = 4 N. Weight W = mg, so m = W/g = 4/10 = 0.4 kg = 400 g. (Strictly, the hanging mass accelerates too, so a little more than 400 g is needed — a good extension discussion.)
3. **Q:** A posho-mill engine pulls a 100 kg cart with a net 200 N force. Find the acceleration, then the speed after 5 s from rest.
   **Worked Answer:** a = F/m = 200/100 = 2 m/s². v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m/s — about cycling speed, from a modest push on a heavy cart.

## Safety Notes
- Falling stones bruise toes — keep the drop zone clear, catch the stones by hand at the end of each run, and wear closed shoes.
- The wire hanger ends are sharp; a fundi should fold the tips, or wrap them with tape before class.
- A full debe is heavy — two learners lift it together; never swing it by the handle alone.
- Do not let the trolley fly off the table end; place a stop (book or hand) beyond the pulley line.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Lesson 045 (inertia) explains why mass resists acceleration; keep that language alive here ("mass fights back").
- Ages 10–13: qualitative only — same push, different debe; more stones, faster car. Leave the formula at recognition level. Ages 14–17: full F = ma calculations, friction discussion, and the 400 g prediction test.
- Local tips: jua kali wire cars are perfect and cheap; test wheels beforehand and oil axles with cooking oil. If no hanger exists, run the string over a smooth nail or a pen barrel clamped at the edge.
- A kitchen scale or a spring balance from the market makes the stones' weights real; otherwise label bags A, B, C by count.
- Assessment: post-test Q4 is the CBE computation checkpoint; record per-learner growth from pre-test Q3.
