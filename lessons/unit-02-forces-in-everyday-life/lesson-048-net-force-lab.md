# Lesson 048: Net Force and Acceleration Lab

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; G10-PHY-1.6  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Set up a falling-mass trolley that accelerates along a smooth board.
2. Measure acceleration with a stopwatch and a marked track.
3. Show that doubling the pulling force doubles the acceleration (a ∝ F).
4. Plot an acceleration vs force graph that passes through the origin.

## Materials
- Toy trolley (or a jua kali roller cart with free-spinning wheels)
- Smooth board or tabletop, at least 1.5 m long (a well-planed mbao plank works)
- String, about 2 m (smooth sisal or fishing line)
- Wire hanger bent into a pulley, or a real pulley from a jua kali fundi
- Stones, 5–6 similar ones weighed in advance (50 g, 100 g, 150 g … on a market scale)
- Stopwatch (phone clock is fine), cellotape, metre stick, chalk

## Vocabulary
- **net force** (jumla ya nguvu) — the overall unbalanced force that changes motion
- **acceleration** (mwendo kasi) — how quickly velocity changes, in m/s²
- **friction** (msuguano) — rubbing force that steals part of the pull
- **gradient** (mwinamo) — slope of a graph; rise over run

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. A trolley is pulled harder and harder. What happens to its acceleration?
2. Write the equation linking force, mass and acceleration.
3. If a graph of acceleration against force is a straight line through the origin, what does that tell us?
4. A 100 g stone hangs from a string. What force does it pull with? (Use g = 10 N/kg.)

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Acceleration increases (accept: it speeds up faster).
2. F = ma.
3. Acceleration is directly proportional to force (a ∝ F).
4. F = mg = 0.1 kg × 10 N/kg = 1 N.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect before the demo.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — hang a single small stone over the pulley and let the trolley zip along the board. Ask: *what is really pulling this trolley — the stone or gravity?* Board the question.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs build and run the lab (see Hands-On Build). Each pair times the trolley over a 1 m mark with 50 g, 100 g, 150 g, then 200 g hanging. They compute a = 2d/t² for each trial and record a results table on the board.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate: plot a against F together on the board. The points fall near a straight line through the origin: acceleration is proportional to net force (Newton's second law). Confront the misconception that "a constant force gives constant speed" — the trolley keeps speeding up the whole run, so force feeds *acceleration*, not speed.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark from the board key.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *net force makes acceleration, in proportion*. Homework: describe where a boda rider feels this law (harder throttle → faster speed gain).

## Hands-On Build: Falling-Mass Trolley
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; weigh and label the stones the day before.*

### Parts
- Toy trolley — 1 per pair — toy shop, or a fundi's roller cart; wheels must spin freely
- Board — 1.5 m+ — planed mbao plank or smooth tabletop
- String — 2 m — fishing line or smooth sisal; rough rope adds friction
- Hanger pulley — 1 — bend a wire hanger on a nail at the board's end; groove must be smooth
- Stones — 50 g to 200 g, labelled — weighed on a market spring balance
- Tape and chalk — to mark start and 1 m lines

### Workflow
1. Tape a start line and a 1 m finish line on the board.
2. Fix the pulley at the board end; lay the string from trolley over the pulley.
3. Hang the 50 g stone so it can fall freely to the floor without swinging.
4. **Checkpoint:** give the trolley a tiny push — it should keep rolling almost to the end. If it stops quickly, clean wheels or tilt the board slightly to balance friction.
5. Release the trolley from rest; time it over the 1 m. Repeat twice, take the average.
6. Repeat with 100 g, 150 g, 200 g stones. Keep the trolley mass unchanged.
7. Calculate a = 2d/t² for each mass and fill the class table.

### Common Mistakes
- **Adding stones to the trolley too** — that changes the mass being accelerated; only the hanging mass changes.
- **String rubbing the board edge** — raises friction; align the pulley so the string runs free.
- **Timing from a push-start** — release from rest, hand flat against the trolley then lifted straight up.
- **Using one timing only** — reaction time errors; always average two or three runs.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. What happens to a trolley's acceleration when the pulling force increases? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Write the equation linking force, mass and acceleration. (mirrors pre-test)
3. A pair timed 1 m in 1.0 s from rest. Find the acceleration.
4. Their graph of a against F is a straight line through (0,0). State the conclusion in one sentence.
5. Challenge: why must the trolley wheels be clean and free-spinning for a fair test?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Acceleration increases in proportion (doubles when force doubles).
2. F = ma.
3. a = 2d/t² = 2 × 1 / (1.0)² = 2 m/s².
4. Acceleration is directly proportional to the net force (Newton's second law).
5. Dirty wheels add friction, so part of the pull is wasted — the measured net force would be less than the stone's weight and spoil the straight line.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** A class plots a (y-axis, m/s²) against F (x-axis, N) and gets a gradient of 0.67 kg⁻¹. Find the total mass of the moving system.
   **Worked Answer:** From a = F/m, gradient = a/F = 1/m. So m = 1/gradient = 1/0.67 ≈ 1.5 kg. That is the trolley plus the hanging stones — the whole system that the force must accelerate.
2. **Q:** Their line does not pass through the origin but cuts the F-axis at 0.3 N. Explain, given the board was not tilted to balance friction.
   **Worked Answer:** About 0.3 N of friction acts on the trolley. The first 0.3 N of hanging weight cancels friction before any acceleration begins, so the line shifts right by 0.3 N. Only the force *above* 0.3 N is net force: a = (F − 0.3)/m. Tilting the board until the trolley just drifts would remove the intercept.
3. **Q:** A 200 g stone hangs from a 1.3 kg trolley. Predict the acceleration, ignoring friction.
   **Worked Answer:** F = mg = 0.2 × 10 = 2 N. Total mass = 1.3 + 0.2 = 1.5 kg. a = F/m = 2/1.5 ≈ 1.3 m/s².

## Safety Notes
- Falling stones land near feet — mark a drop zone and keep sandals and toes clear; drop onto a sand pile or folded sack.
- Wire hanger ends are sharp — tape the cut end or have a fundi file it smooth.
- A runaway trolley can fly off the board end — a learner stands as catcher, hands clear of the wheels.
- Keep strings away from necks when lean­ing over the track.
