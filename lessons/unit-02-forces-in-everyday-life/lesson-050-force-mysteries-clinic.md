# Lesson 050: Everyday Force Mysteries Clinic

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; G10-PHY-1.5  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain why a door handle is fixed far from the hinges using the idea of a moment.
2. Describe how a lever multiplies force when opening a paint tin.
3. Explain why a dry cloth loosens a tight bottle cap (friction and grip).
4. State that a moment equals force × perpendicular distance from the pivot.

## Materials
- A classroom or house door (the whole class can see it)
- Empty paint tin with press-in lid (from any fundi or hardware shop)
- Panga or a flat iron bar for prying (teacher handles it)
- Glass or plastic bottle with a very tight screw cap (e.g. a soda bottle cranked shut)
- Dry cloth or piece of old inner tube (rubber grip)
- Chalk, ruler, and a spring balance for a quick moment measurement

## Vocabulary
- **moment** (nguvu ya mzunguko) — the turning effect of a force: force × distance from pivot
- **fulcrum / pivot** (egemezi) — the fixed point a lever turns about
- **lever** (nguzo ya kupandikiza) — a rigid bar used to multiply force
- **friction** (msuguano) — grip between surfaces; dry rubber grips best
- **mechanical advantage** (faida ya mashine) — how many times a tool multiplies your force

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Why is a door handle placed at the edge far from the hinges and not beside them?
2. You try to open a paint tin with your fingernails and fail. A panga edge opens it in one second. What did the panga give you that your fingers did not?
3. Your hands are oily and a bottle cap will not turn. Why does drying them help?
4. A force of 10 N acts 0.5 m from a pivot. Calculate the moment.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Accept any sensible guess (the lesson shows: bigger distance → bigger turning effect for the same push).
2. Accept: more force / leverage / a stronger edge (refined in the lesson to "a lever multiplies force").
3. Accept: better grip / more friction.
4. Moment = F × d = 10 N × 0.5 m = 5 N·m.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — slips out and collected.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — the "mystery clinic" opens: the teacher presents three patient-problems on the board — the stubborn door, the sealed paint tin, the oily bottle cap. Class votes which is hardest to explain.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — three rotating demo stations. (a) *Door:* one learner pushes the door at the handle, another near the hinges — same push, wildly different ease; measure handle-to-hinge distance with a ruler and read the push on a spring balance. (b) *Paint tin:* teacher pries the lid with the panga; learners identify the fulcrum (the tin's rim), the effort arm (blade length) and the tiny load arm (lid lip). (c) *Bottle cap:* try with oily hands, then dry hands, then a rubber cloth — count who succeeds each time. Groups note one sentence per station.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate on the board: a moment is force × perpendicular distance; the door handle maximises distance; the panga is a lever whose long effort arm beats the short load arm; the cloth raises friction so your hand's full force reaches the cap instead of slipping. Confront the misconception that these are "different tricks" — they are all the same principle: arrange distance and grip so your small force wins.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *move the force away from the pivot, or stop it slipping, and work gets easier*. Homework: find one lever at home (jembe handle, bottle opener, sufuria lid lifter) and name its fulcrum.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Why is a door handle far from the hinges? Answer with the word "moment" in your sentence. (mirrors pre-test)
2. What does a panga give you when opening a paint tin that fingers cannot? (mirrors pre-test)
3. A 20 N push acts 0.8 m from a pivot. Calculate the moment.
4. Two spanners fit a nut: one 10 cm long, one 25 cm long. Which loosens the nut more easily, and by what principle?
5. Challenge: why does a *wet* cloth work worse than a dry rubber one on a bottle cap, even though both are cloth?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The large distance from the hinges gives a big moment (turning effect) for a small push.
2. It acts as a lever — a long effort arm multiplies your force against the lid's short load arm.
3. Moment = 20 N × 0.8 m = 16 N·m.
4. The 25 cm spanner: same force at 2.5× the distance gives 2.5× the moment.
5. Water fills the gaps in the cloth weave and lubricates the contact, killing friction; dry rubber deforms and grips the cap's ridges, so your force transfers without slipping.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** Explain the paint-tin lever with moments. The tin's rim is the fulcrum, the panga blade gives a 15 cm effort arm, and the lid's lip is 2 cm from the rim. Compute the mechanical advantage.
   **Worked Answer:** Moments balance at the point of opening: F_effort × 15 cm = F_lid × 2 cm, so MA = F_lid / F_effort = 15/2 = 7.5. A 40 N push on the panga delivers about 300 N to the lid — enough to pop the seal.
2. **Q:** A door is 80 cm wide. A learner pushes at the handle with 15 N; a second learner pushes 20 cm from the hinges. What force must the second learner apply for the same moment?
   **Worked Answer:** Moments equal: 15 N × 0.8 m = F × 0.2 m → F = 12 / 0.2 = 60 N. Four times the force for a quarter of the distance.
3. **Q:** A cap needs 12 N·m of torque to loosen. Your hand can push with 40 N on a cloth-wrapped cap of radius 3 cm. Can you open it? If not, what tool change fixes it?
   **Worked Answer:** Torque available = 40 N × 0.03 m = 1.2 N·m — far short of 12 N·m. Fix: increase the radius — wrap more cloth for grip and use a jar-opener strap or lever at 30 cm: 40 N × 0.30 m = 12 N·m, exactly enough.

## Safety Notes
- The teacher handles the panga; learners observe from an arm's length — a prying blade can slip sideways.
- A slipping hand on a tight cap can bark knuckles — keep fingers clear of the bottle neck and any glass edge; no glass bottles for younger classes, use plastic.
- Door demo: keep fingers out of the hinge side — a closing door crushes.
- Paint tins have sharp rims once opened — sand or tape the edge before handling, or use an unopened tin for the demo and reopen it yourself.
