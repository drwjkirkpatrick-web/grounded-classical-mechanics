# Lesson 053: What Is Motion? Position and Reference Points

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** UP-FE;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define motion as a change in position relative to a reference point.
2. Identify whether an object is moving or at rest depending on the chosen reference frame.
3. Explain why a boda-boda passenger appears still to a friend sitting next to them.

## Materials
- Chalk or small stones (to mark positions on open ground)
- Optional: a bicycle or wheelbarrow for demonstration
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Motion** *(harakati)* — a change in position of an object over time.
- **Position** *(nafasi)* — where an object is located at a particular moment.
- **Reference point** *(hatua ya kurejelea)* — a fixed object or place used to decide if something is moving.
- **Reference frame** *(mfumo wa kurejelea)* — the point of view from which motion is observed.
- **At rest** *(kustarehe)* — not changing position relative to the chosen reference point.

## Pre-Test
1. You are sitting in a matatu. Are you moving or at rest? Explain.
2. When you walk past a tree, is the tree moving? How do you know?
3. A boda-boda rides past a fruit seller. From the fruit seller's point of view, is the boda-boda moving?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It depends on the reference point — at rest relative to the matatu seat, but moving relative to the road.
2. The tree is not moving relative to the ground, but it appears to move backward relative to a walking person.
3. Yes — the boda-boda is changing position relative to the fruit seller.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle learners and administer the Pre-Test orally. Learners write brief answers.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Is the matatu moving or at rest?" (conflicting answers are expected). Write the two answers on the board. Ask, "Can both be right?" — introduce the idea that motion depends on who is watching.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Place a chalk mark on the ground. One learner walks away from it; another stands still on the mark.
   - Ask: "Who is moving?" Then switch — ask from the walking learner's point of view. The standing learner appears to move backward.
   - **Key insight:** Motion is not a yes/no property of an object — it is a relationship between the object and a chosen reference point.
   - Repeat with a learner pushing a wheelbarrow past a seated group. From the ground: the wheelbarrow moves. From the pusher's shoulder: the wheelbarrow is at rest.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - Formalise: motion = change in position relative to a reference point.
   - Reference frame = "the observer's point of view." A passenger in a matatu is at rest in the matatu's frame, but moving in the roadside tree's frame.
   - Ask learners to give three Kenyan examples (e.g., carrying water on your head, sitting by the jiko, riding a boda-boda).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners write answers; swap papers to mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: one learner summaries the lesson in one sentence. Homework: observe three moving objects on the way home and write down the reference point you used.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* You are sitting in a moving matatu. Are you moving or at rest? Explain using a reference point.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A passenger on a boda-boda looks at their friend sitting next to them. Is the friend moving?
3. A stone sits on the ground. Is it moving? Can you think of a reference frame where the stone IS moving?
4. A mkokoteni cart is pushed past a shopkeeper. From the shopkeeper's point of view, is the cart moving or at rest?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. At rest relative to the matatu seat; moving relative to the roadside tree.
2. The friend is at rest relative to the boda-boda passenger (same frame).
3. The stone is at rest relative to the ground; but it moves relative to a flying bird or the sun.
4. Moving — the cart's position changes relative to the shopkeeper.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** You are sitting in the back of a moving lorry. You toss a coin straight up. Where does it land — behind you, in your hand, or ahead of you? Explain using reference frames.

**Worked Answer:** The coin lands in your hand. In the lorry's reference frame, both you and the coin share the lorry's forward motion. When tossed upward, the coin keeps the same horizontal speed as the lorry (inertia), so it returns to your hand. In the ground's reference frame, the coin traces a parabola: it moves forward with the lorry while also moving up and down. The key insight is that the horizontal motion is unchanged — there is no horizontal force on the coin after release.

**Q:** The Earth orbits the Sun at about 30 km/s. Are you moving right now? Defend your answer using the concept of reference frames.

**Worked Answer:** Yes — from the Sun's reference frame, both you and the Earth are moving at 30 km/s. From the ground's reference frame, you are at rest (sitting in the classroom). From the Milky Way's reference frame, the Sun (and therefore you) is moving even faster. There is no absolute "at rest." Motion is always relative to a chosen reference frame.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure the demonstration area is clear of holes or stones before learners walk backward during the explore activity.
- If using a bicycle or wheelbarrow, choose a flat, open space away from paths.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Learners already know that things move; this lesson introduces the formal concept that motion is relative.
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on "moving compared to what?" with concrete examples. Ages 14–17 should be pushed to articulate that there is no absolute rest and to connect to inertia (from Unit 2).
- **Local sourcing:** No special materials needed — chalk and open ground are sufficient.
- **CBE assessment note:** This lesson prepares for JS-FE "identifying motion" and UP-FE "describing motion using reference points" assessment standards.
