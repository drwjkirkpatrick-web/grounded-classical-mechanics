# Lesson 054: Distance vs Displacement

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Distinguish between distance (total path length) and displacement (straight-line change in position).
2. State whether a quantity is a scalar (distance) or a vector (displacement).
3. Calculate displacement from a simple two-leg journey using a sketch and Pythagoras's idea.

## Materials
- A 30 m tape measure (or pacing)
- Chalk for marking corners
- Compass or sun-direction stick (east-west markers)
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Distance** *(umbali)* — the total length of the path travelled, no direction needed.
- **Displacement** *(mwelekeo wa mwendo)* — the straight-line change in position from start to finish, including direction.
- **Scalar** *(nambari)* — a quantity that has magnitude only (e.g., distance, 5 m).
- **Vector** *(vekta)* — a quantity that has both magnitude and direction (e.g., displacement, 5 m east).

## Pre-Test
1. You walk from your desk to the door and back to your desk. What is your displacement?
2. Is distance always larger than displacement? Give one example where they are equal.
3. A learner walks 20 m to the borehole and 20 m back. What distance did they cover? What is their displacement?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Zero — the start and finish are the same point.
2. No — they are equal when the path is a straight line with no turns.
3. Distance = 40 m; displacement = 0 m (returned to start).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Learners write answers individually.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Draw two paths on the board — a straight line from home to school, and a winding path through the shamba. Ask: "Which path is longer? Which one describes where you end up compared to where you started?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Mark a corner on the ground with chalk. One learner paces 3 m forward, then turns 90° and paces 4 m.
   - Measure the total distance paced (7 m) with the tape.
   - Stretch the tape from the start point directly to the end point. Record this straight-line measurement.
   - **Discovery:** The straight-line measure (~5 m) is the displacement. It is shorter than the distance walked.
   - Repeat with different shapes: a square, a U-shape. Ask learners to predict which will have the largest displacement.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Distance** = total path (scalar, no direction). **Displacement** = straight line from start to finish (vector, needs direction).
   - Introduce the arrow notation: displacement is drawn as an arrow from start → finish.
   - Show on the board: walking 3 m east then 4 m north gives displacement magnitude = 5 m (Pythagoras: √(3² + 4²) = 5) in the northeast direction.
   - Emphasise: displacement can be zero even after a long walk (if you return to start).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then swap to mark.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: A learner summaries: "Distance is the journey; displacement is the result." Homework: draw your route from home to the market today. Label distance and displacement.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* You walk from your desk to the blackboard and back to your desk. What is your displacement?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* A farmer walks 50 m to the jiko, then 50 m back to the house. What distance did they cover? What is their displacement?
3. A learner jogs 6 m north, then 8 m east. Sketch the path and calculate the displacement.
4. Can displacement be negative? Explain with an example using a straight path.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Zero — start and finish are the same point.
2. Distance = 100 m; displacement = 0 m.
3. Distance = 14 m. Displacement magnitude = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 m in the northeast direction.
4. Yes — if we define one direction as positive, the opposite is negative. For example, walking 5 m east (+5 m) then 8 m west gives displacement = −3 m (3 m west).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** You walk 30 m east then 40 m north. Calculate (a) the total distance, (b) the magnitude of the displacement, and (c) the direction of the displacement relative to east.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Distance = 30 m + 40 m = **70 m**.
(b) Displacement magnitude = √(30² + 40²) = √(900 + 1600) = √2500 = **50 m**.
(c) Direction: tan θ = opposite/adjacent = 40/30 = 1.333. θ = arctan(1.333) ≈ **53° north of east** (or N 53° E).

**Q:** Explain why a fully loaded lorry on a circular bypass road can travel a large distance while its displacement over one complete lap is zero. What does this tell you about the usefulness of displacement in traffic engineering?

**Worked Answer:**
On a circular road, the start and finish of one lap are the same point, so displacement = 0. However, the lorry's engine burns fuel proportional to distance, not displacement. In traffic engineering, displacement is useful for analysing congestion at entry/exit points (where start and finish differ), but fuel consumption and tyre wear depend on distance. Both quantities matter — they answer different questions.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure the pacing area is flat and clear of stones or holes.
- Remind learners to look where they are going when walking with a tape measure.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lesson 053 (reference frames) and Unit 1 (measuring length).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on the conceptual distinction and simple straight-line cases. Ages 14–17 should do the Pythagorean calculation and direction angle.
- **Local sourcing:** A tape measure from the fundi or a pacing method works. Sun directions (morning sun = east) can substitute for a compass.
- **CBE assessment note:** Prepares for JS-FE "distinguishing scalar and vector quantities" and "calculating displacement from path data."
