# Lesson 055: Speed: How Fast Is Fast?

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** UP-FE;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State that speed is the distance travelled divided by the time taken (v = d/t).
2. Measure a distance with a tape and time an event with a stopwatch to calculate speed.
3. Compare speeds from a class chart and judge which motion is faster.

## Materials
- A 50 m tape measure (or marked 40 m course with chalk)
- A stopwatch or phone timer (one per group)
- A whistle or clap for starting
- Graph paper or a prepared chart on the board
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Speed** *(kasi)* — how fast something moves; distance travelled per unit time.
- **Average speed** *(kasi wastani)* — total distance divided by total time for a journey.
- **Stopwatch** *(saa ya kuhesabu)* — a device used to measure short time intervals.
- **Rate** *(kiasi)* — a measure of how much of something happens per unit of something else.

## Pre-Test
1. Which is faster: a boda-boda that covers 2 km in 5 minutes, or a walker who covers 1 km in 10 minutes?
2. What two measurements do you need to calculate speed?
3. A learner runs 40 m in 10 s. What is their speed?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The boda-boda is faster (it covers more distance in less time; 400 m/min vs 100 m/min).
2. Distance and time.
3. Speed = 40 m / 10 s = **4 m/s**.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle learners and administer the Pre-Test. Collect quickly by show of hands for Q1.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who has seen a boda-boda overtake a mkokoteni cart? Why does the boda-boda pass?" (Expected: it goes faster.) Introduce the idea that "fast" means covering more distance in the same time — or the same distance in less time.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Mark a 40 m straight course on open ground with chalk.
   - In groups of 4, time one learner walking the 40 m, then running the same 40 m.
   - Each group records: name, distance (40 m), time (s), and calculates speed = distance ÷ time.
   - Groups post their results on the class chart (learner name vs speed in m/s).
   - **Discovery:** Running speed > walking speed for every learner. Compare: who is the fastest walker? Who is the fastest runner? Do they match?
   - Extension: Time a learner pushing a wheelbarrow 20 m. Compute the speed and compare.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - Write the formula **v = d/t** on the board. Label each symbol: v = speed, d = distance, t = time.
   - Units: metres per second (m/s). Explain that "per" means "divided by."
   - Example: A matatu covers 120 m in 8 s. Speed = 120 m / 8 s = **15 m/s**.
   - Conceptual check: If the distance doubles but time stays the same, what happens to speed? (It doubles.)
   - If the time doubles but distance stays the same, what happens to speed? (It halves.)

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: A learner states the formula and one real-life Kenyan example. Homework: time yourself walking to the borehole and back; calculate your average speed.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A boda-boda covers 3 km in 5 minutes. A bicycle covers 2 km in 10 minutes. Which is faster? Show your working.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* A learner walks 60 m in 15 s. What is their speed?
3. A matatu travels 200 m in 12 s. Calculate its speed.
4. If a runner covers 100 m in 20 s, how far would they cover in 40 s at the same speed?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Boda-boda speed = 3000 m / 300 s = **10 m/s**. Bicycle speed = 2000 m / 600 s = **3.33 m/s**. The boda-boda is faster.
2. Speed = 60 m / 15 s = **4 m/s**.
3. Speed = 200 m / 12 s = **16.67 m/s** (accept 16.7 m/s or 17 m/s with rounding).
4. At 5 m/s, in 40 s: distance = 5 m/s × 40 s = **200 m**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A runner covers 100 m in 14.0 s. Calculate their average speed in (a) m/s and (b) km/h.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Average speed = distance / time = 100 m / 14.0 s = **7.14 m/s**.
(b) Convert to km/h: 7.14 m/s × (3600 s / 1000 m) = 7.14 × 3.6 = **25.7 km/h**.
(Shortcut: multiply m/s by 3.6 to get km/h.)

**Q:** Explain why average speed is not the same as the speed at every instant during a journey. Use the example of a matatu stopping at a stage.

**Worked Answer:**
Average speed = total distance / total time. During the journey, the matatu's speed changes: it accelerates from rest, travels at cruising speed, then brakes to a stop at the stage. At the stage, its instantaneous speed is zero. The average speed smooths all of these variations into one number. A matatu might have an average speed of 40 km/h for a 10 km trip, but its speedometer reads 0 km/h at the stage, 60 km/h on the open road, and 20 km/h while braking. Average speed tells you about the whole trip; instantaneous speed tells you about one moment.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure the 40 m course is straight and free of holes, stones, or tripping hazards.
- Learners should walk before running to familiarise themselves with the course.
- No pushing or racing between groups; stagger starts.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 053 (reference frames) and 054 (distance). Unit 1 (measuring distance and time).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on the formula v = d/t, measuring, and comparing. Ages 14–17 should do unit conversions (m/s → km/h) and understand the distinction between average and instantaneous speed.
- **Local sourcing:** A tape measure from the market or a known distance (e.g., 40 m = 20 long strides of an adult) works. Phone stopwatches are common; a hand-clap countdown works if none are available.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "calculating speed from distance and time" and UP-FE "analysing motion data."
