# Lesson 056: Units of Speed: m/s Meets km/h

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Convert between metres per second (m/s) and kilometres per hour (km/h).
2. Choose the appropriate unit of speed for a given situation.
3. Estimate the speed of common Kenyan vehicles using distance markers and a watch.

## Materials
- Conversion cards (prepared by teacher: 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h)
- A simple calculator (optional; mental maths preferred)
- A stopwatch or phone timer
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Metre per second (m/s)** *(mita kwa sekunde)* — a unit of speed useful for short distances and fast events.
- **Kilometre per hour (km/h)** *(kilomita kwa saa)* — a unit of speed useful for long journeys and vehicles.
- **Conversion factor** *(kiwango cha kubadilisha)* — a number used to change from one unit to another.
- **Estimate** *(kukisia)* — to make a rough but sensible calculation.

## Pre-Test
1. Which unit is larger: 1 m/s or 1 km/h?
2. A boda-boda travels at 10 m/s. Is this faster or slower than a car doing 30 km/h?
3. Why do we use km/h for matatus but m/s for a thrown stone?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. 1 m/s is larger (it equals 3.6 km/h).
2. 10 m/s = 36 km/h, so the boda-boda is faster.
3. Matatus travel long distances over hours; a thrown stone covers short distances in seconds.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Quick oral check for Q1.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Write "36 km/h" and "10 m/s" on the board. Ask: "A boda-boda and a bicycle are racing. The boda is at 36 km/h. The bicycle is at 10 m/s. Who wins?" (Expected confusion — same speed, different units.) Introduce the need to compare units.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In pairs, learners receive conversion cards showing 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h.
   - Teacher models: convert 5 m/s → 5 × 3.6 = **18 km/h**.
   - Learners convert: 2 m/s, 8 m/s, 15 m/s, 20 m/s to km/h.
   - Reverse: convert 36 km/h, 72 km/h, 108 km/h to m/s (divide by 3.6).
   - **Discovery:** m/s numbers are smaller than km/h numbers for the same speed. Highway speeds in km/h are easier to say (e.g., 80 km/h vs 22.2 m/s).
   - **Estimation:** Look at a road with kilometre markers. Time how long a matatu takes between two markers 1 km apart. Convert to km/h (e.g., 1 km in 90 s = 40 km/h). Compare with the boda-boda's speed from the previous lesson.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - Derive the conversion: 1 m/s = 1 m / 1 s = (1/1000 km) / (1/3600 h) = 3600/1000 km/h = **3.6 km/h**.
   - Rule of thumb: **×3.6** to go m/s → km/h; **÷3.6** to go km/h → m/s.
   - When to use which:
     - **m/s:** sprinting, falling objects, lab experiments (short times, small distances).
     - **km/h:** matatus, lorries, bicycles on the highway (long distances, hours).
   - Kenyan context: speed limit signs on highways are in km/h. Boda-boda speedometers (if present) often show km/h.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states the conversion rule. Homework: find three speed signs or vehicle speed claims (matatu, boda, bicycle) and convert them to the other unit.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Convert 12 m/s to km/h.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* A matatu travels at 60 km/h. Convert this to m/s.
3. A runner's speed is 5 m/s. A cyclist's speed is 20 km/h. Who is faster? Show your working.
4. A stone falls at 20 m/s. Express this speed in km/h.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. 12 m/s × 3.6 = **43.2 km/h**.
2. 60 km/h ÷ 3.6 = **16.7 m/s** (accept 16.67 m/s).
3. Runner: 5 m/s × 3.6 = **18 km/h**. Cyclist: **20 km/h**. The cyclist is slightly faster.
4. 20 m/s × 3.6 = **72 km/h**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Show by chain conversion that 10 m/s = 36 km/h. Explain each step clearly.

**Worked Answer:**
Start with 10 m/s.
Step 1: Convert metres to kilometres.
10 m = 10 / 1000 km = 0.01 km.
So 10 m/s = 0.01 km/s.

Step 2: Convert seconds to hours.
1 s = 1 / 3600 h.
So 1 / (1/3600 h) = 3600 per hour.

Step 3: Combine.
0.01 km/s × 3600 s/h = **36 km/h**.

Alternative compact form:
10 m/s = 10 × (1/1000) km / (1/3600) h = 10 × (3600/1000) km/h = 10 × 3.6 = **36 km/h**.

**Q:** Which unit — m/s or km/h — is more appropriate for each situation? Justify your choice.
(a) Measuring the speed of water flowing from a jerrycan spout.
(b) A long-distance bus travelling from Nairobi to Mombasa.
(c) A posho mill grinding maize.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) **m/s** — the water exits over a short distance (centimetres) in a fraction of a second. km/h would give an absurdly small number and hide the precision needed.
(b) **km/h** — the journey is hundreds of kilometres over many hours. m/s would give numbers in the hundreds, which is harder to interpret for a driver.
(c) **km/h** would be meaningless — a posho mill does not travel. The relevant quantity is rotational speed (revolutions per minute, rpm), not linear speed. If forced to choose, m/s for the grinding stone's edge speed would be the least wrong, but the question reveals that not all situations need speed in distance/time; some need rate in other units.

## Safety Notes
- No physical activity in this lesson; ensure learners do not run onto roads during the estimation homework.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lesson 055 (speed formula and measurement).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should master the ×3.6 and ÷3.6 rules with whole-number examples. Ages 14–17 should handle decimal conversions and justify unit choice.
- **Local sourcing:** Conversion cards can be handwritten on cardboard. Calculators are helpful but not required — the maths should be manageable mentally for simple cases.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "converting between units of speed" and "selecting appropriate units for measurement."
