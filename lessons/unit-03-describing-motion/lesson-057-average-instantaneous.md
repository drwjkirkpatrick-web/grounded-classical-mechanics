# Lesson 057: Average vs Instantaneous Speed

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Distinguish between average speed (total distance / total time) and instantaneous speed (speed at one moment).
2. Explain why a matatu's average speed is lower than its cruising speed.
3. Judge whether average or instantaneous speed is the right measure for a given question.

## Materials
- Journey data cards (prepared by teacher: printed or handwritten story cards)
- A stopwatch
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Average speed** *(kasi wastani)* — total distance divided by total time for an entire journey.
- **Instantaneous speed** *(kasi ya papo hapo)* — the speed of an object at one specific moment in time.
- **Cruising speed** *(kasi ya kawaida)* — the steady speed a vehicle maintains on open road.
- **Stop time** *(muda wa kusimama)* — time spent stationary during a journey (e.g., at a stage).

## Pre-Test
1. A matatu travels 10 km in 20 minutes. What is its average speed?
2. The same matatu was stopped for 5 minutes at a stage. Did this affect its average speed? Explain.
3. Your boda-boda speedometer reads 40 km/h. Is this average or instantaneous speed?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Average speed = 10 km / 20 min = 0.5 km/min = **30 km/h**.
2. Yes — the total time increased, so average speed dropped.
3. Instantaneous — it shows the speed at that exact moment.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "A matatu leaves town at 8:00 AM and arrives 10 km away at 8:30 AM. Was it going 20 km/h the whole time?" (Expected: no — it stopped, slowed down, sped up.) Introduce the idea that "average" smooths everything out.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 3, learners receive journey data cards describing a trip to the market.
   - Example card: "Walk one kilometre to the stage [taking fifteen minutes]. Wait [ten minutes]. Ride a matatu four kilometres [taking ten minutes]. Walk half a kilometre to the market [taking eight minutes]."
   - Groups calculate total distance and total time, then average speed.
   - **Key discovery:** The wait reduces average speed even though no distance was covered during that time.
   - Groups compare: what was the matatu's cruising speed versus the average speed for the whole trip? The cruising speed is higher than the average because stops drag the average down.
   - Rotate cards among groups so each group sees two journeys.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Average speed** = total distance / total time. It answers "How fast overall?" It includes every stop, slowdown, and speed-up.
   - **Instantaneous speed** = speed at one moment. It answers "How fast right now?" A speedometer shows this.
   - Example: A boda-boda rider sees 50 km/h on the speedometer (instantaneous) but their trip average might be 35 km/h because of traffic lights and passengers.
   - Conceptual check: If you sit still for 1 hour then sprint 100 m in 10 s, what is your average speed? (Very low — the hour dominates.)

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains in one sentence when to use average speed and when to use instantaneous. Homework: time a real journey (to school, to the borehole) and calculate average speed. Note any stops.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A cyclist rides 15 km in 30 minutes. What is their average speed in km/h?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* The cyclist stopped for 10 minutes to fix a puncture. How does this affect average speed?
3. A matatu's speedometer reads 60 km/h on the highway. Is this average or instantaneous speed?
4. A journey has three parts: 2 km in 20 min, rest 10 min, 2 km in 30 min. Calculate the average speed for the whole journey.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Average speed = 15 km / 0.5 h = **30 km/h**.
2. The stop increases total time without increasing distance, so average speed drops. New average = 15 km / 40 min = 22.5 km/h.
3. **Instantaneous** — it is the speed at that exact moment.
4. Total distance = 4 km. Total time = 60 min = 1 h. Average speed = **4 km/h**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A journey consists of three parts: 2 km covered in 20 minutes, a 10-minute rest, then 2 km covered in 30 minutes. Calculate (a) the overall average speed, and (b) the cruising speed during each moving segment. Explain why the average is so much lower than the cruising speeds.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Total distance = 2 km + 2 km = **4 km**.
Total time = 20 min + 10 min + 30 min = 60 min = **1 h**.
Average speed = 4 km / 1 h = **4 km/h**.

(b) Segment 1 cruising speed = 2 km / 20 min = 2 km / (1/3 h) = **6 km/h**.
Segment 2 cruising speed = 2 km / 30 min = 2 km / 0.5 h = **4 km/h**.

The average (4 km/h) is lower than both cruising speeds because the 10-minute rest adds time without adding distance. Average speed penalises every stop; cruising speed ignores them. This is why a matatu's average speed on a town route is often half its cruising speed on the open road.

**Q:** A boda-boda speedometer shows 40 km/h at one instant. Over a 5 km trip with two 3-minute stops, the total time is 15 minutes. Calculate the average speed and explain why it is lower than the instantaneous reading.

**Worked Answer:**
Average speed = total distance / total time = 5 km / 15 min = 5 km / 0.25 h = **20 km/h**.
The instantaneous reading of 40 km/h was taken while the boda was moving freely. The average (20 km/h) is lower because the two 3-minute stops (6 minutes total of zero speed) pull the overall average down. If the boda had not stopped, the trip would take 9 minutes at 40 km/h, giving an average of 33.3 km/h — still lower than 40 km/h because even 40 km/h cruising includes acceleration and braking phases.

## Safety Notes
- No physical activity in this lesson; ensure learners do not run onto roads during the homework journey-timing task.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 055 (speed formula) and 056 (unit conversions).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on the conceptual distinction and simple average-speed calculations. Ages 14–17 should handle multi-segment journeys and explain the mathematical effect of stops.
- **Local sourcing:** Journey data cards can be handwritten on paper. Use real local routes (e.g., home → market → borehole) to make the activity grounded.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "distinguishing average and instantaneous speed" and "calculating average speed from journey data."
