# Lesson 058: Velocity: Speed with a Direction

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State that velocity is speed with a direction.
2. Explain why an object moving in a circle at constant speed still has changing velocity.
3. Draw a simple velocity vector arrow for straight-line motion.

## Materials
- Chalk for drawing circles on open ground
- Ropes or sisal twine (two per group)
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Velocity** *(kasi ya mwelekeo)* — speed in a stated direction; a vector quantity.
- **Vector** *(vekta)* — a quantity that has both magnitude and direction.
- **Constant speed** *(kasi isiyobadilika)* — moving at the same rate all the time.
- **Changing velocity** *(kasi ya mwelekeo inayobadilika)* — velocity changes when speed or direction (or both) change.

## Pre-Test
1. Is velocity the same as speed? Explain.
2. A boda-boda rides north at 30 km/h. What is its velocity?
3. Can an object have constant speed but changing velocity? Give an example.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. No — velocity includes direction; speed does not.
2. 30 km/h north (or "30 km/h towards the north").
3. Yes — an object moving in a circle at constant speed changes direction continuously, so its velocity changes.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Two bodas leave a stage. One rides north at 30 km/h. The other rides south at 30 km/h. Do they have the same velocity?" (Expected: no — same speed, opposite directions.) Introduce velocity as "speed with a direction."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, mark a large circle on the ground with chalk. One learner walks around the circle at a steady pace while holding a rope tied to a central stake.
   - The group times one full lap and estimates the speed (distance around the circle divided by time).
   - **Key discovery:** The speed is roughly constant, but the direction keeps changing. At every point on the circle, the learner faces a different way. Therefore, the velocity is changing even though the speed is not.
   - Repeat: one learner walks a straight line while another walks around the circle. Both keep the same speed. The straight-line walker has constant velocity; the circle walker does not.
   - Groups draw arrows on paper: one arrow for the straight-line walker's velocity, and a series of arrows (tangent to the circle) showing how the circle walker's velocity changes.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Velocity** = speed + direction. It is a vector.
   - **Speed** = magnitude only. It is a scalar.
   - Example: "The matatu travels at 50 km/h" is speed. "The matatu travels at 50 km/h towards Nairobi" is velocity.
   - Changing velocity happens when:
     - Speed changes (accelerating or braking).
     - Direction changes (turning a corner).
     - Both change.
   - Draw on the board: two bodas at 40 km/h — one north, one east. Same speed, different velocity. The arrows have the same length but point in different directions.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner summaries: "Velocity tells you where you are going, not just how fast." Homework: list three journeys you made today and describe each with a velocity (speed + direction).

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A learner says, "Velocity and speed mean the same thing." Is the learner correct? Explain.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* A child runs around the school compound at a steady pace. Is their velocity changing?
3. A matatu travels at 60 km/h west. Write this as a velocity.
4. Draw two velocity arrows of the same length: one pointing north and one pointing east. Label each with its velocity.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. No — velocity includes direction; speed does not. Two objects can have the same speed but different velocities.
2. Yes — the direction keeps changing as the child runs around the compound, even if the speed is steady.
3. Velocity = **60 km/h west**.
4. Two arrows of equal length, one pointing up (north) labelled "v north," one pointing right (east) labelled "v east."

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Two bodas travel at 40 km/h. One rides due north; the other rides due east. Do they have the same speed? The same velocity? Draw their velocity vectors and explain the difference.

**Worked Answer:**
They have the **same speed** (40 km/h) but **different velocities**.
Speed is a scalar — 40 km/h is just a number.
Velocity is a vector — "40 km/h north" is not the same as "40 km/h east."

Drawing:
- Arrow A: points straight up, length proportional to 40 km/h, labelled **40 km/h north**.
- Arrow B: points straight right, same length, labelled **40 km/h east**.

The arrows have equal magnitude but different directions. If you add these vectors tip-to-tail, the resultant is 40√2 ≈ 56.6 km/h in the northeast direction. This shows that direction matters in vector calculations.

**Q:** Explain why a posho mill grinding stone spinning at constant rotational speed does not have constant velocity. What kind of motion does a point on the rim undergo?

**Worked Answer:**
A point on the rim of a spinning grinding stone moves in a circle. Its **speed** is constant (the stone spins at a fixed rate), but its **velocity** is not constant because the direction of motion keeps changing.
At every instant, the velocity vector is tangent to the circle. As the stone rotates, this tangent direction sweeps around continuously. The point on the rim undergoes **uniform circular motion** — constant speed, changing velocity. This changing velocity means there is an acceleration (centripetal acceleration) directed towards the centre of the stone, even though the speed never changes. This is why the stone needs a strong axle and bearings to keep it in circular motion.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure the circle is large enough (at least 3 m radius) so the rope does not trip other learners.
- Do not spin learners rapidly — the activity is a slow walk, not a race.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 054 (distance vs displacement, vectors introduced) and 055 (speed).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should grasp that velocity includes direction and that circular motion means changing direction. Ages 14–17 should draw vectors, explain why constant speed in a circle is accelerated motion, and understand the difference between scalar and vector quantities.
- **Local sourcing:** Chalk and rope are sufficient. A wheelbarrow wheel or bicycle wheel can substitute for the rope circle if space is limited.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "distinguishing speed and velocity" and "describing motion using vectors."
