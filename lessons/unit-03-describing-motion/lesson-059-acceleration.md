# Lesson 059: Acceleration: Changing Speed

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define acceleration as the rate of change of velocity.
2. Identify speeding up, slowing down, and turning as forms of acceleration.
3. Calculate acceleration from a change in velocity and the time taken.

## Materials
- A toy car or small wheeled object (bottle with wheels, e.g., a pen pushed through a plastic bottle)
- A ramp board or plank
- A stopwatch
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Acceleration** *(kasi ya kuharakisha)* — the rate at which velocity changes; change in velocity divided by time.
- **Speeding up** *(kuongeza kasi)* — acceleration in the direction of motion.
- **Slowing down** *(kupunguza kasi)* — acceleration opposite to the direction of motion (deceleration).
- **Change in velocity** *(mabadiliko ya kasi)* — the difference between final velocity and initial velocity (Δv).

## Pre-Test
1. A boda-boda speeds up from 10 m/s to 20 m/s in 5 s. Is it accelerating?
2. A matatu brakes from 15 m/s to 5 m/s in 4 s. Is it accelerating?
3. What two quantities do you need to calculate acceleration?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes — the velocity is increasing.
2. Yes — the velocity is changing (decreasing). Acceleration can be negative.
3. Change in velocity and the time over which the change happens.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Quick oral check.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show the toy car on a flat surface. Push it — it speeds up. Let it roll to a stop — it slows down. Ask: "Is the car accelerating in both cases?" (Expected: confusion — most learners think acceleration only means speeding up.) Introduce the formal definition.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 3, roll the toy car down a ramp from rest. Time how long it takes to reach the bottom.
   - Measure the length of the ramp. Estimate the speed at the bottom (using v = d/t as a rough average).
   - **Key discovery:** The car speeds up as it rolls — its velocity changes. This is acceleration.
   - Now place the car at the bottom and push it up the ramp. It slows down as it climbs — this is also acceleration (negative acceleration, or deceleration).
   - Discuss: a boda-boda leaving a stage speeds up (acceleration). A matatu braking for a speed bump slows down (negative acceleration). A bicycle turning a corner changes direction (acceleration too — the velocity vector changes).
   - Groups list five Kenyan examples of acceleration in their notebooks.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - Write the formula on the board: **a = Δv / t** where a = acceleration, Δv = change in velocity (final − initial), and t = time.
   - Units: metres per second squared (m/s²). Explain: "per second per second" — the speed changes by so many m/s every second.
   - Example: A matatu goes from 0 to 15 m/s in 10 s. a = (15 − 0) / 10 = **1.5 m/s²**.
   - Example: A boda brakes from 20 m/s to 10 m/s in 2 s. a = (10 − 20) / 2 = −10 / 2 = **−5 m/s²**. The negative sign means deceleration.
   - Connect to Unit 2: a = F/m. A loaded lorry needs more force for the same acceleration. A boda with two passengers accelerates more slowly than with one.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states the formula and gives one Kenyan example. Homework: observe a vehicle leaving a stop — estimate its acceleration qualitatively (fast, medium, slow).

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A bicycle speeds up from 2 m/s to 6 m/s in 4 s. Calculate its acceleration.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A matatu brakes from 20 m/s to 10 m/s in 5 s. Calculate its acceleration.
3. A boda-boda maintains a steady speed of 15 m/s for 10 s. What is its acceleration?
4. A runner starts from rest and reaches 8 m/s in 2 s. Calculate the acceleration.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. a = (6 − 2) / 4 = 4 / 4 = **1 m/s²**.
2. a = (10 − 20) / 5 = −10 / 5 = **−2 m/s²** (deceleration).
3. **0 m/s²** — the velocity is not changing.
4. a = (8 − 0) / 2 = 8 / 2 = **4 m/s²**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A matatu accelerates from rest to 15 m/s in 10 s. Calculate (a) the acceleration, and (b) the force required per tonne of mass if friction is ignored (a = F/m). What does this tell you about why a fully loaded matatu accelerates more slowly?

**Worked Answer:**
(a) a = Δv / t = (15 − 0) / 10 = **1.5 m/s²**.
(b) From Newton's Second Law (Unit 2): F = ma. Per tonne (1000 kg): F = 1000 kg × 1.5 m/s² = **1500 N per tonne**.

For a fully loaded matatu of 5 tonnes, the engine must provide F = 5000 kg × 1.5 m/s² = **7500 N** to achieve the same acceleration. If the engine can only provide, say, 6000 N, the actual acceleration is a = 6000 / 5000 = **1.2 m/s²** — slower than the empty matatu. This is why drivers feel the difference: same pedal, less acceleration when loaded.

**Q:** A stone is thrown straight upward at 20 m/s. It slows down, stops momentarily at the top, then falls back down. Describe the acceleration throughout the motion, including at the very top. Explain why the acceleration is not zero at the top.

**Worked Answer:**
The acceleration is constant throughout: **−10 m/s²** (or 10 m/s² downward) due to gravity.
- On the way up: the stone's velocity is positive (upward) but decreasing by 10 m/s every second. At t = 1 s, v = 10 m/s. At t = 2 s, v = 0 m/s.
- At the top: the instantaneous velocity is zero, but the acceleration is still **−10 m/s²**. The stone is still being pulled downward by gravity. Velocity = 0 does not mean acceleration = 0.
- On the way down: velocity becomes negative (downward) and its magnitude increases (speeding up) at 10 m/s every second.

The acceleration is constant because gravity is constant (near Earth's surface). The changing velocity is caused by this constant acceleration, not by any change in the acceleration itself.

## Safety Notes
- Use a gentle ramp slope — steep ramps cause the toy car to launch unpredictably.
- Ensure the landing area is clear of feet and obstacles.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 055 (speed formula), 056 (units), 057 (average vs instantaneous), 058 (velocity as a vector).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should recognise acceleration as speeding up or slowing down and calculate simple cases with the formula. Ages 14–17 should handle negative acceleration, connect to F = ma, and understand that acceleration exists even when velocity is momentarily zero.
- **Local sourcing:** A toy car from the market or a home-made wheeled bottle works. Any plank or mabati sheet can serve as a ramp.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "calculating acceleration from velocity and time data" and "interpreting positive and negative acceleration."
