# Lesson 062: Distance–Time Graphs II: Stops, Speed-ups and Returns

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Interpret distance–time graph shapes for journeys that include stops, speed-ups, and returns.
2. Draw a distance–time graph from a described journey.
3. Match a graph to a motion story and justify the match.

## Materials
- Graph paper or squared paper (one sheet per learner)
- Story cards (prepared by teacher: printed or handwritten journey descriptions)
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Horizontal section** *(sehemu ya mlalo)* — a flat part of a distance–time graph; the object is stationary.
- **Return journey** *(njia ya kurudi)* — motion back toward the starting point; shown as a downward slope on a distance–time graph.
- **Speed-up** *(kuongeza kasi)* — an increasing gradient on a distance–time graph.
- **Slow-down** *(kupunguza kasi)* — a decreasing gradient on a distance–time graph.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a horizontal line on a distance–time graph mean?
2. A learner walks away from a tree, stops, then walks back. Sketch the shape of the distance–time graph.
3. Which is steeper: a graph for a fast runner or a slow walker?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The object is not moving — it is at rest.
2. Rising line (away), horizontal (stop), falling line (back).
3. The fast runner — steeper gradient means faster speed.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by sketching on the board.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Draw a distance–time graph on the board with a rise, a flat, and a fall. Ask, "Tell me the story of this journey." (Expected: walked away, stopped, walked back.) Introduce the idea that graph shapes tell stories.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In pairs, learners receive story cards describing journeys.
   - Example Card A: "A boda-boda rider leaves the stage, rides 2 km to a shop, stops for 5 minutes to fix a puncture, then rides 2 km back to the stage."
   - Example Card B: "A learner walks steadily to the borehole, runs the last 50 m, draws water, then walks back slowly."
   - Pairs draw the distance–time graph for their card on graph paper.
   - **Key discovery:** A stop is horizontal. A return is a downward slope. Speeding up makes the line steeper.
   - Pairs swap graphs with another pair and try to guess the story from the graph shape.
   - Class discussion: which graphs were easiest to interpret? Which were ambiguous?

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Shape language of distance–time graphs:**
     - Rising straight line = steady speed away from start.
     - Horizontal line = at rest.
     - Falling straight line = steady speed back toward start.
     - Curve getting steeper = speeding up.
     - Curve getting flatter = slowing down.
   - **Common pitfall:** Distance from start vs total distance travelled. If a learner walks 3 m away and 1 m back, the distance from start is 2 m (not 4 m). The graph shows distance from start, not total path length.
   - Example: A boda journey — ride out (steep rise), stop at shop (flat), ride back (steep fall to zero). The return line must end at zero distance from start.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner summaries the five graph shapes. Homework: draw the distance–time graph for your journey to school today, including any stops.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does a horizontal section on a distance–time graph mean?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A learner walks away from home, stops to chat, then walks back. Sketch the distance–time graph shape.
3. A graph shows a steep rise, a short flat, then a gentle fall. Describe the journey.
4. A boda-boda rides 3 km to a market, stops for 10 minutes, then rides 3 km home. Sketch the distance–time graph.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The object is **at rest** (not moving).
2. Rising line (walk away), horizontal (chat), falling line (walk back).
3. Fast movement away from start, a stop, then slower movement back toward start.
4. Rising straight line to 3 km, horizontal section for 10 minutes, falling straight line back to 0 km.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Sketch the distance–time graph for a ball thrown straight up and caught at the same height. The vertical axis shows distance from the ground. Defend the shape of your graph.

**Worked Answer:**
The graph is a **smooth curve** that rises to a maximum and then falls back to the starting height.

Reasoning:
- At t = 0: the ball is at hand height (distance from ground = h). The graph starts at (0, h).
- As the ball rises: distance from ground increases. The gradient (speed) decreases because gravity slows the ball. The curve gets flatter near the top.
- At the top: the ball stops momentarily (instantaneous speed = 0). The gradient is zero — the tangent is horizontal.
- As the ball falls: distance from ground decreases. The gradient increases in magnitude (speeding up). The curve gets steeper.
- At catch: the ball returns to hand height (distance = h). The graph ends at (t_total, h).

The curve is **symmetric** (if air resistance is ignored) because the upward and downward motions are mirror images under constant acceleration (gravity). The maximum height is reached at half the total time.

**Q:** A matatu travels from town to a village. Its distance–time graph is a straight line for the first 10 km, then horizontal for 15 minutes (puncture repair), then a steeper straight line for the final 10 km. Explain why the last section is steeper than the first, even though both are straight lines.

**Worked Answer:**
Both straight-line sections represent steady speed, but the **gradients differ**.
- First section: gradient = speed₁ = 10 km / t₁ (time taken for first leg).
- Horizontal section: gradient = 0 (stop for puncture repair).
- Last section: gradient = speed₂ = 10 km / t₂.

The last section is steeper because the matatu is moving **faster** on the return/open road than it was in town traffic. A steeper gradient means a higher speed. Even though both are straight lines (steady speed), the different steepnesses tell us the driver accelerated after the repair and is now cruising faster.

Alternatively, the driver may be hurrying to make up for lost time. In either case, the graph shape communicates that the speed increased between the first and last legs.

## Safety Notes
- No physical activity in this lesson; ensure learners do not run onto roads during the homework sketching task.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lesson 061 (plotting steady walks, gradient = speed).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on recognising stops (horizontal) and returns (downward slope). Ages 14–17 should interpret curved sections (speeding up/slowing down) and sketch motion under gravity.
- **Local sourcing:** Story cards can be handwritten on paper. Graph paper can be squared exercise book paper.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "interpreting distance–time graphs for non-uniform motion" and "matching graphs to described journeys."
