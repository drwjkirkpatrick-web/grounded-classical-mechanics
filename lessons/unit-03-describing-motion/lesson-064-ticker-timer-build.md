# Lesson 064: Build: A Ticker Timer from Simple Parts

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** ticker-timer

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a simple ticker timer using clothespins, rubber bands, and a felt pen.
2. Produce a dotted paper tape that records motion.
3. Analyse dot spacing to determine speed and acceleration.

## Materials
- Paper tape strips (cut from an exercise book or newspaper margin)
- A wooden clothespin or small wooden block
- A rubber band (cut from an old bicycle tube or elastic band)
- A felt pen or marker
- A metronome or a steady hand-clap rhythm
- A toy car or home-made wheeled bottle
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Ticker timer** *(kifaa cha kuhesabu)* — a device that marks a paper tape at regular intervals to record motion.
- **Dot spacing** *(masafa ya nukta)* — the distance between consecutive dots on the tape; related to speed.
- **Uniform spacing** *(masafa sawa)* — equal distances between dots, indicating steady speed.
- **Increasing spacing** *(masafa yanayoongezeka)* — dots getting farther apart, indicating acceleration.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a ticker timer record?
2. If the dots on a tape are equally spaced, what does this tell you about the motion?
3. The dots get farther apart as the tape progresses. Is the object speeding up or slowing down?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The position of a moving object at regular time intervals.
2. The object is moving at a steady (constant) speed.
3. Speeding up — greater distance covered in the same time interval.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a real or drawn ticker-tape with dots. Ask, "What story do these dots tell?" (Expected: someone walked, ran, or a vehicle moved.) Explain that today learners will build their own ticker timer.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In pairs, learners build the ticker timer following the Hands-On Build instructions below.
   - Once built, one learner pulls a paper tape under the tapping pen while the other keeps a steady beat (metronome or hand clap at 1 beat per second).
   - Run the toy car down a gentle ramp while pulling the tape under the timer.
   - Examine the tape: are the dots evenly spaced? Do they get closer or farther apart?
   - Measure dot spacings with a ruler. Record: spacing between dot 1–2, 2–3, 3–4, etc.
   - **Key discovery:** Even spacing = steady speed. Increasing spacing = acceleration.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - If the metronome beats at 1 s intervals, each dot is 1 second apart.
   - Speed = dot spacing / time interval. For example, 2 mm spacing at 1 s intervals = 2 mm/s.
   - Acceleration = change in spacing / time². If spacing goes from 2 mm to 4 mm to 6 mm, the speed is increasing by 2 mm/s each second → acceleration = 2 mm/s².
   - Connect to Lesson 063: the dot tape is a physical speed–time graph. The spacing is like the height; the progression along the tape is like time.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains what dot spacing tells us. Homework: design a journey (steady, speed up, slow down) and predict what the dot tape would look like.

## Hands-On Build: Ticker Timer

### Parts
- Clothespin or small wooden block — 1 — laundry or market
- Rubber band (cut from bicycle tube) — 1 — jua kali fundi or old tyre
- Felt pen or marker — 1 — duka la vitabu
- Paper tape strips — 5–10 — cut from exercise book margins
- Metronome or steady beat source — 1 — phone app or hand clap
- Toy car or wheeled bottle — 1 — home or market
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Attach the felt pen to the clothespin so the nib points downward. The rubber band holds the pen in place, wrapped around the clothespin.
2. Position the clothespin over a table edge so the pen nib just touches a paper tape laid flat on the table.
3. One learner holds the clothespin steady; another pulls the tape smoothly under the pen.
4. A third learner keeps a steady beat (e.g., clap every second). On each beat, the pen holder taps the pen onto the tape.
5. Pull the tape while a toy car rolls past, or simulate motion by pulling at different speeds.
6. Label the tape with the beat number and measure dot spacings with a ruler.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before analysing, verify that the dots are clear and evenly timed. If dots are smudged, press the pen more lightly or pull the tape more smoothly.

### Common Mistakes
- **Smudged dots:** The pen presses too hard or the tape jerks. Fix: lighten the tap, pull smoothly.
- **Uneven timing:** The beat keeper speeds up or slows down. Fix: use a phone metronome app for steady rhythm.
- **Tape tears:** Pulling too fast or using thin paper. Fix: use thicker paper or pull more gently.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* The dots on a ticker tape are equally spaced. What does this tell you about the speed?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* The dots on a ticker tape get farther apart. What is happening to the speed?
3. A tape shows dot spacings of 3 mm, 3 mm, 3 mm, 3 mm. Calculate the speed if the timer beats every second.
4. A tape shows dot spacings of 2 mm, 4 mm, 6 mm. Calculate the speeds and the acceleration.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The speed is **steady (constant)**.
2. The object is **speeding up (accelerating)**.
3. Speed = 3 mm / 1 s = **3 mm/s** (or 0.003 m/s).
4. Speeds: 2 mm/s, 4 mm/s, 6 mm/s. Acceleration = change in speed / time = (4 − 2) / 1 = **2 mm/s²** (or (6 − 4) / 1 = 2 mm/s², consistent).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A ticker timer produces dots at 5 beats per second. The spacing between consecutive dots grows from 2 mm to 4 mm to 6 mm. Calculate the three instantaneous speeds and the acceleration. Explain why the acceleration is constant even though the spacing grows linearly.

**Worked Answer:**
Time interval between dots = 1 / 5 = **0.2 s**.

Speeds (using spacing / time interval):
- v₁ = 2 mm / 0.2 s = **10 mm/s**
- v₂ = 4 mm / 0.2 s = **20 mm/s**
- v₃ = 6 mm / 0.2 s = **30 mm/s**

Acceleration:
a = Δv / Δt = (20 − 10) / 0.2 = **50 mm/s²** (or (30 − 20) / 0.2 = 50 mm/s²).

The acceleration is constant because the speed increases by the same amount (10 mm/s) in each equal time interval (0.2 s). The spacing grows linearly (2, 4, 6 mm) because the speed itself grows linearly under constant acceleration. In fact, for constant acceleration from rest, displacements in successive equal time intervals are in the ratio 1:3:5:7... but here the intervals are consecutive dot-to-dot spacings (which represent speed, not cumulative displacement), so they grow as 2, 4, 6 — an arithmetic progression, confirming constant acceleration.

**Q:** Compare the ticker-timer method with using a stopwatch and tape measure to find speed. What are the advantages and disadvantages of each? In what situations would a ticker timer be more useful?

**Worked Answer:**

**Ticker timer advantages:**
- Records motion continuously, not just at start and finish.
- Shows changes in speed within a single run (acceleration).
- No need to measure short times manually — the beat provides regular intervals.
- Produces a permanent record (the tape) for later analysis.

**Ticker timer disadvantages:**
- Requires building and calibrating the device.
- Timing depends on the beat keeper's regularity (or a metronome).
- Friction between pen and tape can slow the motion being studied.
- Less precise than electronic sensors.

**Stopwatch and tape advantages:**
- Simple and familiar — no special equipment needed.
- Direct measurement of average speed.

**Stopwatch and tape disadvantages:**
- Only gives average speed over the whole distance.
- Cannot show changes in speed during the run.
- Reaction time introduces error.

**When ticker timer is more useful:** Studying acceleration, comparing motion at different points in a journey, or when a stopwatch is too imprecise for short distances.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure the pen nib does not splinter or break; use a felt pen with a secure cap.
- Keep fingers away from the table edge when pulling tape rapidly.
- The rubber band under tension can snap; wear eye protection if available, or stand back when adjusting tension.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 055–060 (speed, acceleration, measuring motion).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on building the device, producing a tape, and recognising even vs uneven spacing. Ages 14–17 should calculate speeds and accelerations from dot spacing, and understand the mathematical relationship.
