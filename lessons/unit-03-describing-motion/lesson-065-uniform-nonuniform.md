# Lesson 065: Uniform and Non-Uniform Motion

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Distinguish uniform motion (steady speed) from non-uniform motion (changing speed) using ticker-tape evidence.
2. Read dot spacing on a tape: equal gaps mean uniform; growing gaps mean acceleration.
3. Classify everyday Kenyan motions — matatu in traffic, falling mango, boda on an open highway — as uniform or non-uniform.
4. Sketch and label distance–time and velocity–time graphs for both kinds of motion.

## Materials
- Ticker timer kit (or a jua kali vibrator: a doorbell buzzer bolted to a block, striking carbon paper discs)
- Paper tape strips, about 2 m (cash-register roll or glued exercise-book strips)
- Toy car or trolley with free wheels (a fundi's roller cart)
- Falling mass: a stone or 500 g weight on a string over a pulley
- Pre-run tapes prepared by the teacher (one steady pull, one falling-mass pull)
- Ruler, pencil, graph-squared exercise book pages, cellotape

## Vocabulary
- **uniform motion** (mwendo sawasawa) — motion at constant speed in a straight line
- **non-uniform motion** (mwendo usio sawasawa) — motion where speed or direction changes
- **ticker timer** (kikosi cha kutia alama) — device stamping dots at equal time intervals on tape
- **displacement** (makazi) — distance moved in a stated direction
- **velocity–time graph** (grafu ya kasi na muda) — graph showing how speed changes with time

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. A boda covers 20 m every second on a straight road. What name do we give this motion?
2. On a ticker tape, dots get further and further apart. What is the object doing?
3. Give one example from home or the market of motion that is NOT uniform.
4. Sketch the shape of a distance–time graph for an object at rest.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Uniform motion (constant speed).
2. Speeding up — accelerating (non-uniform motion).
3. Accept any: a matatu in jam, a falling mango, a mkokoteni starting off.
4. A horizontal straight line (distance does not change with time).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect before the demo begins.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — tell the story of two journeys: a bus cruising at a steady 60 km/h on the Nairobi–Nakuru highway, and the same bus crawling away from a stage. Ask: *same bus, same driver — what is different about the motion?* Board the word "steady".
3. **Explore (15 min)** — teacher demonstrates the ticker timer twice before the class: first pulling the tape through by hand as steadily as possible behind the toy car, then letting the falling stone drag the tape. Small groups receive one tape of each kind, mark every fifth dot, measure the gaps with rulers, and tabulate "distance in each interval" for both tapes. Groups announce: which tape has equal gaps, which has growing gaps.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate: equal gaps per equal time = uniform motion; the steady hand pull approximates it. Growing gaps = non-uniform motion; the falling stone accelerates (gaps grow by roughly the same extra amount each interval — a taste of uniform acceleration). Sketch the four graph panels on the board: d–t straight sloping line (uniform), d–t curve bending upward (accelerating), v–t horizontal line (uniform), v–t straight rising line (accelerating).
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark from the board key.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *the tape never lies — spacing tells the motion*. Homework: list three motions you see on the way home and classify each as uniform or non-uniform, with a reason.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. A matatu covers equal distances in equal times on a straight stretch. Name the motion. (mirrors pre-test)
2. Dots on a tape get closer and closer together. What is the object doing? (mirrors pre-test)
3. A tape has gaps of 2 cm, 4 cm, 6 cm, 8 cm in successive equal time intervals. Uniform or non-uniform? Justify in one sentence.
4. Sketch and label the v–t graph for (a) steady 5 m/s and (b) starting from rest and speeding up steadily.
5. Challenge: why is "uniform motion" rare in real life? Give one reason using a Kenyan road example.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Uniform motion.
2. Slowing down — decelerating (non-uniform motion).
3. Non-uniform: the object covers more distance in each equal time, so it is accelerating.
4. (a) Horizontal line at v = 5 m/s; (b) straight line rising from the origin. Axes labelled v (m/s) and t (s).
5. Accept: friction, traffic, hills, stops — e.g., a matatu must brake for bumps and stages, so perfect steady speed is almost never kept.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** On a falling-mass tape, successive 5-dot intervals measure 3 cm, 5 cm, 7 cm, 9 cm. The timer makes 5 dots per 0.1 s. Find the average speed in the first and last intervals and state what the pattern shows.
   **Worked Answer:** Each 5-dot interval is 0.1 s. First: v = 0.03 m / 0.1 s = 0.3 m/s. Last: v = 0.09 m / 0.1 s = 0.9 m/s. Speed tripled across four intervals, and the gaps grow by a constant 2 cm each step — the signature of uniform (constant) acceleration.
2. **Q:** Sketch four labelled panels: d–t and v–t for uniform motion, and d–t and v–t for accelerating motion. Explain the gradient of each d–t panel in words.
   **Worked Answer:** Uniform: d–t is a straight sloping line whose gradient = constant speed; v–t is a horizontal line at that speed. Accelerating: d–t is a curve bending upward whose gradient increases with time (getting faster); v–t is a straight line rising from the origin whose gradient = acceleration. All four panels carry axis labels: d (m) or v (m/s) against t (s).
3. **Q:** A ticker timer stamps 50 dots per second. A gap of 4 cm is measured between two dots on a steadily pulled tape. Find the tape's speed.
   **Worked Answer:** Time per dot = 1/50 = 0.02 s. Speed = distance/time = 0.04 m / 0.02 s = 2 m/s.

## Safety Notes
- The ticker timer uses mains or battery — check frayed wires on jua kali builds; a fundi should insulate all joints with tape.
- Falling stones land near feet — mark a drop zone and keep toes clear; drop onto a folded sack.
- Carbon-paper discs stain clothes and fingers — handle by the edges; wash hands after.
- Keep long tape strips away from necks when learners lean across the bench.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lessons 061–064 (distance, speed, time, stopwatch use) have run; this lesson opens the graphing strand of Unit 3.
- Younger learners (10–13): skip the numeric speeds; they only compare gaps by eye — "equal" vs "growing" — and trace the two d–t shapes. Older learners (14–17): measure gaps, compute interval speeds, and attempt the Advanced Questions.
- No ticker timer? Punch dots by hand: one learner pulls the tape while another taps a charcoal stick on it to a steady "one-and-two-and" count. Results are rougher but the comparison still works.
- A doorbell-buzzer vibrator from a jua kali fundi costs little and lasts years; register tape from any duka replaces official paper.
- CBE note: the gap table and four-panel sketch are evidence for the "data handling and interpretation" competency; file one labelled sketch per learner.
