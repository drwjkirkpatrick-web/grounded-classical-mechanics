# Lesson 067: Relative Motion: Passing and Being Passed

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State that motion is measured relative to a chosen observer or "frame".
2. Add speeds when two objects move towards each other, and subtract speeds when one overtakes the other.
3. Compute closing speeds for walkers, bodas and boats on a flowing river.
4. Solve simple upstream/downstream river problems using effective speed.

## Materials
- Open ground: a school field, church compound or clear stretch of veranda, about 30 m
- Stopwatch (phone clock is fine)
- Two chalk lines or sisal strings to mark a start and finish, 20 m apart
- Two learners as walkers, one as timer, class as observers
- Whistle or hand-clap for a start signal
- Exercise books, pencil; board for the worked examples

## Vocabulary
- **relative motion** (mwendo wa kulinganisha) — motion of one object as seen from another
- **frame of reference** (msingi wa kurejea) — the observer or point you measure motion from
- **closing speed** (kasi ya kukaribiana) — how fast the gap between two objects shrinks
- **upstream** (kuelekea chanzo cha mto) — against the river's flow
- **downstream** (kuelekea baharini) — with the river's flow

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. You sit in a matatu and another matatu beside you moves off. For a moment you feel you are moving backwards. Why?
2. Two learners walk towards each other at 2 m/s each. How fast does the gap between them close?
3. A boda at 10 m/s overtakes a cyclist at 4 m/s going the same way. How fast does the boda gain on the cyclist?
4. A boat engine pushes the boat at 3 m/s in still water. The river flows at 1 m/s downstream. What is the boat's speed past the bank when heading downstream?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. You are judging motion against the other matatu (the moving frame), not the ground.
2. 2 + 2 = 4 m/s.
3. 10 − 4 = 6 m/s.
4. 3 + 1 = 4 m/s.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect before heading outside.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — ask who has sat in a matatu and "moved backwards" when the neighbouring vehicle pulled away. Board the puzzle: *were you really moving?* Motion depends on who is watching.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — field demonstrations. Demo A: two walkers start 20 m apart and walk towards each other; the timer records the meeting time (~5 s at 2 m/s each) and the class computes the closing speed 20 m / 5 s = 4 m/s = the SUM. Demo B: both walk the same direction, the faster starting behind; time how long the gap takes to close and confirm it matches the DIFFERENCE of speeds. Demo C (seated): the class watches a walker pass a fixed chalk line, then imagines the same walk seen from a second walker moving the other way — the speed past each other is again the sum.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate on the board with three rules: towards each other → add; same direction, faster chasing → subtract; river problems → boat speed ± flow speed (downstream add, upstream subtract). Tell the ferry story: a boat that does 3 m/s on a river flowing 1 m/s makes 4 m/s downstream past the trees but only 2 m/s upstream — that is why upriver journeys take longer, and why swimmers must not fight a strong current.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark from the board key.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *towards: add. Chasing: subtract. River: add or subtract the flow.* Homework: describe one relative-motion moment from a matatu ride in two sentences.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Two friends walk towards each other at 1.5 m/s and 2.5 m/s. Find their closing speed. (mirrors pre-test)
2. A matatu at 20 m/s overtakes a mkokoteni at 2 m/s going the same way. How fast does the gap close? (mirrors pre-test)
3. Two runners 60 m apart jog towards each other and meet after 12 s. Find their closing speed; if one jogged at 2 m/s, how fast was the other?
4. A boat does 5 m/s in still water. A river flows at 2 m/s. Find its speed past the bank going (a) downstream and (b) upstream.
5. Challenge: you drop a mango from a moving boda. To you it falls straight down. What does a person standing on the road see, and whose description is correct?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. 1.5 + 2.5 = 4 m/s.
2. 20 − 2 = 18 m/s.
3. Closing speed = 60 / 12 = 5 m/s; the other jogged at 5 − 2 = 3 m/s.
4. (a) 5 + 2 = 7 m/s; (b) 5 − 2 = 3 m/s.
5. The roadside observer sees it curve forward (a parabola) because the mango keeps the boda's forward speed. BOTH descriptions are correct — each is relative to a different frame of reference.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)
*For older or faster learners. Worked solutions included for the teacher.*

1. **Q:** A boat travels at 3 m/s in still water on a river flowing at 1 m/s. Find its effective speeds upstream and downstream, then the round-trip time for two legs of 600 m each (downstream out, upstream back).
   **Worked Answer:** Downstream: 3 + 1 = 4 m/s → t = 600/4 = 150 s. Upstream: 3 − 1 = 2 m/s → t = 600/2 = 300 s. Round trip = 150 + 300 = 450 s = 7.5 min. (Note: slower than 400 s for the same 1,200 m in still water — current always costs time on a round trip.)
2. **Q:** Two matatus approach a stage from opposite ends of a 900 m straight, one at 15 m/s and one at 10 m/s. After how many seconds do they meet, and how far from the faster matatu's start?
   **Worked Answer:** Closing speed = 15 + 10 = 25 m/s. Time to meet = 900/25 = 36 s. Faster matatu covers 15 × 36 = 540 m from its start (the slower covers 360 m; 540 + 360 = 900 m ✓).
3. **Q:** A swimmer can do 1.2 m/s in still water. A river flows at 0.8 m/s. Can she make any headway swimming straight upstream? If she aims across the 40 m-wide river heading perpendicular to the bank, how long does crossing take and how far downstream is she carried?
   **Worked Answer:** Upstream effective speed = 1.2 − 0.8 = 0.4 m/s, so yes, slow headway. Crossing perpendicular: her across-river component is 1.2 m/s → t = 40/1.2 ≈ 33.3 s. Drift = 0.8 × 33.3 ≈ 27 m downstream.

## Safety Notes
- Walking demos only — NO running in the timed trials; a collision at walking speed is a joke, at running speed an injury.
- Inspect the ground for holes, stones and glass before the demos; mark a corridor with stones and keep spectators behind the chalk lines.
- Walkers pass shoulder-to-shoulder, never head-on; brief them to step to the right as they meet.
- Sun and heat: hats and water for outdoor blocks; keep the field work to the 15-minute Explore window.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lessons 061–066 (speed, units, uniform motion); relative motion is the capstone of Unit 3's kinematics strand and feeds JS-FE reasoning outcomes.
- Younger learners (10–13): keep the two rules (add towards, subtract chasing) with whole-number speeds; act the demos physically before any arithmetic. Older learners (14–17): full river problems and the frame-of-reference discussion (the mango in post-test Q5).
- No stopwatch? Count "one-and-two-and" steadily; closing speeds within 10% still demonstrate the add/subtract rules.
- Local anchors: the Lake Victoria ferry vs a motorboat, canoes on River Tana, and the daily matatu overtake drama on any highway — every learner has SEEN relative motion; this lesson only names it.
- A common error: learners add speeds for same-direction chasing. Confront it directly in Demo B by timing the real chase and comparing with both predictions.
- CBE note: the meeting-time prediction and measurement (Explore) is evidence for the "investigation and prediction" competency; record each group's predicted vs measured time.
