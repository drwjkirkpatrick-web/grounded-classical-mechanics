# Lesson 071: Rolling Down Ramps: Bottles, Balls and Cylinders

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Compare the rolling speeds of different objects down a ramp.
2. Observe that shape and mass distribution affect rolling motion.
3. Predict which object will win a race based on its properties.

## Materials
- A ramp board or plank (at least 1 m long)
- Books or bricks for propping
- A solid rubber ball or cricket ball
- Two identical plastic bottles (one filled with water, one empty)
- A stopwatch or phone timer
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Rolling** *(kuzunguka)* — motion where an object rotates while moving along a surface.
- **Sliding** *(kuteleza)* — motion where an object moves without rotating.
- **Mass distribution** *(mgawanyo wa uzito)* — how the mass is spread inside an object.
- **Rotation** *(mzunguko)* — spinning around an axis.

## Pre-Test
1. Will a full bottle or an empty bottle roll faster down a ramp?
2. What is the difference between rolling and sliding?
3. If you drop a ball and a bottle from the same height, which hits the ground first?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Learners may guess either; the lesson will reveal the answer through experiment.
2. Rolling involves both moving forward and spinning; sliding is just moving forward without spinning.
3. They hit at the same time (if air resistance is ignored), but rolling down a ramp is different from falling.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands and record guesses.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show the ball, full bottle, and empty bottle. Ask, "If I race these down a ramp, which wins?" (Expected: mixed guesses.) Explain that today's experiment will settle it.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, set up a ramp at moderate steepness (2 books under one end).
   - **Race 1:** Release the full bottle and empty bottle simultaneously from the top. Record which reaches the bottom first. Repeat three times.
   - **Race 2:** Release the ball and the full bottle simultaneously. Record the winner. Repeat three times.
   - **Key discovery:** The ball usually wins, followed by the full bottle, then the empty bottle — or the full bottle and ball may be close. The empty bottle is slowest because its mass is concentrated at the edges (the plastic shell), making it harder to get spinning.
   - Groups discuss: why does the full bottle beat the empty one? (The water inside moves with the bottle, adding mass near the centre. The empty bottle's mass is all at the edges.)
   - **Concept:** Rolling objects share their energy between moving forward and spinning. Objects with mass closer to the centre spin more easily and roll faster.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - When an object rolls, gravity gives it energy. Some energy goes into moving forward, some into spinning.
   - The ball wins because its mass is concentrated at the centre — it spins easily.
   - The full bottle has water distributed throughout — some mass near the centre, some near the edges.
   - The empty bottle has almost all its mass at the edges (the plastic walls) — hardest to spin, so slowest.
   - **Teaser for future:** In senior physics, this is called "moment of inertia" — how mass distribution affects rotation.
   - Connection to Kenyan life: bicycle wheels (mass at rim = harder to spin), posho mill stones (heavy rim stores energy).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states why the ball rolls fastest. Homework: find two rolling objects at home and predict which would win a ramp race.

## Hands-On Build: Rolling Race

### Parts
- Plank or mabati sheet — 1 m — classroom or jua kali
- Books or bricks — 2–3 — classroom
- Solid ball — 1 — home or market
- Plastic bottles — 2 identical — home
- Water — to fill one bottle — tap
- Stopwatch — 1 — phone or classroom

### Workflow
1. Prop one end of the plank on two books to create a ramp.
2. Mark a start line 10 cm from the top and a finish line at the bottom.
3. Fill one bottle with water and seal it tightly. Leave the other empty.
4. Hold both bottles at the start line. Release them simultaneously on a count of "three."
5. Observe which reaches the finish line first. Record the winner.
6. Repeat three times for reliability.
7. Repeat the race with the ball vs the full bottle.
8. **Checkpoint:** Before declaring a winner, ensure all three trials agree. If results are mixed, check that the ramp is smooth and the releases are truly simultaneous.

### Common Mistakes
- **Releasing at different times:** One bottle gets a head start. Fix: use a ruler to hold both back, then lift it cleanly.
- **Ramp too steep:** All objects finish too quickly to tell the difference. Fix: use a gentler slope (one book).
- **Bottle cap loose:** Water leaks and changes the mass distribution mid-race. Fix: seal caps tightly.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* In the race, which object usually rolled fastest down the ramp?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What is the difference between rolling and sliding?
3. Why does a solid ball roll faster than an empty bottle of the same size?
4. Predict: would a bicycle wheel (mass at rim) roll faster or slower than a solid disc of the same mass and size?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The **solid ball** usually wins.
2. Rolling involves both forward motion and spinning; sliding is forward motion without spinning.
3. The ball's mass is concentrated at the centre, making it easier to spin. The empty bottle's mass is at the edges, making it harder to spin.
4. **Slower** — the bicycle wheel has mass at the rim (edges), so it is harder to spin than a solid disc where mass is spread throughout.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain using the idea of energy sharing between translation (moving forward) and rotation (spinning) why the solid ball finishes before the full bottle, which finishes before the empty bottle.

**Worked Answer:**
When an object rolls down a ramp, gravitational potential energy converts into two forms:
1. **Translational kinetic energy** — energy of moving forward: KE_trans = ½mv².
2. **Rotational kinetic energy** — energy of spinning: KE_rot = ½Iω², where I is moment of inertia and ω is angular speed.

The total energy is fixed by the height of the ramp: mgh = ½mv² + ½Iω².

For a given total energy, more energy spent on rotation means less available for forward motion (translation).

- **Solid ball:** Mass concentrated near the centre → small moment of inertia (I = 2/5 mr²) → little energy needed for rotation → most energy goes to translation → fastest.
- **Full bottle:** Water distributed throughout → moderate moment of inertia → moderate energy for rotation → moderate translation speed.
- **Empty bottle:** Mass concentrated at the walls (far from centre) → large moment of inertia → much energy needed for rotation → less energy for translation → slowest.

This is why the ball wins: it is the most "efficient" at converting gravitational energy into forward motion.

**Q:** A bicycle wheel and a solid disc of the same mass and radius roll down identical ramps. Predict which reaches the bottom first and justify using the energy-sharing argument.

**Worked Answer:**
The **solid disc** reaches the bottom first.

Reasoning:
- A bicycle wheel has most of its mass at the rim (far from the centre), giving it a large moment of inertia.
- A solid disc has mass distributed throughout, giving it a smaller moment of inertia than the wheel (I_disc = ½mr² vs I_wheel ≈ mr² for a thin rim).
- With less moment of inertia, the disc needs less rotational energy for the same angular speed, leaving more energy for translation.
- Therefore, the disc accelerates faster and reaches the bottom first.

This is why engineers design flywheels (like posho mill stones) with heavy rims — the large moment of inertia stores energy and keeps them spinning steadily. But for racing, you want the opposite: mass near the centre for quick acceleration.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure the ramp landing area is clear of feet.
- Bottles should be sealed to prevent water spillage.
- Do not stand directly behind the ramp where objects may roll out.
- If using glass bottles, wrap them in tape or use plastic bottles instead.
