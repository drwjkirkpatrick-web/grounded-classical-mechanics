# Lesson 072: Deceleration: Slowing Down Is Acceleration Too

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define deceleration as negative acceleration.
2. Identify everyday situations where objects decelerate.
3. Use sign conventions to describe acceleration direction.

## Materials
- A ball or stone
- A patch of grass or rough ground
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Deceleration** *(kupungua kasi)* — acceleration that reduces speed; negative acceleration.
- **Negative acceleration** *(kasi hasi)* — acceleration in the opposite direction to motion.
- **Sign convention** *(utawala wa alama)* — a rule for assigning positive and negative directions.
- **Friction** *(kikwazo)* — a force that opposes motion and causes deceleration.

## Pre-Test
1. A ball rolls on grass and slows down. Is it accelerating?
2. What force causes a moving bicycle to slow down when you stop pedalling?
3. If forward motion is positive, what sign does deceleration have?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes — the velocity is changing (decreasing).
2. Friction (air resistance and rolling resistance).
3. Negative — deceleration is acceleration opposite to the direction of motion.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Roll a ball across the ground. Ask, "Is the ball accelerating as it slows down?" (Expected: confusion — many learners think acceleration only means speeding up.) Explain that any change in velocity is acceleration, including slowing down.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, roll a ball on different surfaces: smooth concrete, rough grass, sandy ground.
   - Measure how far each ball rolls before stopping. Time how long it takes to stop.
   - **Key discovery:** Rougher surfaces stop the ball faster (greater deceleration). Smoother surfaces let it roll farther (less deceleration).
   - Groups discuss: what is causing the ball to slow down? (Friction between the ball and the ground.)
   - **Swing observation:** Push a learner on a swing. Count how many swings before it stops. Discuss: what is slowing the swing? (Air resistance, friction at the pivot.)

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Deceleration = negative acceleration.** If we call forward motion "positive," then slowing down is "negative acceleration."
   - Formula: a = (v − u) / t. If v < u, then a is negative.
   - Example: A ball rolls at 4 m/s and stops in 2 s. a = (0 − 4) / 2 = **−2 m/s²**.
   - Example: A bicycle slows from 6 m/s to 2 m/s in 2 s. a = (2 − 6) / 2 = −4 / 2 = **−2 m/s²**.
   - Sign conventions: choose one direction as positive. If motion is forward (+), then backward acceleration is (−). If motion is upward (+), then gravity is downward (−).
   - Kenyan examples: a matatu braking at a speed bump (deceleration), a mkokoteni cart slowing on uphill sand, a thrown ball rising then falling (acceleration changes sign at the top).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states why deceleration is still acceleration. Homework: list five examples of deceleration seen on the way home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A bicycle slows down as it approaches a junction. Is it accelerating?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* A car moving forward brakes. If forward is positive, what is the sign of the acceleration?
3. A ball rolls at 5 m/s and stops in 2.5 s. Calculate the deceleration.
4. Explain why a ball rolls farther on concrete than on grass.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Yes** — the velocity is decreasing, which is negative acceleration.
2. **Negative** — the acceleration opposes the forward motion.
3. a = (0 − 5) / 2.5 = −5 / 2.5 = **−2 m/s²**. Deceleration = 2 m/s².
4. Concrete has **less friction** than grass, so the deceleration is smaller. The ball takes longer to stop and covers more distance.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A ball enters grass at 4 m/s and stops in 2.5 m. Find the deceleration using v² = u² + 2as.

**Worked Answer:**
Given: u = 4 m/s, v = 0 m/s, s = 2.5 m.
Using v² = u² + 2as:
0 = (4)² + 2a(2.5)
0 = 16 + 5a
5a = −16
a = −16 / 5 = **−3.2 m/s²**.

The deceleration is **3.2 m/s²**.

This means the ball loses 3.2 m/s of speed every second. In the first second, it drops from 4 m/s to 0.8 m/s. In the next 0.25 s, it stops. The total stopping time = (v − u) / a = (0 − 4) / (−3.2) = **1.25 s**.

**Q:** Explain why "no acceleration at constant speed" is not always true, using the example of a child on a swing and a car going around a roundabout at steady speed.

**Worked Answer:**
"No acceleration at constant speed" is false when the **direction of motion changes**.

**Swing:** The child moves back and forth at varying speed, but even at the bottom where speed is maximum, the direction is changing (from down-and-forward to up-and-forward). The velocity vector changes direction, so there is acceleration toward the pivot (centripetal acceleration).

**Roundabout:** A car travels at constant speed around a circular roundabout. The speedometer reads the same number, but the direction keeps changing. The velocity vector rotates continuously. Therefore, there is acceleration toward the centre of the roundabout (centripetal acceleration).

In both cases, the speed is constant but the velocity is not — and acceleration is defined as the rate of change of **velocity**, not speed. This is why physics distinguishes carefully between speed (scalar) and velocity (vector).

## Safety Notes
- Roll balls away from groups, not toward them.
- Ensure the grass or rough area is free of sharp objects.
- Swings should be pushed gently; no standing on swings.
