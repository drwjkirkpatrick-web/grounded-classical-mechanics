# Lesson 078: Unit Review: Motion Field Day

**Unit:** 3 — Describing Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate understanding of motion concepts through station activities.
2. Apply measurement and graphing skills in practical contexts.
3. Identify areas of strength and weakness in their Unit 3 knowledge.

## Materials
- All materials from Unit 3 (tape measures, stopwatches, toy cars, ramp boards, chalk, graph paper)
- Station instruction cards (prepared by teacher)
- Unit quiz paper (one per learner)
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Review** *(kujirudia)* — going over material again to reinforce learning.
- **Station** *(kituo)* — a designated activity area in a rotation system.
- **Field day** *(siku ya uwanjani)* — a day of practical, hands-on activities.
- **Self-assessment** *(kujipima mwenyewe)* — judging your own understanding.

## Pre-Test
1. Define motion in your own words.
2. What is the difference between distance and displacement?
3. Name three ways to measure speed.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Motion is a change in position relative to a reference point.
2. Distance is the total path length; displacement is the straight-line change in position from start to finish.
3. Any three of: pacing + timing, stopwatch + tape measure, ticker timer, two-gate speed trap, speedometer, kilometre markers + watch.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands for Q3.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Explain that today learners rotate through stations, each testing a different Unit 3 skill. The goal is to practise, review, and self-assess.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Station 1: Dash Timing.** Time a 20 m sprint. Calculate speed. Compare with Lesson 060 results.
   - **Station 2: Graph Reading.** Given a distance–time graph, identify: steady speed, stops, returns, fastest leg.
   - **Station 3: Reaction Test.** Ruler-drop test (Lesson 068). Measure reaction time. Compare with class average.
   - **Station 4: Relative Motion.** Two learners walk toward each other. Observers describe motion from different reference frames.
   - **Station 5: Speed Estimation.** Estimate the speed of a rolling ball without instruments. Check with a stopwatch.
   - Groups rotate every 3 minutes. Learners record one key observation per station.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - Review key formulas: v = d/t, a = Δv/t, gradient of d–t graph = speed.
   - Review key concepts: reference frames, vectors vs scalars, average vs instantaneous speed.
   - Self-assessment: learners rate themselves 1–5 on each concept. Identify one strength and one area for improvement.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (unit quiz, see below). Learners work individually.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner shares their strength and one area for improvement. Homework: review notes for the weakest area.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Define motion and give one example from your daily life.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* You walk 30 m east then 40 m north. What is your displacement?
3. A car travels 150 km in 2.5 hours. What is its average speed in km/h?
4. Explain why a passenger in a moving matatu is at rest relative to the seat but moving relative to the road.
5. A speed–time graph shows a flat line at 15 m/s for 10 s. What distance was covered?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Motion is a change in position relative to a reference point. Example: walking to school.
2. Displacement magnitude = √(30² + 40²) = √2500 = **50 m** northeast.
3. Average speed = 150 km / 2.5 h = **60 km/h**.
4. Relative to the seat: the passenger is not changing position. Relative to the road: the passenger's position changes as the matatu moves.
5. Distance = speed × time = 15 m/s × 10 s = **150 m**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Write one higher-order motion question (graph interpretation) with a full marking scheme for the unit quiz.

**Worked Answer:**
**Question:** The graph below shows the distance–time journey of a boda-boda rider from home to the market and back. Describe the motion in each section and calculate the speed for the fastest leg.

*(Graph description for teacher: A–B is a straight line rising to 4 km in 10 min. B–C is horizontal for 5 min. C–D is a straight line falling to 0 km in 15 min.)*

**Marking scheme:**
- **Section A–B:** Rider travels from home to market at steady speed. (1 mark)
- **Section B–C:** Rider stops at the market for 5 minutes. (1 mark)
- **Section C–D:** Rider returns home at steady speed. (1 mark)
- **Speed A–B:** 4 km / 10 min = 0.4 km/min = **24 km/h** (or 6.67 m/s). (2 marks)
- **Speed C–D:** 4 km / 15 min = 0.267 km/min = **16 km/h** (or 4.44 m/s). (2 marks)
- **Fastest leg:** A–B at 24 km/h. (1 mark)
- **Total marks: 8**

**Higher-order thinking:** This question requires interpretation of graph shape, calculation of two speeds, comparison, and explanation — not just recall.

**Q:** A matatu travels from Nairobi to Mombasa (approximately 500 km). The outward journey takes 8 hours. The return journey takes 6 hours. Calculate (a) the average speed for each leg, (b) the average speed for the round trip, and (c) explain why the average speed for the round trip is not simply the average of the two leg speeds.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Outward average speed = 500 km / 8 h = **62.5 km/h**.
    Return average speed = 500 km / 6 h = **83.3 km/h**.

(b) Round-trip average speed = total distance / total time = 1000 km / 14 h = **71.4 km/h**.

(c) The average of the two leg speeds = (62.5 + 83.3) / 2 = **72.9 km/h**. This differs from the true average (71.4 km/h) because the two legs took different times. The slower leg (8 hours) has more "weight" in the total time than the faster leg (6 hours). Average speed is always total distance divided by total time — never a simple average of individual speeds unless the times are equal.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure sprinting areas are clear of obstacles.
- Ruler-drop tests should be conducted with plastic rulers, not metal ones.
- Toy car ramps should be stable and not aimed toward groups.
- All rotation activities should be supervised.
