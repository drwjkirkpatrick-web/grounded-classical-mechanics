# Lesson 080: Do Heavy Things Fall Faster? A Fair Test

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a fair test to compare falling speeds of objects with different masses.
2. Identify air resistance as a confounding variable in falling experiments.
3. Predict and observe that mass alone does not determine falling speed.

## Materials
- Two stones of different sizes
- A sheet of paper
- A coin
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Fair test** *(jaribio la haki)* — an experiment where only one variable is changed at a time.
- **Confounding variable** *(kigezo cha kuchanganya)* — a factor that affects the result and makes it hard to draw clear conclusions.
- **Air resistance** *(upinzani wa hewa)* — the force of air pushing against a moving object.
- **Controlled experiment** *(jaribio lililodhibitiwa)* — a test where all conditions except the one being studied are kept the same.

## Pre-Test
1. Drop a coin and a flat sheet of paper from the same height. Which hits the ground first?
2. Now crumple the paper into a ball. Drop it with the coin. What happens?
3. What factor other than mass might affect how fast something falls?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The coin hits first — the flat paper has large air resistance.
2. They hit at nearly the same time — crumpling reduces air resistance.
3. Air resistance (shape, size).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Hold up a coin and a flat sheet of paper. Ask, "Which falls faster?" (Expected: coin.) Drop them together. The coin wins. Now crumple the paper. Ask, "Now what?" Drop again. They land together. Introduce the idea of a fair test.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design tests to compare falling objects.
   - **Test 1:** Drop a large stone and a small stone from shoulder height. Record which lands first. Repeat three times.
   - **Test 2:** Drop a coin and a flat sheet of paper from the same height. Record. Crumple the paper and repeat.
   - **Test 3:** Drop two identical plastic bottles — one empty, one half-filled with water. Record.
   - **Key discovery:** Large and small stones land together (if dropped fairly). Coin and flat paper do not land together. Coin and crumpled paper do. Empty and full bottles land together.
   - Groups discuss: what is the real difference between the tests? (Air resistance, not mass.)

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Mass does not determine falling speed in a vacuum.** All objects fall at the same rate (g ≈ 10 m/s²) if only gravity acts.
   - **Air resistance** depends on shape and surface area, not mass. A flat paper has large area → large air resistance → falls slowly. A stone has small area → small air resistance → falls fast.
   - **Fair test:** To test mass, keep shape and size the same. The two bottles (same shape, different mass) land together, proving mass does not matter when air resistance is equal.
   - **Same-height release method:** Hold both objects at the same height, release simultaneously without pushing.
   - Connection to Lesson 079: Aristotle was wrong because he did not control for air resistance.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states what makes a falling test fair. Homework: find two objects of the same shape but different mass and predict what would happen if dropped.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* You drop a coin and a flat sheet of paper from the same height. Which lands first? Why?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* Name the factor that makes light objects fall slower in air.
3. Two identical bottles are dropped — one empty, one full of water. Which lands first?
4. Explain why crumpling a sheet of paper makes it fall faster.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The **coin** lands first. The flat paper has large air resistance.
2. **Air resistance** (or shape/surface area).
3. They land **at the same time** (same shape, so same air resistance; mass does not matter).
4. Crumpling **reduces surface area**, which reduces air resistance, allowing gravity to dominate.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a test where mass differs but shape does not (e.g., bottles with different water levels). Predict the outcome and explain why it tests the effect of mass alone.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design:**
- Take three identical plastic bottles.
- Bottle A: empty (mass ≈ 50 g).
- Bottle B: half-filled with water (mass ≈ 300 g).
- Bottle C: full of water (mass ≈ 550 g).
- Seal all caps tightly.
- Drop all three from the same height simultaneously.

**Prediction:** All three bottles will land at **approximately the same time**.

**Explanation:**
- The independent variable is **mass** (50 g, 300 g, 550 g).
- The controlled variables are **shape, size, and surface area** (identical bottles).
- If mass determined falling speed, the full bottle should land first.
- If all land together, mass does **not** determine falling speed when air resistance is controlled.
- This isolates mass as the only difference, making it a fair test of Aristotle's claim.

**Q:** A feather and a hammer are dropped on the Moon (no atmosphere). Explain why they land together, and calculate how far they fall in 2 seconds.

**Worked Answer:**
On the Moon, there is **no atmosphere** → no air resistance. Only gravity acts.

Since gravity accelerates all objects at the same rate regardless of mass, the feather and hammer fall together. This was demonstrated by Apollo astronaut David Scott in 1971.

Moon gravity: g_moon ≈ 1.6 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth's g).

Distance fallen in 2 s:
s = ut + ½gt² = 0 + ½ × 1.6 × (2)² = 0.8 × 4 = **3.2 m**.

On Earth, the hammer would fall:
s = ½ × 10 × 4 = **20 m** in the same time.

The Moon's lower gravity means objects fall more slowly, but the principle — all objects fall at the same rate in vacuum — holds everywhere.

## Safety Notes
- Drop objects away from feet and other learners.
- Use small stones, not large rocks.
- Ensure the landing area is flat and clear.
