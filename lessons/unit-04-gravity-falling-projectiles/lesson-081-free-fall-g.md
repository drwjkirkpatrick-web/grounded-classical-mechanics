# Lesson 081: Free Fall and the Acceleration g

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State that free-falling objects accelerate at approximately 10 m/s² near Earth's surface.
2. Calculate speed and distance fallen for objects dropped from rest.
3. Build a velocity table for successive seconds of free fall.

## Materials
- A ball
- Chalkboard or paper for tables
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Free fall** *(kuduruma huru)* — motion under gravity alone, with no other forces acting.
- **Acceleration due to gravity** *(kasi ya mvutano wa ardhi)* — the rate at which gravity speeds up falling objects; symbol g ≈ 10 m/s².
- **Velocity table** *(jedwali la kasi)* — a table showing speed at different times.
- **Initial velocity** *(kasi ya mwanzo)* — the speed at the start of motion (u).

## Pre-Test
1. What is the speed of a dropped ball after 1 second of free fall?
2. How far does a ball fall in 2 seconds?
3. Does a heavier ball fall faster than a lighter ball in free fall?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. v = gt = 10 × 1 = **10 m/s**.
2. d = ½gt² = ½ × 10 × 4 = **20 m**.
3. No — in free fall, all objects accelerate at the same rate.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Drop a ball and ask, "How fast is it going now? How fast will it be going in one second?" Introduce g as "the acceleration gravity gives to everything."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build a velocity table for free fall.
   - Time (s) | Speed (m/s) | Distance fallen (m)
   - 0 | 0 | 0
   - 1 | 10 | 5
   - 2 | 20 | 20
   - 3 | 30 | 45
   - 4 | 40 | 80
   - Groups calculate each row using v = gt and d = ½gt².
   - **Key discovery:** Speed grows by 10 m/s every second. Distance grows faster (quadratically).
   - Groups discuss: a mango falling from a tall tree — how fast is it going when it hits? (Depends on height — use d = ½gt² to find t, then v = gt.)

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **g ≈ 10 m/s²** means: every second, gravity adds 10 m/s to the downward speed.
   - **Formulas:**
     - Speed after time t: **v = gt** (dropped from rest, u = 0).
     - Distance fallen: **d = ½gt²**.
     - Combined: v² = 2gd.
   - **Examples:**
     - A stone dropped from a 20 m cliff: d = ½ × 10 × t² = 20 → t² = 4 → t = 2 s. Speed at impact: v = 10 × 2 = **20 m/s**.
     - A ball thrown upward at 15 m/s: reaches top when v = 0. Time to top: t = 15/10 = 1.5 s. Height: d = ½ × 10 × (1.5)² = **11.25 m**.
   - Kenyan examples: a coconut falling from a tree, a matatu rolling downhill, raindrops accelerating as they fall.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner recites the formula for distance fallen. Homework: calculate how long a mango takes to fall from a 10 m tree.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is the speed of a dropped object after 2 seconds?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How far does an object fall in 3 seconds?
3. A stone is dropped from a height of 45 m. How long does it take to hit the ground?
4. A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. How long until it reaches its highest point?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. v = 10 × 2 = **20 m/s**.
2. d = ½ × 10 × 9 = **45 m**.
3. 45 = ½ × 10 × t² → t² = 9 → t = **3 s**.
4. v = u − gt → 0 = 20 − 10t → t = **2 s**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Compute the speed and distance fallen at t = 1, 2, and 3 seconds for an object in free fall. Tabulate your results and explain why distance grows faster than speed.

**Worked Answer:**
| Time (s) | Speed v = gt (m/s) | Distance d = ½gt² (m) |
|----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 0        | 0                 | 0                      |
| 1        | 10                | 5                      |
| 2        | 20                | 20                     |
| 3        | 30                | 45                     |

**Why distance grows faster than speed:**
- Speed grows **linearly** (× time): v ∝ t.
- Distance grows **quadratically** (× time squared): d ∝ t².
- In the first second, the object covers 5 m. In the second second, it covers 15 m more (20 − 5). In the third second, it covers 25 m more (45 − 20).
- Each second, the object is moving faster than the previous second, so it covers more distance in that second. The increasing speed causes the distance to grow faster and faster.

**Q:** A stone is dropped from a height of 80 m. Calculate (a) the time to hit the ground, (b) the speed at impact, and (c) the distance fallen during the last second of motion.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) d = ½gt² → 80 = ½ × 10 × t² → t² = 16 → t = **4 s**.

(b) v = gt = 10 × 4 = **40 m/s** (or v² = 2gd = 2 × 10 × 80 = 1600 → v = 40 m/s).

(c) Distance fallen in the first 3 seconds: d₃ = ½ × 10 × 9 = 45 m.
Distance fallen in 4 seconds: d₄ = 80 m.
Distance in the last second: 80 − 45 = **35 m**.

This is much more than the distance in the first second (5 m), confirming that the stone accelerates throughout the fall.

## Safety Notes
- No physical dropping from heights in the classroom; use hypothetical examples.
- If demonstrating with a ball, use a soft ball and drop from shoulder height only.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 079–080 (history of gravity, fair tests).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on building the table and understanding that g = 10 m/s². Ages 14–17 should solve multi-step problems and explain quadratic growth.
- **Local sourcing:** A ball and notebook are sufficient.
