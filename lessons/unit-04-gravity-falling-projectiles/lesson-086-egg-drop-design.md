# Lesson 086: Egg Drop Challenge I: Design and Build

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** egg-drop

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a protective container for an egg using local materials.
2. Apply knowledge of air resistance and impact cushioning to a real problem.
3. Build a prototype following a design brief.

## Materials
- One raw egg per group (wrapped in a small plastic bag to reduce mess)
- Local cushioning materials: dry grass, cotton, newspaper, cloth scraps
- Container: a small box, plastic cup, or woven basket
- String or tape for securing
- Scissors
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Prototype** *(mfano wa kwanza)* — a first model built to test an idea.
- **Cushioning** *(kubanwa)* — material that absorbs impact by compressing.
- **Impact** *(mgongano)* — the force when two objects collide.
- **Design brief** *(maelezo ya ubunifu)* — a statement of what a design must achieve.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to an egg when it hits the ground from a height?
2. Name two ways to protect a falling egg.
3. Why does a crumpled newspaper cushion better than a flat sheet?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It breaks — the shell is brittle and cannot absorb the impact force.
2. Any two of: cushioning inside, parachute to slow fall, container to distribute force.
3. Crumpled paper has air pockets that compress and absorb energy.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show an egg. Ask, "If I drop this from the roof, what happens?" (Expected: it breaks.) "Your challenge: design a container so it does NOT break."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners receive the design brief and materials.
   - **Design brief:** Build a container that protects an egg dropped from 2 m. The container must:
     - Fit in a 20 cm × 20 cm × 20 cm space.
     - Use only provided materials.
     - Be ready to test in Lesson 087.
   - Groups brainstorm ideas:
     - **Air resistance:** Add a parachute or large surface area to slow the fall.
     - **Cushioning:** Surround the egg with soft, compressible material.
     - **Force distribution:** Place the egg in a rigid container so the shell does not take all the force.
   - Groups sketch their design and label key features.
   - **Build time:** Groups construct their prototype.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Physics of impact:** Force = change in momentum / time. Cushioning increases the time of impact, which reduces the force on the egg.
   - **Air resistance recap:** Slowing the fall reduces the impact speed. A parachute is effective but adds weight.
   - **Trade-offs:** More cushioning = more weight = faster fall. The best design balances cushioning and air resistance.
   - **Kenyan context:** Packaging fruit for transport, wrapping fragile items in cloth for market travel — same physics.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present their design sketch in 30 seconds. Homework: improve the design based on today's build.

## Hands-On Build: Egg Protector

### Parts
- Raw egg — 1 per group — kitchen
- Plastic bag — 1 — market
- Cushioning (dry grass, cotton, newspaper, cloth) — handful — home or school
- Container (small box, cup, basket) — 1 — home or school
- String or tape — 1 m — classroom
- Scissors — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Wrap the egg in the plastic bag to reduce mess if it breaks.
2. Sketch a design showing where the egg sits and how cushioning surrounds it.
3. Place the egg in the container.
4. Pack cushioning tightly around the egg on all sides. The egg should not touch the container walls directly.
5. If adding a parachute, attach it to the container with string.
6. Check that the container fits within the 20 cm limit.
7. Label the container with group names.
8. **Checkpoint:** Before finishing, gently shake the container. The egg should not move inside. If it rattles, add more cushioning.

### Common Mistakes
- **Egg touches the container wall:** Direct force transfers to the shell. Fix: ensure cushioning on all sides.
- **Too little cushioning:** Thin layers do not absorb enough energy. Fix: use thick, compressible layers.
- **Parachute too small:** Does not slow the fall enough. Fix: use a larger canopy or add multiple layers.
- **Container too heavy:** Falls fast. Fix: use light materials where possible.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Name two ways to protect a falling egg.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* Why is crumpled newspaper good for cushioning?
3. Explain why increasing impact time reduces the force on the egg.
4. List one trade-off in egg-drop design.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any two of: cushioning, parachute, rigid container, soft padding.
2. It has air pockets that compress and absorb impact energy.
3. Force = change in momentum / time. More time → less force for the same momentum change.
4. Examples: more cushioning = more weight; parachute = slower fall but harder to control; rigid container = good distribution but heavy.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain why a well-cushioned egg survives a fall, using the impulse equation FΔt = mΔv. Calculate the average force on a 0.05 kg egg that hits the ground at 6 m/s and stops in 0.02 s (bare ground) vs 0.2 s (well-cushioned).

**Worked Answer:**
**Impulse equation:** FΔt = mΔv → F = mΔv/Δt.

Given: m = 0.05 kg, Δv = 6 m/s (stops from 6 m/s).

**Bare ground (Δt = 0.02 s):**
F = 0.05 × 6 / 0.02 = 0.3 / 0.02 = **15 N**.

**Well-cushioned (Δt = 0.2 s):**
F = 0.05 × 6 / 0.2 = 0.3 / 0.2 = **1.5 N**.

The cushioned landing reduces the force by a factor of **10**.

The egg shell can withstand ~1–2 N without cracking. The bare ground force (15 N) shatters it; the cushioned force (1.5 N) is survivable.

**Q:** Design an egg container that uses BOTH cushioning and air resistance. Draw a labelled diagram and explain how each feature contributes to egg survival.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design:**
- A small basket (lightweight, distributes force).
- Inside: the egg wrapped in cotton and surrounded by crumpled newspaper (cushioning, increases impact time).
- Above: a plastic-bag parachute attached with 40 cm strings (air resistance, slows fall speed).
- Below: a flat cardboard base that hits first, compressing the newspaper before the egg feels force.

**How each feature helps:**
1. **Parachute:** Reduces terminal velocity from ~14 m/s to ~3 m/s. Lower impact speed means lower momentum change (mΔv is smaller).
2. **Cotton wrap:** Provides initial soft layer that absorbs energy and prevents direct shell contact.
3. **Crumpled newspaper:** Compresses on impact, increasing Δt from ~0.02 s to ~0.2 s. This dramatically reduces peak force.
4. **Basket:** Distributes any remaining force over the entire egg surface, rather than concentrating it at one point.
5. **Cardboard base:** Hits first and flattens, adding another layer of deceleration before the egg contacts anything.

Combined effect: the egg experiences a slow fall, a soft landing, and distributed force — all three principles working together.

## Safety Notes
- Handle raw eggs carefully; wash hands after handling.
- Use plastic bags to contain broken eggs.
- Do not eat eggs that have been dropped (even if unbroken, cracks may harbour bacteria).
- Ensure drop zones are clear of people.
