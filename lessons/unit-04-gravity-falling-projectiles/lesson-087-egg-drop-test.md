# Lesson 087: Egg Drop Challenge II: Test and Autopsy

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** egg-drop

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Test their egg-drop designs and record the results.
2. Analyse why designs succeeded or failed.
3. Suggest improvements based on physics principles.

## Materials
- Completed egg-drop containers from Lesson 086
- A ladder or raised platform (2 m)
- A stopwatch or phone timer
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Autopsy** *(uchunguzi)* — a detailed examination after a test to find causes of success or failure.
- **Iteration** *(kurekebisha)* — repeating a design with improvements based on feedback.
- **Failure mode** *(namna ya kushindwa)* — the specific way in which a design did not work.
- **Survivability** *(uwezekano wa kuishi)* — the ability of a design to protect its contents.

## Pre-Test
1. What will you observe during the egg-drop test?
2. If the egg breaks, what physical principle did the design fail to address?
3. How can you improve a design that broke?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Whether the egg survives, how fast it fell, how it landed.
2. Insufficient cushioning, excessive speed, or force concentration.
3. Add more cushioning, slow the fall with a parachute, or distribute force better.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Review the designs from Lesson 086. Ask each group to predict: will their egg survive? Why?

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Groups take turns dropping their containers from the 2 m platform.
   - Observers time the fall and note how the container lands.
   - After each drop, the group opens the container and checks the egg.
   - **Record:** survive/break, fall time, landing orientation, visible damage to container.
   - **Key discovery:** Some designs survive; others fail. The best designs combine cushioning and air resistance.
   - Groups with broken eggs identify the failure mode:
     - Egg touched the wall = insufficient cushioning.
     - Container landed on a corner = uneven force distribution.
     - Fall was too fast = parachute too small or missing.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why designs survive:** Effective cushioning increases impact time; air resistance reduces impact speed; rigid containers distribute force.
   - **Why designs fail:** Common failure modes:
     - **Direct contact:** Egg hits the container wall — cushioning was too thin or displaced during fall.
     - **Excessive speed:** No parachute or too small — impact force is too high even with cushioning.
     - **Uneven landing:** Container hits corner first — force concentrates at one point.
   - **Iteration:** The design process is cyclical. Build → test → analyse → improve → rebuild.
   - **Physics connection:** F = mΔv/Δt. Survivors maximised Δt (cushioning) and minimised Δv (parachute).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups vote on the best design and explain why. Homework: sketch an improved design based on today's test.

## Hands-On Build: Egg Drop Test

### Parts
- Completed egg containers — 1 per group — from Lesson 086
- Raised platform (2 m) — 1 — school
- Stopwatch — 1 — phone or classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Groups line up in order.
2. One learner climbs to the platform and holds the container.
3. On "go," the learner releases the container (no pushing).
4. A timer on the ground records the fall time.
5. After landing, the group retrieves the container and opens it.
6. Record: survive or break, fall time, and observations.
7. All groups test. Winners are celebrated; failures are analysed.
8. **Checkpoint:** Before opening, predict the outcome. Then check. If surprised, discuss why the prediction was wrong.

### Common Mistakes
- **Pushing the container:** Adds horizontal speed and may cause tumbling. Fix: release gently.
- **Opening before recording:** Forgetting to note fall time or landing orientation. Fix: assign a recorder before the test.
- **Blaming the egg:** Assuming the egg was weak. Fix: analyse the design objectively.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What should you observe during an egg-drop test?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* A design broke because the egg touched the container wall. What improvement is needed?
3. Explain why a parachute reduces the force on the egg.
4. Name two failure modes and their fixes.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Whether the egg survives, fall time, landing orientation, and container damage.
2. **More cushioning** on all sides of the egg.
3. It reduces impact speed, which reduces momentum change and impact force.
4. Examples: direct contact → add cushioning; excessive speed → add parachute; uneven landing → add a flat base.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 0.05 kg egg in a container falls from 2 m and hits the ground at 5 m/s. The container's cushioning stops the egg in 0.15 s. Calculate the average force on the egg. Would it survive if the shell cracks at 2 N?

**Worked Answer:**
F = mΔv/Δt = 0.05 kg × 5 m/s / 0.15 s = 0.25 / 0.15 = **1.67 N**.

Since 1.67 N < 2 N, the egg **survives**.

If the cushioning were thinner and the stop time only 0.05 s:
F = 0.05 × 5 / 0.05 = **5 N**.

This exceeds the shell's limit, so the egg would **break**.

The difference between survival and failure is a factor of 3 in cushioning thickness — showing how sensitive egg drops are to design details.

**Q:** Analyse the class results statistically. How would you determine whether cushioning mass or parachute area has a stronger effect on survival rate? Describe the data you would collect and how you would present it.

**Worked Answer:**
**Data to collect:**
- For each group: cushioning mass (g), parachute area (cm²), fall time (s), survive/break.

**Analysis:**
1. Calculate survival rate for groups with parachutes vs without.
2. Calculate survival rate for groups with above-average vs below-average cushioning mass.
3. Compare the two rates. The feature with the larger difference in survival rate has the stronger effect.

**Presentation:**
- Table: cushioning mass vs survival (yes/no).
- Table: parachute area vs survival.
- Bar chart: survival rate for each category.
- Scatter plot: fall time vs cushioning mass (surviving points in green, broken in red).

**Limitation:** Small sample size (one class). A larger study across multiple classes would be needed for statistical significance. Also, designs vary in many ways at once, making it hard to isolate one variable.

## Safety Notes
- Only one learner on the platform at a time.
- Platform must be sturdy and safe.
- Ensure the landing zone is clear before each drop.
- Handle broken eggs carefully; clean up spills promptly.
