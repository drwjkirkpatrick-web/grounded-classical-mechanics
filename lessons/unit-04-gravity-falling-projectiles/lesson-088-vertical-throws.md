# Lesson 088: Throwing Straight Up

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe the motion of an object thrown vertically upward.
2. Calculate the time to reach maximum height and the maximum height itself.
3. Explain why the upward and downward motions are symmetric.

## Materials
- A ball or stone
- A stopwatch or phone timer
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Vertical throw** *(kutupa wima)* — throwing an object straight up or down.
- **Maximum height** *(urefu wa juu kabisa)* — the highest point reached by a thrown object.
- **Symmetric motion** *(mwenendo sawa)* — motion where the upward and downward paths are mirror images.
- **Deceleration** *(kupungua kasi)* — slowing down due to gravity during upward motion.

## Pre-Test
1. You throw a ball straight up. What happens to its speed as it rises?
2. At the very top of its flight, what is the ball's velocity?
3. A ball is thrown up at 15 m/s. How long until it reaches the top?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It decreases — gravity is slowing it down.
2. Zero — momentarily at rest before falling back down.
3. t = u/g = 15/10 = **1.5 s**.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Throw a ball up and catch it. Ask, "When was the ball moving fastest? When was it slowest? When was it not moving at all?" Introduce the idea of symmetric motion.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, one learner throws a ball straight up. Another times the flight. Others observe.
   - Measure: time to reach top (estimate by watching), total time up and down.
   - **Key discovery:** Time up ≈ time down. Speed at any height is the same going up and coming down (just opposite direction).
   - Groups sketch a velocity–time graph: positive velocity decreasing to zero at top, then negative velocity increasing downward.
   - **Concept:** Gravity acts downward throughout. Going up, gravity opposes motion (deceleration). Coming down, gravity aids motion (acceleration).

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formulas for vertical throws:**
     - Time to top: t = u/g (where u = initial upward velocity).
     - Maximum height: h = u²/2g.
     - Time of flight: T = 2u/g.
   - **Symmetry:** The path up is the reverse of the path down. Speed at any height is the same; only direction changes.
   - **Example:** Throw a ball at 20 m/s.
     - Time to top: 20/10 = 2 s.
     - Max height: (20)²/(2×10) = 400/20 = **20 m**.
     - Total flight: 4 s.
   - **Kenyan context:** Throwing a fruit up to knock another from a tree; tossing a water bottle to a friend on a roof.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why a ball thrown up at 10 m/s takes 1 s to reach the top. Homework: throw a ball up and time the flight; calculate initial speed.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A ball is thrown upward. What happens to its speed as it rises?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* At the highest point of a vertical throw, what is the velocity?
3. A ball is thrown upward at 12 m/s. Calculate (a) the time to reach the top, and (b) the maximum height.
4. Explain why the time up equals the time down for a vertical throw.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The speed **decreases** — gravity decelerates the ball.
2. **Zero** — momentarily at rest.
3. (a) t = 12/10 = **1.2 s**. (b) h = (12)²/(2×10) = 144/20 = **7.2 m**.
4. Gravity provides the same acceleration (10 m/s² downward) throughout. The deceleration going up is the mirror of the acceleration coming down. At every height, the speed is the same; only direction changes. Therefore, the time up equals the time down.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A ball is thrown upward at 20 m/s. Calculate (a) the time to reach maximum height, (b) the maximum height, (c) the velocity 1.5 s after throw, and (d) the total time of flight.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Time to top: t = u/g = 20/10 = **2.0 s**.

(b) Max height: h = u²/2g = (20)²/(2×10) = 400/20 = **20 m**.

(c) At t = 1.5 s (going up):
v = u − gt = 20 − 10×1.5 = 20 − 15 = **5 m/s upward**.

(d) Total time: T = 2u/g = 2×20/10 = **4.0 s**.

**Check:** At t = 3 s (1 s after top):
v = 0 − 10×1 = **−10 m/s** (10 m/s downward).
At t = 4 s: v = −20 m/s — same speed as launch, opposite direction.

**Q:** Explain why a ball thrown upward on the Moon reaches a higher maximum height than the same ball thrown upward on Earth with the same initial speed. Calculate the ratio of maximum heights if g_moon = 1.6 m/s².

**Worked Answer:**
**Why higher on the Moon:**
Maximum height h = u²/2g. For the same initial speed u, h is inversely proportional to g.

On the Moon, g is smaller (1.6 m/s² vs 10 m/s² on Earth). Therefore, the deceleration is weaker, and the ball travels farther before stopping.

**Ratio calculation:**
h_moon / h_earth = (u²/2g_moon) / (u²/2g_earth) = g_earth / g_moon = 10 / 1.6 = **6.25**.

The ball reaches **6.25 times higher** on the Moon.

For u = 20 m/s:
- Earth: h = 400/20 = 20 m.
- Moon: h = 400/3.2 = **125 m**.

This is why astronauts can jump so high on the Moon — weaker gravity means less deceleration during upward motion.

## Safety Notes
- Throw in an open area away from other learners.
- Use a soft ball, not a hard stone.
- Ensure the landing area is clear.
- Do not throw near windows or fragile objects.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lessons 081 (free fall, g), 072 (deceleration).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on the symmetry concept and simple calculations. Ages 14–17 should solve multi-part problems and compare Earth/Moon scenarios.
- **Local sourcing:** Any ball and stopwatch are sufficient.
