# Lesson 091: Projectile Motion II: Angle and Range

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Predict that a 45° launch angle gives the maximum range for a projectile.
2. Test different launch angles and compare ranges.
3. Explain why steeper or shallower angles produce shorter ranges.

## Materials
- A small ball or stone
- A ruler or protractor for angle measurement
- A launcher (ruler as a ramp, or a rubber band)
- A tape measure
- Chalk for marking landing spots
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Launch angle** *(pembe ya kurusha)* — the angle between the launch direction and the horizontal.
- **Range** *(umbali wa kurusha)* — the horizontal distance a projectile travels before landing.
- **Trajectory** *(njia ya mwenendo)* — the curved path of a projectile.
- **Optimal angle** *(pembe bora)* — the angle that produces the maximum range (45° in ideal conditions).

## Pre-Test
1. If you throw a ball straight up (90°), how far does it travel horizontally?
2. If you throw a ball straight forward (0°), how high does it go?
3. What angle do you think gives the farthest throw?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Zero — it goes straight up and down.
2. Zero (if launched from ground level) — it never rises above the launch point.
3. 45° (teaser for the lesson).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who can throw a ball the farthest?" Have a volunteer throw at different angles. Ask the class to guess which angle went farthest.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners launch a ball at different angles using a ruler as a ramp.
   - Angles to test: 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°.
   - Mark the landing spot with chalk for each angle.
   - Measure the horizontal distance (range) for each.
   - **Key discovery:** 45° typically gives the longest range. Steeper angles (60°, 75°) go higher but not as far. Shallower angles (15°, 30°) don't go high enough to take advantage of hang time.
   - Groups plot angle vs range on a simple graph.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why 45° is optimal:** At 45°, the initial velocity is split equally between horizontal and vertical components. This gives the best balance of forward speed and hang time.
   - **Components:** v_horizontal = v cos θ, v_vertical = v sin θ.
   - At 45°: cos 45° = sin 45° ≈ 0.707, so both components are equal.
   - At 30°: more horizontal speed, less hang time → shorter range.
   - At 60°: more hang time, less horizontal speed → shorter range.
   - **Formula preview:** Range = v² sin(2θ) / g. Maximum when sin(2θ) = 1 → 2θ = 90° → θ = 45°.
   - Kenyan context: Spear throwers, cricket bowlers, and football kickers all intuitively use angles near 45° for maximum distance.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states the optimal angle and why. Homework: test throwing a ball at 45° vs your usual angle — which goes farther?

## Hands-On Build: Angle Launcher

### Parts
- Small ball or stone — 1 — ground or classroom
- Ruler or protractor — 1 — classroom
- Rubber band or ruler ramp — 1 — classroom
- Tape measure — 1 — classroom
- Chalk — 1 piece — classroom

### Workflow
1. Mark a launch point on the ground with chalk.
2. Use the ruler as a ramp. Prop one end on a book to create a 15° angle.
3. Place the ball at the top and release it.
4. Mark where it lands with chalk.
5. Repeat with the ramp at 30°, 45°, 60°, and 75° (adjust book height).
6. Measure the horizontal distance for each angle.
7. Record in a table and plot angle vs range.
8. **Checkpoint:** Before measuring, ensure the launch height is the same for all angles. If the ramp height changes, the comparison is unfair.

### Common Mistakes
- **Pushing the ball:** Adds extra force. Fix: release from rest.
- **Different launch heights:** Changes the fall time. Fix: keep the ramp base at the same height.
- **Measuring to the wrong point:** Measure horizontal distance, not the slanted path. Fix: measure straight across from launch to landing.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* What launch angle gives the maximum range (in ideal conditions)?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A ball is thrown straight up. What is its horizontal range?
3. Why does a 30° launch produce less range than a 45° launch?
4. A ball is launched at 45° and travels 20 m. Predict what happens if the angle is changed to 60° (same speed).

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **45°**.
2. **Zero** — no horizontal motion.
3. At 30°, there is more horizontal speed but **less hang time**, so the ball lands before it can travel far.
4. The range will be **less than 20 m** — 60° gives more height but less horizontal distance.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Using the range formula R = v² sin(2θ) / g, show that 45° gives the maximum range. Calculate the range for v = 15 m/s at θ = 30°, 45°, and 60°.

**Worked Answer:**
R = v² sin(2θ) / g.

The maximum value of sin(2θ) is 1, which occurs when 2θ = 90° → θ = **45°**.

**Calculations (v = 15 m/s, g = 10 m/s²):**
- θ = 30°: R = (15)² × sin(60°) / 10 = 225 × 0.866 / 10 = **19.5 m**.
- θ = 45°: R = 225 × sin(90°) / 10 = 225 × 1 / 10 = **22.5 m**.
- θ = 60°: R = 225 × sin(120°) / 10 = 225 × 0.866 / 10 = **19.5 m**.

Notice that 30° and 60° give the **same range** — this is because sin(60°) = sin(120°). Complementary angles (adding to 90°) give the same range.

**Q:** A football is kicked at 20 m/s. Calculate the maximum range and the two angles that produce half the maximum range.

**Worked Answer:**
**Maximum range:**
R_max = v² / g = (20)² / 10 = 400 / 10 = **40 m** (at 45°).

**Half maximum range = 20 m:**
20 = v² sin(2θ) / g = 400 sin(2θ) / 10 = 40 sin(2θ).
sin(2θ) = 20/40 = 0.5.
2θ = 30° or 150°.
θ = **15°** or **75°**.

Both 15° and 75° produce 20 m range. The 15° kick stays low and fast; the 75° kick goes high and drops steeply. A goalkeeper faces this choice when clearing the ball.

## Safety Notes
- Launch in an open area away from other learners.
- Use soft balls, not hard stones.
- Ensure the landing area is clear of obstacles.
- Do not aim at people or fragile objects.

## Teacher Notes
- **Prior knowledge:** Lesson 090 (projectile motion, independence of horizontal and vertical motion).
- **Differentiation:** Ages 10–13 should focus on observing that 45° gives the longest range. Ages 14–17 should derive the formula and calculate ranges.
- **Local sourcing:** Any ball, ruler, and protractor are sufficient. A rubber band can substitute for a ramp.
- **CBE assessment note:** Aligns with JS-FE "investigating launch angle and range" and "applying trigonometry to projectile motion."
