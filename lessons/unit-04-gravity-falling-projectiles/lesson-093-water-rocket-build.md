# Lesson 093: The Water Rocket Project II: Build Day

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** water-rocket

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Assemble a water rocket following their design from Lesson 092.
2. Test-fit and seal all components securely.
3. Prepare the rocket for launch by filling with the optimal water amount.

## Materials
- All materials from Lesson 092
- Duct tape or strong adhesive tape
- Scissors
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Assembly** *(kujenga)* — putting together the parts of a device.
- **Seal** *(kufunga)* — making a joint airtight so pressure does not escape.
- **Test-fit** *(kujaribu kufunga)* — checking that parts fit before final assembly.
- **Pre-launch check** *(ukaguzi kabla ya kurusha)* — a final inspection before launch.

## Pre-Test
1. What water amount should you use for the rocket?
2. Why must the cork fit tightly?
3. What should you check before launching?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. About 1/3 of the bottle volume.
2. To prevent air from leaking before launch.
3. Fins secure, cork tight, nose cone firm, no leaks.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Review designs from Lesson 092. Groups share one key design decision.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Groups assemble their rockets.
   - Attach fins securely with tape or glue. Space evenly.
   - Attach nose cone. Ensure it is centred.
   - Test-fit the cork. Wrap with tape if too loose.
   - Fill with water to the 1/3 mark.
   - **Key discovery:** A rocket that looks good on paper may have assembly challenges. Groups problem-solve as they build.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Assembly tips:**
     - Fins must be straight — crooked fins cause spin or drift.
     - Nose cone should be symmetrical — off-centre cones cause tumbling.
     - Cork must seal but release under pressure.
   - **Launch prep:**
     - Check launch angle (90° for vertical, 45° for maximum distance).
     - Clear the area.
     - Assign roles: launcher, timer, observer, safety monitor.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups hold up completed rockets. Homework: write a launch prediction (how high will it go?).

## Hands-On Build: Water Rocket Assembly

### Parts
- All materials from Lesson 092
- Duct tape — 1 roll — classroom
- Scissors — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Lay out all materials.
2. Attach fins: tape or glue each fin to the bottle base. Space evenly (120° for 3 fins).
3. Attach nose cone: tape securely to the bottle top.
4. Test-fit the cork. If loose, wrap with tape until snug.
5. Fill bottle with water to the 1/3 mark.
6. Insert cork firmly.
7. **Checkpoint:** Shake the rocket gently. Fins and nose cone should not move. Cork should stay in place under gentle pressure.

### Common Mistakes
- **Fins crooked:** Rocket spins. Fix: use a ruler to align fins straight.
- **Cork too tight:** Won't launch. Fix: adjust tape thickness.
- **Cork too loose:** Leaks air. Fix: add more tape.
- **Nose cone off-centre:** Rocket tumbles. Fix: re-centre and re-tape.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is the optimal water amount?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why must the cork fit tightly?
3. List three things to check before launch.
4. Why should fins be evenly spaced?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **1/3** of the bottle volume.
2. To **prevent air leaks** before launch.
3. Fins secure, cork tight, nose cone firm, no leaks, clear area.
4. Even spacing provides **balanced stability** and prevents spinning.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain why crooked fins cause a rocket to spin or curve, using the idea of unbalanced forces.

**Worked Answer:**
If fins are crooked, the airflow over them is not symmetric. One fin pushes air more to one side than the opposite fin pushes to the other. This creates an **unbalanced sideways force** (torque) on the rocket.

The torque causes the rocket to **rotate** around its long axis (spin) or **yaw** to one side (curve). Like a car with unequal tyre pressure, the rocket veers toward the side with less resistance.

**Fix:** Align fins perfectly straight and evenly spaced. This ensures symmetric airflow and balanced forces.

**Q:** A rocket with 4 fins is more stable than one with 3 fins. Explain why, and discuss whether adding more fins always improves stability.

**Worked Answer:**
**4 fins vs 3 fins:**
- 4 fins provide symmetry in both horizontal and vertical planes. At any orientation, there is a fin to correct drift.
- 3 fins provide symmetry at 120° intervals. They work well but are slightly less stable against sideways gusts.

**More fins:**
- More fins = more stability correction.
- But more fins also = more weight and more air resistance (drag). A rocket with 8 fins would be very stable but very heavy and slow.

**Optimum:** 3–4 fins are best for bottle rockets. More than 4 provides diminishing returns and increases drag.

## Safety Notes
- Use scissors carefully.
- Wear eye protection when handling pressurised bottles.
- Do not over-pump.
- Launch only in open areas.
