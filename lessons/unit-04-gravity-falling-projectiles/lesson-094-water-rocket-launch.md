# Lesson 094: The Water Rocket Project III: Launch and Measure

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** water-rocket

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Launch water rockets safely and measure their flight performance.
2. Compare results for different water fill levels and launch angles.
3. Estimate launch speed from flight time and angle data.

## Materials
- Completed water rockets from Lessons 092–093
- Bicycle pump with needle adapter
- Water
- Tape measure or marked distance
- Stopwatch or phone timer
- Open field or large open area
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Launch** *(kurusha)* — the moment the rocket leaves the ground.
- **Flight time** *(muda wa kuruka)* — the total time from launch to landing.
- **Range** *(umbali)* — the horizontal distance travelled.
- **Champion** *(mshindi)* — the rocket with the best performance.

## Pre-Test
1. What should you check before launching a water rocket?
2. How can you measure the rocket's flight time?
3. What two factors will you vary and compare?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Fins secure, cork tight, clear area, safe distance.
2. With a stopwatch from launch to landing.
3. Water fill level and launch angle.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review safety rules.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Groups set up in the launch area. Review launch procedure.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Groups launch rockets with different parameters.
   - **Test 1:** Vary water fill (1/4, 1/3, 1/2). Keep angle at 45°. Measure range and flight time.
   - **Test 2:** Vary angle (30°, 45°, 60°). Keep fill at 1/3. Measure range.
   - Record all results in a table.
   - **Key discovery:** 1/3 fill typically gives best height/range. 45° angle typically gives best range.
   - Groups compare and identify the class champion.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why 1/3 is optimal:** Balances thrust and mass (Lesson 092).
   - **Why 45° is optimal:** Best balance of horizontal speed and hang time (Lesson 091).
   - **Estimating launch speed:** From flight time T, max height h = g(T/2)²/2. Then v = √(2gh).
   - **Energy recap:** Pump energy → pressure → kinetic → potential → kinetic on descent.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Champion rocket team explains their design. Homework: write a launch report with data table.

## Hands-On Build: Water Rocket Launch

### Parts
- Completed rockets — 1 per group — from Lesson 093
- Pump with needle — 1 — school
- Water — tap
- Tape measure — 1 — school
- Stopwatch — 1 — phone

### Workflow
1. Fill rocket to chosen level (1/4, 1/3, or 1/2).
2. Insert cork firmly.
3. Attach pump needle through cork.
4. Pump to firm resistance (~5–10 pumps).
5. Clear area. Count down: "3, 2, 1, launch!"
6. Pull needle quickly. Rocket launches.
7. Timer records flight time. Observer marks landing.
8. Measure range with tape measure.
9. Repeat for each test condition.
10. **Checkpoint:** Before each launch, verify: clear area, pump firm, all observers ready.

### Common Mistakes
- **Needle not through cork:** Air escapes, no launch. Fix: ensure needle pierces cork.
- **Over-pumping:** Bottle may burst. Fix: stop at firm resistance.
- **Cork stuck:** Rocket won't launch. Fix: adjust cork fit during build.
- **Poor angle:** Rocket curves or lands close. Fix: use a simple launch guide (ruler or stick).

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How do you measure flight time?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* Which two factors did you vary?
3. Why does 1/3 water usually give the best results?
4. A rocket flies for 4 seconds. Estimate its maximum height.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Stopwatch** from launch to landing.
2. **Water fill** and **launch angle**.
3. It balances **thrust** (enough water) and **mass** (not too heavy).
4. Time to top = 2 s. h = ½gt² = ½ × 10 × 4 = **20 m**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** From a flight time of 3.5 s, estimate the launch speed and maximum height. Show your working.

**Worked Answer:**
Time to top = 3.5 / 2 = **1.75 s**.

Launch speed: v = gt = 10 × 1.75 = **17.5 m/s**.

Max height: h = ½gt² = ½ × 10 × (1.75)² = 5 × 3.0625 = **15.3 m**.

Or using energy: h = v²/2g = (17.5)²/20 = 306.25/20 = **15.3 m**. Same result.

**Q:** A rocket with 1/2 water flies lower than one with 1/3 water, even though it has more thrust. Explain using F=ma and the concept of impulse.

**Worked Answer:**
**Mass issue:** 1/2 water means more mass to accelerate. From F=ma, for the same thrust F, heavier rocket → smaller acceleration a.

**Impulse issue:** Impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv). More water means longer thrust time, but the thrust pressure drops faster because the air expands into a larger water volume. The total impulse may not be much greater.

**Result:** The 1/2 water rocket is heavier and does not accelerate enough to overcome its mass. The 1/3 rocket is lighter, accelerates faster, and reaches higher speed before thrust ends. Higher launch speed → higher flight.

## Safety Notes
- Launch outdoors only.
- Clear area of 20 m radius.
- Wear eye protection.
- Do not over-pump.
- Face away from rocket during pumping.
- Assign a safety monitor.
