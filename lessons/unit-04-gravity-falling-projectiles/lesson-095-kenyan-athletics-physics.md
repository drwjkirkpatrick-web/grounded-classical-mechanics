# Lesson 095: Physics of Kenyan Athletics

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Analyse the physics of javelin throwing and long-distance running.
2. Calculate speed and pacing from race data.
3. Connect projectile motion to real Kenyan athletic achievements.

## Materials
- Data cards with race splits (prepared by teacher)
- A stick or javelin for demonstration
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Pacing** *(kasi ya kukimbia)* — maintaining a steady speed over a distance.
- **Split** *(sehemu)* — the time for a segment of a race.
- **Javelin** *(mkuki)* — a spear-like projectile thrown for distance.
- **Endurance** *(uvumilivu)* — the ability to maintain effort over time.

## Pre-Test
1. What angle gives the maximum range for a javelin throw?
2. If a runner covers 10 km in 30 minutes, what is their pace per kilometre?
3. Why do Kenyan runners dominate long-distance races?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. About 35°–45° (optimal for human throwers, slightly less than 45° due to aerodynamics).
2. 30 min / 10 km = **3 min/km**.
3. Altitude training, body composition, cultural emphasis on running, and dedication.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who knows Julius Yego? Eliud Kipchoge?" Briefly introduce these Kenyan athletic legends.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners receive data cards.
   - **Card 1: Javelin (Julius Yego).** A javelin thrown at 30 m/s at 35°. Calculate range using R = v² sin(2θ) / g.
   - **Card 2: Marathon (Eliud Kipchoge).** 42.195 km in 2:01:09. Calculate average speed and per-km pace.
   - **Card 3: 1500 m (Faith Kipyegon).** Splits for each 400 m. Calculate if pace was steady or varied.
   - Groups analyse their card and present findings.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Javelin physics:** Optimal angle is slightly less than 45° because the javelin generates lift (like a wing). Human throwers also release at an upward angle naturally.
   - **Marathon physics:** Steady speed = energy efficiency. Kipchoge's even splits show mastery of pacing.
   - **Altitude advantage:** Training at high altitude (e.g., Iten, Kenya) increases red blood cells, improving oxygen delivery at sea level.
   - Kenyan context: Running is woven into Kenyan culture — children run to school, herders run with cattle, and communities celebrate athletic achievements.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states one physics principle applied to Kenyan athletics. Homework: time yourself running 400 m and compare your pace to Kipchoge's.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What angle is optimal for a javelin throw?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A runner covers 5 km in 15 minutes. What is the pace per kilometre?
3. Calculate the range of a javelin thrown at 25 m/s at 35°.
4. Why is steady pacing important in a marathon?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. About **35°–45°** (slightly less than 45° for aerodynamic reasons).
2. 15 min / 5 km = **3 min/km**.
3. R = (25)² × sin(70°) / 10 = 625 × 0.94 / 10 ≈ **58.8 m**.
4. Steady pacing prevents early fatigue and lactic acid buildup, allowing the runner to maintain speed throughout.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Eliud Kipchoge ran 42.195 km in 2:01:09. Calculate his average speed in m/s and his pace per kilometre. Compare with a typical secondary school student's 100 m sprint speed.

**Worked Answer:**
Time = 2 h 1 min 9 s = 7200 + 60 + 9 = **7269 s**.
Distance = 42,195 m.

Average speed = 42,195 / 7269 = **5.80 m/s** (or 20.9 km/h).

Pace per km = 7269 s / 42.195 km = **172.3 s/km** = **2 min 52 s per km**.

Typical student 100 m sprint: ~15 s → speed = 100/15 = **6.67 m/s**.

**Comparison:** Kipchoge's marathon speed (5.80 m/s) is only slightly slower than a typical student's sprint speed (6.67 m/s) — but Kipchoge maintains this for 2 hours! This is why marathon running is considered one of the most impressive human endurance feats.

**Q:** Julius Yego's javelin throw can be modelled as projectile motion. Explain why the optimal angle is slightly less than 45°, and calculate the range for a throw at 30 m/s and 34° (Yego's typical angle).

**Worked Answer:**
**Why less than 45°:**
- The javelin is aerodynamic — it generates lift as it flies. This lift extends the flight time beyond the vacuum prediction.
- However, too steep an angle causes the javelin to stall (lose lift) or land tail-first (reducing measured distance).
- Human throwers naturally release at a slightly lower angle because the arm motion is more comfortable and powerful at 30°–35° than at 45°.

**Calculation:**
R = v² sin(2θ) / g = (30)² × sin(68°) / 10 = 900 × 0.927 / 10 = **83.4 m**.

Yego's actual throws exceed 90 m because the javelin's aerodynamics provide extra lift and glide. The simple projectile model underestimates the true range.

## Safety Notes
- No throwing in the classroom; use hypothetical calculations.
- If demonstrating with a stick, use a soft object and clear area.
