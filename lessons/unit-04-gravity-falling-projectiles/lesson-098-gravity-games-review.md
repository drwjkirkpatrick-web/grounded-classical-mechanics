# Lesson 098: Unit Review: Gravity Games

**Unit:** 4 — Gravity, Falling Bodies & Projectiles | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Review and consolidate understanding of gravity, free fall, and projectile motion.
2. Apply concepts to solve problems involving falling objects and projectiles.
3. Self-assess strengths and areas for improvement in Unit 4.

## Materials
- Unit 4 review questions (prepared by teacher)
- Ball for demonstration
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Review** *(kujirudia)* — going over material to reinforce learning.
- **Consolidate** *(kujenga nguvu)* — bringing ideas together into a coherent whole.
- **Self-assessment** *(kujipima mwenyewe)* — judging your own understanding.
- **Gravity games** *(michezo ya mvutano)* — fun activities that apply gravity concepts.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the acceleration due to gravity near Earth's surface?
2. What angle gives the maximum range for a projectile?
3. Why do astronauts feel weightless in orbit?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Approximately **10 m/s²**.
2. **45°** (in ideal conditions).
3. They are in continuous free fall — they and their spacecraft are falling together.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Announce "Gravity Games" — a series of quick challenges reviewing Unit 4 concepts.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Challenge 1: Drop Race.** Drop two objects of different mass but same shape. Predict and observe.
   - **Challenge 2: Angle Guess.** One learner throws a ball. The class guesses the angle. Measure with a protractor.
   - **Challenge 3: Height Hunt.** Given a fall time, calculate the height. Given a height, calculate the fall time.
   - **Challenge 4: Orbit Draw.** Sketch an orbit and explain why it is "falling around" Earth.
   - Groups rotate through challenges, scoring points for correct answers.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - Review key formulas:
     - v = gt (speed in free fall)
     - d = ½gt² (distance fallen)
     - R = v² sin(2θ) / g (projectile range)
     - h = u²/2g (maximum height)
   - Review key concepts:
     - All objects fall at the same rate in vacuum.
     - Air resistance affects light, large-surface objects.
     - Horizontal and vertical motion are independent.
     - Orbits are continuous free fall.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (unit quiz, see below). Learners work individually.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups tally scores. Highest-scoring group is crowned "Gravity Champion." Homework: complete the self-assessment.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is g near Earth's surface?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What launch angle gives maximum range?
3. A stone is dropped from a height of 45 m. How long until it hits the ground?
4. A ball is thrown upward at 15 m/s. What is its maximum height?
5. Explain why a feather and a coin fall at the same rate on the Moon.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **10 m/s²**.
2. **45°**.
3. t = √(2d/g) = √(90/10) = √9 = **3 s**.
4. h = u²/2g = 225/20 = **11.25 m**.
5. The Moon has **no atmosphere**, so there is no air resistance. Both objects accelerate at the same rate (g_moon ≈ 1.6 m/s²).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A projectile is launched at 20 m/s at 45°. Calculate (a) the maximum height, (b) the time of flight, and (c) the range.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Max height:
Vertical component: u_y = 20 × sin(45°) = 20 × 0.707 = **14.14 m/s**.
h = u_y²/2g = (14.14)²/20 = 200/20 = **10 m**.

(b) Time of flight:
Time to top: t = u_y/g = 14.14/10 = **1.414 s**.
Total time: T = 2 × 1.414 = **2.83 s**.

(c) Range:
Horizontal component: u_x = 20 × cos(45°) = **14.14 m/s**.
R = u_x × T = 14.14 × 2.83 = **40 m**.

Or using formula: R = v² sin(2θ)/g = 400 × sin(90°)/10 = 400/10 = **40 m**.

**Q:** Write a higher-order question about gravity that tests understanding rather than recall. Provide a marking scheme.

**Worked Answer:**
**Question:** A satellite orbits Earth at 400 km altitude. Explain why the satellite does not crash into Earth, and calculate its orbital speed using the given data (Earth radius = 6400 km, g_surface = 10 m/s²).

**Marking scheme:**
- States that the satellite is in continuous free fall (1 mark).
- Explains that sideways motion prevents crashing (1 mark).
- Calculates orbital radius: r = 6400 + 400 = 6800 km (1 mark).
- Calculates g at altitude: g' = 10 × (6400/6800)² ≈ 8.85 m/s² (2 marks).
- Calculates orbital speed: v = √(g'r) = √(8.85 × 6.8 × 10⁶) ≈ 7.76 km/s (3 marks).
- Correct units and significant figures (1 mark).
- **Total: 9 marks**
