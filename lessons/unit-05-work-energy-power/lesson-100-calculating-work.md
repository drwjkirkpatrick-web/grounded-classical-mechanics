# Lesson 100: Calculating Work: Fetching Water and Lifting Loads

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Calculate work done in lifting objects against gravity.
2. Distinguish between horizontal carrying and vertical lifting in terms of work.
3. Apply the work formula to everyday Kenyan tasks.

## Materials
- A jerrican or debe with water
- A spring balance
- A tape measure
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Joule** *(joule)* — the unit of work; 1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre.
- **Lifting** *(kuinua)* — moving an object upward against gravity.
- **Horizontal carrying** *(kubeba kwa mlalo)* — moving an object sideways while keeping its height constant.
- **Against gravity** *(kinyume cha mvutano wa ardhi)* — in the opposite direction to gravity's pull.

## Pre-Test
1. A 200 N jerrican is lifted 1.5 m onto a stand. How much work is done?
2. The same jerrican is carried 20 m across level ground. How much work is done against gravity?
3. Why does horizontal carrying require effort but do no physics work against gravity?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. W = 200 × 1.5 = **300 J**.
2. **0 J** — movement is perpendicular to gravity.
3. Muscles must contract to hold the object up, but there is no displacement in the force's direction.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who fetches water? Is it harder to carry it far or lift it high?" Introduce the idea that physics distinguishes these.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure and calculate work in two scenarios.
   - **Scenario 1: Lifting.** A learner lifts a jerrican from the ground to a stand (1.5 m). Another measures the force with a spring balance. Calculate W = F × d.
   - **Scenario 2: Carrying.** The same jerrican is carried 20 m across level ground. Calculate work against gravity. (Answer: 0 J.)
   - **Scenario 3: Against friction.** Carry the jerrican across rough ground. It takes effort. Is this physics work? (Answer: Yes, but against friction, not gravity.)
   - **Key discovery:** Lifting does work against gravity. Horizontal carrying does no work against gravity (but may do work against friction).

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Work against gravity:** W = mgh or W = F × h. Lifting increases gravitational potential energy.
   - **Work against friction:** W = F_friction × d. This is harder to calculate because friction force varies.
   - **Why carrying tires us:** Muscles hold the load up (no physics work), and overcome small friction/imbalances.
   - **Combined task:** Fetching water involves lifting (work) + carrying (no work against gravity) + lifting again (work).
   - Kenyan context: A woman carrying water on her head walks kilometres — the horizontal walk does no physics work against gravity, but the initial lift and final set-down do.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states when work is done in fetching water. Homework: calculate the work you do lifting your school bag onto your desk.

## Hands-On Build: Work Measurement

### Parts
- Jerrican with water — 1 — tap
- Spring balance — 1 — classroom
- Tape measure — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Measure the weight of the jerrican with the spring balance (record in N).
2. Lift the jerrican 1.5 m onto a stand. Calculate work: W = F × 1.5.
3. Carry the jerrican 20 m across level ground.
4. Calculate work against gravity for the carry: W = F × 0 = 0 J.
5. If available, carry across rough ground and estimate friction force.
6. **Checkpoint:** Before calculating, verify the spring balance reading is steady. If it fluctuates, average three readings.

### Common Mistakes
- **Confusing weight and mass:** Weight is in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg). Fix: read the spring balance directly in N.
- **Using horizontal distance in work-against-gravity:** Work against gravity only uses vertical distance. Fix: measure height change, not path length.
- **Forgetting units:** Work is in joules (J). Fix: always include units.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A 250 N jerrican is lifted 2 m. Calculate the work done.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* The same jerrican is carried 30 m horizontally. How much work is done against gravity?
3. A farmer lifts a 150 N sack 1 m onto a cart, then pushes the cart 10 m. Calculate total work against gravity.
4. Why is carrying a load horizontally tiring even though no physics work against gravity is done?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. W = 250 × 2 = **500 J**.
2. **0 J** — horizontal movement.
3. Only the lift counts against gravity: W = 150 × 1 = **150 J**.
4. Muscles continuously contract to hold the load; internal work is done by muscles, converted to heat.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 200 N jerrican is carried 20 m on level ground and then lifted 1.5 m onto a stand. Calculate the total physics work done, and explain why the level carry contributes zero work against gravity but still requires energy from the body.

**Worked Answer:**
**Total physics work:**
- Level carry: W = 200 N × 0 m = **0 J** (perpendicular to gravity).
- Lift: W = 200 N × 1.5 m = **300 J**.
- Total = **300 J**.

**Why the body still expends energy:**
The body must continuously generate upward force to hold the jerrican against gravity. Muscle fibres contract and relax microscopically, consuming ATP (chemical energy) and producing heat. This internal work is not physics work on the jerrican (which doesn't move up or down), but it is real biological work.

Efficiency: The human body is about 20–25 percent efficient at converting chemical energy to mechanical work. Most energy becomes heat. For 300 J of physics work, the body may consume 1200–1500 J of food energy.

**Q:** A porter carries a 300 N load up a 10 m hill that rises 2 m vertically. Calculate the work done against gravity. If the porter also does 400 J of work against friction, what is the total physics work?

**Worked Answer:**
**Work against gravity:**
W_gravity = F × h = 300 N × 2 m = **600 J**.

(Only vertical height matters, not path length. The 10 m path length is irrelevant for gravity work.)

**Total physics work:**
W_total = W_gravity + W_friction = 600 + 400 = **1000 J**.

**Note:** The 400 J against friction depends on the 10 m path length. Friction work = F_friction × 10 m = 400 J → F_friction = 40 N.

This shows how different forces contribute separately to total work, and why the path matters for friction but not for gravity.

## Safety Notes
- Ensure jerrican is not too heavy (max 20 kg for learners).
- Use proper lifting technique (bend knees, keep back straight).
- Clear walking areas of obstacles.
