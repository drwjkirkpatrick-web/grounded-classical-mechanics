# Lesson 101: When There Is No Work Done

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6 | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify situations where physics work is zero.
2. Explain why force without displacement does no work.
3. Distinguish between effort and physics work.

## Materials
- A wall
- A heavy book
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Zero work** *(kazi sifuri)* — a situation where force is applied but no displacement occurs in the force's direction.
- **Displacement** *(mwelekeo wa mwendo)* — a change in position; movement from one point to another.
- **Perpendicular** *(mlalo)* — at a 90° angle.
- **Effort** *(juhudi)* — physical exertion; may or may not result in physics work.

## Pre-Test
1. You push a wall with all your strength. How much work do you do on the wall?
2. A satellite orbits Earth under gravity. Does gravity do work on the satellite?
3. Why is holding a book above your head tiring if you do no physics work?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Zero — the wall does not move.
2. No — gravity is perpendicular to displacement in circular orbit.
3. Muscles use energy internally to maintain tension, even without external movement.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask a strong learner to push the classroom wall. Ask, "Are you doing work?" (Expected: yes — they are tired.) Explain that physics says otherwise.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test three scenarios.
   - **Scenario 1:** Push a wall. Measure force (spring balance). Wall does not move. Calculate work. (Answer: 0 J.)
   - **Scenario 2:** Hold a book at constant height for 30 seconds. Calculate work against gravity. (Answer: 0 J — no displacement.)
   - **Scenario 3:** Carry a book horizontally across the room. Calculate work against gravity. (Answer: 0 J — perpendicular.)
   - **Key discovery:** Physics work requires force AND displacement in the force's direction. Effort without displacement is not physics work.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Three cases of zero work:**
     1. No displacement (pushing a wall).
     2. No force (object moving by itself).
     3. Force perpendicular to displacement (carrying horizontally).
   - **Why effort feels like work:** Muscles consume chemical energy to generate force. This internal work becomes heat, making us tired.
   - **Everyday examples:**
     - Holding a baby — tiring but no physics work.
     - Standing still — no physics work.
     - Pushing a stuck car that doesn't move — no physics work on the car.
   - Kenyan context: A guard standing at a gate all day does no physics work but is exhausted from maintaining posture.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner gives three examples of zero work. Homework: observe your family and list five activities — classify as work or zero work.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* You push a wall with 300 N but it does not move. How much work is done?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A satellite orbits Earth. Does gravity do work?
3. A porter carries a 200 N load on their head for 2 km on flat ground. Calculate work against gravity.
4. Explain why standing at attention for an hour is tiring even though no physics work is done.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **0 J** — no displacement.
2. **No** — force is perpendicular to displacement.
3. **0 J** — horizontal movement, no vertical displacement.
4. Muscles continuously contract to maintain posture. Internal chemical energy is consumed and converted to heat.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain why gravity does zero work on a satellite in circular orbit, and derive this result mathematically using W = Fd cos θ.

**Worked Answer:**
For circular orbit:
- Gravitational force F points toward Earth's centre (radial).
- Instantaneous displacement d is tangent to the circle.
- Angle θ between force and displacement = **90°**.
- cos(90°) = **0**.

Therefore: W = F × d × 0 = **0 J**.

**Alternative explanation:**
Work equals change in kinetic energy (work-energy theorem). In circular orbit, speed is constant, so kinetic energy is constant. Therefore, ΔKE = 0 → W = 0.

This is consistent: gravity changes the satellite's direction (acceleration) but not its speed, so no energy is transferred.

**Q:** A person pushes a 500 N shopping cart 10 m across a supermarket floor at constant speed. The pushing force is horizontal. (a) Calculate the work done by the person. (b) Explain why the work done by gravity is zero. (c) If the person stops pushing and the cart coasts to a halt, how much work does friction do?

**Worked Answer:**
(a) W = F × d = 500 N × 10 m = **5000 J**. (Assuming force equals friction at constant speed.)

(b) Gravity acts vertically downward; displacement is horizontal. θ = 90° → cos(90°) = 0 → W_gravity = **0 J**.

(c) When pushing stops, friction decelerates the cart. The cart loses kinetic energy. By work-energy theorem, W_friction = ΔKE. Since the cart stops, all KE is lost. If initial KE was from the push, W_friction = **−5000 J** (negative because friction opposes motion).

The negative sign indicates energy is removed from the cart by friction, converted to heat.
