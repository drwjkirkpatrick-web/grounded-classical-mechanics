# Lesson 103: Kinetic Energy: Energy of Motion

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define kinetic energy as energy of motion.
2. Calculate kinetic energy using KE = ½mv².
3. Demonstrate that faster objects have more kinetic energy.

## Materials
- A ball or stone
- A ramp or inclined plank
- A stopwatch
- A tape measure
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Kinetic energy** *(nishati ya harakati)* — energy an object has due to its motion.
- **Mass** *(mzito)* — the amount of matter in an object.
- **Speed** *(kasi)* — how fast an object moves.
- **Joule** *(joule)* — unit of energy; 1 J = 1 kg × (1 m/s)².

## Pre-Test
1. Which has more kinetic energy: a slowly moving bicycle or a fast-moving bicycle?
2. What happens to kinetic energy if you double the speed?
3. Does a heavy truck at 10 m/s have more or less kinetic energy than a small car at 10 m/s?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The fast-moving bicycle — kinetic energy increases with speed.
2. It quadruples — KE ∝ v².
3. More — kinetic energy increases with mass.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Roll a ball slowly, then fast. Ask, "Which roll could knock over a cup?" Introduce kinetic energy.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners roll a ball down a ramp from different heights.
   - Release from 10 cm: measure speed at bottom (or observe distance rolled on flat).
   - Release from 40 cm: measure again.
   - **Key discovery:** Higher release = faster at bottom = more kinetic energy.
   - Groups discuss: why does height affect speed? (Potential energy converts to kinetic energy.)
   - **Formula test:** If m = 0.5 kg, calculate KE at v = 2 m/s and v = 4 m/s.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formula:** KE = ½mv² where m = mass (kg), v = speed (m/s).
   - **Why v²:** Doubling speed quadruples KE. A car at 100 km/h has 4× the KE of a car at 50 km/h.
   - **Why ½:** For constant acceleration from rest, average speed is half final speed. Work = force × distance = ma × (½at²) = ½m(at)² = ½mv².
   - **Examples:**
     - 0.5 kg ball at 4 m/s: KE = ½ × 0.5 × 16 = **4 J**.
     - 70 kg runner at 5 m/s: KE = ½ × 70 × 25 = **875 J**.
     - 1000 kg matatu at 20 m/s: KE = ½ × 1000 × 400 = **200,000 J**.
   - Kenyan context: A fast boda-boda has more KE than a slow mkokoteni — explaining why fast collisions are more dangerous.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states why KE depends on speed squared. Homework: calculate your KE when walking to school.

## Hands-On Build: Kinetic Energy Demonstration

### Parts
- Ball or stone — 1 — ground
- Ramp or plank — 1 — classroom
- Stopwatch — 1 — phone
- Tape measure — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Set up a gentle ramp (1 m long, one end raised 20 cm).
2. Mark a start line at the top and a finish line at the bottom.
3. Release the ball from the top. Time how long it takes to reach the bottom.
4. Calculate average speed: v = ramp length / time.
5. Calculate KE: KE = ½mv².
6. Repeat with the ramp raised to 40 cm.
7. Compare: higher ramp = faster = more KE.
8. **Checkpoint:** Ensure the ball is released from rest (no pushing). Measure time accurately.

### Common Mistakes
- **Pushing the ball:** Adds extra energy. Fix: release gently.
- **Timing error:** Reaction time affects short times. Fix: repeat three times and average.
- **Wrong units:** Mass in kg, speed in m/s. Fix: convert before calculating.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A bicycle moves faster. Does its kinetic energy increase or decrease?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens to kinetic energy if speed doubles?
3. Calculate the KE of a 0.2 kg stone thrown at 10 m/s.
4. A 1000 kg car travels at 10 m/s. What speed must a 500 kg car travel to have the same KE?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Increases**.
2. It **quadruples** (×4).
3. KE = ½ × 0.2 × 100 = **10 J**.
4. For same KE: ½(1000)(10)² = ½(500)v² → 100,000 = 250v² → v² = 400 → v = **20 m/s**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Derive the kinetic energy formula KE = ½mv² from the definitions of work and constant acceleration.

**Worked Answer:**
**Step 1: Work done by net force**
W = F × d (force in direction of motion).

**Step 2: Newton's Second Law**
F = ma.

**Step 3: Distance with constant acceleration from rest**
Using v² = u² + 2as with u = 0:
v² = 2as → s = v²/2a.

**Step 4: Substitute into work**
W = ma × (v²/2a) = ½mv².

**Step 5: Work-Energy Theorem**
Net work done = change in kinetic energy.
Since starting from rest (KE_initial = 0):
W = KE_final = **½mv²**.

This derivation shows that kinetic energy is a direct consequence of Newton's laws and the definition of work.

**Q:** A matatu of mass 1500 kg travels at 20 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy. If it brakes uniformly and stops in 40 m, calculate the braking force.

**Worked Answer:**
**Kinetic energy:**
KE = ½mv² = ½ × 1500 × (20)² = 750 × 400 = **300,000 J** (300 kJ).

**Braking force:**
Work done by brakes = change in KE = 0 − 300,000 = **−300,000 J**.

W = F × d → −300,000 = F × 40 → F = −300,000 / 40 = **−7,500 N**.

The braking force is **7,500 N** (negative sign indicates opposite to motion).

**Check with kinematics:**
v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 400 + 2a(40) → a = −400/80 = **−5 m/s²**.
F = ma = 1500 × (−5) = **−7,500 N**. Same result.
