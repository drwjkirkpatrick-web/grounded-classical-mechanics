# Lesson 104: Gravitational Potential Energy: Tanks on Stands

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define gravitational potential energy as energy due to height.
2. Calculate PE = mgh.
3. Relate water tank height to the energy available for doing work.

## Materials
- Notebook
- A diagram or photo of a water tank on a stand
- A calculator

## Vocabulary
- **Gravitational potential energy** *(nishati ya uwezekano wa mvutano)* — energy stored due to an object's height above the ground.
- **Reference level** *(kiwango cha kumbukumbu)* — the height from which potential energy is measured (usually ground level).
- **Water tank** *(tangi ya maji)* — an elevated container that stores water using potential energy.
- **Head** *(kichwa cha urefu)* — the height of water above a point; determines water pressure.

## Pre-Test
1. Which has more potential energy: water in a tank on the ground or water in a tank on a 5 m stand?
2. What three quantities are multiplied to calculate gravitational PE?
3. A 50 kg water tank is raised 3 m. How much PE does it gain?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The tank on the 5 m stand — higher height = more PE.
2. Mass (m), gravity (g), and height (h).
3. PE = 50 × 10 × 3 = **1500 J**.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Why do we put water tanks on stands?" (Expected: so water flows.) Explain that height = energy.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners calculate PE for various scenarios.
   - A 1000 L (1000 kg) water tank on a 3 m stand: PE = 1000 × 10 × 3 = 30,000 J.
   - The same tank on a 6 m stand: PE = 1000 × 10 × 6 = 60,000 J.
   - A 20 kg jerrican lifted 1.5 m: PE = 20 × 10 × 1.5 = 300 J.
   - **Key discovery:** PE depends on height. Double the height, double the PE (for same mass).
   - Groups discuss: where does the energy go when water flows down? (Converts to kinetic energy, then to pressure and flow.)

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formula:** PE = mgh where m = mass (kg), g = 10 m/s², h = height (m).
   - **Why height matters:** Higher water has more energy to do work — push water through pipes, drive turbines, create pressure.
   - **Water tanks in Kenya:** Elevated tanks are common in schools and homes. The height provides water pressure without pumps.
   - **Energy conversion:** PE → KE → pressure → flow. When water reaches ground level, all PE has converted to other forms.
   - **Examples:**
     - 500 kg tank at 4 m: PE = 500 × 10 × 4 = **20,000 J**.
     - 50 kg pupil on second floor (5 m): PE = 50 × 10 × 5 = **2,500 J**.
     - Mt. Kenya (5199 m): a 1 kg stone has PE = 1 × 10 × 5199 = **51,990 J**.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why water tanks are elevated. Homework: calculate the PE of your water tank at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Which has more PE: water in a ground tank or an elevated tank?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What three quantities multiply to give PE?
3. A 200 kg water tank sits on a 4 m stand. Calculate its PE.
4. Explain why doubling the height of a water tank doubles its potential energy.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The **elevated tank** — higher height = more PE.
2. **Mass, gravity, and height** (mgh).
3. PE = 200 × 10 × 4 = **8,000 J**.
4. PE = mgh. If h doubles and m and g stay the same, PE doubles proportionally.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A village water system uses a 5000 L tank on an 8 m stand. Calculate the potential energy stored. If this water flows through a pipe to ground level, calculate the kinetic energy it would have just before impact (ignoring friction).

**Worked Answer:**
**Potential energy:**
m = 5000 L × 1 kg/L = **5000 kg**.
PE = mgh = 5000 × 10 × 8 = **400,000 J** (400 kJ).

**Kinetic energy at ground level:**
By conservation of energy (ignoring friction):
PE_top = KE_bottom.
KE = **400,000 J**.

**Speed at impact:**
KE = ½mv² → 400,000 = ½ × 5000 × v² → v² = 160 → v = **12.65 m/s**.

**Real-world note:**
In reality, friction in pipes and air resistance reduce this. The actual speed might be 8–10 m/s. But the energy is still there — converted to heat in the pipe walls and turbulence.

**Q:** Compare the energy stored in a 1000 L tank at 6 m vs a 2000 L tank at 3 m. Which stores more energy? Which provides better water pressure at a tap 1 m above ground?

**Worked Answer:**
**Energy comparison:**
Tank A: PE = 1000 × 10 × 6 = **60,000 J**.
Tank B: PE = 2000 × 10 × 3 = **60,000 J**.

Both store the **same total energy**.

**Water pressure at tap:**
Pressure depends on the **height difference** between water surface and tap.
- Tank A: head = 6 − 1 = **5 m**.
- Tank B: head = 3 − 1 = **2 m**.

Tank A provides **2.5× more pressure** at the tap despite the same total energy.

**Conclusion:** For water pressure, height matters more than total volume. This is why water towers are tall and narrow rather than short and wide.
