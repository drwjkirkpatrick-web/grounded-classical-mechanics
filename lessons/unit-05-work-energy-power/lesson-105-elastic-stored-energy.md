# Lesson 105: Elastic and Stored Energy

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define elastic potential energy as energy stored in stretched or compressed objects.
2. Identify examples of elastic energy in everyday life.
3. Describe how elastic energy converts to kinetic energy.

## Materials
- Rubber bands
- A slingshot or elastic
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Elastic potential energy** *(nishati ya uwezekano wa kunyoosha)* — energy stored in objects when they are stretched or compressed.
- **Spring constant** *(tani ya springi)* — a measure of stiffness; higher k = stiffer spring.
- **Stretch** *(kunyoosha)* — to increase length beyond rest position.
- **Compression** *(kubana)* — to decrease length below rest position.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to a rubber band when you stretch it?
2. Where does the energy go when a stretched rubber band is released?
3. Name two objects that store elastic energy.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It stores energy — elastic potential energy.
2. It converts to kinetic energy (motion of the rubber band).
3. Rubber band, spring, elastic waistband, bow and arrow, slingshot.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Stretch a rubber band. Ask, "Where is the energy now?" Release it — energy converts to motion.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners stretch rubber bands by different amounts and observe the results.
   - Stretch 2 cm: releases gently.
   - Stretch 10 cm: releases faster and farther.
   - **Key discovery:** More stretch = more stored energy = more kinetic energy on release.
   - Groups test: can a stretched rubber band launch a paper clip? How far with different stretches?
   - **Formula preview:** PE_elastic = ½kx² where k = spring constant, x = stretch distance.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Elastic PE:** Stored when objects are deformed (stretched, compressed, twisted, bent).
   - **Examples:**
     - Rubber band stretched → launches paper.
     - Spring compressed → pushes toy car.
     - Bowstring drawn → launches arrow.
     - Elastic waistband → returns to shape.
     - Bouncy ball compressed → bounces back.
   - **Conversion:** Elastic PE → KE on release. Some energy lost as heat and sound.
   - **Hooke's Law:** F = kx. More stretch requires more force. The area under the force-stretch graph = stored energy.
   - Kenyan context: Slingshots (rungi) used by herders; bows for hunting; bungee cords for securing loads on bodas.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains elastic energy with an example. Homework: find three elastic energy examples at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What happens to a rubber band when stretched?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Where does the stored energy go when released?
3. A spring stores 10 J when compressed 5 cm. How much energy does it store when compressed 10 cm (assuming Hooke's Law)?
4. Name two Kenyan uses of elastic energy.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. It stores **elastic potential energy**.
2. It converts to **kinetic energy**.
3. Since PE ∝ x², doubling stretch quadruples energy: 10 J × 4 = **40 J**.
4. Slingshots (rungi), bows and arrows, bungee cords on bodas, rubber bands for packaging.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A slingshot elastic has a spring constant k = 200 N/m. It is pulled back 0.3 m. Calculate (a) the force required, and (b) the elastic potential energy stored. If this converts entirely to kinetic energy of a 0.05 kg stone, calculate the launch speed.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Force: F = kx = 200 × 0.3 = **60 N**.

(b) Elastic PE = ½kx² = ½ × 200 × (0.3)² = 100 × 0.09 = **9 J**.

**Launch speed:**
KE = ½mv² → 9 = ½ × 0.05 × v² → v² = 9 / 0.025 = 360 → v = **18.97 m/s**.

This is about 68 km/h — a dangerous speed for a small stone. This explains why slingshots can injure.

**Q:** Explain why elastic energy is proportional to the square of stretch distance (PE ∝ x²) using the idea of average force during stretching.

**Worked Answer:**
**Hooke's Law:** F = kx. Force increases linearly with stretch.

When stretching from 0 to x:
- Initial force: F_initial = 0.
- Final force: F_final = kx.
- Average force: F_avg = (0 + kx)/2 = kx/2.

Work done (energy stored) = F_avg × distance = (kx/2) × x = **½kx²**.

This shows that:
- Doubling stretch doubles the average force AND doubles the distance.
- Energy = force × distance → (×2) × (×2) = **×4**.

This is why a bow drawn to full draw stores much more energy than one half-drawn — the archer must work harder per centimetre as the draw increases.

## Safety Notes
- Do not aim rubber bands or slingshots at people.
- Be careful when releasing stretched rubber bands — they can snap back.
- Standard classroom safety.
