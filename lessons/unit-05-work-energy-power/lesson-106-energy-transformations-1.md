# Lesson 106: Energy Transformations I: Falling, Swinging, Shining

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Trace energy transformations in falling, swinging, and glowing objects.
2. Identify the energy forms at each stage of a transformation.
3. State that energy is conserved during transformations.

## Materials
- A pendulum (string + weight)
- A torch
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Energy transformation** *(mabadiliko ya nishati)* — the conversion of one form of energy to another.
- **Pendulum** *(kikungu)* — a weight on a string that swings back and forth.
- **Conservation** *(uhifadhi)* — the principle that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed.
- **Dissipation** *(kupotea)* — energy spreading out and becoming less useful (usually as heat).

## Pre-Test
1. A ball falls from a height. What energy transformation occurs?
2. A pendulum swings. Where is its energy highest at the top?
3. Where does a torch's light energy come from?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy.
2. At the highest points (turning points) — all PE, zero KE.
3. Chemical energy in the battery → electrical energy → light and heat.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Swing a pendulum. Ask, "Where is it moving fastest? Where does it stop? Where is the energy?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners trace energy in three systems.
   - **Falling ball:** Lift a ball. Release. PE → KE → heat/sound on impact.
   - **Swinging pendulum:** Pull back. Release. PE → KE → PE. Discuss: does it swing forever? (No — friction and air resistance convert KE to heat.)
   - **Torch:** Turn on. Battery → electricity → light + heat.
   - **Key discovery:** Energy transforms from one form to another. Total energy is conserved, but useful energy decreases.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Falling object:** PE (height) → KE (motion) → heat/sound (impact). If it bounces, some KE returns to PE.
   - **Pendulum:** At highest point: maximum PE, zero KE. At lowest point: maximum KE, minimum PE. Total energy stays constant (ignoring friction).
   - **Torch:** Chemical (battery) → electrical → light + heat. The bulb is hot because some energy becomes heat, not light.
   - **Efficiency:** No energy transformation is 100% efficient. Some always becomes heat.
   - **Conservation:** Energy is never lost — it just becomes less useful.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces energy from food to motion. Homework: trace energy from the Sun to a turning fan.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A ball falls. What is the energy transformation?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A pendulum at its highest point has maximum __________ and zero __________.
3. A torch converts battery energy to __________ and __________.
4. Why does a pendulum eventually stop swinging?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy**.
2. **Potential energy**, **kinetic energy**.
3. **Light** and **heat**.
4. **Friction and air resistance** convert kinetic energy to heat.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 0.5 kg pendulum bob is pulled to a height of 0.2 m and released. Calculate (a) its initial PE, (b) its maximum KE, and (c) its speed at the lowest point.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Initial PE = mgh = 0.5 × 10 × 0.2 = **1 J**.

(b) At lowest point, all PE has converted to KE (ignoring friction):
KE_max = **1 J**.

(c) Speed at lowest point:
KE = ½mv² → 1 = ½ × 0.5 × v² → v² = 4 → v = **2 m/s**.

**Q:** Explain why no real machine is 100% efficient, using the pendulum as an example. Where does the "lost" energy go?

**Worked Answer:**
In an ideal pendulum (no friction, no air resistance), energy would convert perfectly between PE and KE forever. In reality:

1. **Air resistance:** Pushes against the bob, converting some KE to heat in the air.
2. **Friction at pivot:** Rubbing at the string's top converts KE to heat at the pivot.
3. **Internal friction:** The string flexes slightly, converting energy to heat.

The "lost" energy does not disappear — it becomes **heat** in the surroundings. The total energy is conserved, but it is no longer useful for swinging.

After many swings, all the initial PE has become heat. The pendulum stops. To keep it going, you must add energy (push it).

This is why perpetual motion machines are impossible — some energy always becomes heat.
