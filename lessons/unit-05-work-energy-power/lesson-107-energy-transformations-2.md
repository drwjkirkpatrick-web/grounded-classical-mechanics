# Lesson 107: Energy Transformations II: The Jiko and the Body

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Trace energy transformations in a jiko (cooking fire).
2. Trace energy from food to body movement.
3. Explain why not all chemical energy becomes useful work.

## Materials
- Charcoal or firewood (if available)
- A notebook for each learner
- A diagram of the human body

## Vocabulary
- **Combustion** *(kuchoma)* — burning that releases chemical energy as heat and light.
- **Metabolism** *(matabolism)* — chemical processes that convert food to energy in the body.
- **Waste heat** *(joto la kupotea)* — energy lost as heat during energy transformations.
- **Efficiency** *(uwezo wa kufanya kazi)* — the percentage of input energy that becomes useful output.

## Pre-Test
1. What energy transformation occurs when charcoal burns in a jiko?
2. Where does the energy in your muscles come from?
3. Why does a jiko get hot but not all the heat cooks the food?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Chemical energy → heat + light.
2. Chemical energy in food → metabolism → muscle work.
3. Some heat escapes to the surroundings; only some reaches the pot.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who cooked with a jiko this morning? Where did the fire's energy come from?" Trace back: fire → charcoal → tree → Sun.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners trace energy in two systems.
   - **The jiko:**
     - Charcoal (chemical energy) → burns → heat + light.
     - Heat → warms pot → cooks food.
     - Light → visible glow.
     - Heat lost: to air, to ground, to hands.
   - **The body:**
     - Food (chemical energy) → digestion → glucose.
     - Glucose → metabolism → ATP (energy currency).
     - ATP → muscle contraction → movement (kinetic energy).
     - Waste: heat (body warmth), CO₂, water.
   - **Key discovery:** Both systems convert chemical energy to heat and work. Neither is 100% efficient.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Jiko efficiency:**
     - Only ~10–20% of charcoal's energy heats the pot.
     - Most heat escapes to surroundings.
     - Insulated jikos (like rocket stoves) improve efficiency.
   - **Body efficiency:**
     - Only ~20–25% of food energy becomes mechanical work.
     - The rest becomes heat (keeps us warm) or is lost.
     - This is why exercise makes us hot.
   - **Energy chains:**
     - Sun → plants → charcoal → heat → cooked food.
     - Sun → plants → food → body → movement.
   - **Why inefficiency is unavoidable:**
     - Heat flows from hot to cold (Second Law of Thermodynamics).
     - Some energy must become heat in any real process.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces energy from the Sun to a running pupil. Homework: trace energy from Sun to a boiling kettle.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What energy transformation occurs when charcoal burns?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Where does muscle energy come from?
3. Why is a jiko not 100% efficient?
4. What happens to the energy in food that does not become movement?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Chemical → heat + light**.
2. **Chemical energy in food**.
3. Most heat **escapes** to surroundings instead of heating the pot.
4. It becomes **heat** (body warmth) or is lost as waste.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A jiko burns 1 kg of charcoal, which releases 30 MJ of chemical energy. If the jiko is 15% efficient, calculate (a) the useful energy for cooking, and (b) the energy lost to the surroundings. How much water could this useful energy heat from 20°C to boiling?

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Useful energy = 30 MJ × 0.15 = **4.5 MJ**.

(b) Energy lost = 30 − 4.5 = **25.5 MJ**.

**Water heating:**
Energy needed = mcΔT where c = 4200 J/kg°C.
4,500,000 = m × 4200 × (100 − 20) = m × 4200 × 80 = m × 336,000.
m = 4,500,000 / 336,000 = **13.4 kg** (about 13.4 L).

This shows that even with 1 kg of charcoal, only a small amount of water can be boiled efficiently.

**Q:** The human body is about 20% efficient at converting food energy to mechanical work. A person eats 2500 kcal (10.5 MJ) per day. Calculate the mechanical work they can do, and explain where the remaining energy goes.

**Worked Answer:**
Mechanical work = 10.5 MJ × 0.20 = **2.1 MJ**.

Remaining energy = 10.5 − 2.1 = **8.4 MJ**.

**Where it goes:**
1. **Heat:** Body warmth, sweating, breathing warm air.
2. **Metabolic processes:** Digestion, cell repair, brain activity.
3. **Waste:** Urine and faeces contain undigested energy.

This 20% efficiency is similar to a petrol engine. The human body is a heat engine — most energy becomes heat.
