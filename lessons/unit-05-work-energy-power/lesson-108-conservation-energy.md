# Lesson 108: Conservation of Energy: Nothing Lost, Everything Moved

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State the law of conservation of energy.
2. Apply conservation to simple mechanical systems.
3. Distinguish between energy conservation and energy usefulness.

## Materials
- A ball
- A pendulum
- A notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Conservation of energy** *(uhifadhi wa nishati)* — the principle that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed.
- **Total energy** *(nishati jumla)* — the sum of all forms of energy in a system.
- **Isolated system** *(mfumo huru)* — a system with no energy entering or leaving.
- **Dissipated** *(kupotea)* — energy that has spread out and become less useful.

## Pre-Test
1. Can energy be created?
2. Can energy be destroyed?
3. A ball bounces lower each time. Is energy being lost?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. No — energy is conserved.
2. No — energy changes form but is not destroyed.
3. No — energy converts to heat and sound. Total energy is conserved.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Drop a ball. It bounces, but lower each time. Ask, "Where did the energy go?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners trace energy in a bouncing ball.
   - Drop 1: PE → KE → PE (rebound) + heat + sound.
   - Drop 2: Rebound lower — some energy became heat in the ball and ground.
   - Drop 3: Even lower. Eventually stops.
   - **Key discovery:** Total energy is constant, but useful energy decreases. Energy is not lost — it becomes heat.
   - Groups calculate: if initial PE = 10 J, and rebound PE = 8 J, then 2 J became heat.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Law of conservation:** Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only change form.
   - **Bouncing ball:**
     - Start: PE = 10 J, KE = 0.
     - Impact: PE = 0, KE = 10 J.
     - Rebound: PE = 8 J, KE = 0, heat = 2 J.
     - Total: 10 J = 8 + 2 = 10 J. Conserved!
   - **Real systems:** No system is perfectly isolated. Energy always leaks as heat.
   - **Why perpetual motion is impossible:** Some energy always becomes heat, which cannot be fully recovered.
   - Kenyan context: A mkokoteni rolling downhill converts PE to KE, but friction converts some to heat. It eventually stops.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states conservation in their own words. Homework: trace energy conservation in a swinging gate.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Can energy be created?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Can energy be destroyed?
3. A ball starts with 20 J PE. After bouncing, it has 15 J PE. How much energy became heat?
4. Explain why a bouncing ball eventually stops, using conservation of energy.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **No**.
2. **No**.
3. **5 J** became heat (20 − 15 = 5).
4. With each bounce, some energy becomes **heat and sound**. The ball has less and less mechanical energy until it stops. Total energy is conserved, but it is no longer useful for bouncing.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 0.2 kg ball is dropped from 2 m. Calculate (a) its initial PE, (b) its KE just before impact, (c) its rebound height if 30% of energy is lost on impact, and (d) show that total energy is conserved.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) PE = mgh = 0.2 × 10 × 2 = **4 J**.

(b) Just before impact, all PE → KE: KE = **4 J**.

(c) After impact, 30% lost → 70% remains = 4 × 0.7 = **2.8 J**.
Rebound PE = 2.8 J → h = PE/mg = 2.8/(0.2×10) = 2.8/2 = **1.4 m**.

(d) **Energy conservation check:**
- Start: PE = 4 J, KE = 0, heat = 0. Total = **4 J**.
- Before impact: PE = 0, KE = 4 J, heat = 0. Total = **4 J**.
- After impact: PE = 2.8 J, KE = 0, heat = 1.2 J. Total = **4 J**.

Energy is conserved at every stage.

**Q:** Explain why a perpetual motion machine is impossible, using the laws of thermodynamics.

**Worked Answer:**
A perpetual motion machine would run forever without energy input. There are two types:

**Type 1 (creates energy):**
Violates the **First Law of Thermodynamics** (conservation of energy). You cannot create energy from nothing. Every machine needs an energy source.

**Type 2 (100% efficient):**
Violates the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**. In any real process, some energy becomes heat. This heat spreads out and cannot be fully recovered to do work.

Even a perfectly frictionless machine in a vacuum would lose energy as heat through internal friction or electromagnetic radiation. Eventually, all energy becomes heat, and the machine stops.

This is why energy resources (fuel, food, sunlight) must be continuously replenished. The universe tends toward maximum entropy (disorder), with all energy eventually becoming useless heat.
