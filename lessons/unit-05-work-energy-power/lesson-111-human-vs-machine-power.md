# Lesson 111: Human Power vs Machine Power

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Compare typical power outputs of humans and common machines.
2. Explain why machines replace human labour for certain tasks.
3. Discuss the trade-offs between human and machine power.

## Materials
- Data cards with power ratings
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Power output** *(tokeo la nguvu)* — the rate at which a system does work.
- **Labour** *(kazi)* — physical work done by people.
- **Mechanisation** *(kumekanisha)* — using machines to replace human effort.
- **Trade-off** *(kubadilishana)* — accepting one disadvantage for another advantage.

## Pre-Test
1. Which produces more power: a person or a boda-boda engine?
2. Why do farmers use tractors instead of hand ploughing?
3. What is one advantage of human power over machine power?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Boda-boda engine — about 30× more.
2. Faster, more consistent, less tiring.
3. No fuel cost, no pollution, more flexible.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who has used a posho mill? Who has pounded maize by hand?" Compare the two experiences.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners receive data cards showing power outputs.
   - Human walking: ~75 W.
   - Human sprinting: ~400 W (peak).
   - Boda engine: ~3000 W.
   - Posho mill: ~5000 W.
   - Electric kettle: ~2000 W.
   - Groups rank from lowest to highest power.
   - Calculate: how many humans equal one boda engine? (3000/75 = 40 humans.)
   - **Key discovery:** Machines produce vastly more power than humans.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Human power:** ~75 W sustained. Limited by food intake and fatigue.
   - **Machine power:** Thousands of watts. Limited by fuel and maintenance.
   - **Why machines replace humans:**
     - Speed: A tractor ploughs 1 hectare in hours; a human takes days.
     - Consistency: Machines don't tire or need breaks.
     - Scale: One machine replaces many workers.
   - **Why humans still matter:**
     - Flexibility: Humans adapt to varied tasks.
     - Precision: Delicate work (surgery, craft) needs human judgement.
     - Cost: No fuel needed for small tasks.
     - Environment: No emissions from human work.
   - Kenyan context: Mkokoteni vs boda for transport; hand pounding vs posho mill; jembe vs tractor.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states one advantage of machines and one of humans. Homework: interview a family member about tools they use.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* A boda engine or a person: which has higher power output?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why might a farmer choose a tractor over hand ploughing?
3. How many 75 W humans are needed to match a 3000 W boda engine?
4. Name one task where human power is better than machine power.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Boda engine** (~3000 W vs ~75 W).
2. Tractor is **faster, more consistent, less tiring**.
3. 3000 / 75 = **40 humans**.
4. Any reasonable answer: delicate craft, tasks requiring judgement, small-scale gardening, interpersonal work.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A boda engine produces 3 kW and consumes 0.15 L of petrol per kilometre. A human produces 75 W and consumes food equivalent to 2000 kcal/day. Calculate the energy efficiency of each in terms of useful work per unit energy input.

**Worked Answer:**
**Boda engine:**
Energy in petrol: 0.15 L/km × 34 MJ/L = **5.1 MJ/km**.
Useful work: 3 kW × time. At 30 km/h, time per km = 120 s.
Work = 3000 × 120 = **360,000 J** = 0.36 MJ.
Efficiency = 0.36 / 5.1 = **7%**.

**Human:**
Energy in food: 2000 kcal = 8.4 MJ/day.
Useful work (8 hours): 75 W × 28,800 s = **2.16 MJ**.
Efficiency = 2.16 / 8.4 = **26%**.

**Conclusion:**
Humans are **more efficient** at converting fuel to work than boda engines (26% vs 7%). However, engines produce much more power. The choice depends on the task.

**Q:** Discuss the environmental trade-offs between human labour and machine labour in rural Kenya, considering energy sources, emissions, and economic factors.

**Worked Answer:**
**Human labour:**
- **Energy source:** Food (renewable, solar-derived).
- **Emissions:** Minimal direct emissions. Waste is biodegradable.
- **Economic:** Low capital cost (no machine purchase). High labour cost (time, fatigue).
- **Limitation:** Low power, slow, limited by human capacity.

**Machine labour:**
- **Energy source:** Petrol, diesel, electricity (often from fossil fuels).
- **Emissions:** CO₂, particulates, noise. Contributes to climate change and air pollution.
- **Economic:** High capital cost (purchase, maintenance). Low operating cost per unit work.
- **Advantage:** High power, fast, scalable.

**Trade-offs:**
- Small farms: Human labour may be more economical and sustainable.
- Large farms: Machines necessary for scale and timeliness (planting before rains).
- Transition: As farms grow, mechanisation becomes necessary. But this requires capital and fuel access.

Kenya's challenge is to find the right balance — using machines where essential while preserving sustainable practices.
