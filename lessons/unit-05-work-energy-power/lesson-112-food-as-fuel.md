# Lesson 112: Food as Fuel: Kilojoules on the Plate

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify foods as stores of chemical energy.
2. Compare energy content of common Kenyan foods.
3. Calculate daily energy needs from food intake.

## Materials
- Food packaging labels (or printed examples)
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Calorie** *(kalori)* — a unit of energy in food; 1 kcal = 4184 J.
- **Kilojoule** *(kilojoule)* — 1000 joules; the SI unit for food energy.
- **Metabolism** *(matabolism)* — processes that convert food to usable energy.
- **Energy density** *(umizo wa nishati)* — energy per unit mass of food.

## Pre-Test
1. Which has more energy: 100 g of ugali or 100 g of cooking oil?
2. Why does a manual worker need more food than a desk worker?
3. How many joules are in 1 kilocalorie (food calorie)?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Cooking oil — fats have about 9 kcal/g vs 4 kcal/g for carbohydrates.
2. More work requires more energy input.
3. About 4184 J (often rounded to 4200 J).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Why do you eat breakfast before school?" Trace the energy chain: food → digestion → ATP → brain work.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners examine food energy data.
   - Compare: ugali (4 kcal/g), beans (3.5 kcal/g), oil (9 kcal/g), sukuma wiki (0.5 kcal/g).
   - Calculate: a plate with 200 g ugali + 100 g beans + 10 g oil.
     - Ugali: 200 × 4 = 800 kcal.
     - Beans: 100 × 3.5 = 350 kcal.
     - Oil: 10 × 9 = 90 kcal.
     - Total: 1240 kcal = 1240 × 4.2 = **5208 kJ**.
   - Compare to daily needs: ~2000 kcal for an adult.
   - **Key discovery:** Food is fuel. Different foods provide different energy. Quantity matters.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Food energy:**
     - Carbohydrates: ~4 kcal/g (ugali, rice, chapati).
     - Proteins: ~4 kcal/g (beans, meat, eggs).
     - Fats: ~9 kcal/g (oil, butter, nuts).
   - **Daily needs:**
     - Child (10–13): ~1800 kcal.
     - Teenager: ~2200 kcal.
     - Adult manual worker: ~2500+ kcal.
   - **Energy balance:**
     - Eat more than you use → weight gain.
     - Eat less than you use → weight loss.
     - Eat what you use → stable weight.
   - Kenyan context: A farm worker needs more calories than a student. A cup of porridge before school provides energy for morning lessons.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states why food is called fuel. Homework: calculate the energy in your last meal.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Which has more energy per gram: ugali or oil?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why does a manual worker need more food?
3. A 50 kg teenager needs 2200 kcal/day. How many joules is this?
4. A plate contains 150 g ugali (4 kcal/g) and 50 g beans (3.5 kcal/g). Calculate total energy in kJ.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Oil** — 9 kcal/g vs 4 kcal/g.
2. More physical work requires more energy input.
3. 2200 × 4184 = **9,204,800 J** ≈ **9.2 MJ**.
4. Ugali: 150 × 4 = 600 kcal. Beans: 50 × 3.5 = 175 kcal. Total = 775 kcal = 775 × 4.2 = **3255 kJ**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** An athlete consumes 3000 kcal/day and trains for 2 hours at 300 W. Calculate (a) the training energy in kJ, and (b) the percentage of daily intake used for training.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Training energy = 300 W × 7200 s = **2,160,000 J** = **2160 kJ**.
   In kcal: 2160 / 4.2 = **514 kcal**.

(b) Percentage = (514 / 3000) × 100% = **17.1%**.

The remaining 83% is used for: basal metabolism (breathing, heartbeat, brain), digestion, and non-training activity.

**Q:** Compare the energy density of traditional Kenyan foods. Discuss why a balanced diet is important for energy and health, not just total calories.

**Worked Answer:**
**Energy densities (kcal/g):**
- Ugali: ~3.5
- Rice: ~3.6
- Beans: ~3.0
- Sukuma wiki: ~0.3
- Cooking oil: ~9.0
- Groundnuts: ~5.5
- Meat: ~2.5

**Balanced diet importance:**
1. **Macronutrients:** Carbs for quick energy, proteins for growth/repair, fats for long-term storage.
2. **Micronutrients:** Vitamins and minerals from vegetables (sukuma wiki, spinach) are essential for health.
3. **Fibre:** Aids digestion; found in whole grains and vegetables.
4. **Water:** Essential for all metabolic processes.

A diet of only ugali provides calories but lacks protein, vitamins, and minerals. A balanced plate includes: staple (ugali/rice), protein (beans/meat), vegetables (sukuma), and a small amount of fat (oil).
