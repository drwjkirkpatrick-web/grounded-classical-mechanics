# Lesson 113: Kenya's Energy Story: Firewood to Geothermal

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe Kenya's main energy sources and their uses.
2. Compare renewable and non-renewable energy in the Kenyan context.
3. Explain the role of geothermal energy in Kenya's power grid.

## Materials
- Map of Kenya showing energy sources
- Data on Kenya's electricity generation
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Renewable energy** *(nishati inayojizatiti)* — energy from sources that replenish naturally (sun, wind, geothermal).
- **Non-renewable energy** *(nishati isiyojizatiti)* — energy from finite sources (fossil fuels).
- **Geothermal** *(nishati ya joto la ardhi)* — energy from Earth's internal heat.
- **Grid** *(gridi)* — the network that distributes electricity.

## Pre-Test
1. What is Kenya's main source of electricity?
2. Name two renewable energy sources in Kenya.
3. Why is firewood still widely used for cooking?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Geothermal (about 40% of grid electricity).
2. Geothermal, hydro, wind, solar.
3. It is cheap, available, and traditional.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "How is your home powered?" List responses on board: solar, grid, generator, none.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse Kenya's energy mix.
   - **Data:**
     - Geothermal: ~40% (Olkaria, Menengai).
     - Hydro: ~30% (Turkwell, Seven Forks).
     - Fossil fuels: ~15% (imported).
     - Wind/solar: ~10% (Lake Turkana Wind, Garissa Solar).
     - Off-grid: diesel generators, solar home systems.
   - **Cooking energy:**
     - Firewood/charcoal: ~70% of households.
     - LPG: ~10%.
     - Electricity: ~5%.
     - Other: ~15%.
   - **Key discovery:** Kenya has diverse energy sources, but many people still rely on biomass for cooking.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Geothermal:**
     - Hot underground water/steam drives turbines.
     - Olkaria is one of the world's largest geothermal fields.
     - Reliable (unlike solar/wind which depend on weather).
   - **Hydro:**
     - Depends on rainfall — unreliable during drought.
     - Large dams have environmental impacts.
   - **Solar:**
     - Abundant sunshine (5–7 peak hours/day).
     - Growing rapidly for homes and institutions.
   - **Wind:**
     - Lake Turkana Wind Power: 310 MW.
     - Requires consistent wind.
   - **Challenges:**
     - Grid access: ~75% of Kenyans have grid electricity.
     - Cooking: Most still use biomass (health impacts from smoke).
     - Cost: Solar panels expensive upfront but cheap long-term.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states one advantage of geothermal energy. Homework: research your local energy source.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is Kenya's largest source of electricity?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Name two renewable energy sources in Kenya.
3. Why is geothermal more reliable than solar for grid power?
4. What percentage of Kenyan households still use firewood/charcoal for cooking?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Geothermal** (~40%).
2. Any two of: geothermal, hydro, wind, solar.
3. Geothermal runs **continuously** regardless of weather. Solar depends on sunshine.
4. Approximately **70%**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Kenya generates about 10 TWh (10 billion kWh) of electricity per year. If 40% comes from geothermal, 30% from hydro, and 30% from other sources, calculate the annual generation from each source. Discuss why diversifying energy sources improves energy security.

**Worked Answer:**
**Annual generation:**
- Geothermal: 10 TWh × 0.40 = **4 TWh**.
- Hydro: 10 TWh × 0.30 = **3 TWh**.
- Other: 10 TWh × 0.30 = **3 TWh**.

**Energy security:**
Diversification reduces dependence on any single source. If drought reduces hydro output, geothermal and fossil fuels compensate. If fossil fuel prices spike, renewables buffer the impact.

**Kenya's advantage:**
Kenya has multiple renewable sources (geothermal, hydro, wind, solar), reducing vulnerability to global fuel markets and climate variability.

**Q:** A solar home system provides 100 W for 5 hours per day. Calculate daily energy in kWh. If this replaces kerosene lamps that use 0.5 L/month at 150 KSh/L, calculate the annual savings.

**Worked Answer:**
**Daily energy:**
E = P × t = 0.1 kW × 5 h = **0.5 kWh/day**.

**Annual energy:**
0.5 × 365 = **182.5 kWh/year**.

**Kerosene cost:**
0.5 L/month × 12 months = 6 L/year.
Cost = 6 × 150 = **900 KSh/year**.

**Savings:**
If solar system costs 15,000 KSh, payback = 15,000 / 900 = **16.7 years** (ignoring battery replacement).

With cheaper solar panels and government subsidies, payback is improving. Solar also provides health benefits (no indoor air pollution from kerosene).
