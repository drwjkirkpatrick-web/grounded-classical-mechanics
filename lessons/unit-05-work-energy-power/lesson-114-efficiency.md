# Lesson 114: Efficiency: Useful Out Over Total In

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define efficiency as the ratio of useful output to total input.
2. Calculate efficiency as a percentage.
3. Explain why no real device is 100% efficient.

## Materials
- Examples of appliance labels
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Efficiency** *(uwezo wa kufanya kazi)* — the percentage of input energy that becomes useful output.
- **Useful output** *(tokeo la manufaa)* — energy that accomplishes the desired task.
- **Waste** *(kupotea)* — energy lost to surroundings, usually as heat.
- **Percentage** *(asilimia)* — parts per hundred.

## Pre-Test
1. What does efficiency measure?
2. Can a machine be more than 100% efficient?
3. A jiko uses 1000 J of charcoal energy but only 150 J heats the food. What is its efficiency?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. How much input energy becomes useful output.
2. No — that would violate conservation of energy.
3. Efficiency = (150/1000) × 100% = 15%.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Why does a phone get hot when charging?" (Expected: some energy becomes heat, not battery charge.) Introduce efficiency.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners calculate efficiency for various devices.
   - Jiko: 1000 J in, 150 J useful → 15% efficient.
   - Light bulb: 60 W in, 5 W light out → 8.3% efficient (old incandescent).
   - LED bulb: 10 W in, 8 W light out → 80% efficient.
   - Human body: 2000 kcal in, 400 kcal work → 20% efficient.
   - **Key discovery:** Most devices are inefficient. The "waste" becomes heat.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formula:** Efficiency = (useful output / total input) × 100%.
   - **Why less than 100%:**
     - Friction in moving parts.
     - Heat in electrical resistance.
     - Sound and vibration.
     - Incomplete combustion.
   - **Improving efficiency:**
     - Better insulation (jikos).
     - LED bulbs (vs incandescent).
     - Lubrication (engines).
     - Streamlined shapes (vehicles).
   - Kenyan context: Improved jikos (like rocket stoves) reach 30–40% efficiency vs 10–15% for open fires. This saves firewood and reduces smoke.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states why efficiency matters. Homework: read an appliance label and calculate its efficiency.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does efficiency measure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Can efficiency exceed 100%?
3. A motor uses 500 J of electrical energy and produces 100 J of mechanical work. Calculate efficiency.
4. Suggest two ways to improve a jiko's efficiency.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The ratio of **useful output to total input**.
2. **No** — violates conservation of energy.
3. Efficiency = (100/500) × 100% = **20%**.
4. Any two of: better insulation, enclosed fire, chimney, using dry wood, pot with flat bottom.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A coal power plant produces 500 MW of electricity from 1500 MW of coal energy. Calculate its efficiency. If an improved design reaches 45% efficiency, how much less coal is needed for the same electrical output?

**Worked Answer:**
Current efficiency = (500/1500) × 100% = **33.3%**.

At 45% efficiency:
Electrical output = 0.45 × coal input.
500 = 0.45 × coal_input → coal_input = 500/0.45 = **1111 MW**.

Coal saved = 1500 − 1111 = **389 MW** (26% reduction).

This demonstrates why improving efficiency matters: less fuel, less pollution, lower cost.

**Q:** Explain why a 100% efficient machine is impossible, using the Second Law of Thermodynamics. Include the concept of entropy.

**Worked Answer:**
**The Second Law:** In any energy transfer, some energy must become heat that cannot be fully converted back to work.

**Entropy:** A measure of disorder. Energy conversions increase entropy. Organised energy (fuel, electricity) becomes disorganised (heat spread in surroundings).

**Why 100% is impossible:**
To be 100% efficient, all input energy would need to become useful work with zero heat loss. But the Second Law requires that some energy becomes heat — increasing entropy.

**Example:**
A petrol engine burns fuel. The organised chemical energy could theoretically all become motion. But the hot exhaust, friction, and cooling system all carry away heat. This heat has high entropy — it is spread out and cannot be recaptured efficiently.

Even a hypothetical frictionless machine would radiate heat and lose energy. Nature always demands an entropy tax.
