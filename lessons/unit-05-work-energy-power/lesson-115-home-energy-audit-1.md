# Lesson 115: Project: Home Energy Audit I — Collect the Data

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** home-energy-audit

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Inventory energy uses in a typical Kenyan home.
2. Collect data on energy consumption from appliances and fuels.
3. Organise data for analysis in the next lesson.

## Materials
- Energy audit worksheet (prepared by teacher)
- Pen or pencil
- Optional: access to home electricity meter
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Energy audit** *(ukaguzi wa nishati)* — a systematic review of how energy is used.
- **Inventory** *(orodha)* — a complete list of items.
- **Consumption** *(matumizi)* — the amount of energy used.
- **Baseline** *(kiwango cha mwanzo)* — the starting point for comparison.

## Pre-Test
1. List three things in your home that use energy.
2. How do you measure electricity use?
3. What fuel does your family use for cooking?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Any three: lights, phone charger, radio, TV, jiko, etc.
2. With an electricity meter (kWh) or by estimating from appliance ratings.
3. Firewood, charcoal, LPG, electricity, or kerosene.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "How much does your family spend on energy per month?" Most learners will not know. Explain that today we start finding out.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design a home energy audit.
   - **Categories to audit:**
     - Lighting: bulbs, torches, lamps.
     - Cooking: fuel type, amount used per week.
     - Electronics: phone, radio, TV, computer.
     - Heating/cooling: fans, heaters (if any).
     - Transport: boda, matatu, walking.
   - **Data to collect:**
     - Appliance power rating (W) and hours used per day.
     - Fuel amount and cost per week.
     - Whether energy comes from grid, solar, or other.
   - Groups create a data collection sheet.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why audit:** Understanding use is the first step to reducing waste and cost.
   - **Electricity calculation:**
     - Energy (kWh) = Power (kW) × Time (hours).
     - Cost = Energy × price per kWh.
   - **Fuel calculation:**
     - Firewood: bundles per week × cost.
     - Charcoal: bags per month × cost.
     - LPG: kg per month × cost.
   - **Baseline:** Record current use. Next lesson we will analyse and suggest savings.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups exchange data sheets for feedback. Homework: complete the audit at home (interview family members).

## Hands-On Build: Home Energy Audit Sheet

### Parts
- Audit worksheet — 1 per learner — prepared by teacher or learner
- Pen — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. List all energy uses in the home (lighting, cooking, electronics, transport).
2. For each item, record:
   - What it is (e.g., "3 bulbs, 10 W each").
   - How long it runs per day.
   - What fuel or electricity it uses.
   - Approximate cost per week.
3. Sum all electricity uses (in kWh per day).
4. Sum all fuel uses (in shillings per week).
5. **Checkpoint:** Before finishing, check that every energy use is listed — it is easy to forget phone chargers and standby devices.

### Common Mistakes
- **Forgetting small devices:** Phone chargers, torches, clocks. Fix: walk through each room mentally.
- **Guessing power:** Use appliance labels or typical values. Fix: if unsure, estimate conservatively.
- **Ignoring fuel:** Only counting electricity misses charcoal and firewood. Fix: include all energy sources.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* List three home energy uses.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How is electricity consumption measured?
3. Why is it important to include fuel costs in the audit?
4. What does "baseline" mean in this context?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any three: lighting, cooking, electronics, transport.
2. In **kilowatt-hours (kWh)**.
3. Many Kenyan homes spend more on fuel than electricity. Ignoring fuel gives an incomplete picture.
4. The **starting point** — current energy use before any changes.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A home uses four 10 W bulbs for 5 hours/day, a 50 W TV for 3 hours/day, and a 5 W phone charger for 8 hours/day. Calculate daily electricity consumption in kWh. If electricity costs 20 KSh/kWh, calculate the monthly cost.

**Worked Answer:**
Bulbs: 4 × 10 W × 5 h = 200 Wh = **0.2 kWh**.
TV: 50 W × 3 h = 150 Wh = **0.15 kWh**.
Phone: 5 W × 8 h = 40 Wh = **0.04 kWh**.

Total daily: 0.2 + 0.15 + 0.04 = **0.39 kWh**.
Monthly: 0.39 × 30 = **11.7 kWh**.

Cost: 11.7 × 20 = **234 KSh/month**.

**Q:** Explain why a home energy audit is more useful for identifying savings than simply looking at the total electricity bill.

**Worked Answer:**
A bill shows total cost but not **where** the energy goes. An audit breaks down use by category:

1. **Pinpoints waste:** A single old fridge may use 30% of total electricity. Replacing it saves more than turning off lights.
2. **Prioritises actions:** Small changes with big impact (insulating a jiko) vs small changes with little impact (unplugging a clock).
3. **Tracks behaviour:** Comparing audits over time shows if habits are improving.
4. **Includes all energy:** Bills miss firewood and charcoal costs, which may exceed electricity spending.

Without an audit, savings efforts may target the wrong areas.
