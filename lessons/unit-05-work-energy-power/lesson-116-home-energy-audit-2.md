# Lesson 116: Project: Home Energy Audit II — Present the Savings Plan

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** home-energy-audit

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Analyse energy audit data to identify savings opportunities.
2. Propose practical changes to reduce energy consumption.
3. Present a savings plan to the class.

## Materials
- Completed energy audit sheets from Lesson 115
- Calculator
- Poster paper or notebook
- Markers (if available)

## Vocabulary
- **Savings plan** *(mpango wa kuokoa)* — a proposal for reducing energy use and cost.
- **Payback period** *(muda wa malipo)* — the time for savings to equal the cost of an improvement.
- **Low-hanging fruit** *(matunda ya chini)* — easy changes with big savings.
- **Behavioural change** *(mabadiliko ya tabia)* — adjusting habits to save energy.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the easiest way to save electricity at home?
2. Why might replacing bulbs save money even though new bulbs cost money?
3. What does "payback period" mean?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Turn off lights and appliances when not in use.
2. New bulbs use less energy, so savings eventually exceed purchase cost.
3. The time for accumulated savings to cover the initial cost.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review audit data from Lesson 115.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Groups share one surprising finding from their audit.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Groups analyse their audit data and propose savings.
   - **Low-cost changes:**
     - Turn off lights when leaving rooms.
     - Use natural light during the day.
     - Cook with lids on pots.
     - Unplug chargers when not in use.
   - **Medium-cost changes:**
     - Replace incandescent bulbs with LEDs.
     - Insulate cooking pots (fireless cookers).
     - Fix drafts in doors and windows.
   - **High-cost changes:**
     - Install solar panels.
     - Buy an improved jiko.
     - Replace old appliances.
   - Calculate potential savings for each proposal.
   - **Key discovery:** Behavioural changes cost nothing but save significantly.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Analysing data:**
     - Which category uses the most energy? (Usually cooking or lighting.)
     - Which changes give the biggest saving for least cost?
   - **Payback period:**
     - LED bulb costs 200 KSh, saves 50 KSh/month → payback = 4 months.
     - Solar panel costs 15,000 KSh, saves 500 KSh/month → payback = 30 months.
   - **Behavioural vs technological:**
     - Behavioural: free, immediate, but requires discipline.
     - Technological: costs money, but savings continue automatically.
   - Kenyan context: Many homes can halve cooking fuel use with simple habits (using lids, right-sized pots, dry wood).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present their top three savings proposals. Class votes on the best plan. Homework: implement one change at home and report next week.

## Hands-On Build: Savings Plan Poster

### Parts
- Audit data — from Lesson 115 — learner
- Poster paper or notebook — 1 — classroom
- Markers or pen — 1 — classroom
- Calculator — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Review audit data. Identify top three energy uses.
2. Brainstorm changes for each use.
3. Estimate cost of each change and monthly savings.
4. Calculate payback period for each.
5. Rank changes by savings and feasibility.
6. Create a poster or one-page plan.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before presenting, verify calculations. A common error is confusing power (W) with energy (Wh or kWh).

### Common Mistakes
- **Overestimating savings:** Claiming a 50% reduction without evidence. Fix: base estimates on actual data.
- **Ignoring behaviour:** Only proposing expensive gadgets. Fix: include habit changes.
- **Wrong units:** Mixing watts and kilowatt-hours. Fix: convert everything to kWh.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is one free way to save energy at home?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why replace old bulbs even if new ones cost money?
3. An LED bulb costs 250 KSh and saves 30 KSh/month. What is the payback period?
4. Name one behavioural change and one technological change for saving cooking fuel.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Turn off lights, use natural light, cook with lids, etc.
2. Savings exceed cost over time.
3. Payback = 250 / 30 = **8.3 months**.
4. Behavioural: use lids, right-sized pots, dry wood. Technological: improved jiko, fireless cooker, LPG.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A household spends 1000 KSh/month on electricity and 2000 KSh/month on charcoal. Propose a savings plan that reduces total energy spending by 30% with a payback period under 2 years. Show calculations.

**Worked Answer:**
Current total: 3000 KSh/month.
Target: 3000 × 0.70 = **2100 KSh/month** (saving 900 KSh/month).

**Proposals:**
1. **Replace 6 incandescent bulbs with LEDs** (cost: 6 × 200 = 1200 KSh; saves 150 KSh/month). Payback: 8 months.
2. **Use lids and right-sized pots** (cost: 0 KSh; saves 300 KSh/month on charcoal).
3. **Buy improved jiko** (cost: 800 KSh; saves 250 KSh/month on charcoal). Payback: 3.2 months.
4. **Unplug idle devices** (cost: 0 KSh; saves 50 KSh/month).

**Total savings:** 150 + 300 + 250 + 50 = **750 KSh/month**.
This is 25% reduction, not quite 30%.

**Additional:** Replace old fridge with efficient model (cost: 15,000 KSh; saves 200 KSh/month). Payback: 75 months (too long).

Better: Combine all low-cost measures for 750 KSh/month savings (25%). To reach 30%, add switching some cooking to LPG ( saves additional 150 KSh/month).

**Total investment:** 1200 + 800 = **2000 KSh**.
**Monthly savings:** 900 KSh.
**Payback:** 2000 / 900 = **2.2 months** (well under 2 years).

**Q:** Discuss why some households may not adopt energy-saving measures even when they save money. What barriers exist beyond financial cost?

**Worked Answer:**
**Financial barriers:**
- Upfront cost: Poor households cannot afford LEDs or improved jikos even with payback.
- Credit access: No loans available for energy investments.
- Priority: Food and school fees come before energy savings.

**Non-financial barriers:**
1. **Knowledge:** People may not know about efficient options.
2. **Availability:** Improved jikos and LEDs may not be sold locally.
3. **Habit:** Old habits are hard to change (e.g., cooking without lids).
4. **Social norms:** Using firewood may be seen as traditional and proper.
5. **Trust:** Skepticism about claimed savings.
6. **Time:** Busy households lack time to implement changes.
7. **Gender roles:** Women (who cook) may not control purchasing decisions.

**Solutions:**
- Subsidies for efficient appliances.
- Community demonstrations (seeing is believing).
- Microfinance for energy investments.
- Education campaigns in local languages.
