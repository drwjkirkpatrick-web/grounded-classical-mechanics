# Lesson 117: Teacher Demo: Burning a Groundnut to Heat Water

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Measure the energy released by burning food.
2. Calculate the energy per gram of a simple food.
3. Compare results to known food energy values.

## Materials
- Groundnuts (peanuts) or any nuts — 10 g
- A metal can or sufuria with 50 mL water
- Thermometer
- Matches or lighter
- Needle or wire for holding nut
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Calorimetry** *(kupima joto)* — measuring heat changes in chemical reactions.
- **Heat capacity** *(uwezo wa kupokea joto)* — the amount of heat needed to raise a substance's temperature.
- **Combustion** *(kuchoma)* — burning that releases chemical energy.
- **Energy content** *(kiasi cha nishati)* — energy stored per unit mass of a substance.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to the chemical energy in food when it burns?
2. Will a groundnut heat water more than a piece of paper? Why?
3. How can we measure the energy released by burning?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It converts to heat and light.
2. Yes — nuts contain more concentrated chemical energy.
3. By measuring temperature rise of water heated by the flame.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review safety rules.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Teacher holds up a groundnut. "This small nut contains enough energy to boil a few spoons of water. Let's see."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Demo:** Teacher burns a groundnut under a small can of water.
   - Measure initial water temperature.
   - Burn groundnut completely. Measure final water temperature.
   - Record: mass of nut, mass of water, temperature change.
   - **Calculation:**
     - Heat gained by water: Q = mcΔT.
     - Water: 50 g = 0.05 kg, c = 4200 J/kg°C.
     - If ΔT = 20°C: Q = 0.05 × 4200 × 20 = **4200 J**.
     - Energy per gram = 4200 J / 10 g = **420 J/g**.
   - Compare to known: groundnuts ~2400 J/g. Why different? (Heat loss to air, can, incomplete burning.)

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Calorimetry basics:** Burning releases chemical energy. Water absorbs some as heat. The rest escapes.
   - **Why the result is low:**
     - Heat escapes to surroundings.
     - Can absorbs heat.
     - Nut may not burn completely.
   - **Food energy values:**
     - Fats: ~9 kcal/g = ~38,000 J/g.
     - Carbohydrates: ~4 kcal/g = ~17,000 J/g.
     - Proteins: ~4 kcal/g.
   - **Kenyan context:** Understanding food energy helps with diet planning and avoiding malnutrition.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states why the measured energy was less than the true value. Homework: check food labels for energy values.

## Hands-On Build: Simple Calorimeter

### Parts
- Small sufuria or can — 1 — kitchen
- Water — 50 mL — tap
- Groundnut — 10 g — market
- Thermometer — 1 — school
- Needle/wire — 1 — classroom
- Matches — 1 box — classroom

### Workflow
1. Measure 50 mL water into the can.
2. Record initial water temperature.
3. Spear groundnut on needle. Weigh if possible.
4. Light groundnut. Hold under can.
5. When nut burns out, stir water and record highest temperature.
6. Calculate Q = mcΔT and energy per gram.
7. **Checkpoint:** Ensure the flame touches the can bottom. If not, heat transfer is poor.

### Common Mistakes
- **Incomplete burning:** Nut falls off or goes out. Fix: use a wire cage to hold it.
- **Heat loss:** Can is too large, water too deep. Fix: small can, shallow water.
- **Wrong calculation:** Forgetting to divide by nut mass. Fix: energy per gram = Q / m_nut.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What happens to chemical energy when food burns?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* How do we measure energy released by burning?
3. Why is the measured energy usually less than the true food energy?
4. A 5 g nut heats 50 g water from 20°C to 40°C. Calculate energy per gram.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Converts to **heat and light**.
2. By measuring **temperature rise of water**.
3. **Heat loss** to surroundings, incomplete burning.
4. Q = 0.05 × 4200 × 20 = 4200 J. Energy/g = 4200 / 5 = **840 J/g**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A calorimetry experiment gives 800 J/g for groundnuts, while the true value is 2400 J/g. Calculate the percentage error and list three sources of heat loss that explain it.

**Worked Answer:**
Percentage error = ((2400 − 800) / 2400) × 100% = (1600/2400) × 100% = **66.7%**.

**Heat loss sources:**
1. **To air:** Hot air rises around the flame, carrying heat away.
2. **To container:** Metal can absorbs significant heat.
3. **Incomplete combustion:** Nut may char rather than burn completely.
4. **Radiation:** Flame radiates heat in all directions, not just to the can.

**Improvement:** Use insulated container, smaller water volume, and wind shield.

**Q:** Design an improved calorimeter using locally available materials. Explain how each feature reduces heat loss.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design:**
- Small aluminium can (conducts heat well) suspended by wire.
- Surrounded by a larger can with insulation (dry grass, paper) between them.
- Lid with hole for thermometer to reduce evaporation.
- Groundnut held by wire mesh in the centre.

**Features:**
1. **Insulation:** Reduces conduction and convection losses.
2. **Lid:** Reduces evaporation and convection.
3. **Small water volume:** Heats faster, reducing time for heat loss.
4. **Central flame:** Maximises heat transfer to water.

Even with improvements, some heat loss is inevitable — calorimetry is approximate.

## Safety Notes
- Perform outdoors or in well-ventilated area.
- Keep flammable materials away.
- Handle burning nuts with tongs or wire, not fingers.
- Have water or sand nearby for fire safety.
- Wear eye protection if available.
