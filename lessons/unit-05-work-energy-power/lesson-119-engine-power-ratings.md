# Lesson 119: Power Ratings: Reading Machines

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Read and interpret power ratings on appliances and engines.
2. Estimate energy consumption from power ratings and usage time.
3. Compare power requirements of different devices.

## Materials
- Appliance labels (real or photocopied)
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Power rating** *(kadiri ya nguvu)* — the maximum power an appliance uses when operating.
- **Nameplate** *(kibao cha jina)* — a label showing electrical specifications.
- **Standby power** *(nguvu ya kusubiri)* — electricity used when a device is "off" but plugged in.
- **Maximum load** *(mizigo ya juu)* — the highest power the device can handle safely.

## Pre-Test
1. What does "1000 W" on a kettle mean?
2. If a 100 W bulb runs for 10 hours, how much energy does it use?
3. Why might a device have two power ratings (e.g., 1000 W / 1500 W)?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It uses 1000 joules per second when heating.
2. E = 100 W × 10 h = 1000 Wh = **1 kWh**.
3. Different settings (e.g., low/high heat).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show an appliance label. Ask, "What do these numbers mean?" Introduce power ratings.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners examine appliance labels.
   - Read: voltage (V), current (A), power (W).
   - Calculate: if power is not given, P = V × I.
   - Compare: phone charger (5 W), LED bulb (10 W), TV (50 W), iron (1000 W), kettle (2000 W).
   - Estimate: daily cost of running each for typical hours.
   - **Key discovery:** Small devices (bulbs, chargers) use little power but many add up. High-power devices (heaters, motors) use a lot.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Reading labels:**
     - Voltage: what the device needs (220–240 V in Kenya).
     - Current: how much it draws.
     - Power: V × I (for resistive devices).
   - **Energy cost:**
     - E (kWh) = P (kW) × t (hours).
     - Cost = E × price per kWh.
   - **Standby power:**
     - TVs, chargers, computers draw power even when "off."
     - A typical home loses 50–100 kWh/year to standby.
   - **Engine ratings:**
     - Boda engines: 2–5 kW.
     - Posho mills: 3–7 kW.
     - Generator: 1–10 kW (depending on size).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner reads a label and explains it. Homework: read three appliance labels at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does "1500 W" on an iron mean?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* A 60 W TV runs for 5 hours. How many kWh does it use?
3. A device is labelled "220 V, 2 A." What is its power?
4. Why do many small devices use more total energy than one large device?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. It uses **1500 joules per second** when heating.
2. E = 0.060 kW × 5 h = **0.3 kWh**.
3. P = V × I = 220 × 2 = **440 W**.
4. They run for **many more hours** in total. Ten 10 W bulbs running 5 hours = 0.5 kWh. One 1000 W iron running 10 minutes = 0.17 kWh.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A school has 20 fluorescent tubes rated at 40 W each. They run for 8 hours/day, 5 days/week. Calculate (a) weekly energy consumption in kWh, and (b) annual cost at 20 KSh/kWh. Propose a switch to LED tubes at 18 W each costing 300 KSh each, and calculate the payback period.

**Worked Answer:**
**(a) Weekly energy:**
Total power = 20 × 40 = 800 W = 0.8 kW.
Daily energy = 0.8 × 8 = 6.4 kWh.
Weekly energy = 6.4 × 5 = **32 kWh**.

**(b) Annual cost:**
Annual energy = 32 × 52 = 1664 kWh.
Cost = 1664 × 20 = **33,280 KSh/year**.

**LED proposal:**
New power = 20 × 18 = 360 W = 0.36 kW.
New weekly energy = 0.36 × 8 × 5 = 14.4 kWh.
New annual energy = 14.4 × 52 = 748.8 kWh.
New annual cost = 748.8 × 20 = **14,976 KSh/year**.

**Savings:**
33,280 − 14,976 = **18,304 KSh/year**.

**Investment:**
20 × 300 = **6,000 KSh**.

**Payback:**
6,000 / 18,304 = **0.33 years** = **4 months**.

After payback, the school saves 18,304 KSh/year. LEDs also last longer (reducing replacement costs).

**Q:** Explain why standby power is a significant energy issue, and calculate the annual cost of a TV drawing 5 W in standby for 20 hours/day at 20 KSh/kWh.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why significant:**
- Most homes have 10+ devices on standby (TV, decoder, phone chargers, computers).
- Each uses 1–10 W continuously.
- Total: 50–100 W per home × 20 hours/day = 1–2 kWh/day.
- National scale: millions of homes = hundreds of megawatt-hours wasted daily.

**Annual cost:**
Daily energy = 0.005 kW × 20 h = 0.1 kWh.
Annual energy = 0.1 × 365 = **36.5 kWh**.
Annual cost = 36.5 × 20 = **730 KSh**.

For one TV, this seems small. But for a home with 10 devices: **7300 KSh/year** — enough to buy food for a month.

**Solution:** Unplug devices or use power strips with switches.
