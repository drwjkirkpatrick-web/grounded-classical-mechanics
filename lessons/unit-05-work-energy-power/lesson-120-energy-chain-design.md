# Lesson 120: Project: Rube Goldberg Energy Chain I — Design

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** rube-goldberg

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a multi-step energy transformation chain using simple machines.
2. Identify at least five energy transformations in their design.
3. Create a labelled diagram of their planned machine.

## Materials
- Paper and pencils
- Examples of Rube Goldberg machines (videos or diagrams)
- Cardboard, bottles, string, marbles (for planning)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Rube Goldberg machine** *(mashine ya Rube Goldberg)* — a deliberately over-complicated device that performs a simple task through a chain of reactions.
- **Energy chain** *(mnyororo wa nishati)* — a series of connected energy transformations.
- **Trigger** *(kichocheo)* — the initial event that starts the chain.
- **Final action** *(hatua ya mwisho)* — the simple task accomplished by the machine.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a Rube Goldberg machine?
2. Name three energy forms that could appear in an energy chain.
3. Why make a simple task complicated?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A complex chain of simple devices that performs a simple task.
2. Any three: potential, kinetic, elastic, chemical, heat, sound.
3. To demonstrate physics principles and creativity.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Show a short video of a Rube Goldberg machine.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "What simple task could we perform?" (e.g., knock over a cup, turn on a light, ring a bell.) Introduce the design challenge.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design their energy chain.
   - **Task:** Pop a balloon (or similar simple task).
   - **Requirements:**
     - At least 5 energy transformations.
     - Uses at least 3 different simple machines.
     - Starts with a single trigger.
   - **Brainstorming:**
     - Step 1: Roll a marble down a ramp (PE → KE).
     - Step 2: Marble hits a domino (KE → KE).
     - Step 3: Dominoes fall, last one hits a lever (KE → elastic PE → KE).
     - Step 4: Lever releases a toy car (elastic PE → KE).
     - Step 5: Car hits a needle that pops a balloon (KE → work → sound).
   - Groups draw labelled diagrams.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Design principles:**
     - Each step must reliably trigger the next.
     - Energy must decrease at each step (inefficiency).
     - Start with plenty of energy (high ramp, stretched spring).
   - **Common failures:**
     - Misaligned components.
     - Too much friction.
     - Insufficient initial energy.
   - **Physics connections:**
     - PE → KE conversions.
     - Simple machines (levers, ramps, pulleys).
     - Conservation of energy (total energy decreases as heat).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present their design sketches. Homework: gather materials for build day.

## Hands-On Build: Design Sketch

### Parts
- Paper — several sheets — classroom
- Pencils — 1 per learner — classroom
- Rulers — 1 per group — classroom
- Optional: coloured pencils

### Workflow
1. Agree on the final simple task.
2. Brainstorm at least 8 possible steps.
3. Select the best 5–6 steps.
4. Draw a side-view diagram showing each step.
5. Label each step with the energy transformation.
6. Identify materials needed for each component.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before finalising, trace the energy chain. Ensure each step has enough energy to trigger the next.

### Common Mistakes
- **Too ambitious:** 20 steps with unreliable triggers. Fix: keep it simple and testable.
- **No energy analysis:** Forgetting to label transformations. Fix: include physics labels.
- **Vague diagrams:** Others cannot understand the design. Fix: draw clearly with dimensions.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is a Rube Goldberg machine?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Name three energy forms in an energy chain.
3. Why must the first step have plenty of energy?
4. List three simple machines you could include.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. A **complex chain of simple devices** performing a simple task.
2. Any three of: potential, kinetic, elastic, chemical, heat, sound, light.
3. Energy **decreases at each step** due to inefficiency. The initial energy must be enough to reach the end.
4. Any three of: lever, inclined plane, pulley, wheel and axle, wedge, screw.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a Rube Goldberg machine with at least 8 steps that includes at least 4 different energy transformations and 3 simple machines. Draw a labelled diagram and explain the energy flow at each step.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design: Water a plant**

1. **Trigger:** Burn a string (chemical → heat).
2. String burns through, releasing a weight (elastic PE → KE).
3. Weight falls, pulling a string over a pulley (PE → KE).
4. String pulls a toy car down a ramp (PE → KE).
5. Car hits a marble into a curved track (KE → KE).
6. Marble falls into a cup on a lever (KE → PE → KE).
7. Lever tips, pouring water into a pipe (PE → KE).
8. Water flows to the plant (KE → PE of water in soil).

**Energy flow:**
- Start: chemical (burning string).
- Intermediate: elastic PE, gravitational PE, KE.
- End: PE of water in soil.
- Lost at each step: heat, sound, friction.

**Q:** A marble rolls down a 0.5 m ramp. Calculate its speed at the bottom (ignoring friction). If it loses 30% of its KE hitting a domino, what speed does the domino have if the marble's mass is 0.01 kg and the domino's mass is 0.05 kg?

**Worked Answer:**
**Marble speed at bottom:**
PE = mgh = 0.01 × 10 × 0.5 = 0.05 J.
KE = 0.05 J → ½mv² = 0.05 → v² = 10 → v = **3.16 m/s**.

**After 30% loss:**
Remaining KE = 0.05 × 0.70 = 0.035 J.

**Domino speed:**
Assuming all remaining KE transfers to domino:
KE_domino = 0.035 J.
½ × 0.05 × v² = 0.035 → v² = 1.4 → v = **1.18 m/s**.

In reality, energy transfer is inefficient. The marble may bounce, and the domino may not move at all if the hit is glancing. This is why Rube Goldberg machines need careful calibration.
