# Lesson 121: Project: Rube Goldberg Energy Chain II — Build and Run

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** rube-goldberg

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Assemble a multi-step energy chain from their design.
2. Test, troubleshoot, and refine their machine.
3. Analyse why each step succeeded or failed.

## Materials
- All materials from Lesson 120 design
- Cardboard, bottles, string, marbles, dominoes, tape
- Scissors
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Assembly** *(kujenga)* — putting parts together.
- **Troubleshoot** *(kutatua shida)* — identifying and fixing problems.
- **Iterate** *(kurekebisha)* — repeating a process with improvements.
- **Calibration** *(kurekebisha)* — adjusting components for reliable operation.

## Pre-Test
1. What should you do if your machine fails at step 3?
2. Why does a marble need a smooth track?
3. How can you increase the energy of the first step?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Check alignment, energy, and friction at steps 2 and 3.
2. Rough surfaces cause friction, which reduces kinetic energy.
3. Increase the ramp height or use a heavier marble.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and review designs from Lesson 120. Groups collect materials.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Groups set up their first three components. Test before building the full chain.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Groups build and test their machines.
   - Build step 1 → test → fix.
   - Add step 2 → test steps 1–2 → fix.
   - Continue until all steps work reliably.
   - **Key discovery:** Building is harder than designing. Small alignment errors cause failures.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Common problems:**
     - Misalignment: components don't line up.
     - Energy loss: friction absorbs too much KE.
     - Timing: steps happen too fast or slow.
   - **Fixes:**
     - Use guides (channels, walls) to keep marbles on track.
     - Reduce friction (smooth surfaces, lubrication).
     - Adjust heights and angles.
   - **Physics:** Each step loses energy. The chain must have enough initial energy.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups demonstrate working machines. Class votes on most reliable and most creative. Homework: write a reflection on the build process.

## Hands-On Build: Rube Goldberg Machine

### Parts
- Cardboard — several pieces — classroom
- Plastic bottles — 3–4 — collected
- String — 2 m — classroom
- Marbles or stones — 3–4 — ground
- Dominoes or blocks — 10 — classroom
- Tape — 1 roll — classroom
- Scissors — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Set up the first component (e.g., ramp) securely.
2. Test the first step 5 times. Must work reliably.
3. Add the second component. Test steps 1–2.
4. Continue adding one component at a time, testing after each addition.
5. When complete, run the full chain 3 times.
6. Record: number of successes, where failures occurred, improvements made.
7. **Checkpoint:** After each addition, test before proceeding. Do not build the whole machine before testing.

### Common Mistakes
- **Building all at once:** Errors compound. Fix: build and test incrementally.
- **Ignoring friction:** Marbles stop on rough cardboard. Fix: use smooth tracks or wax.
- **Unstable components:** Ramp falls over. Fix: tape securely, use supports.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Your machine fails at step 3. What do you check?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why does a marble need a smooth track?
3. List two ways to increase initial energy.
4. Why build one step at a time?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Alignment, energy, friction at steps 2–3.
2. **Friction** slows the marble.
3. Increase ramp height, heavier marble, steeper angle.
4. Errors are easier to find and fix one at a time.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your marble loses 20% of its KE at each collision. Starting with 0.1 J, calculate the KE after 3 collisions. Will it reach step 4 if each step needs 0.04 J?

**Worked Answer:**
After 1st collision: 0.1 × 0.80 = **0.08 J**.
After 2nd: 0.08 × 0.80 = **0.064 J**.
After 3rd: 0.064 × 0.80 = **0.0512 J**.

Step 4 needs 0.04 J. Since 0.0512 J > 0.04 J, the marble **reaches step 4**.

After step 4 (4th collision): 0.0512 × 0.80 = **0.0410 J**.
Step 5 needs 0.04 J. Barely reaches!

After step 5 (5th collision): 0.0410 × 0.80 = **0.0328 J**.
Step 6 needs 0.04 J. **Fails at step 6**.

Maximum reliable chain: **5 steps** with these parameters.

**Q:** Explain how friction affects the reliability of a Rube Goldberg machine, and suggest three ways to reduce it using locally available materials.

**Worked Answer:**
**Effect of friction:**
Friction converts KE to heat at every contact point. After several steps, the remaining KE may be insufficient to trigger the next step. Friction also makes motion unpredictable — a marble may stop in one trial and roll in another.

**Ways to reduce friction:**
1. **Smooth tracks:** Use wax paper, plastic sheets, or polished cardboard instead of rough paper.
2. **Lubrication:** Apply cooking oil or soap to axles and contact points.
3. **Rolling elements:** Use marbles or ball bearings instead of sliding blocks. Rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction.
4. **Reduce contact area:** Use narrow tracks so the marble touches only a small area.

Even with these measures, some friction is unavoidable. Good designs have plenty of initial energy to compensate.
