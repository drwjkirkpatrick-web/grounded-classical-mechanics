# Lesson 122: Renewable vs Non-Renewable: The Charcoal Debate

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define renewable and non-renewable energy sources.
2. Debate the advantages and disadvantages of charcoal as a fuel.
3. Propose balanced energy policies for rural Kenya.

## Materials
- Debate format guidelines
- Data on charcoal production and use
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Renewable** *(inayojizatiti)* — energy from sources that replenish naturally.
- **Non-renewable** *(isiyojizatiti)* — energy from finite sources.
- **Sustainable** *(endelevu)* — meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
- **Deforestation** *(kukata miti)* — clearing forests faster than they regrow.

## Pre-Test
1. Is charcoal renewable or non-renewable?
2. What are two problems with charcoal use?
3. Name two alternatives to charcoal for cooking.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Non-renewable in practice (trees take decades to regrow).
2. Deforestation, indoor air pollution, soil erosion.
3. Firewood (also problematic), LPG, biogas, electricity, improved jikos.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Divide class into two teams: "Pro-Charcoal" and "Anti-Charcoal."

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Announce a debate: "Should Kenya ban charcoal production?" Each team prepares arguments.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Teams research and prepare arguments.
   - **Pro-Charcoal arguments:**
     - Cheap and accessible for poor households.
     - Supports livelihoods (charcoal makers, sellers).
     - Tradition and cultural preference.
     - No immediate alternative for many.
   - **Anti-Charcoal arguments:**
     - Deforestation (Kenya lost ~5% forest cover in recent decades).
     - Indoor air pollution (respiratory disease, especially for women and children).
     - Soil erosion and climate change.
     - Inefficient (open fires waste 85% of energy).
   - **Key discovery:** Both sides have valid points. The issue is complex.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **The dilemma:**
     - Banning charcoal hurts the poor immediately.
     - Continuing charcoal use hurts the environment long-term.
   - **Balanced solutions:**
     - Improved jikos (use 50–70% less charcoal).
     - Sustainable charcoal (from fast-growing trees, plantations).
     - Alternatives: LPG subsidies, biogas from manure, solar cookers.
     - Reforestation programs.
   - **Kenyan context:**
     - ~80% of urban households use charcoal.
     - Charcoal contributes to deforestation in arid and semi-arid lands.
     - Kenya has banned charcoal exports but domestic use continues.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Class votes on the most balanced proposal. Homework: write a letter to a county official proposing one policy.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Is charcoal renewable?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Name two problems with charcoal.
3. Suggest one way to reduce charcoal use without banning it.
4. Why might a ban on charcoal hurt poor households?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Non-renewable** in practice.
2. Any two: deforestation, air pollution, soil erosion, inefficiency.
3. Promote improved jikos, LPG subsidies, biogas, sustainable plantations.
4. Charcoal is **cheap and accessible**. Poor households may not afford alternatives.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A family uses 2 bags of charcoal per month at 800 KSh/bag. An improved jiko reduces charcoal use by 60% and costs 1500 KSh. Calculate (a) monthly savings with the jiko, (b) the payback period, and (c) annual charcoal reduction in kg if one bag = 30 kg.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Current cost: 2 × 800 = 1600 KSh/month.
    With jiko: 1600 × 0.40 = 640 KSh/month.
    Savings = 1600 − 640 = **960 KSh/month**.

(b) Payback = 1500 / 960 = **1.56 months**.

(c) Annual charcoal: 2 bags × 12 months = 24 bags = 720 kg.
    Reduction: 720 × 0.60 = **432 kg/year**.

This single household saves 432 kg of charcoal annually — equivalent to several trees.

**Q:** Write a balanced policy brief for a Kenyan county on charcoal use. Include environmental, economic, health, and social considerations.

**Worked Answer:**
**Policy Brief: Sustainable Charcoal Use in [County]**

**Problem:** Charcoal provides affordable cooking energy but drives deforestation and causes respiratory illness.

**Environmental:**
- Deforestation reduces rainfall, increases erosion, and releases carbon.
- Solution: Reforestation with fast-growing species; certified sustainable charcoal.

**Economic:**
- Charcoal supports thousands of jobs (producers, transporters, sellers).
- Solution: Retrain workers for alternative fuels; subsidise LPG and biogas.

**Health:**
- Indoor air pollution from charcoal causes respiratory disease.
- Solution: Promote improved jikos (reduce smoke); encourage outdoor cooking where possible.

**Social:**
- Charcoal is culturally embedded and affordable.
- Solution: Phase transitions gradually; do not ban abruptly.

**Recommendations:**
1. Subsidise improved jikos (50% cost coverage).
2. Establish community woodlots for sustainable charcoal.
3. Pilot biogas digesters at schools and households.
4. Ban charcoal from unsustainable sources (enforce existing laws).
5. Public health campaign on indoor air pollution.
