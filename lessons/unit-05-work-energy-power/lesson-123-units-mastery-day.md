# Lesson 123: Units Mastery Day: Joules, Watts, Newtons

**Unit:** 5 — Work, Energy & Power | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Distinguish between joules, watts, and newtons.
2. Convert between related units correctly.
3. Solve mixed-unit problems involving work, energy, and power.

## Materials
- Practice worksheets
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Joule** *(joule)* — unit of energy and work.
- **Watt** *(wati)* — unit of power; joules per second.
- **Newton** *(nyuton)* — unit of force.
- **Dimensional analysis** *(kuchambua vipimo)* — checking that units match in equations.

## Pre-Test
1. What unit measures work?
2. What unit measures power?
3. What unit measures force?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Joule (J).
2. Watt (W).
3. Newton (N).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Write on board: "100 W bulb burns for 5 hours. How many joules?" (Answer: 100 × 5 × 3600 = 1,800,000 J.) Stress unit conversion.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners solve conversion problems.
   - Convert: 2 kWh to joules. (2 × 3,600,000 = 7,200,000 J.)
   - Convert: 500 W to kW. (0.5 kW.)
   - Convert: 10 N × 5 m to joules. (50 J.)
   - Convert: 2000 J in 4 s to watts. (500 W.)
   - **Key discovery:** Units must match before calculating. Watts use seconds; kilowatt-hours use hours.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Relationships:**
     - Work (J) = Force (N) × Distance (m).
     - Power (W) = Work (J) / Time (s).
     - Energy (J) = Power (W) × Time (s).
   - **Dimensional analysis:**
     - Check: N × m = kg·m/s² × m = kg·m²/s² = J. ✓
     - Check: J/s = kg·m²/s³ = W. ✓
   - **Common errors:**
     - Using hours instead of seconds in power calculations.
     - Confusing kW (power) with kWh (energy).
     - Forgetting that 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner summarises the three units and their relationships. Homework: convert your daily energy use to joules.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What unit measures work?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What unit measures power?
3. Convert 5 kWh to joules.
4. A 200 W motor runs for 30 s. How much work does it do?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Joule (J)**.
2. **Watt (W)**.
3. 5 × 3,600,000 = **18,000,000 J** (18 MJ).
4. W = P × t = 200 × 30 = **6,000 J**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A household uses 15 kWh of electricity per day. Calculate (a) the average power in watts, and (b) the total energy in megajoules. If this costs 20 KSh/kWh, calculate the monthly bill.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Average power = 15 kWh / 24 h = 0.625 kW = **625 W**.

(b) Total energy = 15 × 3.6 = **54 MJ/day**.
    Monthly = 54 × 30 = **1,620 MJ/month**.

Monthly bill = 15 × 30 × 20 = **9,000 KSh**.

**Q:** Show by dimensional analysis that power = force × velocity. Use this to calculate the power of a 500 N boda engine moving at 15 m/s.

**Worked Answer:**
**Dimensional analysis:**
Force = [M][L]/[T]².
Velocity = [L]/[T].
Force × velocity = [M][L]²/[T]³.
Power = [M][L]²/[T]³. ✓

**Calculation:**
P = F × v = 500 × 15 = **7,500 W** = **7.5 kW**.

This is a typical boda-boda engine power output.
