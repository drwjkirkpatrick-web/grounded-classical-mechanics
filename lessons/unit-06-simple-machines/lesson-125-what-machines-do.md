# Lesson 125: What Machines Do for Us

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define a machine as any device that makes work easier.
2. Classify machines by what they change: force, distance, or direction.
3. Explain that machines cannot reduce total work.

## Materials
- Jembe
- Mkokoteni or wheelbarrow
- Bottle opener
- Scissors
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Machine** *(mashine)* — a device that makes work easier by changing force, distance, or direction.
- **Force multiplier** *(kuzidisha nguvu)* — a machine that increases force at the expense of distance.
- **Distance multiplier** *(kuzidisha umbali)* — a machine that increases distance moved at the expense of force.
- **Direction changer** *(mabadiliko wa mwelekeo)* — a machine that changes the direction of force without changing magnitude.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a machine do?
2. Does a machine reduce the total amount of work?
3. Name three machines you used today.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Makes work easier by changing force, distance, or direction.
2. No — total work stays the same (ignoring friction).
3. Any three: scissors, door handle, wheelbarrow, jembe, bottle opener, stairs.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "What machines did you use this morning?" (Door, stairs, cup, toothbrush.) Explain that simple tools are machines too.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners survey machines around the school compound.
   - Identify at least 10 machines.
   - Classify each:
     - Force multiplier: jembe, crowbar, bottle opener.
     - Distance multiplier: fishing rod, tweezers.
     - Direction changer: pulley, flagpole rope.
   - **Key discovery:** Machines change how work is done, not how much work is done.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Definition:** A machine is any device that makes work easier. This includes levers, pulleys, inclined planes, screws, wedges, and wheels.
   - **What machines change:**
     - **Force:** A small force over a long distance becomes a large force over a short distance (crowbar lifting a rock).
     - **Distance:** A large force over a short distance becomes a small force over a long distance (fishing rod).
     - **Direction:** Pulling down to lift up (flagpole pulley).
   - **What machines cannot do:**
     - Reduce total work. W = F × d. If F decreases, d must increase proportionally.
     - Create energy. Machines only transfer or transform energy.
   - **Why use machines:** Comfort, speed, precision, safety.
   - Kenyan context: A mkokoteni multiplies force (small effort moves heavy load). A jembe's long handle multiplies the force on the blade.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner defines a machine and gives an example. Homework: list 10 machines at home and classify them.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does a machine do?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Does a machine reduce total work?
3. A crowbar lifts a heavy rock with a small push. What does the crowbar multiply?
4. A flagpole pulley lets you pull down to raise a flag. What does the pulley change?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Makes work easier by changing **force, distance, or direction**.
2. **No** — total work is conserved (ignoring friction).
3. It multiplies **force** (small force over long distance → large force over short distance).
4. It changes **direction**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain why machines cannot reduce total work, using the principle of conservation of energy. Show that if mechanical advantage > 1, then velocity ratio < 1.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why machines cannot reduce work:**
Work = force × distance. A machine that outputs a larger force must do so over a shorter distance. The product F × d stays the same (ignoring friction).

By conservation of energy: energy input = energy output + losses.
A machine cannot create energy, so it cannot output more work than is input.

**MA > 1 means VR < 1:**
Mechanical advantage (MA) = output force / input force.
If MA > 1, output force > input force.

Since work is conserved: F_in × d_in = F_out × d_out (ideal case).
Therefore: d_out / d_in = F_in / F_out = 1/MA.

If MA > 1, then d_out / d_in < 1 → the output moves less distance than the input.
Velocity ratio (VR) = d_in / d_out (or related ratio).
If output distance is smaller, VR > 1 in one definition, but the key point is the inverse relationship: gain in force means loss in distance.

**Example:**
Crowbar: MA = 4 (output force is 4× input).
Then output distance = input distance / 4.
You push down 20 cm, rock rises 5 cm.
Work in = Work out (ideally): F_in × 20 = (4F_in) × 5 → balanced.

**Q:** A lever has MA = 5. If the input moves 30 cm, how far does the output move? Calculate the work done if input force is 20 N and output force is 100 N.

**Worked Answer:**
Since MA = F_out / F_in = 5, and work is conserved:
F_in × d_in = F_out × d_out
20 × 0.30 = 100 × d_out
d_out = 6 / 100 = **0.06 m** = **6 cm**.

Work = F_in × d_in = 20 × 0.30 = **6 J**.
Or Work = F_out × d_out = 100 × 0.06 = **6 J**.

Both give the same result, confirming conservation.
