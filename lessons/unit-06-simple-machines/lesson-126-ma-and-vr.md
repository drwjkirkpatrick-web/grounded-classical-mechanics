# Lesson 126: Mechanical Advantage and Velocity Ratio

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define mechanical advantage (MA) as the ratio of output force to input force.
2. Define velocity ratio (VR) as the ratio of input distance to output distance.
3. Calculate MA and VR for simple machines.

## Materials
- Ruler
- Jembe or crowbar
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Mechanical advantage** *(faida ya kimekaniki)* — the ratio of output force to input force.
- **Velocity ratio** *(uwiano wa kasi)* — the ratio of distance moved by effort to distance moved by load.
- **Effort** *(juhudi)* — the input force applied to a machine.
- **Load** *(mizigo)* — the output force or resistance overcome by a machine.

## Pre-Test
1. What does mechanical advantage measure?
2. If you push 2 m and the load rises 0.5 m, what is the velocity ratio?
3. A machine has MA = 4. What does this mean?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. How much a machine multiplies force.
2. VR = 2 / 0.5 = 4.
3. The output force is 4 times the input force.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a jembe. "The long handle lets you push harder on the soil. How much harder?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure MA and VR for simple machines.
   - **Crowbar:** Measure effort arm and load arm lengths. MA ≈ effort arm / load arm.
   - **Jembe:** Measure handle length and blade-to-hand distance.
   - **Inclined plane:** Measure slope length and height. VR = slope length / height.
   - **Key discovery:** MA and VR are related. In an ideal machine (no friction), MA = VR.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formulas:**
     - MA = Load / Effort = F_out / F_in.
     - VR = Distance of effort / Distance of load = d_in / d_out.
   - **Ideal machine:** MA = VR (100% efficient).
   - **Real machine:** MA < VR due to friction.
   - **Efficiency:** η = (MA / VR) × 100%.
   - **Example:**
     - A lever with effort arm 60 cm, load arm 20 cm.
     - VR = 60/20 = 3.
     - If MA = 2.5, efficiency = (2.5/3) × 100% = 83.3%.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains MA and VR with an example. Homework: measure the VR of a door handle.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does MA measure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Effort moves 3 m, load moves 1 m. What is VR?
3. A machine has VR = 5 and MA = 4. Calculate efficiency.
4. Why is MA usually less than VR in real machines?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. How much a machine **multiplies force**.
2. **3**.
3. Efficiency = (4/5) × 100% = **80%**.
4. **Friction** reduces the output force.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A lever has an effort arm of 80 cm and a load arm of 20 cm. Calculate (a) VR, (b) ideal MA, (c) actual MA if efficiency is 75%, and (d) the effort needed to lift a 600 N load.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) VR = effort arm / load arm = 80/20 = **4**.

(b) Ideal MA = VR = **4**.

(c) Actual MA = ideal MA × efficiency = 4 × 0.75 = **3**.

(d) MA = Load / Effort → Effort = Load / MA = 600 / 3 = **200 N**.

**Q:** Explain why a machine with MA < 1 is still useful, using a fishing rod as an example.

**Worked Answer:**
A fishing rod has MA < 1 because the hand moves a short distance while the tip moves a long distance.

**Why it is useful:**
1. **Speed:** The tip moves fast, allowing quick casting.
2. **Distance:** A small hand movement creates a large tip movement.
3. **Shock absorption:** The rod bends, cushioning the fish's pull.
4. **Precision:** The angler can make small, controlled movements.

The fishing rod multiplies distance, not force. It is a **distance multiplier**, not a force multiplier.
