# Lesson 127: Levers Class 1: Seesaws, Crowbars and Pliers

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify class 1 levers as levers where the fulcrum is between effort and load.
2. Calculate MA for class 1 levers from arm lengths.
3. Give examples of class 1 levers from Kenyan daily life.

## Materials
- Seesaw or ruler balanced on a fulcrum
- Crowbar or jembe
- Plier joints (pliers or scissors)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Class 1 lever** *(ubao wa darasa 1)* — a lever with the fulcrum between effort and load.
- **Fulcrum** *(kitovu)* — the pivot point of a lever.
- **Effort arm** *(mkono wa juhudi)* — the distance from fulcrum to effort.
- **Load arm** *(mkono wa mizigo)* — the distance from fulcrum to load.

## Pre-Test
1. Where is the fulcrum in a class 1 lever?
2. Can a class 1 lever multiply force?
3. Name two class 1 levers you have used.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Between effort and load.
2. Yes — if effort arm > load arm.
3. Seesaw, crowbar, pliers, scissors.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Demonstrate a seesaw. Ask, "Where is the middle? What happens if one person sits farther out?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners balance a ruler on a fulcrum (pencil).
   - Place a coin (load) at 10 cm from fulcrum.
   - Find where to place another coin (effort) to balance.
   - Test with different loads and distances.
   - **Key discovery:** Load × distance = Effort × distance. This is the law of moments.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Class 1 lever:** Fulcrum in the middle. Effort on one side, load on the other.
   - **MA calculation:** MA = effort arm / load arm.
   - **Examples:**
     - Crowbar: fulcrum near load, effort far away → MA > 1.
     - Seesaw: fulcrum in centre → MA = 1 (balanced).
     - Scissors: fulcrum at pivot, blades short → MA < 1 (but blades move fast).
   - **When MA > 1:** Effort arm > load arm → force multiplied.
   - **When MA < 1:** Effort arm < load arm → distance multiplied.
   - Kenyan context: Jembe handle as lever, crowbar lifting rocks, pliers cracking nuts.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner identifies a class 1 lever and calculates its MA. Homework: find three class 1 levers at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Where is the fulcrum in a class 1 lever?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Can a class 1 lever multiply force?
3. A crowbar has effort arm 1.2 m and load arm 0.3 m. Calculate MA.
4. Why are scissors class 1 levers even though they don't multiply force?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Between effort and load**.
2. **Yes** — when effort arm > load arm.
3. MA = 1.2 / 0.3 = **4**.
4. Scissors multiply **distance** (blades move farther than handles), enabling fast cutting.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A crowbar of total length 1.5 m is used to lift a 500 N rock. The fulcrum is placed 0.3 m from the rock. Calculate (a) the effort arm, (b) MA, (c) the effort required, and (d) the distance the rock rises if the effort moves down 0.6 m.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Effort arm = 1.5 − 0.3 = **1.2 m**.

(b) MA = 1.2 / 0.3 = **4**.

(c) Effort = Load / MA = 500 / 4 = **125 N**.

(d) VR = MA = 4 (ideal).
    d_out = d_in / VR = 0.6 / 4 = **0.15 m**.

**Q:** Explain why a seesaw balances when two children of different weights sit at different distances from the centre. A 30 kg child sits 2 m from the fulcrum. Where should a 40 kg child sit to balance?

**Worked Answer:**
**Why it balances:**
For equilibrium: clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment.
Weight₁ × distance₁ = Weight₂ × distance₂.

If the lighter child sits farther out, their smaller weight is compensated by greater distance.

**Calculation:**
30 kg × 2 m = 40 kg × d₂
d₂ = 60 / 40 = **1.5 m**.

The heavier child sits closer to the centre (1.5 m vs 2 m) to balance the lighter child.
