# Lesson 128: Levers Class 2: Wheelbarrows and Bottle Openers

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify class 2 levers as levers where the load is between fulcrum and effort.
2. Calculate MA for class 2 levers.
3. Give examples of class 2 levers from Kenyan daily life.

## Materials
- Wheelbarrow or mkokoteni
- Bottle opener
- Nutcracker or pliers
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Class 2 lever** *(ubao wa darasa 2)* — a lever with the load between fulcrum and effort.
- **Wheelbarrow** *(mkokoteni)* — a class 2 lever used to carry heavy loads.
- **Nutcracker** *(kibanzi)* — a tool that multiplies force to crack nuts.
- **Door stop** *(kizuizi)* — a class 2 lever that holds doors open.

## Pre-Test
1. Where is the load in a class 2 lever?
2. Is MA always greater than 1 for class 2 levers?
3. Name two class 2 levers.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Between fulcrum and effort.
2. Yes — effort arm is always longer than load arm.
3. Wheelbarrow, bottle opener, nutcracker, door stop.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a wheelbarrow. "The wheel is the fulcrum. Where is the load? Where do you push?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse class 2 levers.
   - **Wheelbarrow:** Wheel = fulcrum. Load in middle. Handles = effort.
   - Measure: distance from wheel to load vs wheel to handles.
   - **Bottle opener:** End on bottle = fulcrum. Cap = load. Hand = effort.
   - **Key discovery:** Class 2 levers always have MA > 1. They multiply force.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Class 2 lever:** Load between fulcrum and effort. Effort arm > load arm always.
   - **MA > 1 always:**
     - Wheelbarrow: MA = 2–3. A 200 N load feels like 70 N.
     - Bottle opener: MA = 5–10. Easy to open bottles.
     - Nutcracker: MA = 3–5. Cracks hard shells.
   - **Advantage:** Always multiplies force. Never multiplies distance.
   - **Disadvantage:** Effort moves farther than load.
   - Kenyan context: Mkokoteni carries heavy loads with less effort. Maize shellers use class 2 levers.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner identifies a class 2 lever and explains why MA > 1. Homework: find two class 2 levers at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Where is the load in a class 2 lever?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Is MA always > 1 for class 2 levers?
3. A wheelbarrow has effort arm 1.5 m and load arm 0.5 m. Calculate MA.
4. Why can't a class 2 lever multiply distance?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Between fulcrum and effort**.
2. **Yes** — effort arm is always longer.
3. MA = 1.5 / 0.5 = **3**.
4. Because effort arm > load arm always. To multiply distance, effort arm would need to be shorter.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A wheelbarrow has handles 1.6 m from the wheel and the tray 0.4 m from the wheel. Calculate (a) MA, (b) the effort to lift a 300 N load, and (c) the distance the handles move if the tray rises 0.2 m.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) MA = 1.6 / 0.4 = **4**.

(b) Effort = 300 / 4 = **75 N**.

(c) VR = MA = 4. d_in = VR × d_out = 4 × 0.2 = **0.8 m**.

**Q:** Compare class 1 and class 2 levers. Under what circumstances would you choose each?

**Worked Answer:**
**Class 1:** Fulcrum in middle. MA can be > 1, = 1, or < 1.
- Use when: you need flexibility (e.g., crowbar for different-sized rocks, seesaw for different-sized children).

**Class 2:** Load in middle. MA is always > 1.
- Use when: you need force multiplication (e.g., carrying heavy loads, opening bottles, cracking nuts).

**Class 1 advantage:** Flexible.
**Class 2 advantage:** Always multiplies force.
