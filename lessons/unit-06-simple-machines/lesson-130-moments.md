# Lesson 130: Moments: The Turning Force

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define a moment as force multiplied by perpendicular distance.
2. Calculate moments in simple situations.
3. Explain why a door handle is placed far from the hinge.

## Materials
- Door or gate
- Ruler balanced on a fulcrum
- Coins or stones as weights
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Moment** *(siku)* — the turning effect of a force; force × perpendicular distance.
- **Clockwise** *(kuelekea saa)* — turning in the direction a clock hand moves.
- **Anticlockwise** *(kinyume cha saa)* — turning opposite to a clock hand.
- **Perpendicular distance** *(umbali wa mlalo)* — the shortest distance from the pivot to the line of force.

## Pre-Test
1. What two quantities multiply to give a moment?
2. Why is a door handle far from the hinge?
3. A 10 N force is applied 0.5 m from a pivot. Calculate the moment.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Force and perpendicular distance.
2. Greater distance = greater moment = easier to turn.
3. Moment = 10 × 0.5 = **5 Nm**.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Push a door near the hinge, then near the handle. Ask, "Which is easier? Why?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners balance a ruler on a fulcrum.
   - Place coins at different distances.
   - Find combinations that balance.
   - Record: force (number of coins), distance, product (moment).
   - **Key discovery:** Balanced when clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formula:** Moment = F × d (force × perpendicular distance from pivot).
   - **Units:** Newton-metres (Nm).
   - **Door handle:** Placed far from hinge for large moment with small force.
   - **Examples:**
     - Opening a gate: Push far from hinge.
     - Using a spanner: Long handle = large moment.
     - Tightening a nut: Small spanner needs more force.
   - **Equilibrium:** When clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment, there is no rotation.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why a spanner has a long handle. Homework: find three examples of moments at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What two quantities give a moment?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why is a door handle far from the hinge?
3. Calculate the moment of a 20 N force 0.3 m from a pivot.
4. A 30 N force produces a moment of 12 Nm. How far from the pivot is it applied?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Force** and **perpendicular distance**.
2. Greater distance = **greater moment** = easier to turn.
3. Moment = 20 × 0.3 = **6 Nm**.
4. d = 12 / 30 = **0.4 m**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 40 cm spanner is used to tighten a nut. A force of 150 N is applied at the end. Calculate the moment. If the same nut needs 30 Nm to loosen, what minimum force is needed with a 25 cm spanner?

**Worked Answer:**
Moment = 150 × 0.40 = **60 Nm**.

For 25 cm spanner:
F = 30 / 0.25 = **120 N**.

The shorter spanner needs 120 N vs 150 N for the longer one (but the longer one produces more moment for the same force).

**Q:** Explain why a heavy gate is easier to open if the handle is placed at the edge rather than the middle, using the concept of moment.

**Worked Answer:**
Moment = F × d.

For a given force F, placing the handle at the edge maximises d (distance from hinge), maximising the moment.

If the handle is at the middle: d = half the gate width → moment is halved.
To achieve the same moment, twice the force is needed.

Heavy gates resist turning due to their weight acting through the centre of gravity. A larger moment from the handle overcomes this resistance more easily.
