# Lesson 131: Law of Moments Lab: Weighing Without a Scale

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Verify the law of moments experimentally.
2. Use a lever to measure unknown masses.
3. Calculate mass from moment balance.

## Materials
- Ruler or uniform stick
- Fulcrum (pencil or stone)
- Known masses (coins or stones)
- Unknown objects to weigh
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Law of moments** *(sheria ya siku)* — for equilibrium, sum of clockwise moments equals sum of anticlockwise moments.
- **Unknown mass** *(mzito usiojulikana)* — a mass to be determined by balancing.
- **Known mass** *(mzito ujulikanavyo)* — a mass of known value used for comparison.
- **Balance point** *(kiwango sawa)* — the position where moments are equal.

## Pre-Test
1. What does the law of moments state?
2. How can you weigh an object without a scale?
3. A 50 g mass balances 0.2 m from the fulcrum. Where should a 100 g mass be placed to balance?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments for equilibrium.
2. By balancing it against a known mass on a lever.
3. m₁d₁ = m₂d₂ → 50 × 0.2 = 100 × d₂ → d₂ = 0.1 m.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review safety.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a ruler on a fulcrum. "Can we weigh things with just a stick and a coin?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build a simple balance.
   - Place ruler on fulcrum. Find centre of gravity (balance point).
   - Hang unknown mass at one distance.
   - Balance with known masses at another distance.
   - Record: m_known, d_known, d_unknown.
   - Calculate: m_unknown = m_known × d_known / d_unknown.
   - **Key discovery:** The law of moments allows weighing without standard scales.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Law of moments:** Σclockwise moments = Σanticlockwise moments.
   - **Principle:** For a uniform lever, moments balance when m₁d₁ = m₂d₂.
   - **Weighing:**
     - Advantage: No need for expensive scales.
     - Disadvantage: Requires known masses and careful measurement.
   - **Accuracy:** Depends on ruler uniformity, friction at fulcrum, and measurement precision.
   - Kenyan context: Traditional markets use balance scales (law of moments). Some communities still use stone weights.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups compare their measured masses to actual values. Homework: build a balance at home.

## Hands-On Build: Simple Balance

### Parts
- Ruler or uniform stick — 1 — classroom
- Pencil or stone — 1 — classroom
- Coins (known mass) — 5 — pockets
- Unknown objects — 3 — classroom
- String — 0.5 m — classroom

### Workflow
1. Find the ruler's centre by balancing on the fulcrum.
2. Tie string loops at measured intervals (5 cm apart).
3. Hang unknown mass at a known distance.
4. Add known masses until balanced.
5. Record all distances and masses.
6. Calculate unknown mass using m₁d₁ = m₂d₂.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before calculating, verify the ruler is truly horizontal when balanced. If tilted, adjust.

### Common Mistakes
- **Non-uniform ruler:** Thick end is heavier. Fix: use a thin, uniform stick.
- **String sliding:** Masses move during balance. Fix: knot the string securely.
- **Wrong distances:** Measuring slant distance instead of horizontal. Fix: measure perpendicular to ruler.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* State the law of moments.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How can you weigh without a scale?
3. A 100 g mass at 15 cm balances an unknown mass at 10 cm. Calculate the unknown mass.
4. Why must the ruler be uniform for accurate results?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments** for equilibrium.
2. By **balancing against a known mass** on a lever.
3. 100 × 15 = m × 10 → m = **150 g**.
4. A non-uniform ruler has its **centre of gravity off-centre**, affecting balance.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A non-uniform stick balances at 40 cm when supported at its midpoint (50 cm). When a 200 g mass is hung at 10 cm, it balances with a 300 g mass at 80 cm. Calculate (a) the mass of the stick, and (b) the position of its centre of gravity.

**Worked Answer:**
**(a) Mass of stick:**
Let m = mass of stick, x = distance of CG from support.

Without added masses, the stick balances at 40 cm, so CG is at 40 cm (10 cm left of midpoint).

With masses:
Clockwise moment: 300 g × (80 − 50) = 300 × 30 = 9000 g·cm.
Anticlockwise moments: 200 g × (50 − 10) + m × (50 − 40) = 200 × 40 + m × 10 = 8000 + 10m.

For balance: 9000 = 8000 + 10m → m = **100 g**.

**(b) Centre of gravity:**
At 40 cm mark (as found from initial balance).

**Q:** A traditional market balance has arms of slightly unequal length: left arm 24 cm, right arm 25 cm. A trader claims 1 kg on the left balances 1 kg on the right. Is this fair? Calculate the actual mass on the right if 1 kg is placed on the left.

**Worked Answer:**
For true balance: m_left × d_left = m_right × d_right.
1 × 24 = m_right × 25 → m_right = 24/25 = **0.96 kg**.

The trader's balance is **unfair**. When 1 kg is placed on the left, only 0.96 kg on the right balances it. The trader gives less than 1 kg while claiming it is 1 kg.

This is why standard weights and calibrated scales are important for fair trade.
