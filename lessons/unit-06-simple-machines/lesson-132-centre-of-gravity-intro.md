# Lesson 132: Centre of Gravity: The Balance Point

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define centre of gravity as the point where weight appears to act.
2. Locate the centre of gravity of regular and irregular objects.
3. Explain why a lower centre of gravity increases stability.

## Materials
- Ruler, pencil, cardboard shapes
- String and plumb line (optional)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Centre of gravity** *(kitovu cha uzito)* — the point where an object's weight appears to act.
- **Plumb line** *(mstari wa wima)* — a string with a weight, used to find vertical.
- **Stability** *(utulivu)* — resistance to toppling.
- **Base** *(msingi)* — the area in contact with the ground.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the centre of gravity?
2. Where is the CG of a uniform ruler?
3. Why does a low CG make an object more stable?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The point where weight appears to act.
2. At the midpoint.
3. The line of action stays within the base, preventing toppling.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Balance a ruler on a finger. "Where did you place your finger? Why there?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners find CG of various objects.
   - **Regular shapes:** Ruler (midpoint), rectangle (intersection of diagonals), circle (centre).
   - **Irregular shapes:** Cardboard cut-out. Hang from a corner, draw vertical line with plumb line. Repeat from another corner. Intersection = CG.
   - **Stability test:** Stand a book upright vs lying flat. Which is more stable?
   - **Key discovery:** CG is where weight balances. Lower CG = more stable.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Definition:** The point where the entire weight appears to act.
   - **Regular objects:** CG at geometric centre.
   - **Irregular objects:** Found by balancing or plumb line method.
   - **Stability:**
     - An object topples when CG moves outside the base.
     - Lower CG means the object must tilt more before CG leaves the base.
     - Wider base also increases stability.
   - **Examples:**
     - Racing cars: low CG for cornering stability.
     - Boda-boda: rider leans into turns to keep CG over wheels.
     - A person carrying a load on their head: CG rises, making them less stable.
   - Kenyan context: Women carrying loads on their heads walk carefully — high CG reduces stability.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why a low CG helps stability. Homework: find the CG of three household objects.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is the centre of gravity?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Where is the CG of a uniform ruler?
3. Why does a wide base increase stability?
4. A boda-boda rider leans into a turn. Why?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The point where **weight appears to act**.
2. At the **midpoint**.
3. The CG must move **farther** before leaving the base.
4. To keep the **combined CG over the wheels**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A uniform plank of length 4 m and mass 20 kg rests on two supports at its ends. A 30 kg person stands 1 m from one end. Calculate the reaction forces at each support.

**Worked Answer:**
**Plank CG:** At midpoint (2 m from each end).

Take moments about support A (left end):
- Clockwise: 20 kg × 2 m + 30 kg × 1 m = 40 + 30 = 70 kg·m.
- Anticlockwise: R_B × 4 m.

For equilibrium: R_B × 4 = 70 → R_B = **17.5 kg** (175 N).

Total upward = Total downward:
R_A + R_B = 20 + 30 = 50 kg.
R_A = 50 − 17.5 = **32.5 kg** (325 N).

**Check:**
R_A + R_B = 32.5 + 17.5 = 50 ✓

**Q:** Explain why a racing car is designed with a very low centre of gravity and a wide wheelbase, using diagrams and the concept of toppling.

**Worked Answer:**
**Toppling condition:**
An object topples when the vertical line through its CG falls **outside** the base.

**Low CG:**
When the car tilts in a turn, the CG must move through a larger angle before it reaches the edge of the base. This allows higher cornering speeds without rolling.

**Wide wheelbase:**
The base (area between wheels) is wider. The CG must move farther horizontally before leaving the base.

**Combined effect:**
Low CG + wide base = maximum stability. The car can corner at high speeds while keeping the CG over the wheels.

**Calculation example:**
CG height = 0.3 m. Wheelbase = 1.6 m.
Toppling angle: tan θ = (0.8)/0.3 = 2.67 → θ = **69°**.

High CG (0.6 m): tan θ = 0.8/0.6 = 1.33 → θ = **53°**.

The low CG car can tilt 69° before toppling vs 53° for the high CG car — a significant safety margin.
