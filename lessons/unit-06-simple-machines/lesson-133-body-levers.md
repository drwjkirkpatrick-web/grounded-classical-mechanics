# Lesson 133: Levers in the Human Body

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify lever classes in human joints.
2. Explain why some body levers favour speed over force.
3. Relate muscle attachments to mechanical advantage.

## Materials
- Skeleton diagram or model
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Biomechanics** *(bimekaniki)* — the physics of living movement.
- **Tendon** *(kamba ya neva)* — tissue connecting muscle to bone.
- **Insertion point** *(kiwango cha kuingia)* — where a muscle attaches to a bone.
- **Antagonist muscle** *(mshipa wa kinyume)* — a muscle that opposes another's action.

## Pre-Test
1. Is your arm a lever? If so, what class?
2. Why are muscles attached close to joints?
3. Does the human body favour force or speed?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes — class 3 (effort between fulcrum and load).
2. To maximise distance/speed of hand movement.
3. Generally speed (most body levers are class 3 with MA < 1).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Bend your arm at the elbow. "The elbow is the fulcrum. The bicep pulls here (point). The hand is the load. What class lever is this?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners identify body levers.
   - **Arm (elbow):** Elbow = fulcrum. Bicep insertion = effort (a few cm from elbow). Hand = load (30+ cm from elbow). Class 3.
   - **Leg (ankle):** Ankle = fulcrum. Calf muscle = effort (behind ankle). Foot = load (in front). Class 2 or 3 depending on definition.
   - **Jaw:** Joint = fulcrum. Jaw muscles = effort (behind joint). Teeth = load (in front). Class 3.
   - **Key discovery:** Most body levers are class 3 (MA < 1). The body trades force for distance/speed.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Body as machine:**
     - Bones = rigid levers.
     - Joints = fulcrums.
     - Muscles = provide effort via tendons.
   - **Class 3 dominance:**
     - Muscle insertion close to joint → small effort arm.
     - Load far from joint → large load arm.
     - MA < 1 → muscles generate more force than the load, but the load moves farther and faster.
   - **Why class 3:**
     - Speed: Hand moves 5× faster than muscle contracts.
     - Range of motion: Large hand movement from small muscle movement.
     - Precision: Fine control of finger tips.
   - **Exceptions:**
     - Standing on toes: class 2 (load between fulcrum and effort).
     - Nodding head: class 1 (fulcrum at neck).
   - Kenyan context: A runner's legs are class 3 levers optimised for speed. A farmer's arms are class 3 levers for dexterity.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces a body lever and classifies it. Homework: identify three body levers and classify them.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What class lever is your arm?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why are muscles attached close to joints?
3. What does MA < 1 mean for a body lever?
4. Name one class 1 or class 2 lever in the body.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Class 3**.
2. To maximise **distance/speed** of limb movement.
3. The muscle force must be **greater** than the load, but the load moves farther/faster.
4. Standing on toes (class 2) or nodding head (class 1).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** The bicep inserts 5 cm from the elbow. The hand is 35 cm from the elbow. Calculate (a) MA, (b) the bicep force needed to hold a 5 kg mass, and (c) explain why this arrangement favours speed.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) MA = effort arm / load arm = 5 / 35 = **0.143**.

(b) Load force = 5 × 10 = **50 N**.
    Effort = Load / MA = 50 / 0.143 = **350 N**.

The bicep must generate 350 N to hold 50 N — a large muscle for a small load.

(c) **Why speed:**
    If the muscle contracts 1 cm, the hand moves 35/5 = **7 cm**.
    The hand moves 7× faster than the muscle contracts.
    This allows quick, precise movements.

**Q:** Explain why evolution favoured class 3 levers in the human body over class 1 or class 2, using the trade-off between force and distance.

**Worked Answer:**
**Evolutionary advantage of class 3:**

1. **Speed and agility:** Predators and prey both need fast reactions. Class 3 levers multiply distance, enabling quick movements.

2. **Precision:** Fine motor control (tool use, writing) requires small muscle adjustments to produce large limb movements. Class 3 provides this.

3. **Range of motion:** A small muscle spans a joint; a long limb extends far. Class 3 bridges this gap.

4. **Energy efficiency:** Muscles are already strong. The body doesn't need more force — it needs to apply that force over distance quickly.

**Trade-off accepted:**
The body accepts the need for stronger muscles (which consume more energy) in exchange for speed and dexterity. This trade-off was worth it for survival.

**Class 1 and 2 are rare in limbs because:**
- Class 1: Favours balance, not speed.
- Class 2: Favours force, but limbs don't need to lift extremely heavy loads.

The jaw is class 3 (for speed of biting), but the jaw muscles are exceptionally strong, compensating for low MA.
