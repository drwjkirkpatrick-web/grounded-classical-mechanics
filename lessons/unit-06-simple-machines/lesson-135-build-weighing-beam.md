# Lesson 135: Build: A Graduated Weighing Beam I

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** weighing-beam

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a graduated beam balance using a ruler and known masses.
2. Mark a scale showing mass vs distance from the fulcrum.
3. Prepare the balance for calibration in the next lesson.

## Materials
- Uniform ruler or stick
- Fulcrum (pencil or nail)
- Known masses (coins)
- String and hook for load
- Marker or pencil
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Graduated beam** *(mstari ulioainishwa)* — a balance beam with a marked scale.
- **Calibration** *(kurekebisha)* — marking a scale to match known values.
- **Zero point** *(sifuri)* — the balance position with no load.
- **Sliding mass** *(mzito unaosogea)* — a mass that moves along the beam.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a graduated beam?
2. Why must the beam be uniform?
3. How does a sliding mass measure unknown weights?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A balance beam with marked positions for different masses.
2. So the CG is at the midpoint and moments are proportional to distance.
3. By moving to a position where moments balance.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a traditional balance scale. "Today we build a balance that tells mass directly from position."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build the beam.
   - Find the midpoint of the ruler (CG).
   - Place fulcrum at midpoint. Verify balance.
   - Attach a sliding mass (coins in a paper clip) that can move along the beam.
   - Mark positions for known masses.
   - **Key discovery:** A uniform beam with sliding mass can measure unknown weights.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Principle:** Load at fixed position × sliding mass at variable position = balanced moments.
   - **Graduation:**
     - Place 10 g at load pan. Move sliding mass to balance. Mark "10 g."
     - Repeat with 20 g, 30 g, etc.
     - The marks may not be evenly spaced (inverse relationship).
   - **Why uniform beam:**
     - Non-uniform beam has CG off-centre → calibration is wrong.
     - Uniform beam: moment ∝ distance directly.
   - **Accuracy:** Depends on friction at fulcrum, ruler straightness, and consistent sliding mass.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups show their beams. Homework: gather materials for calibration day.

## Hands-On Build: Graduated Weighing Beam

### Parts
- Uniform ruler — 1 — classroom
- Pencil fulcrum — 1 — classroom
- Coins (known mass) — 10 — pockets
- Paper clip hook — 1 — classroom
- String — 0.5 m — classroom
- Marker — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Find midpoint of ruler by balancing on pencil.
2. Mark midpoint as fulcrum position.
3. Attach string loop at one end for load pan.
4. Attach sliding mass (paper clip with coins) that can move along beam.
5. Test with no load — beam should balance.
6. **Checkpoint:** Before marking scale, verify the beam balances with no load. If not, adjust fulcrum position.

### Common Mistakes
- **Non-uniform ruler:** Different thicknesses cause error. Fix: choose a straight, uniform stick.
- **Heavy sliding mass:** Too heavy for small loads. Fix: use 1–2 coins.
- **Friction at fulcrum:** Pencil rolls. Fix: use a nail or pin through a hole.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is a graduated beam?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why must the beam be uniform?
3. Where should the fulcrum be placed?
4. What happens to the sliding mass position as load increases?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. A balance beam with a **marked scale**.
2. So CG is at midpoint and moments are **proportional to distance**.
3. At the **midpoint** (centre of gravity).
4. It moves **farther from fulcrum**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Derive the relationship between load mass (m_L) and sliding mass position (d_S) for a balance with sliding mass m_S and fixed load distance d_L. Show why the scale is non-linear.

**Worked Answer:**
For balance: m_L × d_L = m_S × d_S.

Therefore: d_S = (m_L × d_L) / m_S.

Since d_S is **directly proportional** to m_L, the scale is actually **linear** if the ruler has uniform markings.

Wait — let me reconsider. If sliding mass is fixed and load moves, or if we use a counterweight:

For a steelyard type: counterweight at variable distance.
Moment: m_unknown × d_fixed = m_counter × d_variable.

d_variable = (m_unknown × d_fixed) / m_counter.

This is **linear**: d ∝ m.

So a simple beam balance produces a **linear scale** when properly designed.

The non-linearity comes from:
1. Non-uniform beam.
2. Changing load position.
3. Friction and other errors.

**Q:** A graduated beam has a 20 g sliding mass. The load pan is 10 cm from the fulcrum. Calculate the position for a 50 g load.

**Worked Answer:**
m_L × d_L = m_S × d_S
50 × 10 = 20 × d_S
d_S = 500 / 20 = **25 cm**.

The sliding mass must be 25 cm from the fulcrum.
