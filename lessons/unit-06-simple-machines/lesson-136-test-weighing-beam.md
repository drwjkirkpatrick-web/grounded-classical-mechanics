# Lesson 136: Build: A Graduated Weighing Beam II — Test and Certify

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** weighing-beam

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Calibrate a weighing beam against known masses.
2. Test accuracy and identify sources of error.
3. Suggest improvements to the design.

## Materials
- Beams from Lesson 135
- Known masses (coins with known weights)
- Objects of unknown mass
- Marker
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Calibration** *(kurekebisha)* — adjusting and marking a measuring instrument to match known standards.
- **Accuracy** *(usahihi)* — how close a measurement is to the true value.
- **Precision** *(uthabiti)* — how repeatable a measurement is.
- **Error** *(kosa)* — the difference between measured and true values.

## Pre-Test
1. What is calibration?
2. Why might your beam give different readings than a real scale?
3. How can you improve accuracy?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Matching instrument readings to known values.
2. Friction, non-uniform beam, inaccurate masses.
3. Reduce friction, use uniform beam, verify masses.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review beams from Lesson 135.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "How accurate is your beam? Let's test it against known masses."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Groups calibrate their beams.
   - Place 10 g at load pan. Move sliding mass to balance. Mark "10 g."
   - Repeat for 20 g, 30 g, 40 g, 50 g.
   - Draw a calibration curve (position vs mass).
   - Test unknown objects: predict mass from position, then verify with a real scale.
   - **Key discovery:** Accuracy depends on beam uniformity, friction, and mass consistency.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Calibration process:**
     - Use known standards → mark positions → draw scale.
     - Test with unknowns → compare to real scale.
   - **Sources of error:**
     - Friction at fulcrum: causes sticky readings.
     - Non-uniform ruler: CG off-centre.
     - Inaccurate masses: coins vary in weight.
     - Reading parallax: eye not level with beam.
   - **Improvements:**
     - Use a needle bearing at fulcrum (reduce friction).
     - Use a longer beam (increase precision).
     - Use verified masses (not random coins).
   - Kenyan context: Market scales require regular calibration by weights and measures officials.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups report their accuracy. Most accurate beam wins. Homework: suggest three improvements.

## Hands-On Build: Calibration and Testing

### Parts
- Beams from Lesson 135 — 1 per group — previous lesson
- Known masses (verified coins) — 5 — classroom
- Unknown objects — 3 — classroom
- Real scale (if available) — 1 — school
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Place known mass on load pan.
2. Adjust sliding mass until balanced.
3. Mark the position with the mass value.
4. Repeat for several known masses.
5. Connect marks to create a scale.
6. Weigh unknown objects using the scale.
7. Verify with real scale if available.
8. **Checkpoint:** Before each reading, gently tap the beam to overcome static friction. This ensures the true balance point.

### Common Mistakes
- **Rushing calibration:** Not waiting for beam to settle. Fix: wait 5 seconds before marking.
- **Using wrong masses:** Old coins may be lighter. Fix: verify with a scale.
- **Ignoring friction:** Marking without tapping. Fix: tap gently to release static friction.

4. How can you reduce friction at the fulcrum?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**

2. **Friction**, non-uniform beam, inaccurate masses.
3. Any two: friction, non-uniform beam, inaccurate masses, parallax.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your beam balance reads 45 g for a true 50 g mass. Calculate the percentage error. Identify which source of error (friction, non-uniform beam, or inaccurate masses) is most likely if the error is consistent across all readings.

**Worked Answer:**
Percentage error = ((45 − 50) / 50) × 100% = (−5/50) × 100% = **−10%**.

**Most likely source:**
If the error is **consistent** (always 10% low), it suggests a systematic error.

- **Non-uniform beam:** Would cause non-linear errors (different % at different masses).
- **Inaccurate masses:** If sliding mass is heavier than labelled, it balances closer to fulcrum → reads low consistently.
- **Friction:** Would cause variable errors (sometimes high, sometimes low).

Most likely: **sliding mass is heavier than assumed**.

**Fix:** Weigh the sliding mass accurately and recalculate.

**Q:** Design an improved beam balance that addresses three sources of error. Include diagrams and explain how each feature improves accuracy.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design:**
1. **Needle bearing fulcrum:** A sewing needle through a hole in the beam, resting on a hard surface.
   - Reduces friction → more sensitive balance.

2. **Longer beam:** 50 cm instead of 30 cm.
   - Increases distance between marks → easier to read, more precise.

3. **Verified masses:** Use a set of masses calibrated against a standard.
   - Eliminates mass error → accurate readings.

4. **Level indicator:** A spirit level or plumb line to ensure horizontal reading.
   - Reduces parallax error.

5. **Enclosed track:** A groove for the sliding mass.
   - Prevents sideways drift.
