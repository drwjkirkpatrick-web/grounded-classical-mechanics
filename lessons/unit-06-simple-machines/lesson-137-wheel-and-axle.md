# Lesson 137: The Wheel and Axle

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify the wheel and axle as a simple machine.
2. Calculate MA and VR for wheel and axle systems.
3. Give examples from Kenyan daily life.

## Materials
- Bicycle wheel or any wheel with axle
- Door knob
- Screwdriver
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Wheel and axle** *(gurudumu na mhimili)* — a simple machine with a large wheel rigidly attached to a smaller axle.
- **Wheel radius** *(nusu kipenyo cha gurudumu)* — the distance from the centre to the rim.
- **Axle radius** *(nusu kipenyo cha mhimili)* — the radius of the axle.
- **Steering wheel** *(mpira wa kuongoza)* — a wheel and axle used to steer vehicles.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a wheel and axle?
2. Which turns faster: the wheel or the axle?
3. Name two wheel and axle machines.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A machine with a large wheel attached to a smaller axle.
2. The axle turns faster (smaller radius, same linear speed at rim).
3. Bicycle wheel, door knob, steering wheel, screwdriver, well windlass.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Turn a door knob. "The knob (wheel) turns the shaft (axle). Which is easier to turn?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse wheel and axle systems.
   - **Bicycle wheel:** Large wheel, small axle. Measure diameters.
   - **Door knob:** Large knob, small shaft. Observe force required.
   - **Screwdriver:** Handle (wheel), shaft (axle). Compare turning handle vs shaft.
   - **Key discovery:** Large wheel = small force but large movement. Small axle = large force but small movement.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Principle:**
     - Force on wheel × wheel radius = Force on axle × axle radius.
     - MA = wheel radius / axle radius.
   - **VR:** Same ratio — wheel turns once, axle turns once, but rim moves farther.
   - **Examples:**
     - Door knob: Easy to turn knob → strong twist on shaft.
     - Steering wheel: Large wheel → precise control of small axle movement.
     - Well windlass: Large wheel → easy to lift water bucket.
     - Screrewdriver: Handle multiplies torque.
   - **Why it works:** The wheel is a continuous lever. Each point on the rim is a longer effort arm.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner identifies a wheel and axle and calculates its MA. Homework: find three wheel and axle machines at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is a wheel and axle?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* Name two wheel and axle machines.
3. A steering wheel has radius 20 cm; the shaft has radius 2 cm. Calculate MA.
4. Why is a door knob easier to turn than a bare shaft?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. A machine with a **large wheel attached to a smaller axle**.
2. Any two: door knob, steering wheel, well windlass, screwdriver, bicycle wheel.
3. MA = 20/2 = **10**.
4. The knob is a **large wheel** that multiplies torque (turning force).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A well windlass has a wheel radius of 0.4 m and an axle radius of 0.05 m. Calculate (a) MA, (b) the force needed to lift a 20 kg bucket, and (c) the distance the handle must turn to raise the bucket 10 m.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) MA = 0.4 / 0.05 = **8**.

(b) Load force = 20 × 10 = 200 N.
    Effort = 200 / 8 = **25 N**.

(c) VR = MA = 8.
    d_handle = VR × d_bucket = 8 × 10 = **80 m**.

The handle moves 80 m to raise the bucket 10 m — a large distance for small force.

**Q:** A bicycle wheel has radius 0.35 m. The sprocket (axle) has radius 0.05 m. Calculate the MA of the pedal system. If the rider pushes with 300 N on the pedal (at wheel radius), what force is transmitted to the chain?

**Worked Answer:**
MA = 0.35 / 0.05 = **7**.

Force on chain = MA × Pedal force = 7 × 300 = **2100 N**.

However, the pedal force is not applied at the wheel radius — the pedal crank is typically 0.17 m.

Correct calculation:
Pedal moment = 300 N × 0.17 m = 51 Nm.
Chain force = 51 / 0.05 = **1020 N**.

The bicycle is a complex system with multiple wheel-and-axle stages.
