# Lesson 140: The Inclined Plane II: Switchbacks and Loading Planks

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how switchbacks reduce slope steepness on hills.
2. Calculate the total length of a switchback path given height and slope angle.
3. Design a switchback path for a given hill.

## Materials
- String or rope
- Sticks or markers
- Protractor or clinometer
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Switchback** *(njia ya mabano)* — a zigzag path up a steep slope.
- **Landing** *(kiwanja cha mapumziko)* — a flat section between switchback segments.
- **Grade** *(kadiri ya mteremko)* — the slope expressed as a percentage.
- **Contour** *(mstari wa urefu sawa)* — a line of constant elevation.

## Pre-Test
1. Why do mountain roads zigzag instead of going straight up?
2. Does a switchback reduce total work?
3. What is the trade-off of a switchback?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. To reduce steepness ( gentler slope = less force).
2. No — same work against gravity, plus extra distance.
3. More distance but less steep (easier/safer).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Why do roads to Kericho or Machakos zigzag up hills?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design switchbacks.
   - Given a hill of 10 m height, design a road with maximum 10% grade.
   - Grade = rise / run × 100%. For 10% grade, run = rise / 0.10 = 100 m.
   - If straight: slope length = √(100² + 10²) ≈ 100.5 m.
   - If switchback: total horizontal distance = 100 m, but spread across hillside.
   - Mark out a model switchback using string on the ground.
   - **Key discovery:** Switchbacks increase distance dramatically but make climbing manageable.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why switchbacks:**
     - Reduce grade (slope steepness).
     - Vehicles can climb without excessive engine strain.
     - Safer for pedestrians and vehicles (less risk of runaway).
   - **Trade-off:**
     - Total path length increases.
     - More land is used.
     - More road to maintain.
   - **Formula:**
     - Grade % = (rise / run) × 100.
     - For a given rise, gentler grade = longer run.
   - **Examples:**
     - Machakos roads: wind up hills with hairpin bends.
     - Mountain paths: switchbacks for foot traffic.
     - Ramps: indoors, switchbacks save space.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups share their switchback designs. Homework: find a switchback road or path and estimate its grade.

## Hands-On Build: Model Switchback

### Parts
- String — 5 m — classroom
- Sticks or markers — 10 — ground
- Protractor — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Mark a "hill" of 2 m height (use a wall or actual slope if available).
2. Design a path with maximum 20% grade.
3. Calculate total horizontal distance needed: run = rise / 0.20 = 10 m.
4. Lay out the switchback with string, making zigzag turns.
5. Measure each segment's angle.
6. Verify no segment exceeds the maximum grade.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before finalising, check that the grade never exceeds the maximum. Steep sections defeat the purpose.

### Common Mistakes
- **Too steep segments:** Forgetting to check all segments. Fix: measure each.
- **Tight turns:** Switchbacks too sharp for vehicles. Fix: allow adequate turning radius.
- **Wrong calculation:** Using vertical rise instead of grade percentage. Fix: grade = rise/run.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Why do roads zigzag up hills?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* What is the trade-off of a switchback?
3. A hill rises 20 m. Design a path with 8% grade. What horizontal distance is needed?
4. Does a switchback reduce the work against gravity?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. To **reduce steepness** (grade).
2. **More distance** but easier/safer.
3. Run = 20 / 0.08 = **250 m**.
4. **No** — work against gravity is the same (mgh).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A mountain road climbs 500 m over 8 km horizontal distance. Calculate (a) the average grade, (b) the slope length if straight, and (c) explain why switchbacks might increase the actual road length to 12 km.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Grade = (500 / 8000) × 100% = **6.25%**.

(b) Straight slope length = √(500² + 8000²) = √(250,000 + 64,000,000) ≈ **8016 m**.

(c) **Why switchbacks increase length:**
   - Switchbacks cannot follow a straight line up a mountainside.
   - Each zigzag adds horizontal distance.
   - Turns require extra length (curve radius).
   - Landings between segments add length.

Actual road length = 12 km vs straight-line slope = 8 km.
The extra 4 km is the cost of making the road drivable.

**Q:** A wheelchair requires a maximum 1:12 grade. Can a straight ramp reach a 1.5 m landing, or must switchbacks be used? Calculate the minimum horizontal space needed.

**Worked Answer:**
Minimum horizontal = 12 × 1.5 = **18 m**.

A straight ramp needs 18 m of horizontal space.

**If only 6 m is available:**
Switchbacks are needed. Each segment can have run = 6 m, rise = 6/12 = 0.5 m.
Number of segments = 1.5 / 0.5 = **3 segments**.
Total ramp length = 3 × √(6² + 0.5²) ≈ 3 × 6.02 = **18.1 m**.

Switchbacks fit in limited space by stacking segments.
