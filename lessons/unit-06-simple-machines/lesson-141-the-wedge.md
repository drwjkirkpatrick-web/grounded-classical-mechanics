# Lesson 141: The Wedge: Pangas, Axes and Jembe Tips

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define a wedge as two inclined planes back-to-back.
2. Explain how a wedge multiplies force to split materials.
3. Give examples of wedges from Kenyan daily life.

## Materials
- Panga, axe, or knife
- Piece of wood
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Wedge** *(kipande cha kukata)* — a simple machine with two inclined planes that split materials.
- **Edge** *(kirefu)* — the thin end of a wedge that enters material.
- **Splitting** *(kuchakaza)* — forcing material apart.
- **Sharpness** *(ukali)* — the thinness of a wedge's edge.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a wedge?
2. Why is a sharp knife easier to cut with than a blunt one?
3. Name three wedges you used today.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Two inclined planes back-to-back.
2. Sharper = thinner wedge = less force needed to penetrate.
3. Knife, panga, nail, axe, jembe tip, zipper teeth.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Hold up a panga. "The blade is a wedge. Why is the edge thin and the back thick?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test wedges.
   - Try cutting paper with a sharp knife vs a blunt object.
   - Try splitting a piece of wood with a wide wedge vs a narrow one.
   - Observe: thin edge enters easily; thick back forces material apart.
   - **Key discovery:** Thin wedges concentrate force over a small area, making splitting easier.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Principle:**
     - A wedge converts downward force into sideways splitting force.
     - Thinner wedge → greater sideways force for the same downward force.
     - MA = length of wedge / thickness at edge.
   - **Examples:**
     - Panga/axe: Splits wood with concentrated force.
     - Jembe tip: Cuts into soil.
     - Nail: Wedges apart wood fibres.
     - Knife: Slices by separating material.
     - Teeth: Natural wedges for chewing.
   - **Sharpness:**
     - Sharp edge = thin wedge = high MA.
     - Dull edge = thick wedge = low MA, more force needed.
   - Kenyan context: Pangas clear bush. Jembes break soil. Axes split firewood. All are wedges.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why sharpness matters. Homework: find five wedges at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is a wedge?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why is a sharp knife easier to use?
3. A wedge is 10 cm long and 2 cm thick. Calculate its MA.
4. Name two Kenyan tools that are wedges.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Two **inclined planes back-to-back**.
2. Thinner edge = **less force** needed to penetrate.
3. MA = 10 / 2 = **5**.
4. Any two: panga, jembe, axe, knife, nail.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** An axe blade is 15 cm long and 3 cm thick at the back. Calculate its ideal MA. If the user applies 200 N, what sideways splitting force is generated? Why might the actual splitting force be less?

**Worked Answer:**
MA = 15 / 3 = **5**.

Sideways force = MA × Downward force = 5 × 200 = **1000 N**.

**Why less:**
- Friction between blade and wood.
- Wood compression absorbs energy.
- Blade may not be perfectly sharp.
- Wood grain direction affects splitting.

**Q:** A panga blade is sharpened from 2 mm thick to 0.5 mm thick at the edge. Explain how this increases MA and reduces the force needed to cut.

**Worked Answer:**
**Before sharpening:**
Thickness = 2 mm = 0.2 cm.
MA = 15 / 0.2 = **75**.

**After sharpening:**
Thickness = 0.5 mm = 0.05 cm.
MA = 15 / 0.05 = **300**.

MA increases from 75 to 300 — a **4× improvement**.

For the same downward force, the sideways cutting force increases 4×.

Alternatively, to achieve the same cutting force, the user needs only **1/4 the effort**.

This is why sharpening tools is essential — it dramatically reduces the work required.

- Cut away from the body.
