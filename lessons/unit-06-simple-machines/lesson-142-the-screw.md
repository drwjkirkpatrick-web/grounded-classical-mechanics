# Lesson 142: The Screw: A Spiral Inclined Plane

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe a screw as a spiral inclined plane.
2. Calculate the mechanical advantage of a screw.
3. Give examples of screws and threaded fasteners in Kenyan life.

## Materials
- Screw and screwdriver
- Bolt and nut
- Bottle cap with thread
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Screw** *(viti)* — an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder.
- **Thread** *(mfululizo)* — the spiral ridge on a screw.
- **Pitch** *(kimo)* — the distance between adjacent threads.
- **Torque** *(nguvu ya kuzungusha)* — turning force applied to a screw.

## Pre-Test
1. What simple machine is a screw?
2. Why does a screw turn easily but hold tightly?
3. Name three uses of screws.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder.
2. The thread spreads force over many turns, creating large holding force from small torque.
3. Fastening wood, bottle caps, jacks, drills, propellers.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a screw. "If you unwrap the thread, it's a ramp. Let's see."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test screws.
   - Wrap a paper triangle around a pencil to show the spiral.
   - Turn a screw into wood with a screwdriver. Count turns vs depth.
   - Measure pitch (distance per turn).
   - Calculate: MA = circumference / pitch.
   - **Key discovery:** Many turns create large holding force from small torque.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Screw as inclined plane:**
     - Unwrap the thread → it's a ramp wrapped around a cylinder.
     - Circumference = πd. Pitch = p.
     - MA = circumference / pitch = πd / p.
   - **Why screws hold:**
     - Thread pushes against material in all directions.
     - Large normal force from many thread contacts.
     - Friction prevents backing out.
   - **Examples:**
     - Wood screw: holds furniture together.
     - Bottle cap: seals bottles with thread.
     - Jack: lifts heavy loads by turning.
     - Drill bit: removes material as it turns.
     - Propeller: pushes air or water.
   - Kenyan context: Building construction uses screws and bolts. Bottle caps seal sodas. Jacks lift vehicles.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner unwraps a screw mentally and explains. Homework: find five screws at home.

## Hands-On Build: Screw Pitch Measurement

### Parts
- Screw — 2 — hardware
- Ruler — 1 — classroom
- Wood block — 1 — classroom
- Screwdriver — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Measure screw diameter. Calculate circumference = π × d.
2. Count threads per cm. Pitch = 1 / (threads per cm).
3. Calculate MA = circumference / pitch.
4. Screw into wood. Count turns for 1 cm depth.
5. Compare: more threads = smaller pitch = larger MA.
6. **Checkpoint:** Measure pitch carefully — use a magnifying glass if needed. Small errors in pitch cause large errors in MA.

### Common Mistakes
- **Confusing pitch and thread count:** Pitch is distance between threads, not threads per cm. Fix: measure 10 threads and divide by 10.
- **Ignoring diameter:** MA depends on both diameter and pitch. Fix: measure both.
- **Wrong units:** Mixing cm and mm. Fix: be consistent.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What simple machine is a screw?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why does a screw turn easily but hold tightly?
3. A screw has diameter 8 mm and pitch 2 mm. Calculate MA.
4. Name two Kenyan uses of screws.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder**.
2. Thread spreads force over **many turns**, creating large holding force.
3. Circumference = π × 8 = 25.1 mm. MA = 25.1 / 2 = **12.6**.
4. Any two: construction, bottle caps, vehicle jacks, furniture.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A car jack has a screw with diameter 20 mm and pitch 4 mm. Calculate (a) MA, (b) the torque needed to lift a 500 kg car (load at axle), and (c) the number of turns to lift 10 cm.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Circumference = π × 20 = 62.8 mm. MA = 62.8 / 4 = **15.7**.

(b) Load = 500 × 10 = 5000 N.
    Effort at screw = 5000 / 15.7 = **318 N**.
    Torque = Effort × radius = 318 × 0.01 = **3.18 Nm**.

(c) Turns = height / pitch = 100 / 4 = **25 turns**.

**Q:** Explain why a screw with fine threads (small pitch) holds more securely than one with coarse threads, using the concept of MA and friction.

**Worked Answer:**
**Fine threads:**
- Smaller pitch → larger MA (MA = circumference / pitch).
- For the same torque, the axial force is greater.
- More thread contact area → more friction resisting loosening.
- Requires more turns for same advance → more work to tighten/loosen.

**Coarse threads:**
- Larger pitch → smaller MA.
- Less axial force for same torque.
- Less thread contact → less friction resisting loosening.
- Faster to tighten/loosen.

**Conclusion:** Fine threads are better for holding (higher MA, more friction). Coarse threads are better for speed (quick assembly/disassembly).
