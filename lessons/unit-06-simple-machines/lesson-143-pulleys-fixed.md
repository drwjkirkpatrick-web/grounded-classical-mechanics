# Lesson 143: Pulleys I: The Fixed Pulley

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe a fixed pulley as a wheel on a fixed axle that changes direction.
2. Explain that a fixed pulley has MA = 1 but changes direction.
3. Give examples of fixed pulleys from Kenyan life.

## Materials
- Pulley or homemade pulley (bottle cap + stick)
- String or rope
- Load (bag or stones)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Fixed pulley** *(kifaa cha kutoa kilichogatwa)* — a pulley attached to a fixed point; changes direction only.
- **Direction changer** *(mabadiliko wa mwelekeo)* — a machine that changes the direction of force.
- **Wheel** *(gurudumu)* — the rotating part of a pulley.
- **Axle** *(mhimili)* — the fixed shaft the wheel rotates on.

## Pre-Test
1. Does a fixed pulley multiply force?
2. What does a fixed pulley change?
3. Name two fixed pulleys.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. No — MA = 1.
2. Direction.
3. Flagpole pulley, clothesline pulley, curtain pulley, well pulley.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a pulley. "Pull down, the load goes up. What changed?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test a fixed pulley.
   - Set up pulley on a fixed support.
   - Attach load. Pull to lift.
   - Measure effort force with spring balance.
   - Compare to load weight.
   - **Key discovery:** Effort = load (approximately). Direction changed but force not multiplied.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Fixed pulley:**
     - Wheel rotates on a fixed axle.
     - MA = 1 (ideally).
     - VR = 1.
     - Changes direction: pull down to lift up.
   - **Why use it:**
     - Convenience: standing on ground to lift flag.
     - Posture: pulling down uses body weight.
     - Safety: pulling away from load.
   - **Examples:**
     - Flagpole: pull down, flag rises.
     - Well: pull down, bucket rises.
     - Clothesline: pull horizontally, clothes move.
     - Theatre curtains: pull sideways, curtains open.
   - **Friction:** Real pulleys have friction. Effort slightly > load.
   - Kenyan context: Well pulleys in rural areas. Clotheslines. Flagpoles at schools.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why fixed pulleys are useful despite MA = 1. Homework: find three fixed pulleys.

## Hands-On Build: Fixed Pulley Demonstration

### Parts
- Pulley or bottle cap — 1 — classroom/home
- String — 1 m — classroom
- Load — 1 — classroom
- Spring balance — 1 — classroom
- Support (stick between chairs) — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Attach pulley to support.
2. Thread string through pulley.
3. Attach load to one end.
4. Pull other end to lift load.
5. Measure effort force with spring balance.
6. Compare to load weight.
7. **Checkpoint:** Ensure pulley rotates freely. A stuck pulley adds friction, making effort seem larger.

### Common Mistakes
- **Stuck pulley:** Wheel doesn't rotate. Fix: oil or replace.
- **Measuring while accelerating:** Force changes. Fix: pull slowly and steadily.
- **Wrong direction:** Pulling sideways instead of down. Fix: pull straight down.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Does a fixed pulley multiply force?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What does a fixed pulley change?
3. A 50 N load is lifted with a fixed pulley. What is the ideal effort?
4. Why pull down rather than lift up?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **No** — MA = 1.
2. **Direction**.
3. **50 N** (ideal).
4. Pulling down uses **body weight** and is more comfortable.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A fixed pulley has 10% friction loss. Calculate (a) the actual MA, and (b) the efficiency. If a 60 N load is lifted 2 m, how much work is done by the person, and how much is lost to friction?

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Ideal MA = 1. With 10% loss: actual MA = 1 × 0.90 = **0.9**.

(b) Efficiency = 90%.

Work output = 60 × 2 = **120 J**.
Work input = 120 / 0.90 = **133.3 J**.
Work lost = 133.3 − 120 = **13.3 J** (heat in bearings).

**Q:** Explain why a fixed pulley is actually a class 1 lever with the fulcrum at the centre. Draw the analogy.

**Worked Answer:**
**Lever analogy:**
- The axle of the pulley = fulcrum.
- The load side of the rope = load arm.
- The effort side of the rope = effort arm.
- Both arms equal (radius of pulley).

**Class 1 lever:** Fulcrum between effort and load.
**Fixed pulley:** Axle (fulcrum) between load rope and effort rope.
**MA = 1:** Equal arm lengths → no force multiplication.

The pulley is simply a lever that has been bent into a circle. The wheel allows the rope to slide, changing the direction continuously.
