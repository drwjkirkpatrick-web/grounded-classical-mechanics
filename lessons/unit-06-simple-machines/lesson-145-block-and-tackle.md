# Lesson 145: Block and Tackle: The Jua Kali Engine Hoist

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a block and tackle system with multiple pulleys.
2. Calculate MA from the number of supporting strands.
3. Explain why jua kali workshops use simple block and tackle systems.

## Materials
- Two or more pulleys
- String or rope
- Load
- Support beam
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Block and tackle** *(sisteme ya makifaa)* — a system of pulleys in blocks that provides large mechanical advantage.
- **Block** *(kifaa)* — the frame holding one or more pulleys.
- **Tackle** *(shikilia)* — the rope running through the pulleys.
- **Strand** *(nyuzi)* — one length of rope between pulleys.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a block and tackle?
2. How does MA relate to the number of strands?
3. Why do workshops use block and tackle instead of cranes?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A system of multiple pulleys for lifting heavy loads.
2. MA equals the number of supporting strands.
3. Cheaper, simpler, no electricity needed.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "How do small workshops lift heavy engines?" Introduce block and tackle.

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build a block and tackle.
   - Use two blocks (one fixed, one movable) with two pulleys each.
   - Thread rope through all pulleys.
   - Count supporting strands.
   - Lift a load and measure effort.
   - **Key discovery:** MA = number of supporting strands. More strands = less effort.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Block and tackle:**
     - Fixed block at top, movable block at load.
     - Rope threaded back and forth.
     - MA = number of rope strands supporting the load.
   - **Jua kali use:**
     - Small workshops lift engines, metal beams, and heavy parts.
     - No electricity needed.
     - Portable and adaptable.
   - **Trade-off:**
     - More MA → more rope to pull.
     - Friction increases with more pulleys.
   - **Safety:**
     - Check rope condition.
     - Secure anchoring.
     - Never stand under a lifted load.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups demonstrate their hoists. Homework: count strands in a block and tackle photo.

## Hands-On Build: Block and Tackle

### Parts
- Pulleys — 4 — hardware
- Rope — 3 m — hardware
- Load — 1 — classroom
- Support — 1 — classroom
- Spring balance — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Attach two pulleys to fixed block (top).
2. Attach two pulleys to movable block (load).
3. Thread rope: start at fixed block, down to movable, up to fixed, down to movable, up to fixed, free end.
4. Secure free end or attach to support.
5. Pull free end to lift load.
6. Count supporting strands.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before lifting, verify all rope is seated in pulley grooves. A rope jumping the groove causes failure.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong threading:** Missing a pulley. Fix: trace rope path carefully.
- **Unequal loading:** Pulleys not aligned. Fix: keep blocks parallel.
- **Frayed rope:** Safety hazard. Fix: inspect before each use.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A jua kali hoist uses a block and tackle with 4 supporting strands to lift a 200 kg engine. Calculate (a) ideal effort, (b) actual effort if efficiency is 75%, and (c) the distance pulled to raise the engine 2 m.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Ideal effort = (200 × 10) / 4 = **500 N**.

(b) Actual effort = 500 / 0.75 = **667 N**.

(c) Distance pulled = 4 × 2 = **8 m**.

**Q:** Compare the block and tackle to a hydraulic jack for lifting vehicle engines. When would each be preferable?

**Worked Answer:**
**Block and tackle:**
- Advantages: No fluid, portable, works anywhere, visible operation.
- Disadvantages: Requires overhead support, slow, limited height.
- Best for: Workshops with beams, outdoor lifting, situations without power.

**Hydraulic jack:**
- Advantages: Compact, fast, high force, stable.
- Disadvantages: Requires hydraulic fluid, can leak, needs flat ground.
- Best for: Vehicle maintenance, controlled lifting, indoor use.

**Conclusion:**
Block and tackle suits small workshops and flexible setups. Hydraulic jacks suit controlled environments and heavy vehicles.

## Safety Notes
- Inspect rope before each use.
- Never stand under lifted load.
- Ensure support can handle load.
- Standard workshop safety.
