# Lesson 147: Gears: Trading Speed for Force

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe gears as toothed wheels that transfer motion and force.
2. Explain the relationship between gear size and speed/force.
3. Calculate gear ratio and its effect on output.

## Materials
- Two interlocking gears or bottle caps with rubber bands
- Bicycle gears or posho mill gears (if available)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Gear** *(gurudumu la meno)* — a toothed wheel that meshes with another to transfer motion.
- **Gear ratio** *(uwiano wa gurudumu)* — the ratio of teeth on driven gear to driving gear.
- **Pinion** *(gurudumu ndogo)* — the smaller gear in a pair.
- **Crown wheel** *(gurudumu kubwa)* — the larger gear in a pair.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a gear?
2. If a small gear drives a large gear, which turns faster?
3. Name two machines that use gears.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A toothed wheel that meshes with another.
2. The small gear turns faster.
3. Bicycle, car, posho mill, clock, washing machine.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show bicycle gears. "Pedalling the small gear turns the big gear slowly. Why?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test gear ratios.
   - Use two interlocking gears (or bottle caps with rubber bands as teeth).
   - Count teeth on each.
   - Turn small gear, observe large gear speed.
   - Turn large gear, observe small gear speed.
   - **Key discovery:** Small gear fast = large gear slow (and vice versa). Teeth ratio = speed ratio.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Gear ratio:**
     - Ratio = teeth on driven / teeth on driver.
     - Ratio > 1: speed decreases, force increases.
     - Ratio < 1: speed increases, force decreases.
   - **Bicycle gears:**
     - Low gear (small front, large back): easy to pedal, slow speed.
     - High gear (large front, small back): hard to pedal, fast speed.
   - **Posho mill:**
     - Large motor gear drives small mill gear.
     - Mill spins fast to grind maize.
   - **Trade-off:**
     - You can't get both speed and force from the same gear pair.
     - Gears redistribute energy between speed and force.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains gear trade-offs. Homework: count teeth on a bicycle gear.

## Hands-On Build: Gear Ratio Demonstration

### Parts
- Two gears (or bottle caps with rubber bands) — 2 — hardware
- Pencil axles — 2 — classroom
- Support — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Count teeth on each gear.
2. Mesh gears together.
3. Turn driver gear 10 times. Count driven gear turns.
4. Calculate ratio: driven turns / driver turns.
5. Compare to teeth ratio: driven teeth / driver teeth.
6. **Checkpoint:** Ensure gears mesh properly. Gaps or slipping give wrong ratios.

### Common Mistakes
- **Not counting teeth:** Estimating leads to error. Fix: mark and count carefully.
- **Slipping gears:** Rubber bands not tight. Fix: use actual gears or tighter bands.
- **Wrong direction:** Confusing driver and driven. Fix: label which gear you turn.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is a gear?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Which turns faster: small or large gear?
3. A 12-tooth gear drives a 36-tooth gear. What is the ratio?
4. Does this increase speed or force?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. A **toothed wheel** that meshes with another.
2. **Small gear** turns faster.
3. 36/12 = **3**.
4. **Force** (speed decreases by 3×).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A bicycle has a 52-tooth front gear and 13-tooth rear gear. Calculate (a) gear ratio, (b) rear wheel speed if pedalling at 60 rpm, and (c) the force advantage if the pedal crank is 170 mm and rear wheel radius 340 mm.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Gear ratio = 52/13 = **4**.

(b) Rear wheel turns 4× faster than pedals.
    Rear wheel speed = 60 × 4 = **240 rpm**.

(c) Pedal circumference = 2π × 0.17 = 1.07 m.
    Wheel circumference = 2π × 0.34 = 2.14 m.
    For one pedal revolution, bike moves 4 × 2.14 = 8.56 m.
    Pedal moves 1.07 m per revolution.
    MA = distance ratio = 8.56 / 1.07 = **8**.

**Q:** Explain why a posho mill uses a small gear on the motor and a large gear on the mill, and calculate the speed increase if the motor runs at 1500 rpm and the gear ratio is 1:5.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why:**
The motor produces high speed but low torque. The mill needs high speed to grind maize effectively.

A small motor gear driving a large mill gear would slow the mill down.

Instead: small motor gear drives large intermediate, then large intermediate drives small mill gear.

Or: direct coupling with ratio favouring speed.

**Calculation:**
Gear ratio = 1:5 means mill gear is 5× smaller than motor gear.

Mill speed = 1500 × 5 = **7500 rpm**.

The mill spins very fast, creating the impact and shear needed to break maize kernels.
