# Lesson 148: Belt Drives: Posho Mills and Sewing Machines

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe a belt drive as a flexible loop connecting two wheels.
2. Explain how pulley diameter affects speed ratio.
3. Give examples of belt drives in Kenyan machines.

## Materials
- Two spools or bottle caps of different sizes
- Rubber band or elastic string
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Belt drive** *(sisteme ya ukanda)* — a flexible belt connecting two pulleys to transfer motion.
- **Pulley** *(kifaa cha kutoa)* — a wheel over which a belt runs.
- **Tension** *(uvutano)* — the tightness of the belt.
- **Slip** *(kuteleza)* — when the belt slides instead of gripping.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a belt drive?
2. Does a large pulley turn faster or slower than a small pulley?
3. Name two machines with belt drives.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A flexible belt connecting two pulleys.
2. Slower (same belt speed, larger circumference = fewer rpm).
3. Posho mill, sewing machine, fan, water pump, alternator.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a rubber band around two fingers. "This is a belt drive. What happens when one finger turns?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build model belt drives.
   - Use two spools of different sizes.
   - Connect with rubber band.
   - Turn small spool, observe large spool speed.
   - Turn large spool, observe small spool speed.
   - **Key discovery:** Small pulley fast, large pulley slow. Diameter ratio = speed ratio.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Principle:**
     - Belt speed is constant (if no slip).
     - rpm₁ × d₁ = rpm₂ × d₂.
     - Small pulley fast, large pulley slow.
   - **Examples:**
     - Posho mill: Motor pulley small, mill pulley large → mill spins fast.
     - Sewing machine: Hand wheel large, machine pulley small → machine spins fast.
     - Fan: Motor pulley small, fan pulley large → fan spins slowly.
   - **Advantages:**
     - Smooth, quiet.
     - Absorbs shock.
     - Simple maintenance.
   - **Disadvantages:**
     - Slip under heavy load.
     - Belt wears out.
     - Limited power transmission.
   - Kenyan context: Posho mills, water pumps, sewing machines, fans.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces a belt drive and explains speed ratio. Homework: find a belt drive machine.

## Hands-On Build: Belt Drive Model

### Parts
- Two spools/bottle caps — 2 — classroom
- Rubber band — 1 — classroom
- Pencils for axles — 2 — classroom
- Support — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Mount spools on pencils.
2. Loop rubber band around both.
3. Turn small spool, count large spool turns.
4. Measure diameters.
5. Calculate expected ratio: rpm_large = rpm_small × (d_small / d_large).
6. **Checkpoint:** Keep belt taut. Loose belts slip, giving wrong ratios.

### Common Mistakes
- **Loose belt:** Slips, wrong ratio. Fix: use tighter band or adjust spacing.
- **Different sizes not measured:** Guessing ratios. Fix: measure diameters.
- **Not counting turns:** Estimating speed. Fix: count revolutions carefully.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is a belt drive?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Does a large pulley turn faster or slower?
3. A small pulley (4 cm) drives a large pulley (12 cm). What is the speed ratio?
4. Name two Kenyan machines with belt drives.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. A **flexible belt** connecting two pulleys.
2. **Slower**.
3. 12/4 = **3** (large pulley turns 1/3 as fast).
4. Any two: posho mill, sewing machine, water pump, fan.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A posho mill motor runs at 1500 rpm with a 10 cm pulley. The mill needs 500 rpm. Calculate the required mill pulley diameter.

**Worked Answer:**
rpm₁ × d₁ = rpm₂ × d₂
1500 × 10 = 500 × d₂
d₂ = 15,000 / 500 = **30 cm**.

The mill pulley must be **30 cm** in diameter.

**Q:** Explain why belt drives are preferred over gear drives in some applications, using a posho mill and a sewing machine as examples.

**Worked Answer:**
**Posho mill:**
- Belt drive absorbs vibration from grinding.
- If a stone jams the mill, the belt slips instead of breaking gears.
- Easier to replace a worn belt than repair gears.
- Allows some distance between motor and mill.

**Sewing machine:**
- Belt drive is quiet — gears would be noisy in a home.
- The hand wheel gives tactile feedback through the belt.
- Easy to disengage (remove belt) for threading.

**When gears are better:**
- Precise speed ratio needed (no slip).
- High power transmission.
- Compact space.
