# Lesson 149: The Bicycle: A Machine Made of Machines I

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify the simple machines in a bicycle.
2. Explain how each machine contributes to bicycle function.
3. Calculate the overall mechanical advantage of a bicycle.

## Materials
- Bicycle (or diagram)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Bicycle** *(baiskeli)* — a human-powered vehicle with two wheels.
- **Pedal crank** *(kifaa cha kukanyaga)* — the lever arm the rider pushes.
- **Chain drive** *(sisteme ya mnyororo)* — a loop of chain connecting front and rear gears.
- **Derailleur** *(kibadilishi)* — the mechanism that shifts chain between gears.

## Pre-Test
1. Name three simple machines in a bicycle.
2. What does the chain do?
3. Why do bicycles have gears?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Lever (pedals), wheel and axle (wheels), pulley (chain sprockets).
2. Transfers motion from pedals to rear wheel.
3. To trade speed for force (climbing vs flat).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a bicycle. "This is not one machine. It is many machines working together."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners identify machines on a bicycle.
   - **Pedals:** Class 1 lever (fulcrum at axle, effort from foot, load at chain).
   - **Chain and sprockets:** Belt drive / gear system.
   - **Wheels:** Wheel and axle.
   - **Brakes:** Levers (class 1 or 2).
   - **Steering:** Wheel and axle (handlebars).
   - **Key discovery:** A bicycle combines multiple simple machines for efficient human transport.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Simple machines in a bicycle:**
     - **Pedal crank:** Lever. Long crank = more torque.
     - **Chain:** Transfers motion without slip.
     - **Gears:** Change speed ratio. Low gear for climbing, high gear for speed.
     - **Wheels:** Wheel and axle. Large wheel rolls over bumps.
     - **Brakes:** Levers multiply hand force to squeeze pads.
   - **Overall advantage:**
     - Human legs produce limited force.
     - The bicycle multiplies this force through levers and gears.
     - Wheels reduce friction (rolling vs sliding).
   - **Energy:**
     - Rider's chemical energy → kinetic energy.
     - Some lost to friction (air, bearings, tires).
   - Kenyan context: Boda-boda bicycles are essential transport. Understanding them means understanding physics.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces energy through the bicycle. Homework: count the simple machines on your bicycle.

## Hands-On Build: Bicycle Machine Hunt

### Parts
- Bicycle — 1 — school/home
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom
- Labels — 10 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Examine a real bicycle.
2. Identify each simple machine.
3. Label: lever, wheel and axle, gear, pulley, wedge.
4. Trace the path of energy from pedal to wheel.
5. **Checkpoint:** Verify each identification. Some parts serve multiple functions.

### Common Mistakes
- **Missing machines:** Only noticing obvious ones. Fix: check every moving part.
- **Wrong classification:** Calling the chain a pulley. Fix: chain is a belt drive/gear system.
- **Ignoring brakes:** Forgetting brakes are levers. Fix: check brake handles.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A bicycle has pedal cranks 170 mm long, front gear 44 teeth, rear gear 11 teeth, and rear wheel diameter 660 mm. Calculate (a) the distance the bike moves per pedal revolution, and (b) the overall mechanical advantage if the rider pushes with 300 N on each pedal.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Gear ratio = 44/11 = 4.
    Wheel circumference = π × 0.66 = 2.07 m.
    Distance per pedal revolution = 4 × 2.07 = **8.28 m**.

(b) Pedal torque = 300 × 0.17 = 51 Nm.
    Chain force = 51 / 0.11 (front sprocket radius) ≈ 464 N.
    Rear wheel torque = 464 × 0.0275 (rear sprocket radius) ≈ 12.8 Nm.
    Force at road = 12.8 / 0.33 = **38.8 N**.

**Overall MA = output force / input force = 38.8 / 300 = 0.13.**

Wait — MA < 1? The bicycle trades force for distance/speed. The rider pushes 300 N but only 38.8 N reaches the road. However, the rider's foot moves ~1 m per pedal stroke while the bike moves 8.28 m.

This is a **distance multiplier**, like a class 3 lever.

**Q:** Explain why a bicycle is more efficient than walking, using concepts of rolling friction, lever advantage, and energy conservation.

**Worked Answer:**
**Rolling friction:**
A bicycle wheel rolls with minimal friction (static contact point, no sliding). Walking involves lifting the body against gravity with each step — more work.

**Lever advantage:**
The pedal crank is a lever that multiplies the rider's leg force. The long crank arm gives mechanical advantage over the chain.

**Energy conservation:**
A bicycle stores some energy as rotational kinetic energy in the wheels. Once moving, less energy is needed to maintain speed. Walking requires continuous muscular effort.

**Efficiency:**
Bicycle efficiency is ~20–25% (human muscle to motion).
Walking efficiency is ~5–7%.
A bicycle is 3–5× more efficient than walking.
