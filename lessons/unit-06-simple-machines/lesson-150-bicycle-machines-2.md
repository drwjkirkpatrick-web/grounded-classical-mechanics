# Lesson 150: The Bicycle II: Measuring Its Advantages

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Measure the gear ratios on a real bicycle.
2. Calculate speed and force advantages for different gears.
3. Design an optimal gear setup for a given terrain.

## Materials
- Bicycle with gears
- Tape measure
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Gear ratio** *(uwiano wa gurudumu)* — the ratio of front to rear sprocket teeth.
- **Cadence** *(kasi ya kukanyaga)* — pedalling speed in rpm.
- **Gear inches** *(inchi za gurudumu)* — a measure of how far the bike travels per pedal revolution.
- **Optimal gear** *(gurudumu bora)* — the gear that balances effort and speed for conditions.

## Pre-Test
1. How do you calculate gear ratio?
2. What happens to speed when you shift to a larger rear gear?
3. What gear is best for climbing a hill?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Front teeth / rear teeth.
2. Speed decreases, but easier to pedal.
3. Small front, large rear (low gear ratio).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "You have a bicycle. How do you choose the right gear?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure bicycle gears.
   - Count teeth on front and rear sprockets.
   - Measure wheel circumference.
   - Calculate gear ratios.
   - Calculate distance per pedal revolution.
   - Test different gears by pedalling.
   - **Key discovery:** Low gear = easy pedalling, slow speed. High gear = hard pedalling, fast speed.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Gear ratio calculation:**
     - Ratio = front teeth / rear teeth.
     - Ratio > 3: high gear (fast, hard).
     - Ratio < 2: low gear (slow, easy).
   - **Distance per revolution:**
     - Distance = ratio × wheel circumference.
   - **Speed at given cadence:**
     - Speed (m/min) = distance × cadence.
   - **Terrain choice:**
     - Flat: high gear for speed.
     - Hill: low gear for torque.
     - Start: low gear to accelerate.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups share optimal gear designs. Homework: calculate your bike's gear ratios.

## Hands-On Build: Bicycle Gear Measurement

### Parts
- Bicycle with gears — 1 — school/home
- Tape measure — 1 — classroom
- Calculator — 1 per group — classroom

### Workflow
1. Count teeth on all front sprockets.
2. Count teeth on all rear sprockets.
3. Measure wheel circumference (roll one revolution).
4. Calculate all gear combinations.
5. Identify highest and lowest gears.
6. **Checkpoint:** Count teeth carefully. Missing a tooth gives wrong ratios.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong tooth count:** Missing teeth or counting gaps. Fix: mark starting tooth, count carefully.
- **Wrong wheel size:** Using nominal size instead of actual. Fix: measure by rolling.
- **Confusing front/rear:** Front is by pedals, rear is on wheel. Fix: label clearly.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How do you calculate gear ratio?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens to speed with larger rear gear?
3. A bike has 48 front teeth and 16 rear teeth. What is the gear ratio?
4. What gear is best for starting from rest?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Front teeth / rear teeth**.
2. **Decreases** (but easier to pedal).
3. 48/16 = **3**.
4. **Low gear** (small front, large rear).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A cyclist pedals at 60 rpm in a gear ratio of 3.5 with 700c wheels (circumference 2.2 m). Calculate (a) speed in km/h, and (b) the force at the road if the rider pushes 250 N on 170 mm cranks.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Distance per pedal revolution = 3.5 × 2.2 = 7.7 m.
    Distance per minute = 7.7 × 60 = 462 m.
    Speed = 462 × 60 / 1000 = **27.7 km/h**.

(b) Pedal torque = 250 × 0.17 = 42.5 Nm.
    Chain force = 42.5 / 0.11 ≈ 386 N.
    Rear torque = 386 × 0.016 (rear sprocket) ≈ 6.2 Nm.
    Force at road = 6.2 / 0.35 = **17.7 N**.

**Q:** Design a 5-speed gear system for a bicycle used on flat roads, moderate hills, and steep hills. Specify front and rear sprocket teeth for each gear.

**Worked Answer:**
**Requirements:**
- Flat: high speed, moderate effort.
- Moderate hills: balanced.
- Steep hills: low speed, easy effort.

**Design:**
Front sprocket: 42 teeth (single front for simplicity).

Rear sprockets:
- Gear 1 (steep hill): 28 teeth → ratio = 42/28 = **1.5**.
- Gear 2 (moderate hill): 21 teeth → ratio = 42/21 = **2.0**.
- Gear 3 (flat/mixed): 16 teeth → ratio = 42/16 = **2.6**.
- Gear 4 (flat fast): 14 teeth → ratio = 42/14 = **3.0**.
- Gear 5 (downhill/sprint): 12 teeth → ratio = 42/12 = **3.5**.

This gives a good range from easy climbing to fast sprinting.
