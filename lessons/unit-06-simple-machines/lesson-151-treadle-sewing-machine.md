# Lesson 151: The Treadle Sewing Machine: Foot to Needle

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Trace the energy path from foot treadle to needle in a sewing machine.
2. Identify all simple machines in a treadle sewing machine.
3. Explain how rotary motion is converted to reciprocating motion.

## Materials
- Treadle sewing machine (or diagram/video)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Treadle** *(kitanda cha mguu)* — a foot-operated lever that drives a machine.
- **Reciprocating motion** *(mchezo wa kurudi)* — back-and-forth linear motion.
- **Rotary motion** *(mchezo wa mzunguko)* — circular motion.
- **Crank** *(kasisi)* — a mechanism that converts rotary to reciprocating motion.

## Pre-Test
1. What simple machines are in a treadle sewing machine?
2. What motion does the foot produce?
3. What motion does the needle need?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Lever (treadle), wheel and axle (flywheel), pulley/belt (drive), wedge (needle).
2. Rotary (circular).
3. Reciprocating (up and down).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a treadle sewing machine. "Push the treadle. What happens?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners trace the energy path.
   - Foot pushes treadle → lever rotates.
   - Lever turns crank shaft → rotary motion.
   - Crank shaft turns flywheel → smooths motion.
   - Flywheel drives belt → transfers to machine head.
   - Belt turns crank → converts to needle's up-down motion.
   - **Key discovery:** Multiple machines convert foot motion to needle motion.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Simple machines:**
     - **Treadle:** Class 1 lever (fulcrum at pivot, effort from foot, load at rod).
     - **Crank shaft:** Converts rotary to reciprocating.
     - **Flywheel:** Stores rotational energy, smooths motion.
     - **Belt drive:** Transfers motion from base to head.
     - **Needle:** Wedge that pierces fabric.
   - **Motion conversion:**
     - Foot: rotary.
     - Crank: rotary → reciprocating.
     - Needle: reciprocating.
   - **Energy flow:**
     - Chemical (food) → muscle → mechanical → fabric piercing.
   - Kenyan context: Treadle machines are still common in rural areas. No electricity needed. Jua kali tailors use them.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces the full energy path. Homework: draw the machine's simple machines.

## Practical Exploration: Treadle Motion Tracing

### Purpose
Trace the motion and simple machines in a treadle sewing machine through observation and labelling.

### Materials
- Treadle machine or diagram — 1 — school/home
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom
- Labels — 10 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Observe treadle machine.
2. Label each simple machine.
3. Trace motion from foot to needle.
4. Identify motion type at each stage.
5. **Checkpoint:** Verify the crank converts rotary to reciprocating. Without the crank, the needle would spin instead of stitch.

### Common Mistakes
- **Missing belt drive:** Only noticing treadle and needle. Fix: check all connections.
- **Wrong motion types:** Calling treadle reciprocating. Fix: it rotates back and forth (oscillatory, which is a form of rotary).
- **Ignoring flywheel:** Forgetting its energy storage role. Fix: observe how it keeps spinning.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What simple machines are in a treadle machine?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What motion does the foot produce?
3. What converts rotary motion to needle motion?
4. Why is a flywheel needed?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any three: lever, wheel and axle, belt drive, crank, wedge (needle).
2. **Rotary** (circular).
3. The **crank**.
4. It **stores energy** and smooths motion between pedal strokes.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A treadle sewing machine has a treadle arm 30 cm long and a crank radius of 2 cm. Calculate (a) the MA of the treadle lever, and (b) the needle speed if the foot operates at 60 cycles per minute.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) MA = effort arm / load arm = 30 / 2 = **15**.

(b) Each treadle cycle = one crank revolution.
    Needle moves up and down once per revolution.
    Needle speed = **60 stitches per minute** (1 per second).

**Q:** Explain why the flywheel must be heavy and have most of its mass at the rim, using concepts of moment of inertia and energy storage.

**Worked Answer:**
**Moment of inertia:**
I = Σmr². Mass far from axis contributes more to I.

**Energy storage:**
Rotational KE = ½Iω². Larger I means more energy stored at same ω.

**Why heavy rim:**
- Concentrating mass at the rim maximises I for given mass.
- More energy stored between pedal strokes.
- Smoother motion — less speed variation.
- Maintains needle motion during the "dead spot" when feet switch direction.

A light flywheel would slow too much between strokes, causing uneven stitching.
