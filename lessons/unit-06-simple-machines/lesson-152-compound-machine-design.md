# Lesson 152: Design Challenge: A Machine for Our Village I

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** village-machine

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify a real problem in their village that a simple machine could solve.
2. Design a machine using available materials.
3. Draw a plan with labelled parts and calculated MA.

## Materials
- Paper and pencils
- Scrap materials (sticks, string, cans)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Design brief** *(maelezo ya ubunifu)* — a statement of the problem and requirements.
- **Prototype** *(mfano wa kwanza)* — a first model for testing.
- **Constraint** *(kizuizi)* — a limit on the design (materials, cost, space).
- **Iteration** *(marudio)* — repeating the design process to improve.

## Pre-Test
1. What problem in your village could a machine solve?
2. What materials are available locally?
3. How will you know if your machine works?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
- Open-ended. Accept any reasonable answer.
- Examples: lifting water, grinding grain, transporting loads, shelling maize.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups brainstorm problems.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Your village needs a machine. You have sticks, string, and cans. What do you build?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design machines.
   - Identify a real problem.
   - Brainstorm solutions using simple machines.
   - Choose materials.
   - Draw a labelled diagram.
   - Calculate expected MA.
   - **Key discovery:** Engineering is solving real problems with available resources.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Design process:**
     1. Identify problem.
     2. Research existing solutions.
     3. Brainstorm new solutions.
     4. Select best idea.
     5. Draw plan.
     6. Calculate MA.
     7. Gather materials.
     8. Build prototype.
     9. Test and iterate.
   - **Constraints:**
     - Materials must be locally available.
     - Cost should be minimal.
     - Must be safe to operate.
   - **Evaluation:**
     - Does it solve the problem?
     - Is the MA sufficient?
     - Is it durable?

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups share designs. Vote on most promising. Homework: gather materials for build day.

## Hands-On Build: Design Plan

### Parts
- Paper — 5 sheets — classroom
- Pencils — 4 — classroom
- Scrap materials — various — ground
- Calculator — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Identify village problem.
2. List possible simple machine solutions.
3. Choose best solution.
4. Draw labelled diagram.
5. Calculate expected MA.
6. List materials needed.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before finalising, verify the machine can actually be built from available materials. Overly complex designs fail.

### Common Mistakes
- **Overly complex:** Using machines not yet learned. Fix: stick to levers, pulleys, inclined planes.
- **Wrong MA calculation:** Not accounting for friction. Fix: estimate 70–80% efficiency.
- **Ignoring safety:** Sharp edges or unstable bases. Fix: design for safe operation.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What problem does your machine solve?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What materials will you use?
3. What simple machines are in your design?
4. How will you test if it works?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
- Open-ended. Evaluate for:
  - Clear problem statement.
  - Appropriate simple machines.
  - Realistic materials.
  - Calculated MA.
  - Testable criteria.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your village needs to lift water from a 5 m deep well. Design a machine using only a wheel and axle, rope, and a bucket. Calculate the required wheel radius if a child can exert 100 N and the bucket holds 10 kg of water.

**Worked Answer:**
Load = 10 × 10 = 100 N.

Ideal case: effort = load → no mechanical advantage needed.
But with friction and bucket weight, assume 120 N total load.

Using a wheel and axle:
MA = Load / Effort = 120 / 100 = 1.2.

MA = wheel radius / axle radius.
If axle radius = 0.05 m (typical):
r_wheel = 1.2 × 0.05 = **0.06 m = 6 cm**.

A small wheel (12 cm diameter) is sufficient for a child to lift water.

For easier operation: larger wheel (20 cm radius) gives MA = 4.
Effort = 120 / 4 = **30 N** — very easy for a child.

**Q:** Evaluate your design against three criteria: cost, durability, and ease of use. Suggest one improvement for each criterion.

**Worked Answer:**
Open-ended. Example for a maize sheller:

**Cost:**
- Current: Uses wood and nails (cheap).
- Improvement: Use reclaimed materials (zero cost).

**Durability:**
- Current: Wood wears at rubbing surfaces.
- Improvement: Add metal plates at wear points.

**Ease of use:**
- Current: Requires two hands.
- Improvement: Add a foot pedal for one-handed operation.
