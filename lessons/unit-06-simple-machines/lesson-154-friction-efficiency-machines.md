# Lesson 154: Why Machines Waste Energy

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define efficiency as useful output energy divided by total input energy.
2. Identify the main sources of energy loss in machines.
3. Suggest ways to improve machine efficiency.

## Materials
- Various machines (or diagrams)
- Thermometer (optional)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Efficiency** *(ufanisi)* — the ratio of useful output to total input.
- **Friction** *(mguso)* — force opposing motion between surfaces.
- **Heat loss** *(potezo la joto)* — energy lost as thermal energy.
- **Lubrication** *(kutia mafuta)* — reducing friction with oil or grease.

## Pre-Test
1. What is efficiency?
2. Where does "lost" energy go?
3. How can you reduce energy loss?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Useful output / total input.
2. Heat (and sound).
3. Oil, smooth surfaces, bearings, alignment.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Rub hands together. "Where did the energy go?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test machine efficiency.
   - Test a simple machine (lever, pulley, or inclined plane).
   - Measure input work and output work.
   - Calculate efficiency.
   - Identify where energy is lost.
   - **Key discovery:** No machine is 100% efficient. Friction always steals some energy.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Efficiency formula:**
     - η = (useful output / total input) × 100%.
   - **Sources of loss:**
     - Friction: surfaces rubbing → heat.
     - Deformation: materials bending or compressing.
     - Sound: vibrations in air.
     - Air resistance: moving through air.
   - **Improving efficiency:**
     - Lubrication: oil reduces friction.
     - Bearings: rolling instead of sliding.
     - Alignment: prevents sideways forces.
     - Smooth surfaces: less roughness = less friction.
   - **Real-world examples:**
     - Bicycle: 90–95% efficient (good bearings).
     - Car engine: 20–30% efficient (most energy lost as heat).
     - Human body: 20–25% efficient (most food energy becomes heat).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains where energy goes. Homework: find three ways to improve a machine's efficiency.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is efficiency?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Where does lost energy go?
3. A machine does 80 J of useful work from 100 J input. Calculate efficiency.
4. Name two ways to reduce friction.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Useful output / total input**.
2. **Heat** (and sound).
3. (80/100) × 100% = **80%**.
4. Any two: **oil, bearings, smooth surfaces, alignment**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A pulley system is 75% efficient. Calculate (a) the actual effort to lift a 300 N load with an ideal MA of 4, and (b) the heat generated per metre the load rises.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Ideal effort = 300 / 4 = 75 N.
    Actual effort = 75 / 0.75 = **100 N**.

(b) Input work per metre = 100 × 4 = 400 J.
    Output work per metre = 300 × 1 = 300 J.
    Heat = 400 − 300 = **100 J per metre**.

**Q:** Explain why a bicycle is more efficient than a car, and why neither can reach 100% efficiency, using the laws of thermodynamics.

**Worked Answer:**
**Bicycle vs car:**
- Bicycle: 90–95% efficient. Direct mechanical transfer (legs → pedals → wheels).
- Car: 20–30% efficient. Chemical → thermal → mechanical (many conversions, each with losses).

**Why neither reaches 100%:**
- **Friction:** Bearings, tires, air resistance always present.
- **Second Law of Thermodynamics:** Heat flows from hot to cold. Any energy conversion produces some waste heat.
- **Irreversibility:** You can't convert heat back to work completely.

Even a perfect bicycle has tire friction and air resistance.
Even a perfect engine has exhaust heat.

100% efficiency is **impossible** for any real machine.
