# Lesson 155: Maintenance Matters: Oiling, Tightening, Aligning

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain why regular maintenance keeps machines efficient.
2. Demonstrate oiling, tightening, and inspecting a machine.
3. Create a maintenance schedule for a common machine.

## Materials
- Old machine (bicycle, posho mill, or sewing machine)
- Oil or grease
- Wrench or screwdriver
- Cleaning cloth
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Maintenance** *(matengenezo)* — regular work to keep a machine working well.
- **Inspection** *(ukaguzi)* — checking a machine for problems.
- **Lubrication** *(kutia mafuta)* — applying oil or grease to reduce friction.
- **Wear** *(kuchakaa)* — gradual damage from use.

## Pre-Test
1. Why do machines need maintenance?
2. What happens if you don't oil a machine?
3. How often should you check a bicycle?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. To prevent wear, reduce friction, and ensure safety.
2. Friction increases, parts wear, efficiency drops.
3. Daily for quick check; weekly for thorough inspection.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a rusty bicycle chain. "What happened? How do we fix it?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners maintain a machine.
   - Inspect for wear, loose parts, and rust.
   - Oil moving parts.
   - Tighten bolts and screws.
   - Clean debris.
   - Test operation before and after.
   - **Key discovery:** Simple maintenance dramatically improves machine performance.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why maintain:**
     - Friction increases without lubrication.
     - Loose parts cause vibration and damage.
     - Wear accumulates, leading to failure.
     - Safety: worn parts can break unexpectedly.
   - **Maintenance tasks:**
     - **Daily:** Visual inspection, clean debris.
     - **Weekly:** Oil moving parts, tighten bolts.
     - **Monthly:** Deep clean, replace worn parts.
     - **Yearly:** Overhaul, replace bearings.
   - **Signs of problems:**
     - Noise: indicates friction or misalignment.
     - Heat: indicates excessive friction.
     - Vibration: indicates loose parts.
     - Sluggishness: indicates wear or poor lubrication.
   - Kenyan context: Boda-boda mechanics maintain bikes daily. Posho mills need regular oiling. Jua kali workshops fix machines constantly.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups create maintenance schedules. Homework: maintain one machine at home.

## Hands-On Build: Machine Maintenance

### Parts
- Old machine — 1 — school/home
- Oil — 1 — hardware
- Wrench — 1 — hardware
- Cloth — 1 — home

### Workflow
1. Inspect machine for problems.
2. List issues found.
3. Oil all moving parts.
4. Tighten all fasteners.
5. Clean debris and dirt.
6. Test operation.
7. Compare before and after.
8. **Checkpoint:** Before oiling, clean old grease. New oil mixed with dirt becomes grinding paste.

### Common Mistakes
- **Over-oiling:** Attracts dirt. Fix: wipe excess.
- **Wrong oil:** Thick grease on fast parts. Fix: match lubricant to speed.
- **Ignoring wear:** Tightening won't fix worn parts. Fix: replace when worn.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Why do machines need maintenance?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens without oil?
3. List two maintenance tasks.
4. What does noise indicate?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. To **prevent wear**, reduce friction, ensure safety.
2. **Friction increases**, parts wear.
3. Any two: oil, tighten, clean, inspect, replace.
4. **Friction** or misalignment.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A posho mill's efficiency drops from 85% to 60% over six months. Calculate the percentage increase in energy cost per kilogram of maize ground, assuming the same input power. Suggest three maintenance actions to restore efficiency.

**Worked Answer:**
**Energy cost increase:**
At 85%: 1 kg requires 1/0.85 = 1.18 units of energy.
At 60%: 1 kg requires 1/0.60 = 1.67 units of energy.

Increase = (1.67 − 1.18) / 1.18 × 100% = **41.5%**.

**Maintenance actions:**
1. Oil bearings and gears.
2. Tighten drive belt.
3. Replace worn grinding plates.
4. Clean air vents (motor cooling).
5. Align pulleys.

**Q:** Create a 6-month maintenance schedule for a village bicycle used daily for transport and carrying goods. Include inspection points, lubrication schedule, and replacement intervals.

**Worked Answer:**
**Daily:**
- Visual inspection: tires, brakes, chain.
- Quick clean: wipe mud off chain.

**Weekly:**
- Oil chain.
- Check tire pressure.
- Tighten bolts.
- Test brakes.

**Monthly:**
- Deep clean entire bike.
- Oil cables and pivot points.
- Check brake pads (replace if worn).
- True wheels (adjust spokes).
- Check chain wear (replace if stretched).

**Every 3 months:**
- Replace brake pads if worn.
- Grease bearings (hubs, headset, bottom bracket).
- Replace cables if frayed.

**Every 6 months:**
- Replace tires if worn.
- Overhaul hubs and bottom bracket.
- Replace chain (typically 3000–5000 km).
