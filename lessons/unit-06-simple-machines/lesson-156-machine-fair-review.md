# Lesson 156: Unit Review: Machine Fair

**Unit:** 6 — Simple Machines | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** village-machine

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Summarise the simple machines covered in Unit 6.
2. Present their village machine project to peers.
3. Evaluate machines using criteria: function, MA, efficiency, and creativity.

## Materials
- Village machines from Lesson 153
- Evaluation rubric
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Rubric** *(kigezo)* — a scoring guide with criteria.
- **Peer evaluation** *(tathmini ya rika)* — assessing each other's work.
- **Presentation** *(utangulizi)* — explaining work to an audience.
- **Criteria** *(vigezo)* — standards for judgement.

## Pre-Test
1. Name the six simple machines.
2. What is mechanical advantage?
3. How do you evaluate a machine?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw.
2. The ratio of output force to input force.
3. Function, MA, efficiency, creativity, practicality.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Set up fair stations.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Welcome to the Machine Fair! Each group presents their machine."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Machine Fair rotations.
   - Each group has 3 minutes to demonstrate.
   - 2 minutes for questions.
   - Peers evaluate using rubric.
   - Rotate until all machines seen.
   - **Key discovery:** Different designs solve the same problem differently.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Evaluation criteria:**
     - **Function:** Does it solve the problem? (30%)
     - **MA:** Is the mechanical advantage sufficient? (25%)
     - **Efficiency:** Does it waste minimal energy? (20%)
     - **Creativity:** Is the design original? (15%)
     - **Presentation:** Is the explanation clear? (10%)
   - **Review of machines:**
     - Lever: changes force/distance.
     - Wheel and axle: transfers motion.
     - Pulley: changes direction or multiplies force.
     - Inclined plane: reduces lifting force.
     - Wedge: splits materials.
     - Screw: converts rotary to linear motion.
   - **Compound machines:**
     - Most real machines combine several simple machines.
     - The bicycle: lever + wheel and axle + gears + chain.
     - The treadle sewing machine: lever + crank + belt + flywheel.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Announce winners. Discuss what made designs successful. Homework: reflect on what you learned.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Name the six simple machines.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What is mechanical advantage?
3. Which machine changes the direction of force?
4. Give an example of a compound machine from your village.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw**.
2. **Output force / input force**.
3. **Fixed pulley**.
4. Any reasonable: bicycle, sewing machine, posho mill, water pump.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Evaluate the village machine project against all five criteria (function, MA, efficiency, creativity, presentation). Calculate a weighted score and justify your ratings.

**Worked Answer:**
Open-ended. Example rubric:

| Criterion | Weight | Score (1–5) | Weighted |
|-----------|--------|-------------|----------|
| Function  | 30%    | 4           | 1.20     |
| MA        | 25%    | 3           | 0.75     |
| Efficiency| 20%    | 3           | 0.60     |
| Creativity| 15%    | 5           | 0.75     |
| Presentation| 10%  | 4           | 0.40     |
| **Total** | **100%** |           | **3.70/5** |

**Q:** Design a compound machine that uses at least three simple machines to lift water from a well. Draw a diagram and calculate the overall MA.

**Worked Answer:**
Open-ended. Example:

**Design:**
1. **Treadle (lever):** Foot pushes down.
2. **Crank (wheel and axle):** Converts treadle motion to rotary.
3. **Chain (belt drive):** Transfers motion to wellhead.
4. **Windlass (wheel and axle):** Lifts bucket.
5. **Pulley:** Changes direction at wellhead.

**MA calculation:**
- Treadle MA = 3.
- Crank MA = 2.
- Windlass MA = 4.
- Pulley MA = 1.

Overall MA = 3 × 2 × 4 × 1 = **24**.

(Note: In series, MA multiplies. In practice, friction reduces this.)
