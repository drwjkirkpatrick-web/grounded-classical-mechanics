# Lesson 157: What Is Pressure? Force Spread Over Area

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define pressure as force per unit area.
2. Explain why spreading force reduces pressure.
3. Distinguish between pressure and force.

## Materials
- Sharp and blunt pencils
- Piece of foam or clay
- Ruler
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Pressure** *(shinikizo)* — force divided by area; measured in pascals (Pa).
- **Pascal** *(paskali)* — the SI unit of pressure; 1 Pa = 1 N/m².
- **Area** *(eneo)* — the surface over which force is spread.
- **Force** *(nguvu)* — a push or pull; measured in newtons (N).

## Pre-Test
1. What is pressure?
2. Does a sharp knife cut better than a blunt one? Why?
3. What units measure pressure?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Force spread over area.
2. Yes — sharp knife has smaller area, so higher pressure.
3. Pascals (Pa) or N/m².

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Press a sharp pencil and a blunt pencil into foam with equal force. "Which goes deeper?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test pressure.
   - Press different objects (sharp pencil, blunt pencil, ruler edge, ruler flat) into foam with the same force.
   - Measure depth of indentation.
   - Record: object, contact area (estimate), depth.
   - **Key discovery:** Same force, smaller area = deeper indentation = higher pressure.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formula:** Pressure = Force / Area (P = F/A).
   - **Units:** Pascals (Pa) = N/m².
   - **Why sharp things cut:**
     - Sharp edge has tiny area.
     - Same force → enormous pressure.
     - Pressure breaks molecular bonds.
   - **Why wide things don't sink:**
     - Wide area spreads force.
     - Same force → low pressure.
   - **Examples:**
     - Walking on snow: flat shoes sink, snowshoes float.
     - Drawing blood: sharp needle pierces skin easily.
     - Sitting on a chair: wide seat comfortable, narrow wire painful.
   - Kenyan context: Herders wear flat sandals on soft ground. Women carrying heavy loads spread weight with head pads.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why spreading force reduces pressure. Homework: find three examples of pressure at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is pressure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why does a sharp knife cut better?
3. A 20 N force acts on 0.01 m². Calculate pressure.
4. How can you reduce pressure without reducing force?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Force divided by area**.
2. Smaller area → **higher pressure**.
3. P = 20 / 0.01 = **2000 Pa**.
4. **Increase the area** over which force acts.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** An elephant's foot has area 0.05 m² and supports 5000 N. A high-heeled shoe has area 0.0005 m² and supports 500 N. Calculate the pressure under each and explain which would damage a wooden floor.

**Worked Answer:**
Elephant: P = 5000 / 0.05 = **100,000 Pa**.

Shoe: P = 500 / 0.0005 = **1,000,000 Pa**.

The shoe exerts **10× more pressure** despite supporting 1/10 the weight.

**Damage:**
Wood damage depends on pressure, not force. The high heel concentrates force on a tiny area, exceeding the wood's compressive strength.

The elephant, despite being much heavier, spreads weight over large padded feet, keeping pressure manageable.

**Q:** A woman carrying a 20 kg load on her head uses a circular pad of radius 8 cm. Calculate (a) the pressure on her head, and (b) the pressure without the pad if the load contacts 4 cm² directly. Explain why the pad is essential.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Force = 20 × 10 = 200 N.
    Pad area = π × 0.08² = 0.0201 m².
    Pressure = 200 / 0.0201 = **9950 Pa**.

(b) Without pad: Pressure = 200 / 0.0004 = **500,000 Pa**.

**Why the pad is essential:**
The pad reduces pressure by **50×** by spreading the load. Without it, the concentrated pressure would cause pain, tissue damage, and long-term injury.
