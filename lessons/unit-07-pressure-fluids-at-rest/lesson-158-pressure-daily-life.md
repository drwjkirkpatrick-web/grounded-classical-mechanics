# Lesson 158: Pressure in Daily Life: Sharp, Wide, Deep

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify how pressure explains everyday phenomena.
2. Predict whether pressure increases or decreases in given situations.
3. Apply the pressure formula to practical examples.

## Materials
- Various objects (knife, spoon, nail, book)
- Soft surface (foam, sand, or mud)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Concentrated force** *(nguvu zilizokusanyika)* — force applied over a small area.
- **Distributed force** *(nguvu zilizosambazwa)* — force spread over a large area.
- **Penetration** *(kuchoma)* — entering a material by exceeding its pressure limit.
- **Support** *(kushikilia)* — holding up weight by providing upward force.

## Pre-Test
1. Why do wide tyres help vehicles drive on sand?
2. Why does a sharp nail enter wood more easily than a blunt one?
3. A woman carries a load on her head. How does she reduce pressure?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Wide tyres spread vehicle weight, reducing pressure on sand.
2. Sharp nail has smaller area, so higher pressure.
3. Uses a wide, soft pad to spread the force.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show a panga and a jembe. "Both cut. Why is the panga sharper?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test pressure with everyday objects.
   - Press knife vs spoon edge vs flat into foam.
   - Press nail point vs nail head into wood.
   - Press book flat vs book edge into clay.
   - Record: object, area, depth/effect.
   - **Key discovery:** Sharp = high pressure. Wide = low pressure.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Everyday pressure:**
     - **Sharp:** Knives, needles, pangas, jembe tips — small area cuts.
     - **Wide:** Snowshoes, elephant feet, head pads — large area supports.
     - **Deep:** Stiletto heels sink, flat shoes don't — pressure difference.
   - **Predicting pressure:**
     - Same force: smaller area → higher pressure.
     - Same area: larger force → higher pressure.
   - **Applications:**
     - Building foundations: wide base supports heavy walls.
     - Vehicle tyres: wide for soft ground, narrow for hard roads.
     - Medical needles: tiny area pierces skin painlessly.
   - Kenyan context: Mkoka (sisal sandals) have wide soles for soft ground. Jembe tips are sharpened before use. Mats spread weight for sitting.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner gives a new pressure example. Homework: photograph three pressure examples.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Why do wide tyres help on sand?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why does a sharp nail enter wood easily?
3. A 500 N person stands on one foot of area 0.02 m². Calculate pressure.
4. Suggest one way to reduce pressure when carrying a heavy load.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Spreads weight**, reducing pressure on sand.
2. **Smaller area** → higher pressure.
3. P = 500 / 0.02 = **25,000 Pa**.
4. Use a **wide pad**, distribute weight, or use a vehicle.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A four-wheeled cart carries 200 kg. Each wheel contacts 0.005 m². Calculate (a) pressure per wheel, and (b) total pressure if two wheels sink and only two carry the load. Explain why wheels sink in mud.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Total weight = 200 × 10 = 2000 N.
    Per wheel = 2000 / 4 = 500 N.
    Pressure = 500 / 0.005 = **100,000 Pa**.

(b) Two wheels: 2000 / 2 = 1000 N each.
    Pressure = 1000 / 0.005 = **200,000 Pa**.

**Why wheels sink:**
Mud has limited bearing strength. When pressure exceeds the mud's strength, it yields (flows), and the wheel sinks.

Wide tyres reduce pressure below the mud's bearing strength, preventing sinking.

**Q:** Design a shoe for walking on deep mud that minimises sinking. Calculate the required sole area for an 80 kg person if mud collapses at 8000 Pa.

**Worked Answer:**
Force = 80 × 10 = 800 N.

Maximum pressure = 8000 Pa.

Minimum area = 800 / 8000 = **0.1 m²**.

**Design:**
- Sole area: 0.1 m² per foot (0.2 m² total).
- Shape: Wide, flat, with ridges to prevent slipping.
- Material: Lightweight, waterproof.
- Attachment: Straps over foot (like snowshoes).

This is impractically large for normal walking, which is why people avoid deep mud or use poles to distribute weight to arms.
