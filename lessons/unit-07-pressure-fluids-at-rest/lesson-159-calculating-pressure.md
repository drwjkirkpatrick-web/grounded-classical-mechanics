# Lesson 159: Calculating Pressure: Feet, Hooves and Jerricans

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Calculate pressure using P = F/A.
2. Compare pressures for different objects and animals.
3. Explain why certain shapes evolve for specific pressures.

## Materials
- Calculator
- Ruler
- Objects with different contact areas
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Contact area** *(eneo la mawasiliano)* — the area where two surfaces touch.
- **Comparative pressure** *(shinikizo la kulinganisha)* — pressure calculated for comparison.
- **Biomechanics** *(bimekaniki)* — physics applied to living things.
- **Evolutionary adaptation** *(kubadilika kwa kujiendeleza)* — traits that improve survival.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the formula for pressure?
2. A 600 N person has feet of total area 0.03 m². Calculate pressure.
3. Why do camels have wide feet?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. P = F/A.
2. P = 600 / 0.03 = 20,000 Pa.
3. Wide feet spread weight on sand.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Who has heavier feet: you or an elephant? Who applies more pressure?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners calculate pressures.
   - Trace your foot on paper. Estimate area.
   - Calculate your standing pressure.
   - Compare to: elephant foot, goat hoof, chicken foot.
   - Estimate: jembe tip area, panga edge area.
   - **Key discovery:** Heavy animals spread weight. Small contact points create high pressure.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Calculations:**
     - Human: 600 N / 0.03 m² = 20,000 Pa (standing).
     - Elephant: 50,000 N / 0.3 m² = 167,000 Pa.
     - Goat: 200 N / 0.008 m² = 25,000 Pa.
     - Chicken: 10 N / 0.0001 m² = 100,000 Pa.
   - **Evolutionary shapes:**
     - Camel: wide pad for sand.
     - Goat: cloven hoof for grip.
     - Chicken: sharp claw for scratching.
     - Jembe: sharp tip for soil penetration.
   - **Formula practice:**
     - P = F/A.
     - F = P × A.
     - A = F/P.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why pressure shapes feet. Homework: calculate pressure for a family member.

## Hands-On Build: Pressure Calculator

### Parts
- Paper — 4 sheets — classroom
- Pencil — 4 — classroom
- Calculator — 1 per group — classroom
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Trace foot on paper.
2. Count grid squares to estimate area.
3. Measure mass with scale.
4. Calculate weight (F = mg).
5. Calculate pressure (P = F/A).
6. Compare with classmates.
7. **Checkpoint:** Measure area carefully. Overestimating area underestimates pressure.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong area:** Tracing shoe instead of bare foot. Fix: remove shoe.
- **Wrong units:** Using cm² instead of m². Fix: convert (1 m² = 10,000 cm²).
- **Forgetting gravity:** Using mass instead of weight. Fix: F = mg.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is the pressure formula?
2. A 40 kg goat has hooves of total area 0.006 m². Calculate pressure.
3. Why are jembe tips sharpened?
4. Which exerts more pressure: a 20 g needle on 0.000001 m² or a 60 kg person on 0.03 m²?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **P = F/A**.
2. F = 40 × 10 = 400 N. P = 400 / 0.006 = **66,667 Pa**.
3. To **reduce area** and increase pressure for soil penetration.
4. Needle: P = 0.2 / 0.000001 = **200,000 Pa**.
   Person: P = 600 / 0.03 = **20,000 Pa**.
   The **needle** exerts more pressure.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Compare the pressures exerted by (a) a 5000 kg elephant standing on four feet of 0.08 m² each, (b) a 70 kg woman in stiletto heels of total area 0.0004 m², and (c) a 2.5 tonne matatu with four tyres of 0.02 m² each. Which causes most damage to a floor and why?

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Elephant: P = 50,000 / (4 × 0.08) = 50,000 / 0.32 = **156,250 Pa**.

(b) Woman: P = 700 / 0.0004 = **1,750,000 Pa**.

(c) Matatu: P = 25,000 / (4 × 0.02) = 25,000 / 0.08 = **312,500 Pa**.

**Most damage:** The **woman in stilettos** (1.75 MPa) — over 5× the matatu and 11× the elephant.

This is why high heels damage wooden floors and why some buildings ban them.

**Q:** A traditional plough (jembe) tip has area 0.0002 m² and the farmer pushes with 150 N. Calculate (a) the pressure on the soil, and (b) the depth it might penetrate if soil yields at 500,000 Pa and penetration resistance increases linearly with depth.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) P = 150 / 0.0002 = **750,000 Pa**.

(b) The tip exceeds soil yield (500,000 Pa) by 250,000 Pa.
    If resistance increases linearly, penetration depth = (excess pressure) / (rate).
    Assuming rate = 100,000 Pa/cm:
    Depth = 250,000 / 100,000 = **2.5 cm**.

This is why jembe tips must be kept sharp — blunt tips have larger area, reducing pressure and making ploughing exhausting.
