# Lesson 160: Pressure in Liquids I: The Jerrican with Three Holes

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate that liquid pressure increases with depth.
2. Show that liquid pressure acts equally in all directions.
3. Explain why water shoots farther from deeper holes.

## Materials
- Plastic jerrican or bottle
- Nail or heated wire for making holes
- Water
- Bucket or basin
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Liquid pressure** *(shinikizo la maji)* — pressure exerted by a fluid at rest.
- **Depth** *(kina)* — the vertical distance below the liquid surface.
- **Jets** *(mashapo)* — streams of liquid forced out by pressure.
- **Hydrostatic** *(hairostatiki)* — relating to fluids at rest.

## Pre-Test
1. Does water pressure change with depth?
2. Which shoots farther: a hole near the top or near the bottom?
3. Does water pressure push sideways?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes — increases with depth.
2. Near the bottom — higher pressure.
3. Yes — pressure acts in all directions.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review safety.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "If I punch holes in a jerrican full of water, what happens?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test liquid pressure.
   - Fill jerrican with water.
   - Punch holes at different heights (top, middle, bottom).
   - Observe water jets.
   - Measure horizontal distance of jets.
   - **Key discovery:** Deeper holes shoot farther. Pressure increases with depth.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Liquid pressure:**
     - Caused by weight of liquid above.
     - Increases with depth.
     - Acts equally in all directions.
   - **Why deeper shoots farther:**
     - More water above = more weight = more pressure.
     - Pressure forces water out faster.
     - Faster water travels farther horizontally.
   - **Formula (preview):**
     - P = ρgh (density × gravity × depth).
     - Not required for ages 10–13.
   - **Real examples:**
     - Dams are thicker at bottom (higher pressure).
     - Deep-sea divers need thick suits.
     - Water towers: height creates pressure.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why dams are thick at bottom. Homework: observe water pressure at different depths.

## Hands-On Build: Jerrican Pressure Demonstration

### Parts
- Plastic jerrican — 1 — home
- Nail or heated wire — 1 — home
- Water — 5 L — tap
- Bucket — 1 — home
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill jerrican to top.
2. Punch hole near top. Measure jet distance.
3. Punch hole at middle. Measure jet distance.
4. Punch hole near bottom. Measure jet distance.
5. Compare distances.
6. **Checkpoint:** Keep jerrican full during all tests. As water level drops, pressure changes.

### Common Mistakes
- **Holes too large:** Water drains too fast. Fix: make small holes (2–3 mm).
- **Not refilling:** Pressure changes as level drops. Fix: keep topped up.
- **Measuring angle:** Jets curve downward. Fix: measure horizontal distance at water level.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Does water pressure change with depth?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Which shoots farther: top or bottom hole?
3. Why are dams thicker at the bottom?
4. Does pressure act only downward?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Yes** — increases with depth.
2. **Bottom** — higher pressure.
3. **Higher pressure** at bottom needs thicker walls.
4. **No** — it acts in **all directions**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A water tank is 3 m tall. Calculate the pressure difference between top and bottom. If a hole at the bottom shoots water horizontally, calculate the initial velocity using P = ½ρv² (Bernoulli approximation).

**Worked Answer:**
Pressure difference: ΔP = ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 3 = **30,000 Pa**.

Velocity: P = ½ρv² → v = √(2P/ρ) = √(2 × 30,000 / 1000) = √60 = **7.75 m/s**.

**Q:** Explain why a submarine must withstand enormous pressure at 4000 m depth (Mariana Trench). Calculate the pressure at this depth.

**Worked Answer:**
P = ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 4000 = **40,000,000 Pa** = **40 MPa**.

This is about **400 times atmospheric pressure**.

**Why submarines need thick walls:**
- The pressure crushes anything that can't push back equally.
- Submarine hull must withstand 40 MPa inward force.
- Steel walls tens of centimetres thick.
- Pressure inside must equal outside (or hull collapses).
- Humans inside need artificial atmosphere.
