# Lesson 161: Pressure in Liquids II: P = ρgh and Dam Walls

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Use the formula P = ρgh to calculate liquid pressure.
2. Explain why dams are thicker at the base.
3. Apply liquid pressure principles to Kenyan water systems.

## Materials
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner
- Diagrams of dams

## Vocabulary
- **Density** *(msisimko)* — mass per unit volume (kg/m³).
- **Hydrostatic pressure** *(shinikizo la maji)* — pressure due to fluid at rest.
- **Dam** *(bwawa)* — a barrier holding back water.
- **Base thickness** *(unene wa msingi)* — the width of a dam at its bottom.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the formula for liquid pressure?
2. Why are dams thicker at the bottom?
3. Calculate pressure at 5 m depth in water.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. P = ρgh.
2. Higher pressure at depth needs thicker walls.
3. P = 1000 × 10 × 5 = 50,000 Pa.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show dam photos. "Why is the bottom thicker than the top?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners calculate pressures.
   - Calculate pressure at 1 m, 5 m, 10 m, 20 m depth.
   - Compare to atmospheric pressure (101,325 Pa).
   - Calculate force on a 1 m wide strip at different depths.
   - **Key discovery:** Pressure increases linearly with depth. Deep water creates enormous forces.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formula:** P = ρgh
     - ρ = density (1000 kg/m³ for water).
     - g = 10 N/kg.
     - h = depth (m).
   - **Units:** Pa (N/m²).
   - **Dam design:**
     - Bottom: thick walls withstand maximum pressure.
     - Top: thin walls suffice (low pressure).
     - Shape: curved upstream directs water weight into ground.
   - **Total force on dam:**
     - Average pressure = ½ρgh (triangular distribution).
     - Force = average pressure × area.
   - Kenyan context: Turkwel Dam, Seven Forks dams. Water towers create pressure for distribution.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains dam thickness. Homework: calculate pressure at the bottom of a local water tank.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is the liquid pressure formula?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why are dams thicker at the bottom?
3. Calculate pressure at 8 m depth.
4. A dam is 15 m high and 20 m wide. Calculate total force on it.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **P = ρgh**.
2. **Higher pressure** at greater depth.
3. P = 1000 × 10 × 8 = **80,000 Pa**.
4. Average P = ½ × 1000 × 10 × 15 = 75,000 Pa.
   Area = 15 × 20 = 300 m².
   Force = 75,000 × 300 = **22,500,000 N**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A cylindrical water tower is 20 m tall and 5 m in diameter. Calculate (a) pressure at the base, (b) total weight of water, and (c) the thickness of concrete needed at the base if concrete can withstand 2 MPa compression.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) P = ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 20 = **200,000 Pa**.

(b) Volume = π × 2.5² × 20 = 392.7 m³.
    Mass = 392,700 kg.
    Weight = **3,927,000 N**.

(c) Force on base = 3,927,000 N.
    Base area = π × 2.5² = 19.63 m².
    Stress = 3,927,000 / 19.63 = 200,000 Pa.

This is well below 2 MPa (2,000,000 Pa).

Minimum thickness isn't determined by compression but by structural requirements (typically 0.3–0.5 m for such tanks).

**Q:** Compare the pressure at the bottom of (a) a 10 m deep swimming pool, (b) the Indian Ocean at 4000 m, and (c) a 2 m water barrel. Explain why scuba divers and submarines face different challenges.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Pool: P = 1000 × 10 × 10 = **100,000 Pa**.

(b) Ocean: P = 1025 × 10 × 4000 = **41,000,000 Pa**.

(c) Barrel: P = 1000 × 10 × 2 = **20,000 Pa**.

**Scuba divers (pool/ shallow):**
- Breathe compressed air equal to water pressure.
- Must ascend slowly to avoid decompression sickness.
- Limited to ~40 m by nitrogen narcosis.

**Submarines (deep ocean):**
- Hull must withstand crushing pressure (41 MPa at 4000 m).
- No direct human exposure.
- Must maintain internal pressure at 1 atm for crew.
- Thick steel walls or specialised materials needed.
