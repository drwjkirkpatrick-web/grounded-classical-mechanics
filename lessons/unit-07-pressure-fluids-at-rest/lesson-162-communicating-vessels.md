# Lesson 162: Water Finds Its Level: Communicating Vessels

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate that connected vessels settle at the same level.
2. Explain why water finds its own level.
3. Apply communicating vessel principles to plumbing and irrigation.

## Materials
- Transparent tubes or straws
- Container with water
- Food colouring (optional)
- Tape or stands
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Communicating vessels** *(vyombo vya mawasiliano)* — connected containers that share liquid.
- **Water level** *(kiwango cha maji)* — the height of liquid surface.
- **Equilibrium** *(usawa)* — when pressures balance and liquid stops moving.
- **Siphon** *(sifoni)* — a tube that moves liquid over a hump.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens when you connect two containers with water at different levels?
2. Will water flow from narrow to wide container?
3. Why do water pipes need air vents?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Water flows until levels equalise.
2. Only if levels differ; shape doesn't matter.
3. To prevent vacuum locks and allow air displacement.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show two connected bottles. "If I fill one, what happens to the other?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build communicating vessels.
   - Connect two or more containers with tubes.
   - Fill one container. Observe levels.
   - Add food colouring to one. Observe mixing.
   - Tilt containers. Observe levels.
   - **Key discovery:** Liquid levels equalise regardless of container shape. Pressure at same depth is equal.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Principle:**
     - Pressure at depth h is P = ρgh.
     - Same depth = same pressure.
     - Liquid flows from high pressure to low pressure.
     - Flow stops when pressures equalise (same level).
   - **Shape independence:**
     - Wide or narrow, liquid levels are equal.
     - Volume differs, but height doesn't.
   - **Applications:**
     - Plumbing: water towers create pressure.
     - Irrigation: levelling fields with water channels.
     - Blood pressure: connected vessels in body.
     - Tea pots: spout level equals body level.
   - Kenyan context: Furrow irrigation channels level across fields. Water towers supply buildings.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why levels equalise. Homework: find communicating vessels at home.

## Hands-On Build: Communicating Vessels Demonstration

### Parts
- Plastic bottles — 3 — home
- Straws or tubes — 3 — classroom
- Water — 2 L — tap
- Food colouring — optional — home
- Tape — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Punch holes near bottoms of bottles.
2. Connect with tubes. Seal with tape.
3. Fill one bottle. Watch others fill.
4. Observe levels.
5. Add colouring to one. Watch mixing.
6. **Checkpoint:** Ensure connections are watertight. Leaks prevent equalisation.

### Common Mistakes
- **Leaky connections:** Water escapes. Fix: seal with tape or wax.
- **Different liquids:** Oil and water don't mix. Fix: use same liquid.
- **Not waiting:** Levels take time to equalise. Fix: wait 2–3 minutes.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What happens when connected containers have different levels?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Will water flow from narrow to wide?
3. Why are water tower and tap at the same level in the building?
4. Name two applications of communicating vessels.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Water **flows until levels equalise**.
2. **Only if levels differ** — shape doesn't matter.
3. The **tower creates pressure** that pushes water to that height.
4. Any two: plumbing, irrigation, blood vessels, tea pot.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Three connected vessels have cross-sectional areas 10 cm², 20 cm², and 30 cm². Water is poured until total volume is 1200 cm³. Calculate the height in each vessel.

**Worked Answer:**
Since levels equalise, height h is the same in all.

Total volume = h × (A₁ + A₂ + A₃)
1200 = h × (10 + 20 + 30) = h × 60
h = 1200 / 60 = **20 cm**.

Height is **20 cm in each vessel**.

Volumes:
- Vessel 1: 10 × 20 = 200 cm³.
- Vessel 2: 20 × 20 = 400 cm³.
- Vessel 3: 30 × 20 = 600 cm³.

**Q:** Explain why a water tower must be taller than the highest tap it serves. Calculate the minimum tower height needed to deliver water to a tap 15 m above ground with 50,000 Pa pressure at the tap.

**Worked Answer:**
Pressure at tap = ρg(h_tower − h_tap)
50,000 = 1000 × 10 × (h − 15)
h − 15 = 5
h = **20 m**.

The tower must be at least **20 m tall**.

**Why taller:**
- Water needs pressure to overcome pipe friction.
- Extra height provides reserve pressure.
- Pressure drops during peak usage (multiple taps open).
- Typical design adds 5–10 m above minimum.
