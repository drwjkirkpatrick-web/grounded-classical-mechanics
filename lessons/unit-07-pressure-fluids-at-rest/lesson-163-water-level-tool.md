# Lesson 163: Build: The Hose Water Level (Fundi's Tool)

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a working water level using a hose and water.
2. Use the water level to mark equal heights across a distance.
3. Explain how liquid pressure ensures equal levels.

## Materials
- Garden hose — 5–10 m
- Funnel
- Water with food colouring
- Two sticks or markers
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Water level** *(kiwango cha maji)* — a tool using liquid in a tube to find equal heights.
- **Hose** *(pau)* — a flexible tube.
- **Funnel** *(chombo cha kujaza)* — a cone-shaped tool for pouring into narrow openings.
- **Datum** *(kitovu cha kurejelea)* — a reference height.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a water level used for?
2. Why does water in a hose settle at equal heights?
3. Can you use a water level around corners?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Finding equal heights over distance.
2. Same pressure at same depth (communicating vessels).
3. Yes — flexible hose goes anywhere.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "How do builders make sure a floor is level without a laser?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build and use a water level.
   - Fill hose with coloured water.
   - Hold both ends up. Verify levels match.
   - One person holds one end against a wall.
   - Other person moves around room, marks where water surface is.
   - **Key discovery:** Water finds equal level everywhere. Flexible hose reaches around obstacles.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How it works:**
     - Water in hose is a communicating vessel.
     - Same pressure at same depth → same height.
     - Flexible hose goes around corners, over hills.
   - **Advantages:**
     - Cheap (just a hose).
     - Works over long distances.
     - No batteries or electronics.
     - Goes where lasers can't (around corners).
   - **Use by fundis:**
     - Marking foundation levels.
     - Setting window heights.
     - Checking floor flatness.
     - Landscaping and irrigation.
   - Kenyan context: Builders use hose levels on construction sites. Farmers use them for levelling fields.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups show their levels. Homework: level something at home.

## Hands-On Build: Hose Water Level

### Parts
- Garden hose — 5 m — home
- Funnel — 1 — kitchen
- Water with colour — 1 L — tap
- Sticks — 2 — ground
- Marker — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill hose with coloured water.
2. Hold both ends upright.
3. Verify water levels match.
4. One end at reference point.
5. Move other end, mark water surface.
6. Repeat around room or outside.
7. **Checkpoint:** Remove air bubbles. Bubbles break the liquid column and give wrong readings.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air bubbles:** Breaks continuity. Fix: tap hose to dislodge.
- **Kinks:** Block water flow. Fix: keep hose straight where possible.
- **Leaks:** Water escapes. Fix: check connections.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 20 m hose is filled with water. One end is held 1.5 m above ground. The other end is on ground level. Calculate (a) the height difference, and (b) explain why the water doesn't all flow out the lower end.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Height difference = **1.5 m**.

(b) Water does flow until levels equalise. If you hold one end up, water flows out the lower end until the upper end's water level drops to match.

But if both ends are held above the water level in the hose, water stays in.

In practice: when using a water level, both ends are held up, and the water settles at equal heights in both vertical sections.

**Q:** A builder needs to mark a level foundation line around a corner and up a slope. Explain why a water level works better than a spirit level for this task, and calculate the maximum height difference measurable with a 10 m hose.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why water level is better:**
- Spirit level is rigid — can't go around corners.
- Spirit level has limited length (typically 1–2 m).
- Water level is flexible — follows any path.
- Water level works over 10+ m in one setup.

**Maximum height difference:**
With a 10 m hose, you can measure height differences up to nearly 10 m (limited by hose length and practicality).

For a foundation, typical height differences are 0.1–0.5 m, well within range.
