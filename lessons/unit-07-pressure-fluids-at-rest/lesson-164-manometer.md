# Lesson 164: Build: The U-Tube Manometer

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a U-tube manometer from simple materials.
2. Use it to measure gas pressure differences.
3. Explain how liquid column height relates to pressure.

## Materials
- Transparent tube (U-shaped or bendable)
- Water with food colouring
- Funnel
- Straw or small tube
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Manometer** *(mchomo)* — a device measuring pressure using liquid columns.
- **U-tube** *(nyuzi ya U)* — a tube bent into U shape.
- **Column height** *(urefu wa safu)* — the vertical difference in liquid levels.
- **Gauge pressure** *(shinikizo la kipimo)* — pressure above atmospheric.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a manometer measure?
2. Why use coloured water?
3. If one side is higher by 5 cm, what does this mean?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Pressure difference.
2. Easier to see the liquid level.
3. The connected gas has pressure 500 Pa above the other side.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Blow into a straw in water. "The water rises. Can we measure how hard I blew?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build and test manometers.
   - Fill U-tube with coloured water.
   - Mark reference level.
   - Blow gently into one side. Measure height difference.
   - Blow harder. Measure again.
   - Suck gently. Observe opposite effect.
   - **Key discovery:** Height difference ∝ pressure difference.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How it works:**
     - Pressure difference pushes liquid down on one side, up on other.
     - ΔP = ρgΔh.
     - Δh = height difference (m).
   - **Calibration:**
     - 1 cm water = 100 Pa (since ρg = 10,000 Pa/m).
     - 10 cm water = 1000 Pa.
   - **Uses:**
     - Measuring gas pressure.
     - Checking tyre pressure (relative).
     - Medical: measuring blood pressure (traditional).
     - Industrial: process control.
   - Kenyan context: Clinics use mercury manometers for blood pressure. Jua kali workshops build simple manometers for tyre testing.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains ΔP = ρgΔh. Homework: measure your lung pressure.

## Hands-On Build: U-Tube Manometer

### Parts
- Transparent tube — 1 m — hardware
- Water with colour — 0.5 L — tap
- Funnel — 1 — kitchen
- Straw — 1 — home
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Bend tube into U shape (or use pre-formed).
2. Fill with coloured water.
3. Mark reference level on both sides.
4. Connect straw to one end.
5. Blow into straw. Measure Δh.
6. Calculate ΔP = ρgΔh.
7. **Checkpoint:** Ensure no air bubbles in the U. Bubbles compress, giving false readings.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air bubbles:** Compress under pressure. Fix: fill slowly, tap to dislodge.
- **Not vertical:** Δh must be vertical, not along tube. Fix: hold ruler vertically.
- **Overflow:** Too much water. Fix: fill to ¾ height.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does a manometer measure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* What does 5 cm height difference mean?
3. Calculate pressure for Δh = 8 cm water.
4. Why is water better than mercury for a classroom manometer?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Pressure difference**.
2. Pressure difference of **500 Pa**.
3. ΔP = 1000 × 10 × 0.08 = **800 Pa**.
4. **Safer** (non-toxic), cheaper, visible with colour.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A U-tube manometer using water shows 15 cm difference when connected to a gas supply. Calculate the gauge pressure. If mercury (ρ = 13,600 kg/m³) were used instead, what would the height difference be for the same pressure?

**Worked Answer:**
Water manometer:
ΔP = 1000 × 10 × 0.15 = **1500 Pa**.

Mercury manometer:
Δh = ΔP / (ρg) = 1500 / (13,600 × 10) = 1500 / 136,000 = **0.011 m = 1.1 cm**.

Mercury gives a **much smaller reading** for the same pressure because it's denser.

This is why mercury manometers are compact but dangerous (toxic).

**Q:** Explain how a doctor's blood pressure measurement works using a manometer and stethoscope. Calculate the typical systolic pressure (120 mmHg) in pascals.

**Worked Answer:**
**How it works:**
1. Cuff inflates around arm, blocking blood flow.
2. Doctor releases pressure slowly.
3. Stethoscope detects Korotkoff sounds (blood squirting past cuff).
4. First sound = systolic pressure (heart contracting).
5. Last sound = diastolic pressure (heart relaxing).
6. Mercury column height measures these pressures.

**Systolic pressure:**
120 mmHg = 0.12 mHg.
ΔP = 13,600 × 10 × 0.12 = **16,320 Pa**.

Or using standard conversion: 1 mmHg ≈ 133.3 Pa.
120 mmHg = 120 × 133.3 = **16,000 Pa**.
