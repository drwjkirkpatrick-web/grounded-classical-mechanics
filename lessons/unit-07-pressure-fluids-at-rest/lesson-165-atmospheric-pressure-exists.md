# Lesson 165: Air Has Weight: Atmospheric Pressure Exists

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain that air has weight and exerts pressure.
2. Describe atmospheric pressure as the weight of air above.
3. Estimate the magnitude of atmospheric pressure (≈ 100,000 Pa).

## Materials
- Plastic cup
- Cardboard piece larger than cup
- Water
- Syringe (optional)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Atmospheric pressure** *(shinikizo la anga)* — pressure exerted by the weight of air above a surface.
- **Air column** *(safu ya hewa)* — the vertical column of air from surface to space.
- **Barometer** *(kifaa cha kupima shinikizo)* — instrument measuring atmospheric pressure.
- **Vacuum** *(utupu)* — space with no air.

## Pre-Test
1. Does air have weight?
2. What pushes on you right now?
3. Estimate atmospheric pressure in pascals.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes — about 1.2 kg/m³.
2. Air pressure from all directions.
3. About 100,000 Pa (or 101,325 Pa).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Fill a cup with water, cover with cardboard, invert. "Why doesn't the water fall?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test atmospheric pressure.
   - Inverted cup trick: Cardboard stays due to air pressure pushing up.
   - Crushed bottle: Hot water bottle sealed and cooled collapses.
   - Syringe: Pull plunger, seal end, try to pull further.
   - **Key discovery:** Air pressure is strong and real. It pushes in all directions.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why air has pressure:**
     - Air is matter — it has mass.
     - Gravity pulls air down.
     - The weight of air above creates pressure.
   - **Magnitude:**
     - 1 atm = 101,325 Pa ≈ 100,000 Pa.
     - Equivalent to 10 m of water.
     - Equivalent to 760 mm of mercury.
   - **Why we don't feel it:**
     - Pressure inside body equals outside.
     - Balanced forces feel like nothing.
   - **Demonstrations:**
     - Magdeburg hemispheres (historical).
     - Suction cups.
     - Drinking straws.
   - Kenyan context: Weather changes with pressure. Low pressure brings rain. High pressure brings clear skies.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why we don't get crushed. Homework: find evidence of air pressure at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Does air have weight?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What pushes on you now?
3. Estimate atmospheric pressure in pascals.
4. Why don't we feel atmospheric pressure?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Yes**.
2. **Air pressure** from all directions.
3. **~100,000 Pa** (accept 90,000–110,000).
4. Internal pressure **balances** external pressure.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Calculate the mass of air above 1 m² of ground. If air density averages 1.2 kg/m³ and the atmosphere is effectively 10 km thick, estimate the total mass of Earth's atmosphere given Earth's surface area is 5.1 × 10¹⁴ m².

**Worked Answer:**
Mass above 1 m² = density × volume = 1.2 × (1 × 10,000) = **12,000 kg**.

Total mass = 12,000 × 5.1 × 10¹⁴ = **6.1 × 10¹⁸ kg**.

(Actual: ~5.1 × 10¹⁸ kg. Our estimate is close.)

**Q:** A 5 cm × 5 cm suction cup sticks to a ceiling. Calculate the force holding it up due to atmospheric pressure, and explain why it eventually falls.

**Worked Answer:**
Area = 0.05 × 0.05 = 0.0025 m².
Force = P × A = 100,000 × 0.0025 = **250 N**.

**Why it falls:**
- Air slowly leaks under the seal.
- Internal pressure rises toward atmospheric.
- Net upward force decreases.
- Gravity eventually wins.

A perfect seal (like in space) would hold indefinitely.
