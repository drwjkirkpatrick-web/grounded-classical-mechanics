# Lesson 166: Using Air Pressure: Straws, Syringes and Suckers

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a straw "pulls" liquid using pressure difference.
2. Demonstrate syringe operation using atmospheric pressure.
3. Distinguish between suction and pressure difference.

## Materials
- Drinking straws
- Glass of water
- Syringes (various sizes)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Suction** *(kuvuta)* — the apparent pulling force caused by lower pressure.
- **Pressure difference** *(tofauti ya shinikizo)* — the difference in pressure between two points.
- **Partial vacuum** *(utupu wa sehemu)* — a space with pressure below atmospheric.
- **Plunger** *(kifaa cha kunyima)* — the moving part of a syringe.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a straw lift water?
2. What happens when you pull a syringe plunger?
3. Is "suction" a real force?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Reducing pressure inside lets atmospheric pressure push water up.
2. Creates partial vacuum; atmospheric pressure pushes liquid in.
3. No — it's a pressure difference, not a pulling force.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Suck water through a straw. "Am I pulling water, or is something pushing it?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test pressure effects.
   - Straw in water: suck, observe rise.
   - Straw in sealed bottle: punch hole, try to suck — harder.
   - Syringe: pull plunger with sealed tip — resistance.
   - Syringe in water: pull, water enters.
   - **Key discovery:** Liquids move from high pressure to low pressure. "Suction" is just lower pressure.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Straw physics:**
     - Sucking reduces pressure inside straw.
     - Atmospheric pressure (101,000 Pa) pushes on liquid surface.
     - Pressure difference pushes water up straw.
     - Maximum height: ~10 m (limited by atmospheric pressure).
   - **Syringe:**
     - Pulling plunger increases volume, decreases pressure.
     - Atmospheric pressure pushes liquid in.
     - Pushing plunger decreases volume, increases pressure.
   - **No such thing as suction:**
     - Nature abhors a vacuum — liquids move to fill low-pressure zones.
     - The pushing force is atmospheric pressure.
   - Kenyan context: Traditional water pumps use the same principle. Medical injections use syringes.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why "suction" is misleading. Homework: count how many pressure-driven devices you used today.

## Hands-On Build: Pressure Difference Demonstrations

### Parts
- Straws — 4 — home
- Water — 1 L — tap
- Syringes — 2 — clinic/pharmacy
- Sealed bottle — 1 — home

### Workflow
1. Suck water through straw. Observe rise.
2. Try with sealed bottle (hole punched). Compare difficulty.
3. Pull syringe plunger with sealed tip. Feel resistance.
4. Place syringe in water, pull — water enters.
5. **Checkpoint:** For the sealed bottle test, ensure the hole is small. Large holes equalise pressure too quickly.

### Common Mistakes
- **Thinking suction pulls:** It's pressure pushing. Fix: remember atmospheric pressure.
- **Leak in syringe:** Can't create vacuum. Fix: check seal.
- **Straw too long:** Won't work above 10 m. Fix: use normal straws.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How does a straw lift water?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens when you pull a syringe plunger?
3. Is "suction" a real force?
4. What is the maximum height water can rise in a perfect straw?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Atmospheric pressure** pushes it up when inside pressure drops.
2. Creates **partial vacuum** — atmospheric pressure pushes liquid in.
3. **No** — it's a pressure difference.
4. About **10 m** (limited by 1 atm).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A syringe has a 2 cm² piston. Calculate (a) the force needed to pull the plunger against atmospheric pressure, and (b) the maximum height a perfect water pump could lift water.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) F = P × A = 100,000 × 0.0002 = **20 N**.

(b) h = P / (ρg) = 100,000 / (1000 × 10) = **10 m**.

This is why hand pumps can't lift water more than ~10 m. For deeper wells, pumps must be placed at the bottom.

**Q:** Explain why astronauts can't sip drinks in space without sealed containers, using the concept of atmospheric pressure.

**Worked Answer:**
**In space:**
- No atmosphere = no atmospheric pressure.
- A straw in an open container would not lift liquid.
- Liquid would float out due to surface tension, not rise in straw.

**Why sealed containers are needed:**
- Sealed bag with straw uses internal pressure to push liquid out.
- Astronaut squeezes bag (or uses pressurised container).
- No "suction" possible without atmospheric pressure.

This demonstrates that what we call "sucking" is actually atmospheric pressure pushing.
