# Lesson 167: Altitude and Air Pressure: From the Coast to the Highlands

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain that air pressure decreases with altitude.
2. Calculate approximate pressure at given heights.
3. Relate altitude effects to Kenyan geography.

## Materials
- Map of Kenya showing altitudes
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Altitude** *(kimo cha juu)* — height above sea level.
- **Sea level** *(kiwango cha bahari)* — the reference surface for measuring altitude.
- **Pressure lapse rate** *(kadiri ya kupungua kwa shinikizo)* — how quickly pressure drops with height.
- **Acclimatisation** *(kuzoea)* — body adjusting to lower pressure.

## Pre-Test
1. Does air pressure increase or decrease as you go up?
2. Why is it harder to breathe on Mount Kenya?
3. What is the approximate pressure at 3000 m?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Decreases.
2. Less oxygen pressure in each breath.
3. About 70,000 Pa (sea level is 100,000 Pa).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Ask, "Who has been to the coast? To Nairobi? To the mountains? Did your ears pop?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners research Kenyan altitudes.
   - Sea level (Mombasa): 0 m, ~101,000 Pa.
   - Nairobi: 1800 m, ~80,000 Pa.
   - Nakuru: 1900 m, ~79,000 Pa.
   - Mount Kenya base: 2400 m, ~75,000 Pa.
   - Mount Kenya peak: 5199 m, ~55,000 Pa.
   - **Key discovery:** Pressure drops roughly 12,000 Pa per 1000 m.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why pressure drops:**
     - Less air above = less weight = less pressure.
     - Pressure roughly halves every 5500 m.
   - **Effects on body:**
     - Ears pop: pressure equalises.
     - Shortness of breath: less oxygen per breath.
     - Altitude sickness: above 3000 m for some people.
   - **Weather:**
     - Low pressure areas bring clouds and rain.
     - High pressure brings clear skies.
   - **Formula (approximate):**
     - ΔP ≈ 12 Pa per metre (or 12,000 Pa per km).
   - Kenyan context: Athletes train at altitude (Iten, 2400 m) for better oxygen efficiency.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why athletes train high. Homework: calculate pressure at your home's altitude.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Does pressure increase or decrease with altitude?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why is breathing harder on Mount Kenya?
3. Approximate pressure at 2000 m.
4. Name one Kenyan town at high altitude.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Decreases**.
2. **Less oxygen** pressure in each breath.
3. 101,000 − (12 × 2000) = **77,000 Pa** (approximate).
4. Any: Nairobi, Nakuru, Eldoret, Iten, Nanyuki.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** The atmospheric pressure formula is P = P₀e^(−h/8500) where P₀ = 101,325 Pa. Calculate pressure at (a) Nairobi (1800 m), (b) Mount Kenya peak (5199 m), and (c) the height where pressure is half sea level.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Nairobi: P = 101,325 × e^(−1800/8500) = 101,325 × e^(−0.212) = 101,325 × 0.809 = **81,900 Pa**.

(b) Mount Kenya: P = 101,325 × e^(−5199/8500) = 101,325 × e^(−0.612) = 101,325 × 0.542 = **54,900 Pa**.

(c) Half pressure: 0.5 = e^(−h/8500)
    ln(0.5) = −h/8500
    h = −8500 × (−0.693) = **5890 m**.

**Q:** Explain why airplane cabins are pressurised to ~75,000 Pa (equivalent to 2400 m) even when flying at 10,000 m. What would happen without pressurisation?

**Worked Answer:**
**Why pressurise:**
- At 10,000 m, pressure ≈ 26,000 Pa.
- Humans can't survive below ~50,000 Pa for long.
- 75,000 Pa (2400 m equivalent) is safe and comfortable.

**Without pressurisation:**
- Oxygen starvation (hypoxia) within minutes.
- Blood boils at body temperature below 63,000 Pa.
- Death at 10,000 m within 1–2 minutes.

**Why not sea level:**
- Pressure differential (75,000 vs 26,000) is less stressful on fuselage.
- Less fuel needed to maintain lower pressure.
- Most people tolerate 2400 m equivalent well.
