# Lesson 169: Pascal's Principle: Pressure Travels Through Fluids

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State Pascal's principle: pressure applied to confined fluid transmits equally.
2. Demonstrate Pascal's principle with a syringe system.
3. Explain applications in hydraulic systems.

## Materials
- Two connected syringes (or hydraulic kit)
- Water or oil
- Tubing
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Pascal's principle** *(sheria ya Pascal)* — pressure applied to confined fluid transmits undiminished to every portion.
- **Hydraulic system** *(sisteme ya maji ya shinikizo)* — a system using fluid pressure to transmit force.
- **Confined fluid** *(maji yaliyofungwa)* — fluid in a sealed container.
- **Pressure transmission** *(usambazaji wa shinikizo)* — transfer of pressure through a fluid.

## Pre-Test
1. What does Pascal's principle state?
2. If you push a small syringe, what happens to a connected large syringe?
3. Why are hydraulic brakes used in vehicles?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Pressure applied to confined fluid transmits equally everywhere.
2. It moves, but with less distance (force is multiplied if larger).
3. Fluid pressure transmits force evenly to all wheels.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Push small syringe connected to large syringe. "I push a little, it moves a lot. But something else changed."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test Pascal's principle.
   - Connect two syringes with tubing.
   - Fill with water.
   - Push small syringe, observe large syringe.
   - Push large syringe, observe small syringe.
   - Try with three connected syringes.
   - **Key discovery:** Pressure is equal everywhere. Small force on small area = same pressure as large force on large area.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Pascal's principle:**
     - Pressure applied to confined fluid transmits equally in all directions.
     - P₁ = P₂ → F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂.
   - **Hydraulic advantage:**
     - Small force on small piston creates pressure.
     - Same pressure on large piston creates large force.
     - MA = A₂/A₁.
   - **Applications:**
     - Hydraulic brakes: foot pressure → all wheels.
     - Car jacks: small pump lifts heavy car.
     - Excavators: hydraulic arms.
   - Kenyan context: Vehicle repair shops use hydraulic jacks. Construction equipment uses hydraulics.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains Pascal's principle with an example. Homework: find hydraulic machines in your area.

## Hands-On Build: Pascal's Principle Demonstration

### Parts
- Syringes (10 mL and 50 mL) — 2 — pharmacy
- Tubing — 0.3 m — hardware
- Water — 0.1 L — tap
- Load (book) — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Connect syringes with tubing.
2. Fill with water.
3. Place load on large syringe.
4. Push small syringe to lift load.
5. Measure force on small syringe.
6. Calculate MA.
7. **Checkpoint:** Remove all air bubbles. Air compresses, absorbing pressure instead of transmitting it.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air bubbles:** Compress instead of transmitting. Fix: fill carefully, purge air.
- **Leaks:** Pressure escapes. Fix: ensure tight connections.
- **Wrong ratio:** Expecting large movement. Fix: hydraulics multiply force, not distance.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does Pascal's principle state?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens to a connected large syringe?
3. A 2 cm² piston pushes with 10 N. What pressure is created?
4. Why are hydraulic systems useful?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Pressure transmits **equally** in confined fluid.
2. It moves with **multiplied force** (if larger).
3. P = 10 / 0.0002 = **50,000 Pa**.
4. They **multiply force** using fluid pressure.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A hydraulic car jack has a pump piston of 2 cm² and a lift piston of 200 cm². Calculate (a) the pressure created by a 200 N force on the pump, (b) the lifting force, and (c) the distance the pump must move to lift the car 10 cm.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) P = 200 / 0.0002 = **1,000,000 Pa**.

(b) F = P × A = 1,000,000 × 0.02 = **20,000 N** (lifts 2000 kg).

(c) Volume displaced = A_lift × h_lift = 0.02 × 0.1 = 0.002 m³.
    d_pump = V / A_pump = 0.002 / 0.0002 = **10 m**.

The pump must move 10 m to lift the car 10 cm — hydraulics trade distance for force.

**Q:** Explain why hydraulic brakes are better than cable brakes for heavy vehicles, using Pascal's principle and the concept of equal pressure distribution.

**Worked Answer:**
**Hydraulic brakes:**
- Foot pedal creates pressure in brake fluid.
- Same pressure reaches all wheel cylinders.
- Equal braking force on all wheels.
- No stretching or friction losses (as in cables).

**Cable brakes:**
- Mechanical linkage stretches over time.
- Unequal force to wheels (cable length differences).
- Friction in cable housing reduces force.
- Requires frequent adjustment.

**Why hydraulics are better for heavy vehicles:**
- Heavy vehicles need enormous braking force.
- Hydraulics multiply pedal force easily.
- Equal distribution prevents skidding.
- Self-adjusting as pads wear.
