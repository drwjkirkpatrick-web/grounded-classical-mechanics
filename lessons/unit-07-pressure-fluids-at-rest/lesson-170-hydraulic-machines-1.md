# Lesson 170: Hydraulic Machines I: The Syringe Lift

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a hydraulic lift using syringes.
2. Measure the mechanical advantage of a hydraulic system.
3. Explain the trade-off between force and distance in hydraulics.

## Materials
- Syringes of different sizes (10 mL, 20 mL, 50 mL)
- Plastic tubing
- Water
- Load (books or stones)
- Ruler
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Hydraulic lift** *(lifti ya maji ya shinikizo)* — a machine using fluid pressure to raise loads.
- **Piston** *(kifaa cha kunyima)* — the moving part that pushes fluid.
- **Cylinder** *(mchedwa)* — the chamber containing fluid and piston.
- **Displacement** *(kukimbia)* — the volume of fluid moved.

## Pre-Test
1. What simple machines use Pascal's principle?
2. Why does a small pump lift a heavy car?
3. What is the trade-off in hydraulic systems?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Hydraulic brakes, jacks, presses.
2. Small force creates pressure; large piston multiplies force.
3. Force is multiplied, but distance is reduced.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "How does a small hand pump lift a 2-tonne car?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build hydraulic lifts.
   - Connect small syringe (pump) to large syringe (lift).
   - Place load on large syringe.
   - Pump small syringe to lift load.
   - Measure: pump force, lift force, pump distance, lift distance.
   - **Key discovery:** Small force, long distance = large force, short distance.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Hydraulic lift operation:**
     - Pump piston: small area, small force, long stroke.
     - Lift piston: large area, large force, short stroke.
     - Pressure same everywhere: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂.
   - **MA calculation:**
     - MA = F₂/F₁ = A₂/A₁.
     - If A₂ = 10× A₁, MA = 10.
   - **Distance trade-off:**
     - Volume conserved: A₁ × d₁ = A₂ × d₂.
     - If A₂ = 10× A₁, then d₂ = d₁/10.
     - You push 10 cm, load rises 1 cm.
   - Kenyan context: Jua kali garages use hydraulic jacks. Construction uses hydraulic excavators.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups calculate their MA. Homework: find hydraulic machines in your community.

## Hands-On Build: Syringe Hydraulic Lift

### Parts
- Small syringe (10 mL) — 1 — pharmacy
- Large syringe (50 mL) — 1 — pharmacy
- Tubing — 0.3 m — hardware
- Water — 0.1 L — tap
- Load — 1 — classroom
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Connect syringes with tubing.
2. Fill with water.
3. Place load on large syringe.
4. Push small syringe to lift load.
5. Measure pump force and distance.
6. Measure lift force and distance.
7. Calculate MA and verify with area ratio.
8. **Checkpoint:** Ensure no air in system. One bubble can absorb all the pressure.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air in system:** Prevents pressure transmission. Fix: bleed all air.
- **Leaking connections:** Pressure escapes. Fix: use tight-fitting tubing.
- **Wrong expectation:** Expecting large movement. Fix: hydraulics trade distance for force.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What machines use Pascal's principle?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why does small pump lift heavy car?
3. A pump piston is 1 cm², lift piston is 25 cm². Calculate MA.
4. If pump moves 10 cm, how far does lift move?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Hydraulic brakes, jacks, presses**.
2. Small force creates **pressure**; large piston **multiplies force**.
3. MA = 25/1 = **25**.
4. d_lift = (1 × 10) / 25 = **0.4 cm**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A hydraulic jack lifts a 1500 kg car. The pump piston has diameter 1 cm; the lift piston has diameter 10 cm. Calculate (a) the pressure needed, (b) the pump force, (c) the pump distance for 5 cm lift, and (d) the work done by the pump.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Load = 1500 × 10 = 15,000 N.
    Lift area = π × 0.05² = 0.00785 m².
    P = 15,000 / 0.00785 = **1,910,000 Pa**.

(b) Pump area = π × 0.005² = 0.0000785 m².
    F = 1,910,000 × 0.0000785 = **150 N**.

(c) Volume = 0.00785 × 0.05 = 0.000393 m³.
    d_pump = 0.000393 / 0.0000785 = **5 m**.

(d) Work = 150 × 5 = **750 J**.
    Or: Work = 15,000 × 0.05 = **750 J**. ✓

**Q:** Explain why hydraulic systems are preferred over mechanical levers for heavy lifting in confined spaces, using the concepts of force multiplication and compact design.

**Worked Answer:**
**Force multiplication:**
- Hydraulics easily achieve MA = 50–100.
- Levers need very long arms for the same MA.
- A 1 m hydraulic cylinder can lift tonnes; a lever would need 50 m.

**Compact design:**
- Hydraulic cylinders are slim and fit tight spaces.
- Levers need clearance for arc movement.
- Hydraulics can bend around obstacles (flexible hoses).

**Smooth operation:**
- Fluid provides damping — no jerky motion.
- Levers can bounce and shock-load.

**Maintenance:**
- Hydraulic seals last years.
- Lever pivot points wear and need frequent greasing.
