# Lesson 171: Hydraulic Machines II: Brakes, Presses and Excavators

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify hydraulic systems in common machines.
2. Explain how hydraulic brakes, presses, and excavators work.
3. Compare hydraulic systems to mechanical alternatives.

## Materials
- Diagrams of hydraulic systems
- Photos of excavators, brakes, presses
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Hydraulic brake** *(breki ya maji ya shinikizo)* — a brake system using fluid pressure.
- **Hydraulic press** *(pressi ya maji ya shinikizo)* — a machine using fluid pressure to compress or shape materials.
- **Excavator** *(chimbuzi)* — a machine with hydraulic arms for digging.
- **Master cylinder** *(mchedwa mkuu)* — the piston that creates pressure in a brake system.

## Pre-Test
1. Name three machines that use hydraulics.
2. How do hydraulic brakes work?
3. Why are excavators hydraulic?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Brakes, presses, excavators, jacks, lifts.
2. Foot pushes fluid, pressure reaches all wheels.
3. Hydraulics provide enormous force in compact arms.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show excavator video. "How does that tiny cabin move tonnes of earth?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse hydraulic machines.
   - Trace fluid path in brake diagrams.
   - Calculate force multiplication in excavator arms.
   - Compare hydraulic press to mechanical press.
   - **Key discovery:** Hydraulics multiply force, distribute it evenly, and fit in compact spaces.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Hydraulic brakes:**
     - Foot pedal → master cylinder.
     - Fluid pressure → wheel cylinders.
     - Equal pressure to all brakes → even stopping.
     - Self-adjusting as pads wear.
   - **Hydraulic press:**
     - Small pump → large cylinder.
     - Crushes cars, shapes metal, presses oil.
     - Force = pressure × area (enormous with large area).
   - **Excavators:**
     - Multiple hydraulic cylinders control arm, bucket, rotation.
     - Precise control via fluid flow rate.
     - Can lift 10× machine's weight.
   - Kenyan context: Construction sites use excavators. Jua kali workshops use hydraulic presses. Vehicle garages service brake systems.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains hydraulic brakes. Homework: find hydraulic machines in your community.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Name three hydraulic machines.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How do hydraulic brakes work?
3. Why are hydraulic excavators stronger than cable-operated ones?
4. Name one advantage of hydraulic systems.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any three: **brakes, presses, excavators, jacks, lifts**.
2. Foot pushes **fluid**, pressure reaches **all wheels**.
3. **Fluid pressure** multiplies force and provides **smooth, precise control**.
4. Any: force multiplication, even distribution, compact design, smooth operation.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** An excavator's bucket cylinder has a 10 cm diameter piston and operates at 20 MPa. Calculate the lifting force. If the arm is 3 m long and the bucket is at the end, calculate the torque about the pivot.

**Worked Answer:**
Area = π × 0.05² = 0.00785 m².
Force = 20,000,000 × 0.00785 = **157,000 N**.

Torque = 157,000 × 3 = **471,000 Nm**.

This enormous torque can dig through rock and lift heavy loads.

**Q:** Compare hydraulic and pneumatic systems for construction equipment. When would you choose each?

**Worked Answer:**
**Hydraulic (liquid):**
- Higher force (liquids don't compress).
- Precise control (position holds).
- Self-lubricating (oil in system).
- Heavy and messy (leaks are oily).
- Best for: excavators, presses, brakes.

**Pneumatic (gas):**
- Faster movement (air compresses, stores energy).
- Cleaner (air leaks harmless).
- Less precise (springy, bouncy).
- Lower force.
- Best for: drills, hammers, paint sprayers, assembly lines.

**Choice:**
- Need force and precision → hydraulic.
- Need speed and cleanliness → pneumatic.
- Mixed: Some machines use both (hydraulics for power, pneumatics for rapid cycling).
