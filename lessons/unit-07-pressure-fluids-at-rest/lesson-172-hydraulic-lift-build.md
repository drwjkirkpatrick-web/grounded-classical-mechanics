# Lesson 172: Build: The Hydraulic Lift Challenge I

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** hydraulic-lift

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a hydraulic lift using syringes and tubing.
2. Calculate the expected mechanical advantage.
3. Plan materials and assembly for a working prototype.

## Materials
- Syringes (various sizes)
- Plastic tubing
- Water
- Base material (wood or cardboard)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Design brief** *(maelezo ya ubunifu)* — a statement of the problem and requirements.
- **Prototype** *(mfano wa kwanza)* — a first model for testing.
- **Mechanical advantage** *(faida ya kimekaniki)* — the ratio of output force to input force.
- **Assembly plan** *(mpango wa kujenga)* — step-by-step instructions for building.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the goal of a hydraulic lift?
2. What materials do you need?
3. How will you calculate MA?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Lift heavy loads with small force.
2. Syringes, tubing, water, base.
3. MA = area of lift piston / area of pump piston.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups review designs.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Your challenge: lift a 2 kg load using only syringes and water."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design hydraulic lifts.
   - Choose syringe sizes.
   - Calculate expected MA.
   - Draw assembly plan.
   - List materials.
   - **Key discovery:** Design determines performance. Planning prevents problems.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Design considerations:**
     - Syringe size ratio determines MA.
     - Base must be stable.
     - Tubing must be leak-free.
     - Load platform must be level.
   - **Calculation:**
     - MA = A₂/A₁.
     - For 2 kg load (20 N), need 20 N output.
     - If MA = 10, input force = 2 N.
   - **Assembly tips:**
     - Start with stable base.
     - Mount pump syringe vertically.
     - Connect tubing securely.
     - Test for leaks before adding load.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present designs. Homework: gather materials.

## Hands-On Build: Hydraulic Lift Design

### Parts
- Syringes — various — pharmacy
- Tubing — 0.5 m — hardware
- Water — 0.2 L — tap
- Base — 1 — classroom
- Paper — 5 sheets — classroom

### Workflow
1. Choose syringe sizes.
2. Calculate MA.
3. Draw assembly plan.
4. List materials.
5. **Checkpoint:** Verify calculations. A design with MA = 2 won't lift 2 kg with finger pressure.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong ratio:** Too small MA. Fix: choose larger lift syringe.
- **Unstable base:** Tips over. Fix: wide, heavy base.
- **Ignoring leaks:** Pressure lost. Fix: plan for tight seals.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a hydraulic lift to raise a 5 kg load 5 cm using a 10 mL syringe as the pump. Calculate (a) the required lift syringe size, (b) the pump distance, and (c) the input force.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Load = 50 N.
    For comfortable finger force (~5 N):
    MA = 50/5 = 10.
    A_lift = 10 × A_pump.
    If pump = 10 mL ≈ 2 cm²:
    A_lift = 20 cm².
    Diameter = √(4 × 20/π) = **5 cm**.

(b) Volume = 20 × 5 = 100 cm³ (but this is wrong — lift volume = A × h = 20 × 5 = 100 cm³).
    Wait: pump volume = 10 mL = 10 cm³.
    So pump must be stroked 100/10 = **10 times**.
    Or use larger pump.

(c) Input force = **5 N**.

**Q:** Compare the efficiency of a hydraulic lift (85%) to a lever system (90%) for lifting the same load. Calculate the input work for each to lift 100 kg by 0.1 m.

**Worked Answer:**
Output work = 1000 × 0.1 = 100 J.

Hydraulic: Input = 100 / 0.85 = **117.6 J**.
Lever: Input = 100 / 0.90 = **111.1 J**.

The lever is more efficient but can't match the hydraulic's force multiplication in compact space.
