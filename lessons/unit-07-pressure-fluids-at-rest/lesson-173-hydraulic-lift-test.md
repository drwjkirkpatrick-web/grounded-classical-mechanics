# Lesson 173: Build: The Hydraulic Lift Challenge II — Test and Measure

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** hydraulic-lift

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build and test a hydraulic lift prototype.
2. Measure actual vs calculated mechanical advantage.
3. Compete to lift the heaviest load with the smallest input force.

## Materials
- Materials from Lesson 172
- Weights or stones
- Spring balance or scale
- Ruler
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Prototype** *(mfano wa kwanza)* — a first working model.
- **Testing** *(kupima)* — trying the machine to see if it works.
- **Competition** *(mashindano)* — comparing performance against others.
- **Optimisation** *(kuboresha)* — improving design for better performance.

## Pre-Test
1. What will you measure during testing?
2. How will you know if your lift works?
3. What could go wrong?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Input force, output force, lift distance, pump distance.
2. It lifts the load smoothly without leaking.
3. Leaks, air bubbles, unstable base, wrong calculations.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups prepare builds.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Build day! May the best lift win."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build and test lifts.
   - Assemble from Lesson 172 plans.
   - Test for leaks.
   - Add load incrementally.
   - Measure input force and output force.
   - Calculate actual MA.
   - Compare to predicted MA.
   - **Key discovery:** Real machines have friction and leaks. Efficiency matters.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Testing protocol:**
     - Start with light load (0.5 kg).
     - Increase by 0.5 kg until failure.
     - Record maximum load lifted.
     - Record input force at each load.
   - **Competition rules:**
     - Heaviest load lifted wins.
     - Tiebreaker: lowest input force.
     - Disqualification: leaks, air bubbles, external support.
   - **Analysis:**
     - Why did some lifts fail?
     - What design features worked best?
     - How close was actual MA to predicted?

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Announce winners. Discuss lessons learned. Homework: write a reflection.

## Hands-On Build: Hydraulic Lift Test

### Parts

### Workflow
1. Assemble lift.
2. Test for leaks.
3. Add load incrementally.
4. Measure input force at each load.
5. Calculate actual MA = output/input.
6. Compare to predicted MA.
7. **Checkpoint:** Before competition, purge all air. One bubble costs you the win.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air bubbles:** Reduce pressure transmission. Fix: bleed system.
- **Leaks:** Pressure lost. Fix: tighten connections.
- **Overloading:** Exceeds design. Fix: know your limit.

1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What did you measure?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your lift achieved 70% of predicted MA. Identify three possible causes and calculate the efficiency.

**Worked Answer:**
**Causes:**
1. **Friction in syringe:** Rubber seal drags on walls.
2. **Air bubbles:** Compress instead of transmitting pressure.
3. **Leaks:** Fluid escapes, reducing pressure.
4. **Tube expansion:** Flexible tubing stretches under pressure.

**Efficiency:**
η = (actual MA / predicted MA) × 100% = 70%.

**Improvement:**
- Use rigid tubing.
- Grease syringe seals.
- Ensure perfect seal at connections.

**Q:** Design a competition scoring system that rewards both high MA and high efficiency. Explain your weighting.

**Worked Answer:**
**Scoring formula:**
Score = (MA_actual × 0.6) + (η × 0.4).

**Rationale:**
- MA (60%): Primary goal is force multiplication.
- Efficiency (40%): Well-built machines should also be efficient.
- This rewards both strong lifts and good construction.

**Example:**
Team A: MA = 8, η = 70% → Score = 4.8 + 28 = 32.8.
Team B: MA = 10, η = 50% → Score = 6.0 + 20 = 26.0.

Team A wins despite lower MA because of better efficiency.
