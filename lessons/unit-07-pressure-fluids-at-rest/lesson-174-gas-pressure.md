# Lesson 174: Pressure in Gases: Particles on the Attack

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain that gas pressure is caused by particles colliding with surfaces.
2. Describe how temperature and volume affect gas pressure.
3. Relate gas pressure to everyday phenomena.

## Materials
- Balloon
- Syringe (sealed)
- Hot and cold water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Gas pressure** *(shinikizo la gesi)* — force per unit area from gas particles colliding.
- **Particle** *(chembe)* — a tiny piece of matter.
- **Collision** *(mgongano)* — when a particle hits a surface.
- **Kinetic theory** *(nadharia ya harakati)* — the idea that particles are always moving.

## Pre-Test
1. What causes gas pressure?
2. What happens to pressure when you squeeze a balloon?
3. What happens to pressure when you heat a sealed container?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Particles colliding with surfaces.
2. Pressure increases (same particles, less space, more collisions).
3. Pressure increases (particles move faster, collide harder).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Bounce a ball against a wall. "Imagine millions of invisible balls hitting this wall."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test gas behaviour.
   - Squeeze sealed syringe: pressure rises (harder to compress).
   - Heat sealed syringe in hot water: plunger moves out.
   - Cool in cold water: plunger moves in.
   - **Key discovery:** Gas pressure depends on temperature and volume. Particles move faster when hot.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Kinetic theory:**
     - Gas particles are in constant random motion.
     - They collide with container walls.
     - Each collision exerts a tiny force.
     - Billions of collisions = measurable pressure.
   - **Temperature effect:**
     - Hotter = faster particles.
     - Faster = harder collisions.
     - Harder collisions = higher pressure.
   - **Volume effect:**
     - Smaller volume = particles closer together.
     - More collisions per area.
     - Higher pressure.
   - **Everyday examples:**
     - Tyre pressure rises on hot days.
     - Aerosol cans warn against heat (pressure builds).
     - Balloons shrink in cold, expand in heat.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why tyre pressure changes with weather. Homework: observe a sealed container in sun and shade.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What causes gas pressure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens when you squeeze a sealed container?
3. What happens to tyre pressure on a hot day?
4. Why do aerosol cans warn against heat?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Particles colliding** with surfaces.
2. **Pressure increases** (less volume, more collisions).
3. **Increases** (particles move faster).
4. **Pressure builds up** and can **explode** the can.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A sealed syringe contains gas at 100,000 Pa and 20°C. It is heated to 60°C at constant volume. Calculate the new pressure using the relationship P ∝ T (absolute temperature).

**Worked Answer:**
T₁ = 20 + 273 = 293 K.
T₂ = 60 + 273 = 333 K.

P₂ = P₁ × (T₂/T₁) = 100,000 × (333/293) = **113,650 Pa**.

Pressure increases by ~14%.

**Q:** Explain why a pressure cooker cooks food faster, using the concepts of temperature, pressure, and boiling point.

**Worked Answer:**
**Normal boiling:** Water boils at 100°C at 1 atm. Food cooks at 100°C.

**Pressure cooker:**
- Sealed lid traps steam.
- Pressure builds to ~200,000 Pa (2 atm).
- Boiling point rises to ~120°C.
- Food cooks at 120°C instead of 100°C.
- Higher temperature = faster cooking.

**Why faster:**
- Chemical reactions (cooking) speed up with temperature.
- 20°C higher ≈ 2× faster cooking rate.
- Also: steam transfers heat efficiently.
