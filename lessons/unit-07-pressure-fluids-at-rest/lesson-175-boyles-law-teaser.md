# Lesson 175: Squeezing Air: A Qualitative Boyle's Law

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate that gas pressure increases as volume decreases (Boyle's Law qualitatively).
2. Measure and graph pressure vs volume for a fixed amount of gas.
3. Predict pressure changes for given volume changes.

## Materials
- Sealed syringe
- Pressure gauge (or U-tube manometer from Lesson 164)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Boyle's Law** *(sheria ya Boyle)* — pressure and volume are inversely proportional at constant temperature.
- **Inverse proportion** *(kinyume cha moja kwa moja)* — when one increases, the other decreases proportionally.
- **Fixed mass** *(maziwa yaliyokaa)* — gas amount doesn't change.
- **Isothermal** *(isothemu)* — at constant temperature.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to pressure when you squeeze a balloon?
2. If volume halves, what happens to pressure?
3. What must stay constant for Boyle's Law?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Pressure increases.
2. Pressure doubles.
3. Temperature and amount of gas.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "I'm going to squeeze this sealed syringe. Predict what happens to the pressure."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test Boyle's Law.
   - Seal syringe at various volumes.
   - Measure pressure (or feel resistance).
   - Record: volume, pressure (or relative pressure).
   - Graph P vs V.
   - **Key discovery:** Smaller volume = higher pressure. P × V ≈ constant.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Boyle's Law:** P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (at constant temperature).
   - **Why it works:**
     - Same number of particles.
     - Smaller space = more collisions per area.
     - More collisions = higher pressure.
   - **Graph:**
     - P vs V: curve (hyperbola).
     - P vs 1/V: straight line.
   - **Applications:**
     - Breathing: lungs expand (volume up, pressure down), air rushes in.
     - Scuba diving: compressed air tanks.
     - Syringes: pulling back draws liquid in.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains Boyle's Law. Homework: find three Boyle's Law applications.

## Hands-On Build: Boyle's Law Demonstration

### Parts
- Sealed syringe — 1 — pharmacy
- Pressure gauge — 1 — hardware
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Set syringe to 50 mL.
2. Seal end.
3. Measure pressure.
4. Compress to 40 mL. Measure.
5. Compress to 30 mL. Measure.
6. Compress to 20 mL. Measure.
7. Graph P vs V and P vs 1/V.
8. **Checkpoint:** Keep syringe at room temperature. Warming from hand heat changes pressure independently.

### Common Mistakes
- **Temperature changes:** Hand heat warms gas. Fix: handle minimally, wait between measurements.
- **Leaks:** Gas escapes. Fix: ensure perfect seal.
- **Not sealing:** Can't build pressure. Fix: cap the syringe.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What happens when you squeeze a sealed container?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* If volume halves, pressure?
3. A gas occupies 2 L at 100 kPa. What is pressure at 1 L (constant temperature)?
4. Why does a scuba tank hold so much air?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Pressure increases**.
2. **Doubles**.
3. P₂ = 100 × (2/1) = **200 kPa**.
4. **Compressed** to small volume at high pressure.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A gas at 100 kPa occupies 5 L. It is compressed to 2 L at constant temperature. Calculate the new pressure. If the container can withstand 300 kPa, is it safe?

**Worked Answer:**
P₂ = 100 × (5/2) = **250 kPa**.

Yes, **safe** (250 < 300 kPa).

**Q:** Explain how breathing demonstrates Boyle's Law, calculating the pressure change when chest volume increases from 3 L to 4 L at atmospheric pressure.

**Worked Answer:**
**Breathing mechanics:**
- Inhale: diaphragm contracts, chest volume increases.
- By Boyle's Law: volume up → pressure down.
- Lung pressure drops below atmospheric.
- Air rushes in from high to low pressure.

**Calculation:**
P₁ = 101,325 Pa, V₁ = 3 L.
V₂ = 4 L.

P₂ = 101,325 × (3/4) = **75,994 Pa**.

Pressure drop = 101,325 − 75,994 = **25,331 Pa**.

This pressure difference drives air into lungs.
