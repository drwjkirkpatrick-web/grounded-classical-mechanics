# Lesson 176: Levers vs Hydraulics: Choosing a Force Multiplier

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Compare levers and hydraulic systems as force multipliers.
2. Identify situations where each is more appropriate.
3. Evaluate machines using criteria: force, distance, precision, cost.

## Materials
- Diagrams of levers and hydraulic systems
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Force multiplier** *(zidishi la nguvu)* — a machine that increases output force.
- **Leverage** *(faida ya bakora)* — mechanical advantage from lever arms.
- **Hydraulic advantage** *(faida ya maji ya shinikizo)* — force multiplication using fluid pressure.
- **Trade-off** *(kubadiliana)* — exchanging one benefit for another.

## Pre-Test
1. Which multiplies force more: a lever or hydraulics?
2. Why might you choose a lever over hydraulics?
3. What is the main disadvantage of hydraulics?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Hydraulics can achieve higher MA.
2. Simpler, cheaper, no leaks.
3. Leaks, mess, cost, complexity.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "You need to lift a cow. Do you use a lever or hydraulics? Why?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners compare systems.
   - List 5 tasks: lift car, open bottle, dig hole, brake bicycle, crush can.
   - Decide: lever or hydraulics for each.
   - Justify with criteria.
   - **Key discovery:** Each system has strengths. Choice depends on task, cost, and environment.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Levers:**
     - Simple, cheap, reliable.
     - MA = effort arm / load arm.
     - Limited by arm length (impractical beyond MA ~10).
     - No maintenance (if solid).
   - **Hydraulics:**
     - Very high MA (50–100+).
     - Compact for the force produced.
     - Smooth, precise control.
     - Expensive, complex, leaks.
   - **When to choose:**
     - **Lever:** Simple tasks, portable, low cost.
     - **Hydraulics:** Heavy loads, precision needed, stationary.
   - **Combined systems:**
     - Excavators: hydraulic cylinders + lever arms.
     - Brakes: hydraulic fluid + lever pedal.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner gives a new scenario and the class votes. Homework: analyse a machine as lever + hydraulic.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Which can achieve higher MA?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why choose a lever?
3. Give one advantage of hydraulics over levers.
4. Name a machine that combines both.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Hydraulics**.
2. **Simpler**, cheaper, no leaks.
3. Any: higher MA, compact, smooth control.
4. **Excavator**, car brakes, hydraulic press.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A task requires lifting 10,000 N. Compare (a) a lever with MA = 8, and (b) a hydraulic system with MA = 50. Calculate input force, input distance, and efficiency for each if output distance is 0.1 m and lever efficiency is 95% while hydraulic is 80%.

**Worked Answer:**
**(a) Lever:**
- Input force = 10,000/8 = **1250 N**.
- Input distance = 0.1 × 8 = **0.8 m**.
- Work input = 1250 × 0.8 / 0.95 = **1053 J**.

**(b) Hydraulic:**
- Input force = 10,000/50 = **200 N**.
- Input distance = 0.1 × 50 = **5 m**.
- Work input = 200 × 5 / 0.80 = **1250 J**.

**Comparison:**
- Lever needs more force but less distance.
- Hydraulic needs less force but much more distance.
- Lever is more efficient (95% vs 80%).
- Hydraulic is easier to operate (200 N vs 1250 N).

**Q:** Design a village water-lifting system. Justify your choice of lever, hydraulic, or combination, considering cost, maintenance, and available materials.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design: Treadle pump (lever + simple hydraulics)**

**Lever component:**
- Foot treadle provides input force.
- MA = 3–4 from lever arms.
- Simple wood construction.

**Hydraulic component:**
- Piston in cylinder lifts water.
- No complex seals needed (low pressure).
- Leather washers (traditional, local).

**Justification:**
- **Cost:** Wood and leather are locally available. No expensive seals or oil.
- **Maintenance:** Villagers can repair leather washers. No specialist tools.
- **Materials:** Wood from local trees, leather from old shoes or hides.
- **Efficiency:** Moderate (60–70%), but sufficient for village water needs.
- **Portability:** Can be moved between wells.

This is the traditional Indian **treadle pump** design, widely used in Africa.
