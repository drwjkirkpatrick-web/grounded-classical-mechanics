# Lesson 177: Why Water Tanks Stand Tall: Gravity Supply

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain that water height creates pressure for distribution.
2. Calculate the pressure at a tap from a water tower.
3. Evaluate water tower placement for a village.

## Materials
- Photos of water towers
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Water tower** *(mnara wa maji)* — an elevated tank that creates pressure.
- **Gravity supply** *(usambazaji wa mvutano)* — water distribution using height difference.
- **Distribution pressure** *(shinikizo la usambazaji)* — pressure in pipes from tower height.
- **Head** *(kimo)* — height of water above a point.

## Pre-Test
1. Why are water towers tall?
2. Does a higher tank give more pressure?
3. Calculate pressure from a 15 m tank.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Height creates pressure for distribution.
2. Yes — pressure = ρgh.
3. P = 1000 × 10 × 15 = 150,000 Pa.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Show water tower photo. "Why build a tower instead of a ground tank?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners calculate water pressures.
   - For various tower heights: 5 m, 10 m, 15 m, 20 m, 30 m.
   - Calculate pressure at base.
   - Calculate how high water can rise in a pipe.
   - **Key discovery:** Each metre of height gives 10,000 Pa of pressure. Tall towers provide strong flow.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why towers are tall:**
     - P = ρgh.
     - Each metre = 10,000 Pa.
     - Typical tap needs 100,000–200,000 Pa.
     - So tower must be 10–20 m tall.
   - **Distribution:**
     - Water flows from high pressure to low pressure.
     - Multiple taps share the same tower.
     - Pressure drops with distance (friction in pipes).
   - **Alternatives:**
     - Ground tank + pump: needs electricity, breaks down.
     - Gravity: simple, reliable, no moving parts.
   - Kenyan context: Nairobi water towers, village elevated tanks, roof tanks in buildings.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why a 5 m tank gives weak pressure. Homework: find water towers near your area.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Why are water towers tall?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Does higher tank give more pressure?
3. Calculate pressure from a 12 m tank.
4. Name one advantage of gravity over pumped supply.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Height creates pressure**.
2. **Yes**.
3. P = 1000 × 10 × 12 = **120,000 Pa**.
4. Any: **no electricity needed**, reliable, simple.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A village needs water supplied to taps 8 m above ground. The water source is a spring 2 m above ground. Design a system and calculate the minimum tower height needed if each tap requires 150,000 Pa.

**Worked Answer:**
Pressure needed = 150,000 Pa.

Minimum head: h = P/(ρg) = 150,000/(1000 × 10) = **15 m**.

**System:**
- Pump spring water to tower.
- Tower height above taps: 15 m.
- If taps are 8 m above ground, tower must be 8 + 15 = **23 m** above ground.

Or: pump directly (no tower) with pressure of 150,000 + 80,000 (for 8 m rise) = 230,000 Pa.

But gravity tower is simpler and more reliable.

**Q:** Compare the energy efficiency of gravity-fed vs pumped water systems for a village of 500 people using 100 L per person per day. Assume pumped system is 60% efficient and gravity system is 90% efficient.

**Worked Answer:**
Daily water = 500 × 100 = 50,000 L = 50 m³.
Mass = 50,000 kg.

**Gravity system:**
- Energy = mgh (to lift to tower).
- If tower is 15 m: E = 50,000 × 10 × 15 = **7,500,000 J**.
- At 90%: Input = 7,500,000/0.9 = **8,333,333 J**.

**Pumped system:**
- Needs same lift but pumps continuously.
- At 60%: Input = 7,500,000/0.6 = **12,500,000 J**.

**Comparison:**
- Gravity uses **33% less energy**.
- Gravity has no ongoing electricity cost.
- Pumped system has maintenance costs (pumps, generators).

Gravity is better for villages with reliable water source above taps.
