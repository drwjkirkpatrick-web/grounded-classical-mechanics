# Lesson 178: Siphon Theory: The River That Climbs a Hill

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a siphon moves liquid over a hump.
2. Demonstrate a working siphon with a tube and water.
3. Identify where siphons are used in daily life.

## Materials
- Flexible tube (1–2 m)
- Two containers
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Siphon** *(sifoni)* — a tube that moves liquid from higher to lower level over a hump.
- **Priming** *(kujaza)* — filling the tube with liquid to start flow.
- **Hump** *(kilima cha ndani)* — the highest point in a siphon tube.
- **Atmospheric pressure limit** *(kikomo cha shinikizo la anga)* — siphons can't lift water more than ~10 m.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a siphon move water over a hill?
2. Why must the outlet be lower than the source?
3. What is the maximum height a siphon can lift water?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Atmospheric pressure pushes water up, gravity pulls it down.
2. Gravity drives the flow downward.
3. About 10 m (atmospheric pressure limit).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Can water flow uphill? Let's find out."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build siphons.
   - Fill tube with water (priming).
   - Place one end in high container, other in lower container.
   - Let water flow.
   - Raise the hump. Observe when flow stops.
   - **Key discovery:** Water flows as long as outlet is lower than source. Hump height limited by atmospheric pressure.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How siphons work:**
     - Tube is filled with water.
     - Outlet is lower than source.
     - Gravity pulls water column downward on the long side.
     - This creates partial vacuum at top.
     - Atmospheric pressure pushes more water up the short side.
     - Continuous flow results.
   - **Limit:**
     - Can't lift more than ~10 m (atmospheric pressure).
     - Practical limit: 3–5 m for reliable operation.
   - **Applications:**
     - Emptying fish tanks.
    - Transferring fuel between drums.
     - Irrigation systems.
     - Traditional water systems.
   - Kenyan context: Farmers siphon water from raised tanks to fields. Mechanics siphon fuel.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why a siphon can't lift water to the moon. Homework: find siphons in your area.

## Hands-On Build: Siphon Demonstration

### Parts
- Flexible tube — 1.5 m — hardware
- Two containers — 2 — classroom
- Water — 2 L — tap
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill tube with water.
2. Place one end in high container.
3. Keep other end lower.
4. Release. Water flows.
5. Raise hump until flow stops.
6. Measure maximum hump height.
7. **Checkpoint:** Ensure tube is fully primed. Air bubbles break the siphon.

### Common Mistakes
- **Not primed:** Air in tube prevents flow. Fix: fill completely before starting.
- **Outlet too high:** Must be below source. Fix: lower the container.
- **Kinks:** Block flow. Fix: keep tube smooth.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain why a siphon cannot work in a vacuum, and calculate the maximum theoretical siphon height on Earth using atmospheric pressure = 101,325 Pa.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why no vacuum:**
- Siphons rely on atmospheric pressure pushing water up.
- In vacuum, no atmospheric pressure exists.
- No force to push water up the short leg.
- Siphon stops working.

**Maximum height:**
P = ρgh → h = P/(ρg) = 101,325/(1000 × 10) = **10.1 m**.

In practice, siphons work reliably only up to **7–8 m** due to:
- Water vapour forming at low pressure.
- Friction in tube.
- Air bubbles from dissolved gases.

**Q:** A farmer wants to siphon water from a river (2 m above ground) over a 3 m hill to a field 1 m above ground. Will the siphon work? Explain.

**Worked Answer:**
**Source:** 2 m above ground.
**Hump:** 3 m above ground.
**Outlet:** 1 m above ground.

**Analysis:**
- Lift from source to hump: 3 − 2 = **1 m**.
- Drop from hump to outlet: 3 − 1 = **2 m**.
- Net drop: 2 − 1 = **1 m**.

**Yes, it works.**

The siphon lifts water 1 m (well within 10 m limit) and drops it 2 m. The extra 1 m of drop drives the flow.

**Why it works:**
- Atmospheric pressure pushes water 1 m up.
- Gravity pulls water 2 m down.
- Net effect: continuous flow driven by 1 m head.
