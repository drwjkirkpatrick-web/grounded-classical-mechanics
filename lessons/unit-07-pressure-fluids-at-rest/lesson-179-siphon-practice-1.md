# Lesson 179: Siphon Practice I: Starting Methods

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate three methods to start a siphon.
2. Explain why each method works.
3. Choose the best method for a given situation.

## Materials
- Tubes of various sizes
- Water containers
- Jerrican with tap (optional)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Suction start** *(kuanza kwa kuvuta)* — using mouth to start flow.
- **Submerge start** *(kuanza kwa kuzamisha)* — filling tube underwater.
- **Pump start** *(kuanza kwa kupiga pampu)* — using a pump to prime.
- **Self-siphon** *(sifoni ya kujitegemea)* — a siphon that starts automatically when full.

## Pre-Test
1. Name two ways to start a siphon.
2. Why must the tube be full of water?
3. Which method is safest for fuel?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Suction, submerge, pump.
2. Air breaks the siphon; water column must be continuous.
3. Submerge or pump — never suck fuel.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review safety.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "You need to empty a flooded ditch. The water must go over the bank. How do you start the flow?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test three starting methods.
   - **Method 1 — Suction:** Suck tube to fill, then lower outlet.
   - **Method 2 — Submerge:** Push tube underwater until full, seal ends, place in containers.
   - **Method 3 — Fill and invert:** Fill tube from tap, quickly invert into containers.
   - Test each. Record ease, speed, and reliability.
   - **Key discovery:** Submerge is most reliable. Suction is fastest but risky.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Method comparison:**
     - **Suction:** Fast, easy, but dangerous with chemicals.
     - **Submerge:** Safe, reliable, but needs deep source.
     - **Fill and invert:** Works anywhere, but messy.
     - **Pump:** Safest for fuel, but needs equipment.
   - **Why priming matters:**
     - Tube must be full of liquid.
     - Air gaps break the siphon.
     - Once started, siphon runs automatically.
   - **Safety:**
     - Never suck fuel or chemicals.
     - Use submerge or pump methods.
     - Keep outlet lower than source.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups vote on best method. Homework: teach a family member to start a siphon.

## Hands-On Build: Siphon Starting Methods

### Parts
- Tube — 2 m — hardware
- Two buckets — 2 — classroom
- Water — 5 L — tap

### Workflow
1. **Suction:** Suck tube, lower outlet, water flows.
2. **Submerge:** Fill underwater, seal, place.
3. **Fill and invert:** Fill from tap, quickly invert.
4. Compare reliability.
5. **Checkpoint:** For suction method, stop sucking before water reaches your mouth. Spit out immediately if it does.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air in tube:** Siphon fails. Fix: ensure full priming.
- **Outlet too high:** Flow stops. Fix: lower outlet.
- **Sucking fuel:** Dangerous. Fix: never use suction for fuel.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Name two ways to start a siphon.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why must the tube be full?
3. Which method is best for a flooded basement?
4. Why not suck fuel?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Suction, submerge, fill and invert, pump**.
2. **Air breaks** the siphon.
3. **Submerge** or **pump** — safest.
4. **Poisoning, aspiration, fire risk**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A self-siphoning jerrican has a tube that automatically starts when the water level reaches the top. Explain the physics and calculate the maximum flow rate if the tube is 1 cm in diameter and the height difference is 0.5 m.

**Worked Answer:**
**Physics:**
- When water reaches tube entrance, it overflows into tube.
- Tube fills automatically.
- Once full, siphon starts.
- Water flows until level drops below tube entrance.

**Flow rate (Torricelli's approximation):**
v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 0.5) = √10 = **3.16 m/s**.

Area = π × 0.005² = 0.0000785 m².
Q = A × v = 0.0000785 × 3.16 = **0.000248 m³/s** = **0.25 L/s**.

**Q:** Design a safe siphon system for transferring kerosene between drums that eliminates mouth contact and prevents spills.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design:**
1. **Hand pump:** Small bulb pump primes tube.
2. **Check valve:** Prevents backflow.
3. **Spill guard:** Funnel at inlet catches drips.
4. **Grounding wire:** Prevents static sparks.
5. **Shut-off clamp:** Stops flow instantly.

**Operation:**
1. Connect tube to source drum.
2. Squeeze bulb pump to fill tube.
3. Place outlet in receiving drum.
4. Open clamp. Kerosene flows.
5. Close clamp when done.

**Safety features:**
- No mouth contact.
- Grounded to prevent fire.
- Controlled shut-off.
- Spill containment.
