# Lesson 180: Siphon Practice II: Cisterns, Furrows and Ponds

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Apply siphon techniques to real-world water management.
2. Calculate flow rates for irrigation siphons.
3. Design a siphon system for a village water need.

## Materials
- Tubes (various lengths)
- Water containers at different heights
- Ruler
- Timer
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Cistern** *(tangi)* — a tank for storing water.
- **Furrow** *(mshimo wa umwagiliaji)* — a trench for directing water to crops.
- **Flow rate** *(kasi ya mkondo)* — volume of water per unit time.
- **Irrigation** *(umwagiliaji)* — supplying water to crops.

## Pre-Test
1. How can a siphon help irrigate a field?
2. What limits how far a siphon can reach?
3. Why might a pond siphon stop working?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Moves water from source to field over barriers.
2. Height difference (gravity) and tube length (friction).
3. Source level drops below tube entrance.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "A farmer has a pond uphill from his field. How does he get water to his crops without a pump?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design irrigation siphons.
   - Set up "pond" (high container) and "field" (low container).
   - Place barrier between them.
   - Use tube to siphon water over barrier.
   - Measure flow rate (volume/time).
   - Test different tube diameters and heights.
   - **Key discovery:** Wider tubes flow faster. Greater height difference flows faster.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Siphon irrigation:**
     - Pond → tube → furrows.
     - No pump needed — gravity does the work.
     - Multiple tubes can water parallel rows.
   - **Flow rate:**
     - Q = A × v (area × velocity).
     - v ≈ √(2gh) (Torricelli's law).
     - Wider tube = more area = more flow.
   - **Practical limits:**
     - Height difference determines speed.
     - Tube length adds friction.
     - Source must stay above tube entrance.
   - Kenyan context: Furrow irrigation uses siphons from canals. Roof gutters siphon to tanks.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present designs. Homework: design a siphon for your home or school.

## Hands-On Build: Irrigation Siphon

### Parts
- Tube — 2 m — hardware
- Two containers — 2 — classroom
- Water — 5 L — tap
- Timer — 1 — classroom
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Set source 0.5 m above outlet.
2. Prime tube.
3. Start siphon.
4. Collect water for 30 seconds.
5. Measure volume.
6. Calculate flow rate.
7. **Checkpoint:** Ensure source stays full during test. Dropping level reduces flow.

### Common Mistakes
- **Source runs dry:** Flow stops. Fix: maintain water level.
- **Tube kinks:** Blocks flow. Fix: keep smooth curves.
- **Wrong height:** No flow if outlet too high. Fix: lower outlet.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How does a siphon help irrigation?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What limits siphon reach?
3. A tube of 1 cm diameter has flow velocity 2 m/s. Calculate flow rate in L/s.
4. Name one advantage of siphon irrigation over pumped irrigation.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Moves water **over barriers using gravity**.
2. **Height difference** and **tube friction**.
3. Area = π × 0.005² = 0.0000785 m².
   Q = 0.0000785 × 2 = 0.000157 m³/s = **0.157 L/s**.
4. Any: **no fuel needed**, simple, cheap, reliable.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A farmer has a pond 3 m above his field. He uses a 2 cm diameter hose 50 m long. Calculate (a) the expected flow velocity, and (b) the time to fill a 200 L drum. Assume negligible friction.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 3) = √60 = **7.75 m/s**.

(b) Area = π × 0.01² = 0.000314 m².
    Q = 0.000314 × 7.75 = 0.00243 m³/s = 2.43 L/s.
    Time = 200 / 2.43 = **82 seconds**.

**Note:** In practice, friction in a 50 m hose reduces this significantly (perhaps 50–70%).

**Q:** Design a multi-siphon system to water 5 parallel furrows from a single pond. Calculate the total flow needed if each furrow needs 2 L/min.

**Worked Answer:**
**Total flow:** 5 × 2 = **10 L/min** = 0.167 L/s.

**Design:**
- Main tube from pond splits into 5 branches.
- Each branch siphons to one furrow.
- Height difference: 2 m.
- Tube diameter: calculate from Q = A√(2gh).

**Calculation:**
0.000167 = A × √(2 × 10 × 2) = A × 6.32.
A = 0.0000264 m².
d = √(4 × 0.0000264/π) = **0.0058 m = 5.8 mm**.

Each branch needs ~6 mm diameter tubing.
Main tube needs 5× area → 5.8 × √5 = **13 mm** diameter.
