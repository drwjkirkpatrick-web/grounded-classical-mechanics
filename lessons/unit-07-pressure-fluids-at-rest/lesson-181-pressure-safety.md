# Lesson 181: Pressure Safety: Cookers, Cylinders and Sealed Tins

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify the dangers of high-pressure systems.
2. Explain why pressure vessels need safety features.
3. Describe safe practices for using pressure cookers and gas cylinders.

## Materials
- Photos of pressure cookers, gas cylinders
- Diagrams of safety valves
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Pressure vessel** *(chombo cha shinikizo)* — a container designed to hold gas or liquid under pressure.
- **Safety valve** *(valvu ya usalama)* — a device that releases pressure if it exceeds a limit.
- **Burst pressure** *(shinikizo la kuvunjika)* — the pressure at which a container ruptures.
- **Working pressure** *(shinikizo la kazi)* — the safe operating pressure.

## Pre-Test
1. Why do pressure cookers have a weight on top?
2. What happens if a gas cylinder overheats?
3. Why should you never open a hot pressure cooker?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It's a safety valve — releases excess pressure.
2. Pressure builds up — can explode.
3. Sudden pressure release — steam burns and explosion risk.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "What happens if you seal a pot and heat it?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners identify pressure hazards.
   - List pressure devices at home: cookers, cylinders, tyres, aerosols.
   - Identify the danger in each.
   - List safety features.
   - **Key discovery:** Pressure is useful but dangerous if uncontrolled.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Pressure cooker:**
     - Sealed pot, steam builds pressure.
     - Higher pressure = higher boiling point = faster cooking.
     - Safety valve releases at set pressure (typically 200,000 Pa).
     - Never open when pressurised.
   - **Gas cylinders:**
     - LPG stored under pressure (liquid).
     - Heat expands gas → pressure rises.
     - Safety valve releases if over-pressurised.
     - Must be stored upright, away from heat.
   - **General rules:**
     - Never exceed working pressure.
     - Use safety valves and pressure gauges.
     - Regular inspection for corrosion or damage.
     - Vent before opening.
   - Kenyan context: Gas cylinders common in urban homes. Pressure cookers used for tough beans and maize.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner summarises pressure safety rules. Homework: inspect pressure devices at home.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Why do pressure cookers have a weight/valve?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens if a cylinder overheats?
3. Name two safety features of a pressure vessel.
4. Why inspect pressure vessels regularly?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. To **release excess pressure** safely.
2. Can **explode** if pressure exceeds burst pressure.
3. Any two: **safety valve, pressure gauge, thick walls, inspection ports**.
4. To detect **corrosion, cracks, or wear** before failure.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A pressure cooker operates at 200 kPa above atmospheric. The lid has area 0.03 m². Calculate (a) the total force on the lid, and (b) why the lid must lock securely.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Total P = 101,325 + 200,000 = 301,325 Pa.
    F = 301,325 × 0.03 = **9040 N**.
    This is like a 900 kg weight pushing up on the lid.

(b) **Why lock securely:**
    - Without a lock, the lid would be blown off by 9000 N force.
    - Flying lid causes serious injury.
    - Lock transforms upward force into downward clamping force.
    - Some designs use interlocking teeth that engage as pressure rises.

**Q:** Explain the physics of a boiling liquid expanding vapour explosion (BLEVE) using the concepts of pressure, temperature, and phase change. Why are fire fighters trained to spray water on heated gas cylinders?

**Worked Answer:**
**BLEVE physics:**
1. Liquid in cylinder heats up (fire, sunlight).
2. Vapour pressure rises above cylinder's burst pressure.
3. Cylinder ruptures.
4. Liquid flashes to vapour instantly (phase change).
5. Massive expansion — explosion.
6. If flammable (LPG), ignites — fireball.

**Why spray water:**
- Water absorbs heat (evaporative cooling).
- Keeps cylinder below critical temperature.
- Prevents pressure from reaching burst point.
- Buys time to evacuate area.
- Cools surrounding fire.

**Key point:** Don't extinguish flames if gas is still leaking — unignited gas is more dangerous (invisible, explosive).
