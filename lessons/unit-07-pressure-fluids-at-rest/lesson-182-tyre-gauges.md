# Lesson 182: Measuring Big Pressures: Pumps and Tyre Gauges

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a tyre gauge measures pressure.
2. Read and interpret pressure gauges.
3. Relate tyre pressure to vehicle safety and efficiency.

## Materials
- Bicycle pump with gauge
- Bicycle tyre or tube
- Tyre pressure gauge
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Tyre gauge** *(kipimo cha shinikizo la tairi)* — a device measuring air pressure in a tyre.
- **PSI** *(paundi kwa inchi ya mraba)* — pounds per square inch (imperial pressure unit).
- **Inflation** *(kujaza hewa)* — adding air to increase pressure.
- **Optimal pressure** *(shinikizo bora)* — the manufacturer's recommended pressure.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a safe bicycle tyre pressure?
2. What happens if a tyre is under-inflated?
3. How does a pump gauge work?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. 40–60 PSI (≈ 300,000–400,000 Pa).
2. More rolling resistance, risk of pinch flats, poor handling.
3. Measures air pressure pushing against a spring or diaphragm.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Why do bicycles need pumped tyres? What happens if they're flat?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure and inflate tyres.
   - Measure current pressure with gauge.
   - Inflate to recommended pressure.
   - Observe pump gauge during inflation.
   - Feel tyre firmness at different pressures.
   - **Key discovery:** Proper pressure is essential for performance and safety.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How gauges work:**
     - Bourdon tube: pressure straightens a curved tube.
     - Diaphragm: pressure pushes against a flexible disc.
     - Digital: pressure sensor converts to electronic reading.
   - **Units:**
     - PSI (pounds per square inch).
     - kPa (kilopascals) = 1000 Pa.
     - 1 PSI ≈ 6895 Pa.
     - 1 bar = 100,000 Pa.
   - **Effects of wrong pressure:**
     - **Too low:** Rolling resistance increases, risk of flats, rim damage.
     - **Too high:** Harsh ride, reduced grip, risk of blowout.
   - Kenyan context: Boda-boda tyres checked daily. Matatus check weekly. Under-inflation wastes fuel.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups share tyre pressures. Homework: check tyre pressure at home.

## Hands-On Build: Tyre Pressure Measurement

### Parts
- Bicycle pump with gauge — 1 — home
- Bicycle tyre — 1 — school/home
- Pressure gauge — 1 — hardware
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Check current pressure.
2. Inflate to recommended level.
3. Recheck pressure.
4. Feel tyre firmness.
5. Compare before and after.
6. **Checkpoint:** Check pressure when tyre is cold. Riding heats air, increasing pressure reading.

### Common Mistakes
- **Checking hot tyre:** Pressure reads high. Fix: check before riding.
- **Over-inflating:** Risk of blowout. Fix: follow manufacturer's recommendation.
- **Wrong gauge:** Car gauge for bicycle tyre. Fix: use appropriate gauge.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is safe bicycle tyre pressure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What happens if under-inflated?
3. Convert 30 PSI to kPa.
4. Why check pressure before riding?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **40–60 PSI** (or manufacturer's recommendation).
2. **More resistance**, risk of flats, poor handling.
3. 30 × 6.895 = **206.9 kPa**.
4. **Hot tyres read higher** due to expanded air.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A car tyre has volume 30 L at 200 kPa (cold). After driving, the temperature rises from 20°C to 40°C. Calculate the new pressure assuming constant volume.

**Worked Answer:**
T₁ = 293 K, T₂ = 313 K.

P₂ = P₁ × (T₂/T₁) = 200 × (313/293) = **213.7 kPa**.

Pressure increases by **13.7 kPa** (6.8%).

This is why you should never bleed air from hot tyres — they're supposed to be higher.

**Q:** A boda-boda tyre is rated for 350 kPa maximum. The rider inflates to 300 kPa on a cool morning (15°C). By afternoon it's 35°C. Calculate whether the tyre exceeds its rating. Explain the danger.

**Worked Answer:**
T₁ = 288 K, T₂ = 308 K.

P₂ = 300 × (308/288) = **320.8 kPa**.

**Does not exceed** 350 kPa. Safe.

However, if rider had inflated to 330 kPa in the morning:
P₂ = 330 × (308/288) = **352.9 kPa**.

**Exceeds rating!** Risk of blowout, especially at speed.

**Danger:**
- Blowout causes loss of control.
- At high speed, can cause crash.
- Hot road surface further increases temperature.

**Lesson:** Always inflate to cold pressure. Account for temperature rise.

## Safety Notes
- Don't over-inflate — risk of explosion.
- Wear eye protection when inflating.
- Standard workshop safety.
