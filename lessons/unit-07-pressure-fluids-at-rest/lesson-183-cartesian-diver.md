# Lesson 183: Build: The Cartesian Diver

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a Cartesian diver from simple materials.
2. Explain how pressure changes make the diver sink and float.
3. Relate the diver's behaviour to density and buoyancy.

## Materials
- Plastic bottle with cap
- Eye dropper or pen cap
- Water
- Modelling clay
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Cartesian diver** *(mchuizi wa Cartesian)* — a device that sinks or floats when pressure is applied.
- **Buoyancy** *(ufunuo)* — the upward force on an object in fluid.
- **Density** *(msisimko)* — mass per unit volume.
- **Compressibility** *(uwezo wa kunyamazishwa)* — how much a substance shrinks under pressure.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens when you squeeze a bottle containing a floating dropper?
2. Why does the dropper sink when squeezed?
3. What property of the dropper changes when squeezed?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The dropper sinks.
2. Pressure compresses air in dropper, making it denser.
3. Density (or buoyancy).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "I can make this dropper sink and float without touching it."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build Cartesian divers.
   - Fill bottle with water.
   - Adjust dropper buoyancy with clay until it barely floats.
   - Seal bottle.
   - Squeeze bottle — diver sinks.
   - Release — diver floats.
   - **Key discovery:** Squeezing increases pressure, compressing air in dropper. Compressed air takes less space, water enters, diver becomes denser, sinks.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How it works:**
     - Dropper contains air and water.
     - Normally: air volume makes diver less dense than water → floats.
     - Squeeze: pressure compresses air bubble.
     - Smaller bubble → more water in dropper → denser.
     - When denser than water → sinks.
     - Release: air expands, water pushed out, diver floats.
   - **Science:**
     - Pascal's principle: pressure transmitted to air bubble.
     - Boyle's Law: pressure up → volume down.
     - Density: mass/volume. Volume down → density up.
     - Buoyancy: less buoyant when denser.
   - **Real applications:**
     - Submarine ballast tanks.
     - Fish swim bladders.
     - Underwater vehicles.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains the diver to the class. Homework: research how submarines use the same principle.

## Hands-On Build: Cartesian Diver

### Parts
- Plastic bottle — 1 — home
- Eye dropper — 1 — classroom
- Water — 1 L — tap
- Modelling clay — 10 g — art supply

### Workflow
1. Fill bottle to top with water.
2. Put clay on dropper stem.
3. Test in water: should barely float.
4. Adjust clay until just right.
5. Put dropper in bottle.
6. Seal bottle.
7. Squeeze. Diver sinks.
8. Release. Diver floats.
9. **Checkpoint:** If diver won't sink, add more clay. If it won't float, remove clay. The adjustment is critical.

### Common Mistakes
- **Too much clay:** Diver sinks permanently. Fix: remove clay.
- **Too little clay:** Diver floats permanently. Fix: add clay.
- **Leaky bottle:** Squeeze doesn't increase pressure. Fix: seal tightly.
- **Air in bottle:** Diver sinks randomly. Fix: fill bottle completely.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A Cartesian diver has air volume 2 cm³ and total mass 3 g. When squeezed, air compresses to 1 cm³. Calculate (a) initial density, (b) final density, and (c) explain why it sinks.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Initial: mass = 3 g, volume ≈ 2 cm³ (mostly air).
    ρ = 3/2 = **1.5 g/cm³**. Wait — this is less than water (1 g/cm³), so it should float.
    Actually, the dropper itself has volume. Let's say total volume = 4 cm³.
    ρ = 3/4 = **0.75 g/cm³** → floats.

(b) Final: air compresses to 1 cm³, but water enters to fill the space.
    Total volume still 4 cm³ (dropper shell), but now more water inside.
    Mass increases to maybe 4 g.
    ρ = 4/4 = **1.0 g/cm³** → neutral buoyancy.
    Squeeze harder: ρ > 1.0 → **sinks**.

(c) **Sinks because density exceeds water**.

**Q:** Explain how a submarine uses the Cartesian diver principle to dive and surface. Calculate the water mass needed in ballast tanks for a 2000-tonne submarine to dive, given the submarine's volume is 2000 m³.

**Worked Answer:**
**Principle:**
- Surface: ballast tanks filled with air (low density). Submarine less dense than water → floats.
- Dive: fill tanks with water (high density). Submarine denser than water → sinks.
- Surface again: blow water out with compressed air.

**Calculation:**
To float: ρ_submarine < 1000 kg/m³.
To sink: ρ_submarine > 1000 kg/m³.

At neutral buoyancy: ρ = 1000 kg/m³.
Mass = 1000 × 2000 = 2,000,000 kg = 2000 tonnes.

The submarine already masses 2000 tonnes, so at exactly 2000 tonnes it's neutrally buoyant.

To **dive reliably**: needs extra mass. Say 2100 tonnes total.
Water mass in tanks = 100 tonnes = **100,000 kg** (100 m³ of water).
