# Lesson 184: Unit Review: Pressure Lab Stations

**Unit:** 7 — Pressure & Fluids at Rest | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Rotate through stations demonstrating Unit 7 concepts.
2. Explain pressure in solids, liquids, and gases.
3. Apply pressure principles to solve problems.

## Materials
- Station materials (see below)
- Rotation schedule
- Answer sheets
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Lab station** *(kituo cha maabara)* — a demonstration or experiment setup.
- **Rotation** *(mzunguko)* — moving from station to station.
- **Consolidation** *(kufanya imara)* — strengthening understanding through repetition.
- **Demonstration** *(onyesho)* — showing how something works.

## Pre-Test
1. Name the three types of pressure covered in this unit.
2. What is Pascal's principle?
3. How does a siphon work?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Solid, liquid, gas pressure.
2. Pressure in confined fluid transmits equally.
3. Atmospheric pressure pushes up, gravity pulls down.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Explain station rotation.

2. **(30 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Learners rotate through 5 stations (6 minutes each).
   - **Station 1 — Solid Pressure:** Press sharp and blunt objects into foam. Compare indentations.
   - **Station 2 — Liquid Pressure:** Jerrican with holes at different depths. Observe jet distances.
   - **Station 3 — Gas Pressure:** Squeeze sealed syringe. Feel resistance. Measure compression.
   - **Station 4 — Hydraulics:** Two connected syringes. Lift load with small force.
   - **Station 5 — Siphon:** Start siphon between containers. Measure flow rate.
   - **Key discovery:** Pressure appears in many forms, but the principles are universal.

3. **(5 min)** Explain:
   - Review key concepts from each station.
   - Connect to real-world applications.
   - Address common misconceptions.

4. **(3 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually.

5. **(2 min)** Wrap: Collect answer sheets. Preview Unit 8. Homework: write three things you learned.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Name three types of pressure.
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What is Pascal's principle?
3. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* How does a siphon work?
4. Calculate pressure at 5 m depth in water.
5. A 2 cm² piston creates 100,000 Pa. What force on a 20 cm² piston?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Solid**, **liquid**, **gas** pressure.
2. Pressure in confined fluid transmits **equally**.
3. **Atmospheric pressure** pushes up, **gravity** pulls down.
4. P = 1000 × 10 × 5 = **50,000 Pa**.
5. F = 100,000 × 0.002 = **200 N**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a 5-station review for a younger class (ages 10–13) covering the key concepts of Unit 7. Include station name, materials, activity, and the one-sentence takeaway for each.

**Worked Answer:**
**Station 1: "The Sharp Knife"
- Materials: Foam, pencils, ruler.
- Activity: Press sharp and blunt pencils into foam.
- Takeaway: Smaller area means higher pressure.

**Station 2: "The Water Tower"
- Materials: Jerrican with holes, water, bucket.
- Activity: Observe jets from different depths.
- Takeaway: Pressure increases with depth.

**Station 3: "The Syringe Squeeze"
- Materials: Sealed syringe.
- Activity: Squeeze and feel resistance.
- Takeaway: Gases resist compression — pressure rises.

**Station 4: "The Magic Lift"
- Materials: Two syringes, tubing, water, load.
- Activity: Lift load with small syringe.
- Takeaway: Hydraulics multiply force using pressure.

**Station 5: "The Uphill River"
- Materials: Tube, two containers, water.
- Activity: Make water flow over a hump.
- Takeaway: Siphons use gravity and atmospheric pressure.

**Q:** Evaluate the Unit 7 curriculum for a rural Kenyan school with no laboratory, limited water, and few syringes. Suggest three adaptations that maintain learning outcomes.

**Worked Answer:**
**Adaptation 1: Solid Pressure**
- Instead of foam, use mud or soft ground.
- Use sticks with different tip shapes.
- Same concept, local materials.

**Adaptation 2: Liquid Pressure**
- Use a gourd with holes instead of jerrican.
- Collect water in a basin for reuse.
- Demonstrate with small amounts.

**Adaptation 3: Hydraulics**
- Use bamboo tubes instead of plastic syringes.
- Seal with banana fibre or beeswax.
- Demonstrate principle with simple pistons.

**Key:** Focus on concepts, not equipment. Every principle can be demonstrated with local materials.
