# Lesson 185: Measuring Flow: Litres per Second

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define flow rate as volume per unit time.
2. Measure flow rate using a container and timer.
3. Compare flow rates from different sources.

## Materials
- Jerrican or bucket with volume markings
- Timer or stopwatch
- Water source (tap, bottle with hole)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Flow rate** *(kasi ya mkondo)* — volume of fluid passing a point per unit time.
- **Volume** *(ujazo)* — the amount of space occupied by a fluid.
- **Discharge** *(mato)* — the amount of water flowing past a point.
- **Litres per second (L/s)** *(lita kwa sekunde)* — a common unit for flow rate.

## Pre-Test
1. What is flow rate?
2. How do you measure how fast water flows?
3. Does a wide pipe flow faster than a narrow pipe?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Volume per unit time.
2. Collect water for a set time, measure volume.
3. Not necessarily — depends on pressure and restrictions.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "How much water flows from this tap in one minute?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure flow rates.
   - Measure flow from a tap: collect for 10 seconds, measure volume.
   - Try different openings: full open, half open, dripping.
   - Compare with pouring from a bottle.
   - Calculate Q = V/t for each.
   - **Key discovery:** Flow rate varies with source. Same source can have different rates.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Flow rate formula:** Q = V/t.
     - Q = flow rate (L/s or m³/s).
     - V = volume (L or m³).
     - t = time (s).
   - **Units:**
     - L/s for small flows (taps, pipes).
     - m³/s for large flows (rivers, pumps).
     - 1 m³/s = 1000 L/s.
   - **Factors affecting flow:**
     - Pressure: higher pressure = faster flow.
     - Opening size: larger opening = more flow (at same speed).
     - Obstructions: kinks, bends reduce flow.
   - Kenyan context: Measuring river flow for irrigation. Checking pump output. Monitoring water usage.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups compare flow rates. Homework: measure flow at your tap.

## Hands-On Build: Flow Rate Measurement

### Parts
- Jerrican with markings — 1 — home
- Timer — 1 — classroom
- Tap — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Place jerrican under tap.
2. Open tap fully.
3. Start timer.
4. Collect for 10 seconds.
5. Stop. Read volume.
6. Calculate Q = V/t.
7. Repeat with half-open tap.
8. **Checkpoint:** Keep the tap at constant opening during each measurement. Changing mid-test gives wrong results.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong timing:** Starting/stopping late. Fix: practice with dry runs.
- **Reading volume:** Eye level with liquid. Fix: crouch to read.
- **Spillage:** Water overflows. Fix: use larger container.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What is flow rate?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How do you measure flow rate?
3. Calculate Q if 5 L flows in 20 seconds.
4. Name one factor that affects flow rate.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Volume per unit time**.
2. Collect water for **set time**, **measure volume**, divide.
3. Q = 5/20 = **0.25 L/s**.
4. Any: **pressure, opening size, obstructions**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A village pump delivers water through a 5 cm diameter pipe. The flow velocity is 2 m/s. Calculate (a) the flow rate in L/s, and (b) the time to fill a 200 L drum.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Area = π × 0.025² = 0.00196 m².
    Q = A × v = 0.00196 × 2 = 0.00393 m³/s = **3.93 L/s**.

(b) Time = 200 / 3.93 = **50.9 seconds**.

**Q:** A river has width 5 m and average depth 1.2 m. A float takes 30 seconds to travel 10 m. Estimate the river's discharge in m³/s. Discuss the limitations of this method.

**Worked Answer:**
Velocity = 10/30 = 0.333 m/s.
Cross-section = 5 × 1.2 = 6 m².
Q = 6 × 0.333 = **2 m³/s**.

**Limitations:**
- Surface velocity > average velocity (current is slower near bottom).
- Typical correction: multiply by 0.8–0.9.
- Depth varies across width.
- Float may be affected by wind.
- More accurate: current meter at multiple points.
