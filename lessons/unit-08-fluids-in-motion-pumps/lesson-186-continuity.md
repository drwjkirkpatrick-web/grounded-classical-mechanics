# Lesson 186: Narrow Pipe, Faster Flow: Continuity

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State the continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.
2. Demonstrate that water speeds up in narrow pipes.
3. Predict velocity changes for given area changes.

## Materials
- Two bottles with different neck widths
- Water
- Tubes of different diameters
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Continuity** *(mwingiliano)* — the principle that mass flow rate is constant in a pipe.
- **Cross-sectional area** *(eneo la kupitisha)* — the area of a pipe's interior.
- **Velocity** *(kasi)* — speed in a specific direction.
- **Streamline** *(mstari wa mkondo)* — the path followed by a fluid particle.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to water speed when a pipe narrows?
2. If area doubles, what happens to velocity?
3. Why does a hose nozzle squirt far?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It speeds up.
2. It halves.
3. Narrow nozzle increases velocity.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Squeeze a hose partially. "Why does water squirt farther?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test continuity.
   - Pour water through wide tube, then narrow tube.
   - Measure time to fill same container.
   - Observe jet distance from different nozzles.
   - **Key discovery:** Narrower pipe = faster water. Same flow rate means velocity changes inversely with area.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Continuity equation:** A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.
     - For incompressible fluids (like water).
     - What enters must exit.
     - If area halves, velocity doubles.
   - **Why it works:**
     - Water can't pile up in the pipe.
     - Same volume must pass each point per second.
     - Narrower space → must move faster.
   - **Applications:**
     - Hose nozzles: narrow = fast spray.
     - Arteries: narrowed artery → faster blood (problem!).
     - Rivers: narrow gap → rapids.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains continuity. Homework: find three examples at home.

## Hands-On Build: Continuity Demonstration

### Parts
- Two bottles (wide and narrow neck) — 2 — home
- Water — 2 L — tap
- Stopwatch — 1 — classroom
- Container — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill both bottles.
2. Pour from wide neck. Time to empty.
3. Pour from narrow neck. Time to empty.
4. Same volume, different times.
5. Calculate average velocity.
6. **Checkpoint:** Keep tilt angle constant. Steeper tilt increases flow independently.

### Common Mistakes
- **Different heights:** More pressure from higher. Fix: same starting level.
- **Air bubbles:** Block flow. Fix: smooth pouring.
- **Not timing:** Guessing instead of measuring. Fix: use stopwatch.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What happens when pipe narrows?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* If area doubles, velocity?
3. A pipe of 4 cm² narrows to 2 cm². Initial velocity = 1 m/s. Calculate final velocity.
4. Why does a river narrow at rocks?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Speeds up**.
2. **Halves**.
3. v₂ = (4 × 1)/2 = **2 m/s**.
4. Same water must pass through → **rapids**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A water pipe of diameter 10 cm carries water at 2 m/s. It branches into two pipes of 5 cm diameter each. Calculate the velocity in each branch.

**Worked Answer:**
A₁ = π × 0.05² = 0.00785 m².
A_branch = π × 0.025² = 0.00196 m².

A₁v₁ = 2 × A_branch × v_branch
0.00785 × 2 = 2 × 0.00196 × v_branch
v_branch = 0.0157 / 0.00393 = **4 m/s**.

**Q:** Explain why a narrowed artery (stenosis) is dangerous, using the continuity equation and the concept of pressure drop. Calculate the pressure change if an artery narrows from 1 cm² to 0.25 cm² with initial velocity 0.3 m/s.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why dangerous:**
- Narrowed artery → higher velocity (continuity).
- Higher velocity → lower pressure (Bernoulli — preview).
- Low pressure → artery can collapse.
- Blood cells damaged by high shear.
- Clots form more easily.

**Velocity:**
v₂ = (1 × 0.3)/0.25 = **1.2 m/s**.
