# Lesson 187: The Bernoulli Surprise I: Papers That Kiss

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate Bernoulli's principle with simple paper experiments.
2. Explain that faster-moving air has lower pressure.
3. Predict situations where Bernoulli's principle applies.

## Materials
- Two sheets of paper
- Straw
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Bernoulli's principle** *(sheria ya Bernoulli)* — faster fluid flow means lower pressure.
- **Streamline** *(mstari wa mkondo)* — the path of a fluid particle.
- **Pressure gradient** *(kutofautiana kwa shinikizo)* — change in pressure across a distance.
- **Lift** *(nguvu ya kupaa)* — upward force from pressure difference.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens when you blow between two sheets of paper?
2. Does fast-moving air have high or low pressure?
3. Why do airplane wings create lift?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. They move together (surprisingly!).
2. Low pressure.
3. Air flows faster over the top, creating lower pressure.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Hold two papers vertically, a few centimetres apart. "What happens when I blow between them?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test Bernoulli's principle.
   - Blow between papers: they move together.
   - Blow over a single paper strip: it rises.
   - Blow through a straw at a ping-pong ball: it stays suspended.
   - **Key discovery:** Fast-moving air has lower pressure. Surrounding higher pressure pushes things toward the fast air.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Bernoulli's principle:**
     - Where fluid speed increases, pressure decreases.
     - Energy conservation: pressure + kinetic + potential = constant.
   - **Paper experiment:**
     - Still air between papers: normal pressure.
     - Blow between: fast air → low pressure.
     - Outside air: normal pressure → higher.
     - Net force pushes papers together.
   - **Applications:**
     - Airplane wings: curved top → faster flow → lower pressure → lift.
     - Sprayers: fast air over tube → low pressure → sucks liquid up.
     - Roofs in storms: fast wind over roof → low pressure → roof lifts off.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains the paper trick. Homework: find three Bernoulli examples.

## Hands-On Build: Bernoulli Demonstrations

### Parts
- Paper — 2 sheets — classroom
- Straw — 1 — home
- Ping-pong ball — 1 — home

### Workflow
1. Hold two papers vertically, 5 cm apart.
2. Blow between them. Observe they move together.
3. Hold paper strip horizontally, blow over it. It rises.
4. Place ping-pong ball in airstream from straw. It stays suspended.
5. **Checkpoint:** Blow steadily. Puffing creates turbulence that disrupts the effect.

### Common Mistakes
- **Blowing too hard:** Creates turbulence, not smooth flow. Fix: gentle, steady blow.
- **Wrong angle:** Papers must be vertical. Fix: hold upright.
- **Blocked straw:** No airflow. Fix: clear straw.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** An airplane wing has area 20 m². Air flows at 250 m/s over the top and 200 m/s under the bottom. Air density is 1.2 kg/m³. Calculate the pressure difference and the lift force.

**Worked Answer:**
Bernoulli: P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂².

ΔP = ½ρ(v_bottom² − v_top²) = ½ × 1.2 × (200² − 250²)
    = 0.6 × (40,000 − 62,500) = 0.6 × (−22,500)
    = **−13,500 Pa**.

Lift = |ΔP| × A = 13,500 × 20 = **270,000 N**.

(Actual lift is more complex — this is simplified.)

**Q:** Explain why a spinning ball curves, using Bernoulli's principle and the concept of relative velocity.

**Worked Answer:**
**Magnus effect (spinning ball):**
- Ball spinning forward: bottom moves with airflow, top moves against airflow.
- Bottom: ball surface + airflow = faster relative air.
- Top: ball surface − airflow = slower relative air.
- Bernoulli: faster bottom → lower pressure.
- Slower top → higher pressure.
- Net force: **downward** (for topspin) or sideways (for sidespin).

**Curve ball:**
- Spin axis vertical: ball curves left or right.
- Side spinning toward airflow = faster relative = lower pressure.
- Ball curves toward the low-pressure side.
