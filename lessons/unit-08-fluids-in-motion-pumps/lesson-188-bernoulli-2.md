# Lesson 188: The Bernoulli Surprise II: Roofs, Sprays and Dust Devils

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain real-world applications of Bernoulli's principle.
2. Demonstrate how a sprayer works using Bernoulli's principle.
3. Calculate pressure changes in a Venturi tube.

## Materials
- Spray bottle or perfume atomiser
- Straw and cup of water
- Venturi tube or constriction in a pipe (or make one)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Venturi effect** *(athari ya Venturi)* — pressure drop in a constriction.
- **Atomiser** *(kinyunyizaji)* — a device that sprays fine droplets using air pressure.
- **Carburettor** *(karibureta)* — a device mixing fuel and air using Venturi effect.
- **Relative velocity** *(kasi ya uhusiano)* — speed compared to another object.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a perfume spray work?
2. Why do roofs lift off in high winds?
3. What is a Venturi tube?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Fast air over tube reduces pressure, sucks liquid up.
2. Fast wind over roof creates low pressure.
3. A tube with a constriction that changes pressure.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Spray perfume. "I didn't push the liquid up. What did?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build Bernoulli devices.
   - Straw sprayer: blow over straw in water, water rises.
   - Venturi: pass water through constriction, measure pressure drop.
   - Paper roof: fast air over paper makes it lift.
   - **Key discovery:** Bernoulli's principle is everywhere — from sprays to roofs.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Sprayers:**
     - Fast air over vertical tube.
     - Low pressure at top of tube.
     - Atmospheric pressure pushes liquid up.
     - Liquid breaks into droplets.
   - **Roofs in wind:**
     - Still air inside: normal pressure.
     - Fast wind over roof: low pressure.
     - Net upward force can lift roof.
     - Solution: secure roof, vent attic.
   - **Venturi:**
     - Narrow section: fast flow, low pressure.
     - Used in: carburettors, flow meters, vacuum pumps.
   - Kenyan context: Mosquito sprayers, paint sprayers, fuel carburettors in generators.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains how a sprayer works. Homework: find Bernoulli devices at home.

## Hands-On Build: Bernoulli Sprayer

### Parts
- Straw — 2 — home
- Cup of water — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Place one straw in water (vertical).
2. Blow across top of straw with second straw.
3. Water rises in vertical straw.
4. Breaks into spray at top.
5. **Checkpoint:** Blow horizontally across the opening, not down into it. Downward blow just pushes water away.

### Common Mistakes
- **Blowing down:** Doesn't create low pressure. Fix: blow across.
- **Straw too deep:** Harder to lift. Fix: just touch surface.
- **No seal around straw:** Air leaks. Fix: hold straw steady.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A Venturi tube has diameters 4 cm and 2 cm. Water enters at 1 m/s and 50,000 Pa. Calculate (a) velocity in the constriction, and (b) pressure in the constriction.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ → v₂ = v₁ × (A₁/A₂) = 1 × (4/2)² = **4 m/s**.

(b) Bernoulli: P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²
    50,000 + ½(1000)(1) = P₂ + ½(1000)(16)
    50,500 = P₂ + 8000
    P₂ = **42,500 Pa**.

Pressure drops by **7500 Pa** in the constriction.

**Q:** Explain why buildings with open windows survive high winds better than sealed buildings, using Bernoulli's principle.

**Worked Answer:**
**Sealed building:**
- Fast wind over roof → low pressure outside.
- Still air inside → normal pressure.
- Large pressure difference → upward force on roof.
- Roof can lift off.

**Open-window building:**
- Wind flows through building.
- Inside and outside pressures more equal.
- Less pressure difference across roof.
- Reduced lift force.
- Some damage from debris, but structure survives.

**Design lesson:**
- Buildings in windy areas need ventilation.
- Hurricane-resistant designs include openings.
- Pressure equalisation prevents structural failure.
