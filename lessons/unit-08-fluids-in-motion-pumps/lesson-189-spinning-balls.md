# Lesson 189: Curve Balls: The Magnus Effect

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate the Magnus effect with a spinning ball.
2. Explain how spin creates a pressure difference.
3. Relate the Magnus effect to sports and natural phenomena.

## Materials
- Ping-pong ball or lightweight ball
- Hair dryer or fan
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Magnus effect** *(athari ya Magnus)* — the force on a spinning object moving through fluid.
- **Spin** *(mzunguko)* — rotation about an axis.
- **Pressure differential** *(tofauti ya shinikizo)* — difference in pressure between two sides.
- **Deflection** *(kuegemea)* — change in direction due to a sideways force.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to a spinning ball in air?
2. Does topspin make a ball rise or drop?
3. What causes the curve in a football free kick?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It curves (swerves) in the direction of spin.
2. Drops (forward spin pushes air up, ball goes down).
3. The Magnus effect from spinning the ball.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Throw a spinning ball. "Why did it curve?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test the Magnus effect.
   - Use a hair dryer to create an airstream.
   - Drop a spinning ping-pong ball into the stream.
   - Observe: ball deflects sideways.
   - Try backspin, topspin, sidespin.
   - **Key discovery:** Spin creates pressure difference. Ball curves toward the side with lower pressure.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How it works:**
     - Spinning ball drags air with it.
     - One side: spin adds to airflow → faster → lower pressure.
     - Other side: spin opposes airflow → slower → higher pressure.
     - Pressure difference → sideways force.
   - **Sports:**
     - Football: bend it like Beckham.
     - Cricket: swing bowling.
     - Tennis: topspin, backspin, slice.
   - **Natural phenomena:**
     - Spinning seeds (sycamore, maple) glide farther.
     - Flettner rotor ships use spinning cylinders for propulsion.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains how to curve a football. Homework: watch a sports match and identify spin effects.

## Hands-On Build: Magnus Effect Demonstration

### Parts
- Ping-pong ball — 1 — home
- Hair dryer — 1 — home
- String — 0.5 m — home

### Workflow
1. Tie string to ball.
2. Hold in airstream from hair dryer.
3. Spin the ball by twisting string.
4. Observe deflection.
5. Try different spins.
6. **Checkpoint:** The ball must spin rapidly. Slow spin produces weak effect.

### Common Mistakes
- **Ball too heavy:** Doesn't deflect. Fix: use ping-pong ball.
- **Not spinning:** No Magnus effect. Fix: twist string to impart spin.
- **Weak airflow:** Need strong stream. Fix: use hair dryer on high.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A football of mass 0.43 kg and radius 11 cm spins at 600 rpm while moving at 25 m/s. Calculate (a) the tangential velocity at the surface, and (b) explain why this creates a pressure difference.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) ω = 600 rpm = 600 × 2π/60 = 62.8 rad/s.
    v_tangential = ω × r = 62.8 × 0.11 = **6.9 m/s**.

(b) **Pressure difference:**
- Top of ball (backspin): v_air + v_spin = 25 + 6.9 = 31.9 m/s.
- Bottom: v_air − v_spin = 25 − 6.9 = 18.1 m/s.
- Top: faster → lower pressure (Bernoulli).
- Bottom: slower → higher pressure.
- Net force: **upward** (Magnus lift for backspin).

For topspin: reversed — ball drops faster.

**Q:** Explain why a smooth golf ball travels shorter than a dimpled one, despite dimples creating more drag. How does this relate to the Magnus effect and boundary layer separation?

**Worked Answer:**
**Smooth ball:**
- Boundary layer separates early (at ~80° from front).
- Large turbulent wake.
- High drag (pressure drag dominates).
- Short flight.

**Dimpled ball:**
- Dimples trip boundary layer into turbulence.
- Turbulent boundary layer stays attached longer (to ~120°).
- Smaller wake.
- Less pressure drag.
- More lift from backspin (Magnus effect with attached flow).
- Longer flight despite slightly more skin friction.

**Trade-off:**
- More skin friction (bad) but much less pressure drag (good).
- Net: dimples reduce total drag by ~50%.
- Backspin + dimples = maximum distance.
