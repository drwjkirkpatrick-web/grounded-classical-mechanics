# Lesson 191: Lift Pumps: The Afridev and the 10-Metre Limit

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a lift pump works using atmospheric pressure.
2. Demonstrate the 10-metre limit of suction pumps.
3. Identify the parts of a hand pump.

## Materials
- Hand pump or syringe demonstration
- Tube and water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Lift pump** *(pampu ya kuvuta)* — a pump that raises water by suction.
- **Piston** *(kifaa cha kunyima)* — the moving part that creates suction.
- **Valve** *(valvu)* — a one-way gate that lets water pass in one direction.
- **Cylinder** *(mchedwa)* — the chamber where water is lifted.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a hand pump lift water?
2. Why is there a 10 m limit?
3. What are the two valves in a lift pump for?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Creates suction (low pressure), atmospheric pressure pushes water up.
2. Atmospheric pressure can only support ~10 m of water.
3. One lets water into cylinder, other lets water out to spout.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "How deep can a hand pump pull water? Let's find the limit."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test suction limits.
   - Use long tube in water. Suck from top.
   - Measure maximum height water rises.
   - Compare with shorter tube.
   - Try with syringe and tubing.
   - **Key discovery:** Water rises to about 10 m maximum. Beyond that, suction can't overcome atmospheric pressure limit.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How lift pumps work:**
     - Piston moves up → cylinder expands → pressure drops.
     - Atmospheric pressure pushes water up through lower valve.
     - Piston moves down → lower valve closes → water trapped above.
     - Upper valve opens → water pushed to spout.
   - **10 m limit:**
     - Atmospheric pressure = 101,325 Pa.
     - Supports h = P/(ρg) = 101,325/(1000 × 10) = 10.1 m.
     - Practical limit: ~7–8 m (need pressure difference to flow).
   - **Afridev pump:**
     - Common in rural Kenya.
     - Designed for deep wells (up to 45 m with force pump action).
     - Durable, repairable with local parts.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why pumps can't suck water from deep mines. Homework: count hand pumps in your area.

## Hands-On Build: Lift Pump Demonstration

### Parts
- Syringe — 1 — pharmacy
- Tube — 1 m — hardware
- Water container — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill container with water.
2. Submerge tube end.
3. Pull syringe plunger.
4. Water rises in tube.
5. Try with tube lengths: 0.5 m, 1 m, 2 m.
6. **Checkpoint:** Seal connections. Air leaks prevent suction.

### Common Mistakes
- **Air leaks:** Water won't rise. Fix: seal tube to syringe.
- **Tube above water:** Sucks air, not water. Fix: keep submerged.
- **Too long:** Beyond 10 m, impossible. Fix: use force pump or submersible.

1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How does a hand pump lift water?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why is there a 10 m limit?
3. What happens when the piston moves up?
4. Name one part of a hand pump.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Suction** (low pressure) lets **atmospheric pressure** push water up.
2. **Atmospheric pressure** can only support ~10 m of water.
3. **Cylinder expands**, pressure drops, water enters through **lower valve**.
4. Any: **piston**, **valve**, **cylinder**, **spout**, **handle**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A hand pump has a cylinder diameter 10 cm and stroke length 20 cm. Calculate (a) the volume of water lifted per stroke, and (b) the strokes needed to fill a 20 L jerrican.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Volume = π × 0.05² × 0.20 = 0.00157 m³ = **1.57 L**.

(b) Strokes = 20 / 1.57 = **12.7 strokes** (round to 13).

**Q:** Explain why the practical limit for hand pumps is ~7 m, not the theoretical 10.3 m. What design features does the Afridev pump use to exceed this limit?

**Worked Answer:**
**Why ~7 m practical limit:**
- Need pressure difference for flow.
- Piston seals imperfect (leaks).
- Water contains dissolved gases (air bubbles).
- Valves don't seal perfectly.
- Friction losses.
- Safety margin needed.

**Afridev design features:**
- **Force pump conversion:** At bottom of stroke, water is pushed upward under pressure (not just suction).
- **Plunger valve:** Opens on downstroke, pushing water above piston.
- **Two-stage action:** Suction + pressure in one motion.
- **Reaches 45 m** by combining suction and pressure.
- **Replaceable parts:** Leather washers, rubber seals available locally.
