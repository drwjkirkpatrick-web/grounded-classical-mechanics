# Lesson 193: Centrifugal Pumps: Spinning Water Outward

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a centrifugal pump uses spinning motion to move water.
2. Demonstrate the principle with a simple spinning device.
3. Identify where centrifugal pumps are used.

## Materials
- Small electric pump or fan (optional)
- Salad spinner or hand-crank device
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Centrifugal pump** *(pampu ya mzunguko)* — a pump that spins water outward using an impeller.
- **Impeller** *(kipiga mzunguko)* — the spinning part that pushes water.
- **Volute** *(mchedwa wa nje)* — the spiral casing that collects spun water.
- **Cavitation** *(kuvunjika kwa mvuto)* — bubble formation that damages pumps.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a centrifugal pump move water?
2. What part spins in a centrifugal pump?
3. Why are centrifugal pumps common in irrigation?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Spins water outward using centrifugal force.
2. The impeller.
3. High volume, simple design, handles dirty water.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Spin a bucket of water. "Why doesn't water fall out? Where does it want to go?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test centrifugal motion.
   - Spin water in a container with holes at different radii.
   - Water flies farther from outer holes.
   - Use salad spinner: water exits through holes.
   - **Key discovery:** Spinning water moves outward. Faster spin = farther throw.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How centrifugal pumps work:**
     - Impeller spins at high speed.
     - Water enters centre (eye).
     - Blades push water outward.
     - Centrifugal force throws water to rim.
     - Volute casing slows water, converting speed to pressure.
     - Water exits at high pressure.
   - **Advantages:**
     - High flow rates.
     - Simple design, few moving parts.
     - Can handle dirty water (with proper design).
     - Works with electric motors.
   - **Disadvantages:**
     - Limited suction (must be primed).
     - Not good for very viscous fluids.
     - Cavitation if pressure drops too low.
   - Kenyan context: Irrigation pumps, water supply systems, drainage pumps.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why centrifugal pumps need priming. Homework: find centrifugal pumps in your area.

## Hands-On Build: Centrifugal Pump Model

### Parts
- Salad spinner — 1 — kitchen
- Water — 1 L — tap
- Container — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Add water to salad spinner.
2. Spin rapidly.
3. Observe water exiting through holes.
4. Measure throw distance.
5. Spin faster. Measure again.
6. **Checkpoint:** The salad spinner must spin freely. A stuck spinner doesn't demonstrate the principle.

### Common Mistakes
- **Too slow:** Water doesn't exit. Fix: spin faster.
- **No water:** Nothing happens. Fix: add enough water.
- **Wrong device:** Some spinners don't have exit holes. Fix: use one with basket holes.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A centrifugal pump impeller has diameter 20 cm and spins at 3000 rpm. Calculate (a) the tangential velocity at the rim, and (b) the theoretical pressure generated assuming all kinetic energy converts to pressure.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) ω = 3000 × 2π/60 = 314 rad/s.
    v = ω × r = 314 × 0.10 = **31.4 m/s**.

(b) ½ρv² = P → P = ½ × 1000 × 31.4² = **493,000 Pa**.
    This is ~5 atm — a strong pump.

(Actual pressure is less due to inefficiencies.)

**Q:** Explain why centrifugal pumps must be primed (filled with water) before starting, and what happens if they run dry.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why prime:**
- Centrifugal pumps can't suck air effectively.
- Air is compressible — impeller just swirls it, doesn't throw it.
- Water is incompressible — impeller pushes it outward efficiently.
- Priming fills pump with water, displacing air.

**Running dry:**
- Impeller spins in air.
- No cooling from water.
- Friction heats bearings and seals.
- Pump overheats and seizes.
- Seal damage from lack of lubrication.
- Expensive repair.

**Priming methods:**
- Fill suction pipe manually.
- Use foot valve (check valve at intake).
- Self-priming designs retain water in casing.
