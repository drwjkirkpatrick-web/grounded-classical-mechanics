# Lesson 194: The Sump Pump: Draining the Pit

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a sump pump removes water from low areas.
2. Build a simple sump pump model.
3. Identify where sump pumps are needed.

## Materials
- Small container (sump)
- Tube and syringe
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Sump** *(shimo la maji)* — a pit or container that collects water.
- **Sump pump** *(pampu ya shimo)* — a pump that removes water from a sump.
- **Float switch** *(swichi ya kuelea)* — a device that turns the pump on when water rises.
- **Drainage** *(kutoa maji)* — removing excess water.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a sump?
2. Why do we need sump pumps?
3. What turns a sump pump on?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A pit that collects water.
2. To remove water that collects in low areas.
3. A float switch (or manual switch).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Rain fills a pit. How do we empty it without climbing in?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build sump pump models.
   - Create a "sump" (container with water).
   - Use syringe to pump water out.
   - Add a float (cork) that rises with water level.
   - **Key discovery:** Pumps in pits need automatic activation. Float switches detect water level.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How sump pumps work:**
     - Water collects in sump (basement, pit, low ground).
     - Float rises with water.
     - At set level, float switch activates pump.
     - Pump removes water to higher ground or drain.
     - Float falls, switch turns off.
   - **Types:**
     - Submersible: pump sits in water.
     - Pedestal: motor above, pump below.
   - **Applications:**
     - Basement drainage.
     - Construction sites.
     - Flood control.
     - Septic systems.
   - Kenyan context: Pit latrine drainage. Basement buildings in cities. Construction dewatering.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains sump pump need. Homework: find sumps in your area.

## Hands-On Build: Sump Pump Model

### Parts
- Container — 1 — classroom
- Tube — 0.5 m — hardware
- Syringe — 1 — pharmacy
- Cork or float — 1 — home
- Water — 2 L — tap

### Workflow
1. Fill container (sump) with water.
2. Submerge tube.
3. Pump with syringe.
4. Add float that rises with water.
5. Mark "pump on" level.
6. **Checkpoint:** Tube must reach bottom of sump. A tube above the water level can't drain the pit.

### Common Mistakes
- **Tube too short:** Can't drain to bottom. Fix: tube reaches sump floor.
- **No seal:** Air enters, pump loses suction. Fix: seal connections.
- **Wrong float:** Doesn't rise properly. Fix: use buoyant material.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A sump pump must remove 500 L/hour from a basement sump. The discharge pipe rises 3 m above the pump. Calculate the minimum pump power required, assuming 60% efficiency.

**Worked Answer:**
Mass per hour = 500 kg.
Work per hour = mgh = 500 × 10 × 3 = 15,000 J/hour.
Power = 15,000 / 3600 = **4.17 W**.

At 60% efficiency: Input power = 4.17 / 0.60 = **6.9 W**.

A very small pump suffices for this head, but flow rate also matters.

**Q:** Explain why a sump pump needs a check valve in the discharge pipe, and what happens without one.

**Worked Answer:**
**Check valve purpose:**
- Allows water to flow only away from sump.
- Prevents backflow when pump stops.

**Without check valve:**
- Pump stops.
- Water in discharge pipe flows back into sump.
- Sump refills partially.
- Pump restarts immediately.
- Short cycling: pump runs frequently, wears out quickly.
- Energy waste.

**Design:**
- Check valve placed near pump outlet.
- Swing-type or spring-loaded.
- Essential for all sump pump installations.
