# Lesson 195: Project: The Siphon Garden System I — Design

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** siphon-garden

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a siphon-based irrigation system for a garden.
2. Calculate flow rates and pipe sizes needed.
3. Plan materials and layout for the system.

## Materials
- Paper and pencils
- String for measuring
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Irrigation system** *(sisteme ya umwagiliaji)* — a network for delivering water to crops.
- **Layout** *(mpangilio)* — the planned arrangement of pipes and outlets.
- **Header pipe** *(bomba kuu)* — the main pipe distributing water.
- **Lateral pipe** *(bomba la kando)* — a branch pipe serving a row of plants.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the purpose of a garden irrigation system?
2. Why use siphons instead of pumps?
3. What determines how much water each plant gets?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Deliver water to plants efficiently.
2. No fuel or electricity needed — gravity powered.
3. Flow rate, duration, number of outlets.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups review site plans.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Design a garden watering system that needs no electricity."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design siphon gardens.
   - Measure garden dimensions.
   - Calculate water needs (L per plant per day).
   - Plan pipe layout: source → header → laterals → outlets.
   - Choose pipe sizes.
   - Calculate flow rates.
   - **Key discovery:** Design determines efficiency. Gravity systems need careful height planning.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Design considerations:**
     - Source must be higher than garden.
     - Pipe size affects flow rate and pressure.
     - Outlets spaced evenly for uniform watering.
     - Need shut-off valves for control.
   - **Calculations:**
     - Head = height difference (determines pressure).
     - Flow rate = volume needed / time available.
     - Pipe size from flow rate tables.
   - **Materials:**
     - PVC pipes or hoses.
     - Connectors and valves.
     - Supports (stakes, clips).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present designs. Homework: gather materials.

## Hands-On Build: Garden System Design

### Parts
- Paper — 5 sheets — classroom
- Pencil — 4 — classroom
- String — 2 m — home
- Calculator — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Measure garden area.
2. Count plants and rows.
3. Calculate total water needed.
4. Design pipe layout.
5. Choose pipe sizes.
6. List materials.
7. **Checkpoint:** Verify source is higher than garden. A siphon can't flow uphill overall.

### Common Mistakes
- **Source too low:** No flow. Fix: find higher source or use pump.
- **Pipes too small:** Insufficient flow. Fix: calculate from flow rate.
- **Uneven spacing:** Some plants get too much, others too little. Fix: equal outlets.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A garden of 20 m × 10 m has 200 plants needing 2 L each per day. The water source is a tank 3 m above the garden. Design a system with one header pipe and 4 lateral pipes. Calculate the flow rate per lateral and suggest pipe diameters.

**Worked Answer:**
Total water = 200 × 2 = **400 L/day**.

If watering for 2 hours = 7200 s:
Q_total = 400/7200 = 0.0556 L/s = **55.6 mL/s**.

Per lateral (4 laterals): Q = 55.6/4 = **13.9 mL/s**.

**Pipe sizes:**
- Header: 20 mm diameter (handles 56 mL/s easily).
- Laterals: 12 mm diameter each.

**Pressure:**
P = ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 3 = 30,000 Pa.
Sufficient for low-pressure drip system.

**Q:** Compare the cost and reliability of a siphon gravity system vs a small solar pump system for a 1-hectare farm over 5 years.

**Worked Answer:**
**Siphon gravity:**
- Initial cost: Low (pipes, valves).
- Operating cost: Zero.
- Maintenance: Minimal (clearing blockages).
- Reliability: Very high (no moving parts).
- Limitation: Requires elevated water source.

**Solar pump:**
- Initial cost: High (panels, pump, controller).
- Operating cost: Near zero (sun is free).
- Maintenance: Moderate (panel cleaning, pump seals).
- Reliability: Good (panels last 20+ years).
- Advantage: Works from any water source.

**5-year total cost:**
- Siphon: **Very low** (if suitable terrain).
- Solar: **Moderate** (payback in 3–5 years vs diesel).

**Recommendation:**
- Elevated source available → siphon.
- Flat terrain with underground water → solar.
