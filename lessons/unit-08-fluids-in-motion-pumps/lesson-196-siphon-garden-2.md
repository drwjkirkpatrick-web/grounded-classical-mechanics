# Lesson 196: Project: The Siphon Garden System II — Build and Test

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** siphon-garden

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a working siphon irrigation system from the Lesson 195 design.
2. Test the system for leaks and even flow.
3. Adjust the system for optimal performance.

## Materials
- Tubes and connectors from Lesson 195 plan
- Water container (elevated)
- Stakes or clips
- Timer
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Assembly** *(kujenga)* — putting parts together.
- **Leak test** *(kupima kuvuja)* — checking for water escaping.
- **Flow adjustment** *(kurekebisha mkondo)* — changing valves to balance water delivery.
- **Calibration** *(kupiga mizani)* — adjusting to deliver correct amounts.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the first step in building your system?
2. How do you check for leaks?
3. What do you do if one area gets too much water?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Lay out pipes according to design.
2. Run water and look for escaping water.
3. Adjust valve or reduce outlet size.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups collect materials.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Build day! Your design becomes real."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build and test systems.
   - Lay out pipes per design.
   - Connect fittings.
   - Elevate source.
   - Fill and test.
   - Check for leaks.
   - Measure flow at each outlet.
   - Adjust valves for even distribution.
   - **Key discovery:** Real systems need adjustment. Designs don't always work perfectly.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Building tips:**
     - Start at source, work toward outlets.
     - Secure pipes to prevent movement.
     - Use Teflon tape or sealant on threaded fittings.
     - Test sections before full assembly.
   - **Common problems:**
     - Leaks at joints: tighten or reseal.
     - Uneven flow: kinks, elevation changes, wrong pipe size.
     - Air locks: bleed air from high points.
   - **Adjustment:**
     - Partially close valves to high-flow areas.
     - Raise or lower pipe sections.
     - Add or remove restrictions.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups demonstrate systems. Homework: monitor performance over a day.

6. Check for leaks.
7. Adjust valves.
8. Measure flow rates.
9. **Checkpoint:** Before full testing, run water through each section separately. Isolates problems.

### Common Mistakes
- **Leaky joints:** Water escapes. Fix: tighten, add sealant.
- **Kinks:** Block flow. Fix: straighten, add supports.
- **Wrong slope:** Water pools. Fix: ensure continuous downward grade.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your system delivers 30% more water to the first outlet than the last. Explain why and suggest three engineering fixes.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why:**
- Pressure highest at source (least friction loss).
- Friction in pipes reduces pressure along length.
- First outlet sees full pressure; last sees reduced pressure.

**Fixes:**
1. **Larger diameter pipe:** Reduces friction loss.
2. **Pressure-compensating emitters:** Deliver same flow regardless of pressure.
3. **Multiple headers:** Split into shorter parallel runs.
4. **Pressure regulator:** Maintain constant pressure.
5. **Loop design:** Return pipe creates more even pressure distribution.

**Q:** Calculate the actual flow rate if the design predicted 50 mL/s but the source is 0.5 m lower than planned. Explain the effect.

**Worked Answer:**
P_actual = ρgh_actual = 1000 × 10 × (h_planned − 0.5).

If h_planned = 2 m: P = 20,000 Pa.
If h_actual = 1.5 m: P = 15,000 Pa.

Pressure reduced by **25%**.

Flow ∝ √P (Torricelli), so flow reduced by √0.75 = 0.866.

Actual flow = 50 × 0.866 = **43.3 mL/s**.

Or simply: less head = less pressure = less flow.
