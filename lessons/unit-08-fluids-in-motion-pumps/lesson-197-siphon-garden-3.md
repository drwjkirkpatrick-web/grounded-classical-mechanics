# Lesson 197: Project: The Siphon Garden System III — Troubleshoot and Optimise

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** siphon-garden

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify and fix common problems in siphon irrigation systems.
2. Optimise water distribution for even coverage.
3. Document system performance for future reference.

## Materials
- Siphon garden system from Lesson 196
- Repair materials
- Measuring cups
- Timer
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Troubleshoot** *(kutatua shida)* — finding and fixing problems.
- **Optimise** *(kuboresha)* — making the system work as well as possible.
- **Uniformity** *(usawa)* — even distribution across all outlets.
- **Efficiency** *(ufanisi)* — getting the most benefit from available water.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the most common problem in irrigation systems?
2. How do you know if water is distributed evenly?
3. What should you do if a siphon loses its prime?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Leaks, clogs, or uneven pressure.
2. Measure water at each outlet.
3. Re-prime by filling the tube again.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups review their systems.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Your system worked yesterday. Today it's broken. Why?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners troubleshoot systems.
   - Observe system running.
   - Identify problems: leaks, clogs, uneven flow, lost prime.
   - Fix problems.
   - Measure flow at each outlet.
   - Calculate uniformity.
   - **Key discovery:** Systems need regular maintenance. Small problems become big ones.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Common problems:**
     - **Leaks:** Wasted water, reduced pressure. Fix: tighten, replace.
     - **Clogs:** Dirt, algae, debris. Fix: flush, add filter.
     - **Uneven flow:** Wrong slope, kinks, pressure differences. Fix: adjust, resize.
     - **Lost prime:** Air enters tube. Fix: refill, seal.
   - **Optimisation:**
     - Measure and record flow rates.
     - Adjust valves for even distribution.
     - Add pressure-compensating emitters.
     - Document settings.
   - **Maintenance schedule:**
     - Daily: visual check.
     - Weekly: clean filters.
     - Monthly: check for leaks.
     - Seasonally: flush entire system.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present optimised systems. Homework: create a maintenance checklist.

2. Observe problems.

4. Calculate uniformity.
5. Fix problems.
6. Re-measure.
7. Document.
8. **Checkpoint:** Measure before and after each fix. Confirms improvement.

### Common Mistakes
- **Guessing:** Not measuring. Fix: use cups and timer.
- **Over-adjusting:** Fixing one problem creates another. Fix: adjust one thing at a time.
- **Ignoring small leaks:** They add up. Fix: repair immediately.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your system has uniformity coefficient 0.6 (60% even). The first outlet delivers 200 mL/min and the last delivers 80 mL/min. Calculate the average and suggest three changes to achieve uniformity coefficient 0.9.

**Worked Answer:**
Average = (200 + 80) / 2 = **140 mL/min**.

**Changes:**
1. **Pressure-compensating emitters:** Deliver same rate regardless of pressure.
2. **Larger main pipe:** Reduces friction, evens pressure.
3. **Multiple headers:** Parallel paths reduce length-dependent loss.
4. **Flow restrictors at first outlets:** Limit high-flow areas.

**Expected result:** All outlets near 140 mL/min, uniformity coefficient ~0.9.

**Q:** A farmer's siphon system loses prime every morning. The source is a pond with fluctuating water level. Design a self-priming modification that works automatically.

**Worked Answer:**
**Problem:** Falling water level exposes tube inlet.

**Self-priming design:**
1. **Floating intake:** Tube inlet attached to a float that rises and falls with water level.
2. **Check valve at inlet:** Prevents water draining back when level drops.
3. **Siphon break valve:** Opens when level too low, prevents air lock.
4. **Automatic restart:** When level rises, water refills tube, check valve closes, siphon restarts.

**Operation:**
- Water level drops: float follows, check valve closes.
- Water level rises: float rises, tube fills, siphon starts automatically.
- No manual priming needed.
