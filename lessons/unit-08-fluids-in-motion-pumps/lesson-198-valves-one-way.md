# Lesson 198: Valves: The Heart of Every Pump

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how one-way valves control fluid direction.
2. Build and test simple flap and ball valves.
3. Identify valves in everyday devices.

## Materials
- Rubber sheet or balloon
- Ball and tube
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Valve** *(valvu)* — a device that controls fluid flow direction.
- **Check valve** *(valvu ya kukagua)* — a one-way valve that prevents backflow.
- **Flap valve** *(valvu ya kipeperushi)* — a valve with a flexible flap that opens one way.
- **Ball valve** *(valvu ya mpira)* — a valve with a ball that seats to block flow.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a one-way valve do?
2. Why do pumps need valves?
3. Name a place you find a valve.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Lets fluid flow one direction only.
2. To keep water moving forward, not backward.
3. Heart, taps, pumps, tyres.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Your heart has valves. What would happen if they leaked?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build and test valves.
   - **Flap valve:** Rubber sheet over tube. Blow one way — opens. Blow other way — seals.
   - **Ball valve:** Ball in tube. Water flows down, ball lifts. Water flows up, ball seals.
   - Test with actual water flow.
   - **Key discovery:** Valves are simple but essential. They make pumps possible.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How valves work:**
     - Flap valve: pressure from one side lifts flap, from other side presses it shut.
     - Ball valve: gravity or spring seats ball; flow lifts it.
   - **In pumps:**
     - Two valves alternate: one opens while other closes.
     - Creates one-directional flow.
   - **In heart:**
     - Four valves ensure blood flows in correct direction.
     - Leaky valves cause backflow, reduced circulation.
   - **Everyday valves:**
     - Tap: controls flow rate.
     - Tyre valve: one-way air entry.
     - Pressure cooker: releases at set pressure.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner traces water through a pump with valves. Homework: count valves at home.

## Hands-On Build: Simple Valves

### Parts
- Rubber piece — 1 — balloon
- Tube — 0.3 m — hardware
- Ball — 1 — small
- Water — 1 L — tap

### Workflow
1. **Flap valve:** Tape rubber over one end of tube. Blow from each direction.
2. **Ball valve:** Put ball in tube. Water from one direction — flows. From other — ball seals.
3. Test with water.
4. **Checkpoint:** For flap valve, ensure flap is flexible but covers opening completely.

### Common Mistakes
- **Stiff flap:** Doesn't open. Fix: use thinner material.
- **Wrong ball size:** Too small leaks, too big doesn't seat. Fix: ball just smaller than tube.
- **Not sealing:** Leaks both ways. Fix: ensure tight fit.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A pump with two valves lifts water 5 m at 2 L/s. Calculate the power required and explain how valve timing affects efficiency.

**Worked Answer:**
Power = mgh/t = (2 × 5 × 10) = **100 W** (theoretical).

**Valve timing:**
- If valves open/close too early: water slams, loses energy.
- If too late: water flows backward, reduces net flow.
- Optimal: valves switch at exact moment of flow reversal.
- Poor timing: efficiency drops to 50–70%.
- Good timing: efficiency reaches 80–90%.

**Q:** Explain heart valve prolapse using the physics of pressure differentials and leaflet closure. Why does a leaky valve cause reduced cardiac output?

**Worked Answer:**
**Prolapse:**
- Valve leaflets bulge backward instead of sealing.
- Blood flows backward (regurgitation).

**Physics:**
- Normal: pressure difference snaps leaflets shut.
- Prolapse: leaflets don't meet; gap remains.
- Reverse pressure pushes blood through gap.

**Reduced output:**
- Some blood pumped forward flows back.
- Net forward flow reduced.
- Heart must pump harder to compensate.
- Enlarged heart, fatigue, eventual failure.
