# Lesson 199: Build: A Hand Diaphragm Pump I

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** diaphragm-pump

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a hand-operated diaphragm pump from simple materials.
2. Explain how a diaphragm creates suction and pressure.
3. Identify where diaphragm pumps are used.

## Materials
- Plastic bottle
- Balloon or rubber sheet
- Two tubes
- Glue or tape
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Diaphragm** *(kipeperushi)* — a flexible membrane that moves to displace fluid.
- **Suction stroke** *(mzingo wa kuvuta)* — the part of the cycle that draws fluid in.
- **Pressure stroke** *(mzingo wa kushinikiza)* — the part of the cycle that pushes fluid out.
- **Displacement** *(kukimbia)* — the volume of fluid moved per cycle.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a diaphragm pump move water?
2. What happens when you pull a flexible membrane back?
3. Name one use for a diaphragm pump.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Flexing membrane creates suction and pressure.
2. Creates low pressure, drawing fluid in.
3. Air pumps, medical suction, fuel transfer.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Blow up a balloon, then let it snap back. What happens to air inside?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design diaphragm pumps.
   - Cut bottle to create chamber.
   - Attach balloon as diaphragm.
   - Add inlet and outlet tubes.
   - Test by flexing diaphragm.
   - **Key discovery:** Flexing creates suction (pull back) and pressure (push forward).

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How diaphragm pumps work:**
     - Diaphragm pulled back → chamber volume increases → pressure drops → fluid enters through inlet valve.
     - Diaphragm pushed forward → chamber volume decreases → pressure rises → fluid exits through outlet valve.
     - Valves ensure one-way flow.
   - **Advantages:**
     - Simple, few moving parts.
     - Can handle dirty water (diaphragm isolates mechanism).
     - Portable.
   - **Applications:**
     - Bicycle pumps (diaphragm or piston).
     - Medical suction devices.
     - Fuel transfer pumps.
     - Aquarium pumps.
   - Kenyan context: Foot-operated pumps for spraying. Bicycle tire pumps. Medical equipment.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups test designs. Homework: find diaphragm pumps in daily life.

## Hands-On Build: Diaphragm Pump

### Parts
- Plastic bottle — 1 — home
- Balloon — 1 — home
- Two tubes — 0.2 m each — hardware
- Tape — 1 — classroom
- Water — 1 L — tap

### Workflow
1. Cut bottle to create open chamber.
2. Attach balloon over opening.
3. Insert inlet tube (with valve) near bottom.
4. Insert outlet tube (with valve) near top.
5. Place inlet in water.
6. Pull balloon back — water enters.
7. Push balloon forward — water exits.
8. **Checkpoint:** Valves must seal in one direction. Test each valve separately before assembly.

### Common Mistakes
- **Leaky valves:** Water flows both ways. Fix: ensure one-way action.
- **Diaphragm too stiff:** Hard to flex. Fix: use thinner material.
- **Wrong tube placement:** Inlet too high. Fix: inlet near bottom.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A diaphragm pump has chamber volume 200 mL and operates at 1 Hz (one cycle per second). Calculate the theoretical flow rate. If the actual flow rate is 150 mL/s, what is the volumetric efficiency?

**Worked Answer:**
Theoretical flow = 200 mL/cycle × 1 cycle/s = **200 mL/s**.

Actual = 150 mL/s.

Efficiency = (150/200) × 100% = **75%**.

**Losses:**
- Valve leakage.
- Incomplete chamber filling.
- Diaphragm deflection not reaching full volume.
- Air in system.

**Q:** Explain why diaphragm pumps are preferred over piston pumps for medical suction and chemical transfer, using the concepts of contamination and seal integrity.

**Worked Answer:**
**Contamination:**
- Diaphragm isolates pumped fluid from mechanism.
- No lubricant enters fluid.
- No fluid enters mechanism.
- Essential for medical and food applications.

**Seal integrity:**
- Diaphragm provides complete seal.
- Piston requires tight-fitting seal that wears.
- Diaphragm pumps handle fluids that would damage piston seals.

**Chemical transfer:**
- Corrosive chemicals don't touch metal parts.
- Diaphragm material chosen for chemical resistance.
- Piston pumps need expensive corrosion-resistant materials.
