# Lesson 200: Build: A Hand Diaphragm Pump II — Performance Test

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** diaphragm-pump

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Test and measure the performance of a diaphragm pump.
2. Calculate flow rate and efficiency.
3. Compare designs and suggest improvements.

## Materials
- Diaphragm pump from Lesson 199
- Water container
- Timer
- Measuring cup
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Performance test** *(kupima utendaji)* — measuring how well a device works.
- **Flow rate** *(kasi ya mkondo)* — volume moved per unit time.
- **Efficiency** *(ufanisi)* — ratio of actual output to theoretical output.
- **Stroke rate** *(kasi ya mzingo)* — number of pumping cycles per minute.

## Pre-Test
1. How do you measure a pump's performance?
2. What limits a hand pump's flow rate?
3. How can you increase a pump's output?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Measure volume pumped over time.
2. Human speed, diaphragm size, valve leakage.
3. Faster strokes, larger diaphragm, better valves.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups prepare pumps.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Your pump works. But how well? Let's measure."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test pump performance.
   - Measure water pumped in 30 seconds.
   - Try different stroke rates: slow, medium, fast.
   - Measure maximum lift height.
   - Test for leaks.
   - **Key discovery:** Performance depends on design and operation. Faster isn't always better.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Measurements:**
     - Flow rate = volume / time.
     - Stroke rate = cycles / minute.
     - Displacement per stroke = flow rate / stroke rate.
   - **Limitations:**
     - Human power: 50–100 W maximum.
     - Valve leakage: reduces effective flow.
     - Diaphragm fatigue: material stretches over time.
   - **Improvements:**
     - Larger diaphragm = more displacement.
     - Better valves = less leakage.
     - Smoother motion = less energy waste.
   - **Comparison:**
     - Groups compare designs.
     - Identify best features.
     - Vote on most efficient pump.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present results. Homework: suggest three improvements.

## Hands-On Build: Pump Performance Test

### Parts
- Diaphragm pump — 1 — built
- Water — 2 L — tap
- Container — 1 — classroom
- Timer — 1 — classroom
- Measuring cup — 1 — kitchen

### Workflow
1. Set up pump and water source.
2. Pump for 30 seconds at slow rate.
3. Measure water pumped.
4. Repeat at medium and fast rates.
5. Calculate flow rate for each.
6. Find maximum lift.
7. **Checkpoint:** Keep stroke consistent during each test. Varying stroke affects results.

### Common Mistakes
- **Inconsistent rate:** Varies during test. Fix: use metronome or count beats.
- **Not filling:** Pump runs dry. Fix: keep source full.
- **Wrong measurement:** Eye level with water. Fix: read carefully.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Your pump displaces 150 mL per stroke. At 60 strokes per minute, calculate theoretical flow rate. If actual flow is 120 mL/min, calculate volumetric efficiency and identify two likely causes of loss.

**Worked Answer:**
Theoretical = 150 × 60 = **9000 mL/min**.

Actual = 120 mL/min.

Wait — this seems very low. Perhaps stroke rate is per second? Let's assume 60 strokes per minute = 1 per second.

Theoretical = 150 mL/stroke × 1 stroke/s = 150 mL/s = **9000 mL/min**.

Actual = 120 mL/min.

Efficiency = 120/9000 = **1.3%**. This suggests major problems.

**Causes:**
1. **Massive valve leakage:** Valves don't seal.
2. **Diaphragm not displacing:** Flexes but doesn't move volume.
3. **Air in system:** Compresses instead of moving water.
4. **Wrong measurement:** Units confused.

**More realistic example:**
If actual = 6000 mL/min:
Efficiency = 6000/9000 = **67%**.

Causes of 33% loss:
1. Valve leakage: 15%.
2. Incomplete filling: 10%.
3. Diaphragm deflection limits: 8%.

**Q:** Design a competition to compare diaphragm pump performance. Specify rules, scoring, and fairness criteria.

**Worked Answer:**
**Rules:**
1. All pumps must use same materials (provided).
2. Must fit in 30 cm × 30 cm box.
3. Human-powered only.
4. 2-minute test period.

**Scoring:**
- Flow rate (mL/min): 50 points.
- Maximum lift (cm): 30 points.
- Efficiency (actual/theoretical): 20 points.

**Fairness:**
- Same operator for all tests (or average of three operators).
- Same water source height.
- Same timing method.
- Independent judges.
- Reproducible tests (run three times, average).
