# Lesson 201: The Hydraulic Ram: Water That Pumps Itself

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a hydraulic ram uses water hammer to pump water uphill.
2. Identify the parts of a hydraulic ram.
3. Appreciate that no fuel or electricity is needed.

## Materials
- Hydraulic ram or diagram
- Photos or videos of rams in operation
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Hydraulic ram** *(pambu ya maji)* — a pump that uses water flow to lift a portion of water higher.
- **Water hammer** *(pigo la maji)* — a pressure surge from suddenly stopping flowing water.
- **Drive pipe** *(bomba la nguvu)* — the pipe bringing water to the ram.
- **Delivery pipe** *(bomba la kutoa)* — the pipe carrying water to the tank.

## Pre-Test
1. Can water pump itself without fuel or electricity?
2. What is water hammer?
3. Why is a hydraulic ram useful for remote farms?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes — hydraulic ram.
2. Pressure surge when flowing water stops suddenly.
3. No fuel needed, runs continuously from stream flow.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Water flowing downhill can push water uphill. How?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
    - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners model water hammer.
   - Run water through a tube, then quickly clamp it shut.
   - Feel the pressure surge (tube bulges or water spurts).
   - This is the principle behind hydraulic rams.
   - **Key discovery:** Stopping flowing water creates enormous pressure. This pressure can push water uphill.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How hydraulic rams work:**
     - Water flows down drive pipe from a stream or spring.
     - Reaches a valve that slams shut.
     - Water hammer creates high pressure.
     - Pressure forces some water through a check valve into an air chamber.
     - Compressed air in chamber pushes water up delivery pipe.
     - Valve reopens, cycle repeats (60–120 times per minute).
   - **Efficiency:**
     - Only 10–30% of water is lifted.
     - Rest flows out as waste.
     - But it's free energy from gravity!
   - **Applications:**
     - Remote farms with flowing water.
     - No electricity needed.
     - Low maintenance, long life.
   - Kenyan context: Used in highland areas with streams. Can lift water 20–50 m vertically.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains the ram cycle. Homework: find streams that could power a ram.

## Hands-On Build: Water Hammer Demonstration

### Parts
- Tube — 1 m — hardware
- Tap water — running
- Clamp or kink mechanism — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Run water through tube.
2. Quickly clamp or kink the tube.
3. Feel pressure surge (tube bulges).
4. Release.
5. Repeat.
6. **Checkpoint:** The faster you stop the flow, the stronger the hammer. Gradual closing weakens the effect.

### Common Mistakes
- **Slow closing:** Weak pressure. Fix: snap shut quickly.
- **Tube too stiff:** Doesn't show bulge. Fix: use flexible tube.
- **Low pressure source:** Weak flow. Fix: higher tap pressure or longer tube.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A hydraulic ram receives water from a stream falling 2 m. It lifts 10% of the water 20 m above the source. Calculate the maximum theoretical efficiency and explain why actual efficiency is lower.

**Worked Answer:**
Energy in: water falling 2 m (for all water).
Energy out: 10% of water lifted 20 m.

Efficiency = (0.10 × 20) / (1 × 2) = 2 / 2 = **100%**.

This is the theoretical maximum — actual is lower due to:
- Friction in pipes.
- Valve leakage.
- Air chamber losses.
- Turbulence.
- **Actual: 50–70%** of theoretical.

**Q:** Explain why a hydraulic ram needs an air chamber and what happens if the air dissolves in water over time.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why air chamber:**
- Water hammer creates pressure spike.
- Air compresses, absorbing and storing energy.
- Compressed air then pushes water steadily up delivery pipe.
- Without air chamber: pressure spikes are violent, pipes can burst.
- Air acts as shock absorber and energy storage.

**Air dissolving:**
- Over time, air dissolves into water.
- Chamber fills with water.
- No compressible air = no cushioning.
- Water hammer becomes violent.
- System becomes noisy and inefficient.
- **Fix:** Regularly drain and refill air chamber. Some designs have snifter valves that automatically add air.
