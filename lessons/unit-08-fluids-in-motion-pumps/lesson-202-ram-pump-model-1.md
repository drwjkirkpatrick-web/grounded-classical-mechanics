# Lesson 202: Teacher-Led Build: A Mini Ram Pump I

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Observe the construction of a mini hydraulic ram pump.
2. Identify the drive pipe, waste valve, delivery valve, and air chamber.
3. Explain the cycle of valve opening and closing.

## Materials
- PVC pipe and fittings (teacher-provided)
- Check valves
- Small air chamber (bottle or tube)
- Water source
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Drive pipe** *(bomba la nguvu)* — the pipe carrying water to the ram.
- **Waste valve** *(valvu ya kupitisha)* — the valve that opens and closes to create water hammer.
- **Delivery valve** *(valvu ya kutoa)* — the one-way valve to the air chamber.
- **Air chamber** *(chombo cha hewa)* — the chamber storing compressed air.

## Pre-Test
1. What creates the pressure in a hydraulic ram?
2. What does the waste valve do?
3. Why does the ram need two valves?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Water hammer from suddenly stopping flow.
2. Opens to let water flow, then slams shut to create pressure.
3. Waste valve creates pressure; delivery valve lets pressurised water out.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Teacher prepares build area.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Today we build a pump that needs no fuel — just flowing water."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Teacher builds mini ram pump while learners observe and label parts.
   - Connect drive pipe to water source.
   - Install waste valve (flap valve that closes under pressure).
   - Install delivery valve (one-way to air chamber).
   - Connect air chamber.
   - Attach delivery pipe.
   - Test: water flows, waste valve clicks, delivery pipe outputs water.
   - **Key discovery:** The clicking sound is the waste valve cycling. Each click pushes water higher.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Build steps:**
     - Drive pipe: 1–2 m long, downhill from source.
     - Waste valve: weighted flap that closes when flow is fast enough.
     - Delivery valve: spring-loaded or ball check valve.
     - Air chamber: small bottle or tube to store pressure.
     - Delivery pipe: rises to storage tank.
   - **Cycle:**
     1. Water flows, waste valve open.
     2. Flow increases, valve closes.
     3. Water hammer pressure spikes.
     4. Delivery valve opens, water enters air chamber.
     5. Pressure drops, waste valve reopens.
     6. Cycle repeats.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Count valve clicks per minute. Calculate water lifted. Homework: sketch the ram cycle.

## Hands-On Build: Mini Ram Pump

### Parts (teacher-provided)
- PVC pipe — 2 m — hardware
- Tee fitting — 1 — hardware
- Check valve — 2 — hardware
- Flap valve — 1 — hardware
- Air chamber (small bottle) — 1 — home

### Workflow (teacher-led)
1. Connect drive pipe to source.
2. Install waste valve at tee.
3. Install delivery valve to air chamber.
4. Connect delivery pipe upward.
5. Open source.
6. Adjust waste valve weight until cycling begins.
7. **Checkpoint:** Waste valve must close abruptly. Gradual closing = no water hammer = no pumping.

### Common Mistakes
- **Gradual valve closing:** No hammer. Fix: use spring or weight for snap action.
- **No air chamber:** Pressure spikes damage pipes. Fix: always include chamber.
- **Leaky delivery valve:** Water flows back. Fix: ensure one-way seal.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What creates pressure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What does waste valve do?
3. What is the clicking sound?
4. Why is the air chamber needed?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Water hammer**.
2. Opens and closes to create pressure.
3. **Waste valve cycling**.
4. **Stores pressure** and cushions shocks.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A mini ram pump cycles 60 times per minute. Each cycle lifts 5 mL of water 3 m. Calculate the hourly output and the power equivalent.

**Worked Answer:**
Output per minute = 60 × 5 = **300 mL/min**.
Hourly = 300 × 60 = **18,000 mL/hour = 18 L/hour**.

Power = mgh/t per cycle × cycles per second
= (0.005 × 10 × 3) × (60/60)
= 0.15 × 1 = **0.15 W**.

Tiny but continuous — over a day = 432 L lifted 3 m.

**Q:** Explain why the waste valve must be precisely tuned (weighted) for a given flow rate and drive pipe length. What happens if it is too heavy or too light?

**Worked Answer:**
**Too light:**
- Closes too easily.
- Cycles at low flow — inefficient.
- May not build enough pressure.
- Waste water without lifting much.

**Too heavy:**
- Needs high flow to close.
- May not close at all if flow insufficient.
- No water hammer = no pumping.

**Optimal:**
- Closes when flow reaches specific velocity.
- Creates strong hammer at right frequency.
- Tuning requires adjusting weight to match flow conditions.
