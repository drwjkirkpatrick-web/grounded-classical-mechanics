# Lesson 203: Teacher-Led Build: A Mini Ram Pump II — Tuning

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Adjust the waste valve weight for optimal cycling.
2. Measure the ram pump's output and efficiency.
3. Explain how tuning affects performance.

## Materials
- Mini ram pump from Lesson 202
- Weights (washers, nuts)
- Measuring cup
- Timer
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Tuning** *(kuboresha)* — adjusting a device for best performance.
- **Cycle rate** *(kasi ya mzunguko)* — how often the valve clicks per minute.
- **Optimal weight** *(uzito bora)* — the waste valve weight that produces maximum output.
- **Efficiency curve** *(mstari wa ufanisi)* — how output changes with different settings.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens if the waste valve is too heavy?
2. What happens if the waste valve is too light?
3. How do you know when the pump is tuned correctly?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Needs too much flow to close; may not pump.
2. Closes too easily; weak pressure, poor output.
3. Steady clicking, maximum water output, consistent flow.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Review pump from Lesson 202.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "One weight doesn't fit all. How do we find the sweet spot?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Teacher demonstrates tuning while learners measure.
   - Start with current valve weight.
   - Measure output for 1 minute.
   - Add weight. Measure again.
   - Remove weight. Measure again.
   - Record: weight, cycle rate, output.
   - **Key discovery:** There's an optimal weight. Too heavy or too light reduces output.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Tuning process:**
     - Add weight until valve barely closes.
     - Measure output.
     - Add more weight — output may increase.
     - Continue until output drops (valve doesn't close).
     - Back off to best setting.
   - **What affects tuning:**
     - Flow rate: more flow needs heavier valve.
     - Pipe length: longer pipe needs heavier valve.
     - Head: steeper slope changes timing.
   - **Efficiency:**
     - Tuned ram: 50–70% of theoretical.
     - Untuned: 10–30%.
     - Tuning doubles output.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Record best settings. Homework: predict optimal weight for a different flow.

## Hands-On Build: Ram Pump Tuning

### Parts
- Ram pump — 1 — built
- Weights — various — hardware
- Measuring cup — 1 — kitchen
- Timer — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Start pump with current weight.
2. Measure output for 60 seconds.
3. Add weight. Re-measure.
4. Repeat.
5. Plot output vs weight.
6. Find maximum.
7. **Checkpoint:** Wait 30 seconds after each weight change before measuring. Pump needs time to stabilise.

### Common Mistakes
- **Measuring too soon:** Pump not stabilised. Fix: wait 30 seconds.
- **Small changes:** Hard to detect difference. Fix: change weight significantly.
- **Wrong unit:** Confusing grams and washers. Fix: record actual weight.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Too heavy valve?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Too light valve?
3. Why wait before measuring after a change?
4. Name one factor affecting tuning.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Needs too much flow**; may not pump.
2. **Closes too easily**; weak pressure.
3. Pump needs time to **stabilise**.
4. Any: **flow rate**, **pipe length**, **slope**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A ram pump has drive head 2 m and lifts water 10 m. Theoretical efficiency is 20%. After tuning, actual output is 15% of drive flow. Calculate the tuning efficiency and suggest why it's below theoretical.

**Worked Answer:**
Theoretical lift fraction = 20%.
Actual = 15%.

Tuning efficiency = 15/20 = **75%**.

**Why below theoretical:**
- Friction in pipes.
- Valve leakage.
- Incomplete air chamber.
- Turbulence losses.
- Energy in waste water.

**Q:** Explain why a ram pump's cycle rate should not be too fast or too slow for a given installation.

**Worked Answer:**
**Too fast (light valve):**
- Closes before flow fully develops.
- Weak water hammer.
- Low pressure spike.
- Little water lifted.
- Wastes drive water.

**Too slow (heavy valve):**
- Closes after too much flow.
- Strong hammer but infrequent.
- Lower average output.
- Risk of valve not closing at all.

**Optimal:**
- Cycles 60–120 times per minute.
- Each cycle builds maximum pressure.
- Average output maximised.
- Balance between pressure and frequency.
