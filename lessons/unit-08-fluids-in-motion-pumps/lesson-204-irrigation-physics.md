# Lesson 204: Irrigation Physics: Drip, Furrow and Sprinkler

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Compare drip, furrow, and sprinkler irrigation methods.
2. Calculate water delivery for each method.
3. Choose the best method for given conditions.

## Materials
- Drip tube or perforated bottle
- Furrow model (tray with channels)
- Sprinkler or spray bottle
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Drip irrigation** *(umwagiliaji wa matone)* — delivering water directly to plant roots drop by drop.
- **Furrow irrigation** *(umwagiliaji wa mishimo)* — channeling water between crop rows.
- **Sprinkler irrigation** *(umwagiliaji wa kunyunyizia)* — spraying water over crops like rain.
- **Application efficiency** *(ufanisi wa kutumia)* — percentage of water that reaches plant roots.

## Pre-Test
1. Which irrigation method uses least water?
2. Which is best for hilly land?
3. Why might sprinklers waste water?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Drip irrigation.
2. Drip or sprinkler (furrow needs level ground).
3. Wind blows spray away; evaporation losses.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Three ways to water a field. Which is best?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test irrigation methods.
   - **Drip:** Poke holes in bottle, let water drip onto soil.
   - **Furrow:** Channel water through grooves in tray.
   - **Sprinkler:** Spray water over tray.
   - Measure: water used, area covered, evaporation loss.
   - **Key discovery:** Each method has trade-offs. Drip is most efficient; sprinkler covers most area.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Drip:**
     - Pros: 90–95% efficient, no runoff, reduces weeds.
     - Cons: Expensive, clogs easily, needs clean water.
   - **Furrow:**
     - Pros: Cheap, simple, uses gravity.
     - Cons: 50–70% efficient, erosion risk, needs level land.
   - **Sprinkler:**
     - Pros: Covers large area, mimics rain.
     - Cons: 60–75% efficient, wind losses, equipment cost.
   - **Choosing:**
     - Water scarce → drip.
     - Flat land, plenty water → furrow.
     - Large area, mechanised → sprinkler.
   - Kenyan context: Drip gaining popularity for horticulture. Furrow traditional for maize. Sprinklers in flower farms.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups vote on best method for local conditions. Homework: observe irrigation methods nearby.

## Hands-On Build: Irrigation Comparison

### Parts
- Bottle with holes — 1 — home
- Tray with grooves — 1 — classroom
- Spray bottle — 1 — home
- Soil or sand — 2 kg — ground
- Water — 2 L — tap

### Workflow
1. Set up three identical plots.
2. Water each with different method.
3. Measure water used.
4. Observe penetration depth.
5. Calculate coverage.
6. **Checkpoint:** Use same soil type and starting moisture. Different soils absorb water differently.

### Common Mistakes
- **Different soil:** Affects absorption. Fix: use same soil.
- **Different amounts:** Hard to compare. Fix: measure and record.
- **Wind:** Affects sprinkler. Fix: do indoors or on calm day.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Which uses least water?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Which for hilly land?
3. Why is drip most efficient?
4. Name one disadvantage of furrow irrigation.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Drip**.
2. **Drip** or **sprinkler**.
3. **Direct to roots**, minimal evaporation/runoff.
4. Any: **erosion**, **uneven coverage**, **water waste**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A farmer has 1 hectare (10,000 m²) of tomatoes. Drip irrigation needs 5 mm/day equivalent; furrow needs 8 mm/day. Calculate the annual water saving of drip over furrow in m³.

**Worked Answer:**
Daily difference = 8 − 5 = **3 mm**.

Volume saved per day = 10,000 × 0.003 = **30 m³/day**.

Annual = 30 × 365 = **10,950 m³/year**.

At KSh 20/m³ (pumped water), annual saving = **KSh 219,000**.

**Q:** Design an irrigation system for a sloping field that minimises erosion while maximising water use efficiency. Justify your choices.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design: Contour drip irrigation**

**Layout:**
- Drip lines follow contour lines (perpendicular to slope).
- Prevents water from running downhill.
- Each plant gets direct water.

**Components:**
- Header pipe at top of field.
- Pressure regulator (drip needs low pressure).
- Inline drippers spaced at plant intervals.
- Mulch cover to reduce evaporation.

**Why:**
- **Erosion:** Zero runoff with drip.
- **Efficiency:** 90%+ water reaches roots.
- **Slope:** Contour lines prevent channeling.
- **Labour:** Minimal once installed.

**Cost:** Higher initial, but water savings pay back in 2–3 years.
