# Lesson 205: Rivers, Bridges and Floods: Motion at Large Scale

**Unit:** 8 — Fluids in Motion, Siphons & Pumps | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how river flow relates to channel shape and slope.
2. Demonstrate why rivers flood.
3. Identify how bridge design accounts for river flow.

## Materials
- Tray or channel model
- Sand or soil
- Water
- Small bridge model (blocks or sticks)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Channel** *(kituo cha mto)* — the riverbed and banks that contain flow.
- **Discharge** *(mato)* — volume of water flowing past a point.
- **Floodplain** *(eneo la mafuriko)* — flat land beside a river that floods.
- **Bridge pier** *(nguzo ya daraja)* — support in the river that water flows around.

## Pre-Test
1. Why do rivers flood?
2. What happens when a river channel narrows?
3. Why must bridges be higher than normal water level?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Water exceeds channel capacity.
2. Water speeds up, level rises.
3. To allow flood water to pass underneath.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Build a river in this tray. What happens when we add more water?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners model river behaviour.
   - Create channel in sand/soil.
   - Add water. Observe flow.
   - Narrow the channel. Observe speed and level.
   - Add bridge piers. Observe water backing up.
   - Increase flow (flood). Observe overflow.
   - **Key discovery:** Channel shape controls flow. Narrow channels speed up water and increase level. Floods occur when capacity is exceeded.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **River flow:**
     - Discharge = velocity × cross-sectional area.
     - Narrow channel → higher velocity (continuity).
     - Steep slope → faster flow.
   - **Why rivers flood:**
     - Rain increases discharge.
     - Debris reduces channel capacity.
     - Overflow = flood.
   - **Bridge design:**
     - Piers narrow channel → water rises upstream.
     - Bridge must be high enough for flood levels.
     - Span must allow debris passage.
   - Kenyan context: Nzoia, Tana, Athi rivers flood seasonally. Bridge washouts common. Flood management critical.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why narrow bridges cause flooding upstream. Homework: observe a local river.

## Hands-On Build: River Model

### Parts
- Tray — 1 — classroom
- Sand — 2 kg — ground
- Water — 2 L — tap
- Bridge model — 1 — classroom
- Notebook — 1 per learner — classroom

### Workflow
1. Shape channel in sand.
2. Add water. Observe.
3. Narrow channel. Measure speed and level.
4. Place bridge. Observe backup.
5. Add more water (flood). Observe overflow.
6. **Checkpoint:** Mark normal water level before adding flood water. This reference shows how much rise occurs.

### Common Mistakes
- **Too steep:** Water races out. Fix: gentle slope.
- **No outlet:** Water accumulates. Fix: allow drainage.
- **Wrong sand:** Washes away. Fix: damp sand holds shape.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A river has normal width 20 m, depth 2 m, velocity 1 m/s. During flood, width increases to 30 m, depth to 4 m. Calculate (a) normal discharge, (b) flood discharge, and (c) the factor of increase.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Normal: Q = 20 × 2 × 1 = **40 m³/s**.

(b) Flood: Q = 30 × 4 × 1.5 (velocity also increases) = **180 m³/s**.
    (If velocity stays 1 m/s: Q = 30 × 4 × 1 = **120 m³/s**).

(c) Factor = 120/40 = **3× increase** (or 4.5× if velocity increases).

**Q:** Explain why widening a river channel reduces flood risk, using the continuity equation and the concept of channel capacity.

**Worked Answer:**
**Continuity:** Q = A × v.

For fixed discharge:
- Wider channel → larger A → lower v.
- Lower velocity → less energy, less erosion.
- More importantly: larger A means channel holds more water.

**Channel capacity:**
- Capacity = max volume channel holds before overflow.
- Wider/deeper channel → higher capacity.
- Can handle larger floods without overflowing.

**Example:**
- Channel 10 m wide, 2 m deep: capacity ≈ 20 m² cross-section.
- Widened to 20 m: capacity ≈ 40 m².
- Can handle twice the discharge before flooding.
