## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Set up expo stations.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Welcome to the Water Works Expo! Show what you know."

3. **(20 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Expo rotations.
   - Each group or individual presents one project/demonstration.
   - Presentations: siphon garden, diaphragm pump, Bernoulli demos, irrigation models, river models.
   - Audience asks questions.
   - Peers evaluate using rubric.
   - **Key discovery:** Teaching others reinforces learning. Different approaches solve same problems.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Unit 8 summary:**
     - Flow rate: volume per time.
     - Continuity: narrow = faster.
     - Bernoulli: faster = lower pressure.
     - Magnus effect: spin curves paths.
     - Pumps: lift, force, centrifugal, diaphragm, ram.
     - Siphons: gravity-powered flow over hump.
     - Irrigation: drip, furrow, sprinkler.
     - Rivers: channel shape controls flooding.
   - **Evaluation criteria:**
     - Understanding: clear explanation of physics.
     - Demonstration: works, visible, engaging.
     - Creativity: original approach or design.
     - Presentation: clear, confident, answers questions.
   - **Connections to real world:**
     - Water supply and sanitation.
     - Agriculture and food security.
     - Flood management.
     - Renewable energy (hydraulic ram).

5. **(5 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Announce top presentations. Preview Unit 9. Homework: reflect on learning.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a complete water supply system for a village on a hillside with a spring at the top. Include: source, storage, distribution, and irrigation. Justify each component choice using physics principles from Unit 8.

**Worked Answer:**
**System design:**

**1. Source (spring at top):**
- Spring captured in small settling tank.
- Natural head = elevation advantage.

**2. Storage (water tower):**
- Elevated tank 10 m above village.
- Creates 100,000 Pa pressure for distribution.
- P = ρgh = 1000 × 10 × 10 = **100,000 Pa**.

**3. Distribution (gravity-fed pipes):**
- PVC pipes from tower to taps.
- Sufficient head for all buildings.
- Check valves prevent backflow.

**4. Irrigation (siphon + drip):**
- Siphons from distribution channel to fields.
- Drip lines for vegetable plots.
- Siphon: no pump needed, gravity-powered.
- Drip: 90% efficient, minimal waste.

**Justification:**
- Gravity supply: no electricity, reliable.
- Water tower: stores water, provides pressure.
- Siphon: uses existing elevation.
- Drip: conserves water in arid climate.

**Q:** Evaluate the statement: "A hydraulic ram is a perpetual motion machine because it uses water to pump water." Is this true? Explain using energy conservation.

**Worked Answer:**
**Not true.**

**Energy analysis:**
- Input: flowing water with kinetic energy.
- Output: (a) small fraction lifted higher, (b) large fraction flows out as waste.
- Energy out < Energy in (due to losses).

**Why not perpetual motion:**
- Requires continuous input of flowing water.
- Gravity provides energy (water falling from higher to lower).
- If source stops, pump stops.
- Obey energy conservation: output = input − losses.

**Efficiency:**
- Only 10–30% of water lifted.
- 70–90% "wasted" but necessary for operation.
- Overall system efficiency: 50–70%.

**Conclusion:**
A ram is a clever use of water energy, not perpetual motion. It converts gravitational potential energy of falling water into pressure energy for lifting a fraction.
