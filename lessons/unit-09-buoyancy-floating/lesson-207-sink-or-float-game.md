# Lesson 207: Sink or Float? The Prediction Game

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Predict whether objects will sink or float in water.
2. Explain that density determines floating behaviour.
3. Classify objects by their floating properties.

## Materials
- Various objects (wood, stone, plastic, metal, fruit, etc.)
- Bucket of water
- Towel
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Buoyancy** *(ufunuo)* — the upward force on an object in a fluid.
- **Sink** *(kuzama)* — to go down in a fluid.
- **Float** *(kuelea)* — to stay on or near the surface.
- **Prediction** *(utabiri)* — a reasoned guess about what will happen.

## Pre-Test
1. Will a stone sink or float?
2. Will a piece of wood sink or float?
3. What makes some things float and others sink?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Sink.
2. Float.
3. Their density compared to water.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "I have ten objects. Before I drop them, tell me: sink or float?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test objects.
   - Each learner predicts sink/float for each object.
   - Drop objects one by one.
   - Record prediction vs result.
   - **Key discovery:** Density predicts floating. Objects less dense than water float; more dense sink.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **The rule:**
     - If object density < water density → floats.
     - If object density > water density → sinks.
     - If equal → hovers (neutral buoyancy).
   - **Surprises:**
     - Heavy ship floats (shape traps air).
     - Small needle sinks (solid steel).
     - Hollow objects float even if material is dense.
   - **Density of water:** 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³).
   - Kenyan context: Fishing boats float because hollow. Stones sink. Mangoes float. Iron sinks.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains the density rule. Homework: test five objects at home.

## Hands-On Build: Sink or Float Test

### Parts
- Objects — 10 — various
- Water bucket — 1 — classroom
- Towel — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Hold object over water.
2. Predict sink or float.
3. Drop gently.
4. Observe.
5. Record.
6. Dry object.
7. Repeat.
8. **Checkpoint:** Predict BEFORE testing. The game is about reasoning, not just observing.

### Common Mistakes
- **Throwing objects:** Splashes, inaccurate. Fix: place gently.
- **Not drying:** Wet objects affect next test. Fix: towel between tests.
- **Predicting after:** Defeats purpose. Fix: write prediction first.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Will a stone sink or float?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Will wood sink or float?
3. A mango floats. Is it more or less dense than water?
4. Why does a ship float when steel sinks?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Sink**.
2. **Float**.
3. **Less dense**.
4. Ship is **hollow** (air inside), overall density less than water.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A cube of wood (10 cm side) has mass 600 g. Calculate its density. Will it float? If yes, what percentage is submerged?

**Worked Answer:**
Volume = 10³ = 1000 cm³ = 0.001 m³.
Mass = 0.6 kg.
Density = 0.6 / 0.001 = **600 kg/m³**.

Yes, floats (600 < 1000).

Fraction submerged = ρ_object / ρ_water = 600/1000 = 0.6.
**60% submerged, 40% above water**.

**Q:** Explain why a solid iron ball sinks but a hollow iron ball of the same mass can float, using the concept of average density.

**Worked Answer:**
**Solid ball:**
- Volume = m/ρ_iron = small.
- Average density = ρ_iron = 7800 kg/m³ > water.
- Sinks.

**Hollow ball (same mass):**
- Volume = much larger (includes air space).
- Average density = m / (V_iron + V_air).
- Since V_total >> V_iron, average density drops.
- If average density < 1000 kg/m³ → floats.

**Example:**
1 kg iron solid: V = 1/7800 = 0.000128 m³ → sinks.
1 kg iron hollow: if V_total = 0.002 m³ → ρ_avg = 1/0.002 = 500 kg/m³ → floats.
