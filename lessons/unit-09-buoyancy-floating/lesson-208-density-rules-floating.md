# Lesson 208: Density Decides: Wood, Stone and Steel

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Measure the density of various materials.
2. Compare material density to water density (1000 kg/m³).
3. Predict floating behaviour from density values.

## Materials
- Wood block, stone, steel nail
- Scale (or balance)
- Ruler
- Water container
- Calculator
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Density** *(msisimko)* — mass per unit volume.
- **Material density** *(msisimko wa nyenzo)* — density of the substance itself.
- **Bulk density** *(msisimko wa jumla)* — density including air spaces.
- **Specific gravity** *(uzito mahususi)* — density relative to water.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the density of water?
2. Which is denser: wood or steel?
3. Will a material with density 800 kg/m³ float?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. 1000 kg/m³ (or 1 g/cm³).
2. Steel.
3. Yes.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "I have wood, stone, and steel. Which is heaviest for its size?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure densities.
   - Measure mass of each object.
   - Measure volume (ruler for regular shapes, water displacement for irregular).
   - Calculate density = mass/volume.
   - Compare to water.
   - Predict and test floating.
   - **Key discovery:** Material density determines floating. Steel is 7800 kg/m³ — way denser than water.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Density calculations:**
     - Wood: ~500–800 kg/m³ → floats.
     - Stone: ~2500 kg/m³ → sinks.
     - Steel: ~7800 kg/m³ → sinks (unless hollow).
     - Water: 1000 kg/m³ → reference.
   - **Specific gravity:**
     - SG = ρ_material / ρ_water.
     - SG < 1 → floats.
     - SG > 1 → sinks.
   - **Why ships float:**
     - Steel ship is hollow.
     - Average density (steel + air) < 1000 kg/m³.
     - Shape traps air, reducing average density.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why steel sinks but steel ships float. Homework: find densities of five materials at home.

## Hands-On Build: Density Measurement

### Parts
- Wood, stone, steel — 1 each — classroom
- Scale — 1 — classroom
- Ruler — 1 — classroom
- Water container — 1 — classroom
- Calculator — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Measure mass.
2. Measure dimensions (regular) or use water displacement (irregular).
3. Calculate volume.
4. Calculate density.
5. Compare to water.
6. Test in water.
7. **Checkpoint:** Use consistent units. Mixing grams and kilograms gives wrong density.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong units:** g vs kg confusion. Fix: convert to kg and m³.
- **Volume error:** Miscounting dimensions. Fix: measure carefully.
- **Not displacing fully:** Object not submerged. Fix: push under gently.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Density of water?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Which denser: wood or steel?
3. Calculate density: mass 200 g, volume 250 cm³.
4. Will it float?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **1000 kg/m³**.
2. **Steel**.
3. ρ = 200/250 = 0.8 g/cm³ = **800 kg/m³**.
4. **Yes** (800 < 1000).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A steel ship has mass 5000 tonnes and overall volume 6000 m³. Calculate its average density. What percentage of its volume is above water when floating?

**Worked Answer:**
Average density = 5,000,000 / 6000 = **833 kg/m³**.

Fraction submerged = 833/1000 = 0.833.
**83.3% submerged, 16.7% above water**.

Volume above = 6000 × 0.167 = **1000 m³**.

**Q:** Explain why an aircraft carrier made of steel floats despite carrying heavy aircraft, using the concept of displaced volume and average density.

**Worked Answer:**
**Aircraft carrier:**
- Mass: 100,000 tonnes (ship) + 30,000 tonnes (aircraft) = 130,000 tonnes.
- Volume: enormous hull, ~200,000 m³.
- Average density = 130,000,000 / 200,000 = **650 kg/m³**.

**Why it floats:**
- Average density (650) < water (1000).
- Displaces 130,000 m³ of water.
- As aircraft are loaded, ship sinks deeper, displacing more water.
- Buoyancy force increases with displacement.
- Equilibrium: buoyancy = total weight.

**Adding aircraft:**
- Weight increases.
- Ship sinks slightly.
- Displaces more water.
- New equilibrium at deeper draft.
- Still floats as long as average density < 1000.
