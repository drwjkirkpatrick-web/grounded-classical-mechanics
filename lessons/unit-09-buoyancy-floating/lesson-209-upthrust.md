# Lesson 209: Upthrust: Water Pushes Back

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate that water exerts an upward force on submerged objects.
2. Measure upthrust by comparing weight in air and water.
3. Explain that upthrust equals weight of displaced water.

## Materials
- Spring balance or scale
- Objects of various sizes
- Water container
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Upthrust** *(nguvu ya kuelea)* — the upward buoyant force on a submerged object.
- **Apparent weight** *(uzito wa dhahiri)* — weight measured while submerged.
- **Displacement** *(kukimbia)* — volume of fluid pushed aside.
- **Buoyant force** *(nguvu ya ufunuo)* — same as upthrust.

## Pre-Test
1. Does water push up on objects?
2. Is a stone lighter in water or air?
3. What causes upthrust?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Yes.
2. Lighter in water.
3. Pressure difference between top and bottom of object.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: Weigh a stone in air, then in water. "Why is it lighter?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure upthrust.
   - Weigh object in air (true weight).
   - Weigh object submerged in water (apparent weight).
   - Calculate upthrust = weight in air − apparent weight.
   - Try different shapes at same volume.
   - **Key discovery:** Upthrust depends on volume submerged, not on object's weight or material.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **What causes upthrust:**
     - Pressure increases with depth.
     - Bottom of object is deeper → higher pressure.
     - Top is shallower → lower pressure.
     - Net force is upward.
   - **Formula:**
     - Upthrust = weight in air − apparent weight.
     - Upthrust = weight of displaced fluid.
   - **Why volume matters:**
     - Larger volume = more depth difference = more pressure difference = more upthrust.
     - Same volume → same upthrust (regardless of material).
   - **Floating condition:**
     - If upthrust ≥ weight → object floats.
     - If upthrust < weight → object sinks.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why a large steel ship floats but a small steel nail sinks. Homework: weigh an object in and out of water.

## Hands-On Build: Upthrust Measurement

### Parts
- Spring balance — 1 — classroom
- Stone or metal object — 1 — classroom
- Thread — 0.3 m — classroom
- Water bucket — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Tie thread to object.
2. Weigh in air. Record.
3. Submerge in water (don't touch bottom).
4. Weigh in water. Record.
5. Calculate upthrust = difference.
6. Try larger object. Compare.
7. **Checkpoint:** Object must not touch container bottom or sides. Contact forces add to reading.

### Common Mistakes
- **Touching bottom:** Supports weight, reduces apparent weight. Fix: suspend freely.
- **Not fully submerged:** Upthrust less than maximum. Fix: completely under water.
- **Air bubbles:** Stick to object, add buoyancy. Fix: gently shake or wait.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Does water push up?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Is stone lighter in water?
3. Calculate upthrust: weight in air = 5 N, apparent weight = 3 N.
4. Does a larger object get more upthrust?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Yes**.
2. **Yes** (lighter in water).
3. 5 − 3 = **2 N**.
4. **Yes** (more volume displaced).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 2 kg aluminium block (ρ = 2700 kg/m³) is fully submerged in water. Calculate (a) its volume, (b) the upthrust, and (c) its apparent weight.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) V = m/ρ = 2/2700 = **0.000741 m³** = 741 cm³.

(b) Upthrust = weight of displaced water = ρ_water × V × g = 1000 × 0.000741 × 10 = **7.41 N**.

(c) True weight = 2 × 10 = 20 N.
    Apparent weight = 20 − 7.41 = **12.59 N**.

**Q:** Explain why a submarine can control its depth by adjusting ballast tanks, using the concept of upthrust and density.

**Worked Answer:**
**Submarine control:**
- Surface: ballast tanks filled with air. Average density < water. Upthrust > weight.
- Dive: fill tanks with water. Average density increases. When > water, submarine sinks.
- Neutral: adjust water/air ratio until average density = water. Submarine hovers.
- Rise: blow water out with compressed air. Average density < water. Upthrust > weight.

**Physics:**
- Upthrust depends on submarine's volume (constant).
- Weight changes with ballast.
- Net force = upthrust − weight.
- Positive → rises. Negative → sinks. Zero → hovers.
