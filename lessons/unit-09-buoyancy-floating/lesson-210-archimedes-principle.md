# Lesson 210: Archimedes' Principle

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. State Archimedes' principle: upthrust equals weight of displaced fluid.
2. Demonstrate the principle with overflow measurements.
3. Apply the principle to calculate floating and sinking behaviour.

## Materials
- Overflow can or container with spout
- Measuring cylinder
- Objects of known mass
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Archimedes' principle** *(sheria ya Archimedes)* — the buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced.
- **Displacement** *(kukimbia)* — the volume of fluid pushed aside by an object.
- **Overflow** *(kutiririka)* — water flowing out when an object is submerged.
- **Buoyant force** *(nguvu ya ufunuo)* — the upward force equal to weight of displaced fluid.

## Pre-Test
1. What does Archimedes' principle state?
2. If an object displaces 500 g of water, what is the upthrust?
3. A ship displaces 10,000 tonnes of water. What is its weight?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Upthrust = weight of displaced fluid.
2. 5 N (since 500 g = 0.5 kg, weight = 0.5 × 10 = 5 N).
3. 100,000 kN (or weight of 10,000 tonnes of water).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Archimedes ran naked through the streets shouting 'Eureka!' What did he discover?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test Archimedes' principle.
   - Fill overflow can to spout.
   - Weigh object.
   - Submerge object, collect overflow water.
   - Weigh overflow water.
   - Compare: weight of object in air − apparent weight in water = weight of overflow water.
   - **Key discovery:** The two weights match! Upthrust exactly equals weight of displaced water.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Archimedes' principle:**
     - Upthrust = weight of displaced fluid.
     - U = ρ_fluid × V_displaced × g.
   - **Why it works:**
     - Submerged object pushes fluid aside.
     - Fluid exerts pressure on all surfaces.
     - Net upward force equals weight of fluid that would occupy that space.
   - **Applications:**
     - Ship design: know displacement = know carrying capacity.
     - Submarines: calculate ballast needed.
     - Hydrometers: measure density by floating depth.
   - **Story:**
     - King Hiero's crown: was it pure gold?
     - Archimedes measured volume by displacement.
     - Compared to pure gold of same mass.
     - Crown displaced more → contained lighter metals.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner states the principle. Homework: calculate displacement of your foot in water.

## Hands-On Build: Archimedes' Principle Verification

### Parts
- Overflow can — 1 — lab
- Measuring cylinder — 1 — lab
- Object — 1 — classroom
- Water — 2 L — tap
- Scale — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill overflow can to spout.
2. Place measuring cylinder under spout.
3. Weigh object in air.
4. Submerge object in overflow can.
5. Collect overflow water.
6. Measure volume of overflow.
7. Calculate weight of overflow water.
8. Compare to weight loss.
9. **Checkpoint:** Can must be full to spout before submerging. Partial fill gives wrong overflow volume.

### Common Mistakes
- **Not full:** Overflow volume too small. Fix: fill to spout.
- **Splashing:** Water lost, measurement wrong. Fix: gentle submersion.
- **Object touches bottom:** Apparent weight reduced by support. Fix: suspend freely.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does Archimedes' principle state?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Object displaces 500 g water. Upthrust?
3. A ship displaces 50,000 tonnes. What is its weight?
4. Why does a floating object displace its own weight in water?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Upthrust = weight of displaced fluid**.
2. **5 N**.
3. **Weight of 50,000 tonnes of water**.
4. Because **upthrust = weight** for floating objects (equilibrium).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A metal sphere of radius 5 cm is fully submerged in water. Calculate (a) the volume displaced, (b) the upthrust, and (c) the apparent weight if the sphere's mass is 5 kg.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3) × π × 0.05³ = **5.24 × 10⁻⁴ m³**.

(b) Upthrust = ρVg = 1000 × 5.24 × 10⁻⁴ × 10 = **5.24 N**.

(c) True weight = 5 × 10 = 50 N.
    Apparent weight = 50 − 5.24 = **44.76 N**.

**Q:** Explain how Archimedes' principle can be used to determine the purity of a gold crown, and why density measurement alone might not be sufficient.

**Worked Answer:**
**Method:**
1. Measure crown's mass (m).
2. Submerge in water, measure overflow volume (V).
3. Calculate density: ρ = m/V.
4. Compare to pure gold density (19,300 kg/m³).

**If density matches:**
- Crown is pure gold.

**If density is lower:**
- Contains lighter metals (silver, copper).

**Why density alone might fail:**
- Alloy with same average density as gold exists?
- Actually, no simple alloy matches gold's density exactly.
- But sophisticated fraud could use tungsten (ρ = 19,250) plated with gold.
- Archimedes' method would miss this (same density).
- Need additional tests: electrical conductivity, X-ray, melting point.

**Historical note:**
- Archimedes solved it with displacement.
- Modern testing is more sophisticated but same principle.
