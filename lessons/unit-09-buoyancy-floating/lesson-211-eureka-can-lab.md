# Lesson 211: The Eureka Can Lab: Volume by Overflow

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Measure the volume of irregular objects using the overflow method.
2. Relate overflow volume to displaced volume.
3. Verify Archimedes' principle quantitatively.

## Materials
- Eureka can (overflow can with spout)
- Measuring cylinder
- Irregular objects (stones, keys, etc.)
- Water
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Eureka can** *(chombo cha Archimedes)* — a container with a spout for overflow measurement.
- **Irregular shape** *(umbo usio wa kawaida)* — a shape that cannot be measured with a ruler.
- **Volume by displacement** *(kimo kwa kukimbia)* — finding volume from fluid overflow.

## Pre-Test
1. How do you measure the volume of an irregular stone?
2. What volume of water overflows when you submerge a 200 cm³ object?
3. Why does overflow volume equal the object's volume?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. By water displacement (overflow method).
2. 200 cm³.
3. The object pushes aside exactly its own volume of water.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "I have a strange rock. How big is it? No ruler can tell us."

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure volumes by overflow.
   - Fill Eureka can to spout.
   - Submerge object, collect overflow.
   - Measure overflow volume.
   - Try different shapes.
   - Compare: does a heavy small object displace same as a light large object?
   - **Key discovery:** Overflow volume = object's volume. Mass doesn't matter for displacement.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Overflow method:**
     - Object submerged → pushes aside water = its own volume.
     - Water has nowhere to go but out the spout.
     - Collect and measure = object's volume.
   - **Advantages:**
     - Works for any shape.
     - Simple equipment.
     - Direct and accurate.
   - **Limitations:**
     - Object must be waterproof.
     - Must fit in can.
     - Water must not splash.
   - **Relation to Archimedes:**
     - Overflow volume = displaced volume.
     - Weight of this water = upthrust.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains how Archimedes used this for the crown. Homework: measure volume of an object at home.

## Hands-On Build: Volume by Overflow

### Parts
- Eureka can — 1 — lab
- Measuring cylinder — 1 — lab
- Irregular objects — 3 — various
- Water — 2 L — tap

### Workflow
1. Fill can to spout.
2. Place cylinder under spout.
3. Gently submerge object.
4. Collect overflow.
5. Read volume.
6. Repeat with different objects.
7. **Checkpoint:** Object must be fully submerged but not touch bottom. Touching bottom prevents full displacement.

### Common Mistakes
- **Not full can:** Wrong volume. Fix: fill to spout.
- **Splashing:** Water lost. Fix: gentle submersion.
- **Air bubbles:** Extra volume. Fix: shake or wait for bubbles to escape.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How measure irregular volume?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Overflow from 200 cm³ object?
3. Does a heavy object displace more than a light object of same volume?
4. How did Archimedes use this method?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Water displacement / overflow**.
2. **200 cm³**.
3. **No** — displacement depends on volume, not mass.
4. To check if crown was pure gold by comparing density.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A 400 g object displaces 250 cm³ of water when fully submerged. Calculate (a) its density, (b) whether it will float, and (c) the upthrust on it.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) ρ = m/V = 400/250 = **1.6 g/cm³** = **1600 kg/m³**.

(b) **Sinks** (1600 > 1000).

(c) Upthrust = weight of displaced water = 250 g × 10 = **2.5 N**.
    (Or: 0.00025 m³ × 1000 × 10 = 2.5 N).

**Q:** Explain why the overflow method is more accurate than measuring dimensions for irregular objects, and under what conditions it might give incorrect results.

**Worked Answer:**
**More accurate because:**
- Irregular shapes have complex dimensions.
- Ruler measurements miss curves and indentations.
- Overflow captures exact volume regardless of shape.
- Error: ±1 mL with cylinder vs ±5% with approximation.

**Incorrect results when:**
- **Object absorbs water:** (sponge, porous rock) → measured volume < actual.
- **Object floats:** Only partially submerged → measured volume < actual.
- **Air bubbles trapped:** Bubbles add volume → measured > actual.
- **Splashing:** Water lost → measured < actual.
- **Temperature effects:** Water density changes slightly.

**Improvements:**
- Coat absorbent objects with wax.
- Force floating objects under (but then apparent volume = actual).
- Wait for bubbles to escape.
- Be gentle to avoid splashing.
