# Lesson 214: Clay Boat Challenge II: Load Test and Stability

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** clay-boat

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Test clay boat designs from Lesson 213 under load.
2. Measure carrying capacity and stability.
3. Compare designs and identify best features.

## Materials
- Clay boats from Lesson 213
- Weights (stones, coins, washers)
- Water container
- Ruler
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Load test** *(kupima mzigo)* — testing how much weight a vessel can carry.
- **Stability test** *(kupima imara)* — checking resistance to tipping.
- **Freeboard** *(ubora wa kuelea)* — height of hull above waterline.
- **Swamping** *(kujaa maji)* — when water enters a vessel.

## Pre-Test
1. How do you test a boat's capacity?
2. What happens when freeboard reaches zero?
3. How do you make a boat more stable?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Add weights until it sinks or capsizes.
2. Water enters — boat swamps.
3. Wider base, lower centre of gravity.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups retrieve boats.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Test day! Which boat wins?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test boats.
   - Add weights one by one.
   - Record capacity before swamping.
   - Test stability: gently push side — does it return upright?
   - Measure freeboard as weight increases.
   - **Key discovery:** Design predicts performance. Wide, deep boats with thick walls win.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Results:**
     - Best design = most cargo + most stable.
     - Wide base beats narrow.
     - Thick walls resist collapse.
     - Deep hulls carry more but tip easier.
   - **Why designs fail:**
     - Too thin: walls collapse.
     - Too narrow: tips easily.
     - No freeboard: water enters.
     - Uneven loading: lists to one side.
   - **Comparison:**
     - Groups present results.
     - Vote on best design.
     - Discuss what made winners successful.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Announce winner. Homework: write design tips for next year's class.

## Hands-On Build: Load and Stability Test

### Parts
- Clay boats — 1 per group — built
- Weights — 20 — stones/coins
- Water container — 1 — classroom
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Place boat in water.
2. Note freeboard.
3. Add weight #1. Record.
4. Continue until swamping.
5. Test stability: push gently, release.
6. Record maximum stable load.
7. **Checkpoint:** Add weights gently and centrally. Throwing weights in causes tipping unrelated to design.

### Common Mistakes
- **Throwing weights:** Unfair test. Fix: place gently.
- **Off-centre loading:** Artificial instability. Fix: place in centre.
- **Not recording:** Can't compare. Fix: write each weight.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How test capacity?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What at zero freeboard?
3. Why do wide boats carry more?
4. Name one reason a boat might capsize.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Add weights** until sinking/capsizing.
2. **Water enters** (swamping).
3. **More displacement** = more buoyancy.
4. Any: **too narrow**, **top-heavy**, **uneven load**, **waves**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A boat carries 50 g before freeboard reaches zero. The boat's own mass is 30 g. Calculate (a) the total displaced water volume at maximum load, and (b) the boat's average density at that point.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Total mass = 30 + 50 = 80 g.
    Displaced volume = 80 cm³ (since 1 g water = 1 cm³).
    V = **80 cm³** = **8 × 10⁻⁵ m³**.

(b) Average density = total mass / total volume.
    If boat volume at waterline = 80 cm³:
    ρ_avg = 80/80 = **1 g/cm³** = **1000 kg/m³**.
    (Exactly at neutral buoyancy — any more weight sinks it.)

**Q:** Design a scoring rubric for the clay boat challenge that balances capacity, stability, and efficiency (cargo per unit clay mass). Justify each criterion's weighting.

**Worked Answer:**
**Rubric (100 points):**

1. **Capacity (40 points):**
   - Maximum cargo before swamping.
   - Linear scale: 40 points for highest, proportionally less.
   - Justification: Primary purpose of a boat is to carry load.

2. **Stability (30 points):**
   - Returns upright when tilted 30°.
   - Partial credit for less stability.
   - Justification: Unsafe boat is useless regardless of capacity.

3. **Efficiency (20 points):**
   - Cargo mass / clay mass ratio.
   - Higher ratio = better design.
   - Justification: Resource optimisation matters in engineering.

4. **Design documentation (10 points):**
   - Clear sketch with dimensions.
   - Predictions vs results.
   - Justification: Engineering requires documentation.

**Why weighting:**
- Capacity matters most (primary function).
- Stability is critical for safety (second priority).
- Efficiency rewards clever design.
- Documentation reflects professional practice.
