# Lesson 215: The Hydrometer: A Floater That Measures

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a hydrometer measures liquid density.
2. Read a hydrometer scale.
3. Use a hydrometer to compare liquid densities.

## Materials
- Hydrometer (or commercial example)
- Various liquids (water, salt water, oil, sugar water)
- Tall containers
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Hydrometer** *(hidromita)* — a floating instrument that measures liquid density.
- **Specific gravity** *(uzito mahususi)* — density relative to water.
- **Calibration** *(kupiga mizani)* — marking a scale on an instrument.
- **Meniscus** *(mkondo wa maji)* — the curved surface of liquid in a container.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a hydrometer measure?
2. Does a hydrometer float higher in dense or less dense liquid?
3. Where would a hydrometer float highest: in water or in salt water?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Liquid density (or specific gravity).
2. **Denser** — it needs to displace less volume to support its weight.
3. **In salt water** — because salt water is denser than fresh water.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "This glass tube floats. The liquid tells us its density. How?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test hydrometer behaviour.
   - Float hydrometer in water. Note level.
   - Add salt — floats higher.
   - Add sugar — floats higher.
   - Try oil (less dense) — sinks deeper.
   - **Key discovery:** Hydrometer floats at different levels depending on density. Denser liquid = higher float (less submerged).

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How hydrometers work:**
     - Weight is fixed (lead shot at bottom).
     - In dense liquid: less volume needed to displace weight → floats higher.
     - In less dense liquid: more volume needed → sinks deeper.
   - **Reading:**
     - Scale marked on stem.
     - Read at liquid surface level.
     - Higher numbers on scale = denser liquid = higher float position.
   - **Uses:**
     - Brewing: check sugar content.
     - Milk testing: check water content.
     - Battery acid: check charge level.
     - Soil testing: check salinity.
   - Kenyan context: Testing milk quality. Checking battery acid. Monitoring borehole water salinity.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why hydrometer floats higher in salt water. Homework: research one use of hydrometers.

## Hands-On Build: Hydrometer Observation

### Parts
- Hydrometer — 1 — lab or pharmacy
- Water — 500 mL — tap
- Salt — 50 g — kitchen
- Oil — 100 mL — kitchen
- Tall container — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Fill container with water.
2. Float hydrometer. Record level.
3. Add salt, stir. Float again. Record.
4. Add more salt. Record.
5. In new container, float in oil. Record.
6. Compare levels.
7. **Checkpoint:** Read at eye level. Parallax error makes readings wrong.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong reading:** Reading top or bottom of meniscus. Fix: read at middle of curve.
- **Not stirring:** Salt settles. Fix: stir before measuring.
- **Tube touching sides:** Friction affects float. Fix: centre the hydrometer.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What does hydrometer measure?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Float higher in dense or less dense?
3. A hydrometer reads higher after adding salt. What does this tell you?
4. Name one use for hydrometers.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Liquid density** (or specific gravity).
2. **Denser** liquid.
3. **Liquid became denser** (salt increases density).
4. Any: **brewing**, **milk testing**, **battery testing**, **soil testing**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A hydrometer of mass 50 g has a cylindrical stem of diameter 0.5 cm. In water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³) it sinks to the 1.000 mark. In a sugar solution it floats with the stem 2 cm higher. Calculate the density of the sugar solution.

**Worked Answer:**
In water: displaces 50 g of water = 50 cm³.

In sugar solution: floats 2 cm higher, so displaces less volume.
Volume change = πr²h = π × (0.25)² × 2 = **0.3927 cm³**.

New displaced volume = 50 − 0.3927 = **49.61 cm³**.

Density = mass/volume = 50/49.61 = **1.008 g/cm³** = **1008 kg/m³**.

**Q:** Explain why a hydrometer must have a narrow stem for precise density measurement, and calculate the stem sensitivity (mm per kg/m³) for the hydrometer described above.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why narrow stem:**
- Small density change → small volume change needed.
- Narrow stem: small volume change → large height change.
- Easy to read small differences.
- Wide stem: small density differences barely visible.

**Sensitivity:**
- Stem area = π × (0.25)² = 0.196 cm².
- For Δρ = 1 kg/m³ = 0.001 g/cm³:
  - New density = 1.001 g/cm³.
  - New displaced volume = 50/1.001 = 49.95 cm³.
  - ΔV = 0.05 cm³.
  - Δh = 0.05/0.196 = 0.255 cm = **2.55 mm**.

Sensitivity ≈ **2.5 mm per kg/m³**.
