# Lesson 219: Rafts, Canoes and Ferries of Kenya

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe how traditional Kenyan watercraft use buoyancy principles.
2. Compare different vessel types by stability, capacity, and efficiency.
3. Appreciate indigenous engineering solutions.

## Materials
- Photos or diagrams of Kenyan rafts, canoes, and ferries
- Map showing Lake Victoria, Lake Turkana, Tana River
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Dhow** *(dau)* — a traditional sailing vessel with lateen sail.
- **Canoe** *(mashua)* — a narrow boat propelled by paddles.
- **Ferry** *(kivuko)* — a vessel for carrying passengers and vehicles across water.
- **Outrigger** *(kipandikizi)* — a float extending from the side for stability.

## Pre-Test
1. Name a traditional Kenyan boat.
2. How do fishermen on Lake Victoria keep their boats stable?
3. Why are ferries wider than canoes?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Canoe, dhow, raft.
2. Outriggers, wide hulls, low centre of gravity.
3. More stable, carry more weight.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Show photos.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "How do Kenyans cross Lake Victoria? How do fishermen survive storms?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse watercraft.
   - Examine photos of: dugout canoes ( mashua ), outrigger canoes, dhows, ferries.
   - Compare: width, hull shape, materials, propulsion.
   - Discuss: Which is most stable? Which carries most? Which is fastest?
   - **Key discovery:** Traditional designs evolved for local conditions. Wider = stable, narrow = fast.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Dugout canoes:**
     - Hollowed from single log.
     - Narrow, fast, limited capacity.
     - Risk: tip easily.
     - Used: fishing, short crossings.
   - **Outrigger canoes:**
     - Float on side stabilises hull.
     - Prevents tipping.
     - Used: fishing in rougher waters.
   - **Dhows:**
     - Lateen sail for wind power.
     - Used: trade along coast.
     - Carry cargo efficiently.
   - **Ferries:**
     - Wide, flat hulls.
     - Engines for reliability.
     - Safety: life jackets, capacity limits.
   - **Kenyan context:**
     - Lake Victoria: fishing canoes, outriggers.
     - Likoni Ferry: Mombasa commuter crossing.
     - Tana River: dugouts for transport.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why outriggers matter. Homework: sketch a traditional canoe.

2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* How keep boats stable?

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A traditional dugout canoe is 6 m long, 0.5 m wide, and displaces 200 kg of water when loaded. Calculate (a) its draught (depth in water), (b) its stability compared to a ferry of the same displacement but 4 m wide, and (c) why the ferry is safer.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Assuming rectangular cross-section:
    V = 200,000 cm³.
    Cross-section = 600 × 50 = 30,000 cm².
    Draught = V/area = 200,000/30,000 = **6.7 cm**.

(b) Stability ∝ beam².
    Canoe: 0.5² = 0.25 m².
    Ferry: 4² = 16 m².
    Ferry is **64 times more stable**.

(c) **Safer because:**
    - Wider = harder to tip.
    - Lower freeboard = less affected by waves.
    - Multiple compartments = won't sink if holed.

**Q:** Design a water transport system for a village on an island in Lake Victoria that balances cost, safety, and environmental impact. Justify each choice using physics principles.

**Worked Answer:**
**System: Solar-powered ferry with outrigger backup canoes**

**Ferry:**
- Width 5 m for stability.
- Solar panels reduce fuel cost and emissions.
- Life jackets for all passengers.
- Capacity 20 people + cargo.
- Physics: wide beam = stability, solar energy = renewable.

**Backup canoes:**
- Traditional outrigger design.
- Low cost, locally built.
- Physics: outrigger = stability without wide hull.

**Environmental:**
- No diesel pollution.
- Canoes from sustainable timber.
- Ferry scheduled to reduce congestion.

**Cost:**
- Ferry: high initial, low operating.
- Canoes: low initial, moderate operating.
- Combined: resilient system.
