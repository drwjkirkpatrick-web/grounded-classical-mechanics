# Lesson 220: Life Jackets and Water Safety

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how life jackets provide buoyancy.
2. Calculate the buoyant force of a life jacket.
3. Promote water safety in their communities.

## Materials
- Photos of life jackets
- Foam pieces or similar materials
- Water container
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Life jacket** *(koti la kuokolea)* — a wearable flotation device.
- **Flotation material** *(nyenzo ya kuelea)* — material less dense than water that provides buoyancy.
- **Buoyant force** *(nguvu ya ufunuo)* — upward force keeping a person afloat.
- **Water safety** *(usalama wa maji)* — practices to prevent drowning.

## Pre-Test
1. How does a life jacket help you float?
2. What material is used in life jackets?
3. Why should you wear a life jacket on a boat?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Traps air or uses foam less dense than water.
2. Foam, air chambers, cork.
3. Prevents drowning if you fall in.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Show life jacket photos.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "You fall off a boat. You can't swim. What saves you?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test materials.
   - Weigh foam pieces.
   - Submerge in water. Measure upthrust.
   - Calculate: how much weight can different sizes support?
   - **Key discovery:** Low-density materials displace water, creating buoyancy. Volume matters.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How life jackets work:**
     - Foam or air chambers add volume without much weight.
     - Average density (person + jacket) < water.
     - Keeps head above water even if unconscious.
   - **Types:**
     - Foam: reliable, no maintenance.
     - Inflatable: compact, requires activation.
     - Hybrid: combination.
   - **Safety rules:**
     - Wear at all times on water.
     - Check condition regularly.
     - Proper size for wearer.
     - Children need child-sized jackets.
   - **Kenyan context:**
     - Fishing communities: life jackets often unavailable.
     - Lake Victoria accidents: many drownings preventable.
     - Promoting safety awareness critical.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner demonstrates proper life jacket fit. Homework: check if your community has life jackets.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* How does a life jacket help?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What material used?
3. Calculate: foam volume 5000 cm³, density 50 kg/m³. Upthrust?
4. Name one water safety rule.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Traps air/foam**, reduces average density.
2. **Foam**, air chambers, cork.
3. U = ρVg = 1000 × 0.005 × 10 = **50 N**.
4. Any: **wear on water**, **check condition**, **proper size**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A person of mass 70 kg and density 980 kg/m³ falls into water wearing a foam life jacket of volume 8000 cm³ and density 40 kg/m³. Calculate (a) the person's volume, (b) the combined average density, and (c) whether the person will float.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) V_person = 70/980 = **0.0714 m³** = **71,400 cm³**.

(b) Total mass = 70 + (0.008 × 40) = 70 + 0.32 = **70.32 kg**.
    Total volume = 71,400 + 8,000 = **79,400 cm³** = 0.0794 m³.
    ρ_avg = 70.32/0.0794 = **886 kg/m³**.

(c) **Yes**, floats (886 < 1000).
    Fraction submerged = 886/1000 = **88.6%**.
    Freeboard ensures head stays above water.

**Q:** Explain why an unconscious person without a life jacket will typically drown even in calm water, and how a life jacket prevents this regardless of swimming ability.

**Worked Answer:**
**Without life jacket:**
- Human density ≈ 980 kg/m³ (slightly less than water).
- Can float face-up with effort.
- Unconscious: muscles relax, lungs may fill with water.
- Body may roll face-down.
- Head underwater → drowning within minutes.

**With life jacket:**
- Designed to keep wearer face-up.
- Foam at back and collar tilts head back.
- Mouth and nose clear of water even if unconscious.
- Buoyancy exceeds need — keeps head well clear.
- No swimming needed.

**Design features:**
- Collar supports head.
- Bright colour for visibility.
- Whistle for signalling.
- Reflective strips for night rescue.
