# Lesson 221: Plimsoll Lines: Loading Marks on Ships

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain what Plimsoll lines indicate on a ship's hull.
2. Build a simple model demonstrating safe loading.
3. Understand why overloading ships is dangerous.

## Materials
- Plastic container
- Weights
- Water
- Marker
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Plimsoll line** *(mstari wa Plimsoll)* — a mark on a ship showing maximum safe loading.
- **Freeboard** *(ubora wa kuelea)* — distance from waterline to deck.
- **Draft** *(kina)* — depth of vessel below waterline.
- **Overloading** *(kujaza zaidi)* — loading beyond safe capacity.

## Pre-Test
1. What do the lines on a ship's hull mean?
2. Why do ships sink when overloaded?
3. Who was Samuel Plimsoll?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Show maximum safe loading levels.
2. Too heavy = not enough buoyancy = sinks.
3. British MP who campaigned for ship safety laws.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Ships have lines painted on their sides. Why?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners model Plimsoll lines.
   - Fill container with water.
   - Mark "waterline" on empty container.
   - Add weights. Mark new levels.
   - Continue until container sinks.
   - Draw "Plimsoll line" at safe maximum.
   - **Key discovery:** There's a safe limit. Beyond it, water can enter, waves swamp the deck.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Plimsoll lines:**
     - Painted on both sides of ship's hull.
     - Multiple marks for different water types (fresh, salt, tropical).
     - Show maximum safe draft.
   - **Why different lines:**
     - Salt water: denser → ship floats higher → can load more.
     - Fresh water: less dense → ship sinks deeper → load less.
     - Tropical: warmer = less dense → load less than cold.
   - **Samuel Plimsoll (1824–1898):**
     - British MP.
     - "Plimsoll Line" named after him.
     - Campaigned against "coffin ships" overloaded by greedy owners.
     - 1876 Merchant Shipping Act made lines mandatory.
   - **Danger of overloading:**
     - Reduced freeboard.
     - Waves wash over deck.
     - Water enters holds.
     - Ship loses buoyancy → sinks.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why ships load less in fresh water. Homework: find a photo of a ship with Plimsoll lines.

## Hands-On Build: Plimsoll Line Model

### Parts
- Plastic container — 1 — home
- Weights — 10 — stones/coins
- Water — 1 L — tap
- Marker — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Place empty container in water.
2. Mark waterline.
3. Add weights. Mark new line.
4. Continue until near swamping.
5. Mark "MAX SAFE" = Plimsoll line.
6. Test: add more weight → sinks/swamps.
7. **Checkpoint:** The safe line should leave some freeboard. At waterline = no freeboard = unsafe.

### Common Mistakes
- **Marking too high:** No safety margin. Fix: allow freeboard.
- **Not testing:** Guesswork. Fix: observe actual sinking point.
- **Uneven loading:** Tilts. Fix: centre weights.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A ship has displacement 5000 tonnes in fresh water and 5200 tonnes in salt water (ρ = 1025 kg/m³). Calculate (a) the ship's volume, and (b) the difference in draft between fresh and salt water.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) In fresh water: V = 5000 m³.
    In salt water: V = 5200/1.025 = 5073 m³.
    Ship volume is **constant** — let's use fresh water:
    V = **5000 m³**.

(b) Draft depends on cross-sectional area.
    If area A = 500 m²:
    Fresh draft = 5000/500 = **10 m**.
    Salt draft = (5000/1.025)/500 = **9.76 m**.
    Difference = **0.24 m** (about 24 cm less in salt water).

**Q:** Explain why a ship that is safely loaded in Mombasa (salt water) might be dangerously overloaded when it sails up the Tana River (fresh water), and calculate how much cargo should be removed for safety.

**Worked Answer:**
**Problem:**
- Mombasa: salt water (ρ = 1025 kg/m³).
- Tana River: fresh water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³).
- Same ship, same load.

**Physics:**
- Salt water: ship floats with draft d₁.
- Fresh water: less buoyancy per volume → sinks deeper.
- Same load → deeper draft in fresh water.
- If draft exceeds Plimsoll line → overloaded.

**Calculation:**
Assume ship volume V = 10,000 m³.
At Plimsoll line in salt water: displacement = 1025 × V_sub = ship mass.
In fresh water: need same displacement = 1000 × V_sub'.

If ship mass = 8000 tonnes:
- Salt: V_sub = 8000/1.025 = 7805 m³.
- Fresh: V_sub = 8000/1.000 = 8000 m³.
- Extra volume = 195 m³.

If cross-section = 1000 m²: extra draft = 0.195 m.

**Cargo to remove:**
- To maintain same draft: remove (1025−1000) × V_sub = 25 × 7805 = **195,125 kg** ≈ **195 tonnes**.

**Conclusion:**
Ship must unload ~195 tonnes before entering fresh water.
