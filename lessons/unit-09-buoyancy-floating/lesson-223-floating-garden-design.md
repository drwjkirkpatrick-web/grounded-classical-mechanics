# Lesson 223: Design Project: A Floating Garden or Fish Cage

**Unit:** 9 — Buoyancy & Floating | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** floating-garden

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Design a floating structure using buoyancy principles.
2. Calculate buoyant force and carrying capacity.
3. Plan materials and construction for a floating garden or fish cage.

## Materials
- Plastic bottles, foam pieces, or other floatation materials
- String or wire
- Small plants or fish food (optional)
- Water container
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Floating garden** *(shamba la kuelea)* — plants grown on a buoyant platform.
- **Fish cage** *(tundu la samaki)* — an enclosure for fish farming that floats in water.
- **Buoyant platform** *(jukwaa la kuelea)* — a structure that stays afloat due to trapped air or low-density materials.
- **Aquaculture** *(uvuvi wa maji)* — farming of fish and other aquatic organisms.

## Pre-Test
1. What makes a floating garden stay on the water surface?
2. Why are plastic bottles good for floating structures?
3. What do plants need to grow on a floating platform?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Buoyant materials (air-filled bottles, foam) displace water.
2. Light, waterproof, trap air.
3. Soil, water, sunlight, nutrients.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups plan designs.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Can we grow vegetables on a lake? Yes — with physics!"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners design floating structures.
   - Plan dimensions.
   - Calculate buoyant force: how many bottles needed?
   - Sketch design.
   - Build simple prototype.
   - Test in water.
   - **Key discovery:** Volume of displaced water determines carrying capacity. More air = more buoyancy.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Floating gardens:**
     - Used in Bangladesh, Myanmar, and other flood-prone areas.
     - Plants grow on raft of vegetation or plastic.
     - Roots access nutrients directly from water.
   - **Fish cages:**
     - Net enclosures floated on barrels or bottles.
     - Fish grow in natural water body.
     - Easy to harvest.
   - **Design considerations:**
     - Buoyancy: enough to support structure + load.
     - Stability: wide base prevents tipping.
     - Durability: materials resist water and UV.
     - Cost: locally available materials.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Groups present designs. Homework: improve and test at home.

## Hands-On Build: Floating Platform

### Parts
- Plastic bottles — 4 — home
- String — 1 m — home
- Foam pieces — optional — classroom
- Small container — 1 — classroom
- Water — for testing — tap

### Workflow
1. Tie bottles together with string.
2. Place platform in water.
3. Add weights (simulate plants).
4. Test stability.
5. Record maximum load.
6. **Checkpoint:** Ensure bottles are sealed and air-filled. Water-filled bottles don't float!

### Common Mistakes
- **Leaky bottles:** Fill with water, sink. Fix: check seals.
- **Uneven loading:** Tips. Fix: centre weight.
- **Too small:** Can't hold plants. Fix: calculate buoyancy first.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What makes garden float?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why plastic bottles good?
3. Calculate: 5 bottles, each displacing 500 g water. Total buoyancy?
4. Name one advantage of floating gardens.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Buoyant materials** displace water.
2. **Light**, waterproof, trap air.
3. 5 × 0.5 × 10 = **25 N** (supports 2.5 kg).
4. Any: **no land needed**, **flood-proof**, **water access**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a floating garden platform for a 2 m × 1 m plot using 2-litre plastic bottles. Calculate (a) the number of bottles needed if each supports 1.5 kg, and (b) the total cost if bottles cost KSh 5 each.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Plot area = 2 m². Soil depth = 10 cm. Soil density ≈ 1200 kg/m³.
    Soil volume = 2 × 0.1 = 0.2 m³.
    Soil mass = 0.2 × 1200 = **240 kg**.
    Plants + water ≈ 60 kg.
    Total load ≈ **300 kg**.

    Bottles needed = 300 / 1.5 = **200 bottles**.

(b) Cost = 200 × 5 = **KSh 1000**.

**Q:** Explain why a floating fish cage system is more sustainable than pond aquaculture in terms of water quality, and how buoyancy physics enables this design.

**Worked Answer:**
**Water quality:**
- Ponds: waste accumulates, needs water exchange.
- Cages: waste disperses in large water body.
- Natural water flow provides oxygen and removes waste.
- No need for aerators or water pumps.

**Buoyancy physics:**
- Cage floats on surface → access to oxygen-rich top layer.
- Buoyant barrels support net enclosure.
- Net volume determines carrying capacity.
- Easy to lift for cleaning/harvesting.

**Sustainability:**
- Uses existing water body (lake, reservoir).
- No land conversion.
- Natural ecosystem handles waste.
- Lower infrastructure cost.
