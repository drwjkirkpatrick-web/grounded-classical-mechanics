## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Set up presentation stations.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Welcome to Regatta Day! Show your best buoyancy work."

3. **(20 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Expo rotations.
   - Each group presents one project/demonstration.
   - Presentations: clay boats, hydrometers, bottle submarines, floating gardens, density layers.
   - Audience asks questions.
   - Peers evaluate using rubric.
   - **Key discovery:** Teaching others reinforces learning. Different approaches solve same problems.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Unit 9 summary:**
     - Density determines floating: less dense floats, more dense sinks.
     - Upthrust: upward force from displaced fluid.
     - Archimedes' principle: upthrust = weight of displaced fluid.
     - Shape matters: hollow objects float even if dense.
     - Hydrometers: float higher in denser liquids.
     - Submarines and fish: control buoyancy by changing volume.
     - Safety: life jackets, Plimsoll lines, overloading prevention.
     - Gases: hot air rises due to buoyancy.
   - **Connections:**
     - Unit 7 (pressure) → Unit 8 (fluids in motion) → Unit 9 (buoyancy).
     - All relate to fluids and forces.

5. **(5 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Best presentations awarded. Preview Unit 10 (Circular Motion). Homework: complete reflection journal.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a concept map connecting all major topics from Units 7, 8, and 9, showing how pressure, flow, and buoyancy relate to each other.

**Worked Answer:**
**Concept map structure:**

```
FLUIDS
├── Pressure (Unit 7)
│   ├── P = ρgh
│   ├── Hydraulic systems
│   └── Atmospheric pressure
│
├── Flow (Unit 8)
│   ├── Continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
│   ├── Bernoulli: fast = low pressure
│   ├── Pumps: lift, force, centrifugal
│   └── Siphons: gravity flow
│
└── Buoyancy (Unit 9)
    ├── Density: ρ = m/V
    ├── Archimedes: U = ρVg
    ├── Floating: ρ_object < ρ_fluid
    ├── Hydrometers
    ├── Submarines
    └── Hot air balloons
```

**Connections:**
- Pressure → flow (pressure difference drives flow).
- Flow → buoyancy (moving fluids affect floating objects).
- Pressure → buoyancy (upthrust from pressure difference).

**Q:** A student claims that a 1 kg block of wood and a 1 kg block of iron experience the same upthrust in water because they have the same mass. Evaluate this claim using physics principles.

**Worked Answer:**
**Claim is FALSE.**

**Reasoning:**
- Upthrust depends on **volume displaced**, not mass.
- Wood: ρ ≈ 600 kg/m³ → V = 1/600 = 0.00167 m³.
- Iron: ρ = 7800 kg/m³ → V = 1/7800 = 0.000128 m³.
- Wood displaces **13× more water** than iron.

**Upthrust:**
- Wood: U = 1000 × 0.00167 × 10 = **16.7 N**.
- Iron: U = 1000 × 0.000128 × 10 = **1.28 N**.

**Conclusion:**
Same mass → very different upthrust.
Upthrust depends on **volume**, not mass.
