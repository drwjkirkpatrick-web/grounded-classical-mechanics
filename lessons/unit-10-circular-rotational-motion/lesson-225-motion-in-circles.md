# Lesson 225: Motion in Circles: Everywhere You Look

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Identify circular motion in everyday life.
2. Distinguish circular motion from straight-line motion.
3. Describe the key features of circular motion.

## Materials
- Photos or videos of circular motion examples
- Spinning objects (top, wheel, fan)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Circular motion** *(mazunguko)* — movement along a circular path.
- **Rotation** *(mzunguko)* — spinning around an axis.
- **Revolution** *(mzunguko wa mzinga)* — orbiting around a centre.
- **Axis** *(mhimili)* — the line about which something rotates.

## Pre-Test
1. Give two examples of circular motion.
2. What is the difference between rotation and revolution?
3. Does a spinning wheel move forward?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Wheel, fan, clock hands, Earth around Sun.
2. Rotation = spinning on own axis; revolution = orbiting around another object.
3. Not necessarily — it can spin in place.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Spin a wheel. Watch the tyre. Is every part moving the same way?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners observe circular motion.
   - Spin a wheel. Mark one point with tape.
   - Trace the path of the marked point.
   - Compare to a point near the centre vs near the edge.
   - Spin a top. Watch axis.
   - **Key discovery:** All points on a rigid rotating object trace circles. Points farther from centre move faster.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Circular motion features:**
     - Path is a circle (or part of one).
     - Direction constantly changes.
     - Speed may be constant (uniform) or changing.
   - **Rotation vs revolution:**
     - Rotation: spinning on own axis (Earth daily, wheel).
     - Revolution: orbiting around centre (Earth yearly, electron around nucleus).
   - **Examples:**
     - Wheels: rotation.
     - Planets: revolution (and rotation).
     - Whirlpools: circular motion of water.
     - CD/DVD: rotation.
   - **Key quantities:**
     - Radius: distance to centre.
     - Period: time for one complete cycle.
     - Frequency: cycles per second.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains rotation vs revolution. Homework: count circular motions in your room.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Two examples of circular motion?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Rotation vs revolution?
3. Which moves faster: a point near the centre or near the edge of a spinning wheel?
4. Name one quantity that describes circular motion.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Any two: **wheel**, **fan**, **clock**, **planet**.
2. **Rotation** = spin on axis; **revolution** = orbit around centre.
3. **Near the edge** (larger circle in same time).
4. Any: **radius**, **period**, **frequency**, **speed**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A wheel of radius 0.3 m spins at 2 revolutions per second. Calculate (a) the period, (b) the frequency, and (c) the speed of a point on the rim.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Period T = 1/f = 1/2 = **0.5 s**.

(b) Frequency f = **2 Hz**.

(c) v = 2πr/T = 2π × 0.3 / 0.5 = **3.77 m/s**.
    Or: v = 2πrf = 2π × 0.3 × 2 = **3.77 m/s**.

**Q:** Explain why the Moon always shows the same face to Earth, and identify which type of circular motion this involves.

**Worked Answer:**
**Tidal locking:**
- Moon rotates on its axis at the same rate as it revolves around Earth.
- Period of rotation = period of revolution = 27.3 days.
- This synchronous rotation means one face always points to Earth.

**Circular motions involved:**
1. **Revolution:** Moon orbits Earth (centre of Moon traces circle around Earth).
2. **Rotation:** Moon spins on its axis.

**Why it happens:**
- Earth's gravity creates tidal bulges on Moon.
- Over billions of years, tidal friction slowed Moon's rotation until rotation and revolution matched.
- Common for moons orbiting close to their planet.
