# Lesson 229: Speed Around the Circle: v = 2πr/T

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.5;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Measure the period of circular motion.
2. Calculate speed from radius and period.
3. Compare speeds at different radii for the same rotation.

## Materials
- Spinning object (wheel, turntable, or pot lid)
- String or tape marker
- Stopwatch or timer
- Ruler
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Period (T)** *(kipindi)* — time for one complete revolution.
- **Frequency (f)** *(mara kwa muda)* — number of revolutions per second.
- **Linear speed (v)** *(kasi mstari)* — distance travelled per unit time along the circular path.
- **Angular speed** *(kasi ya pembe)* — angle swept per unit time.

## Pre-Test
1. What is the period of a clock's second hand?
2. If a wheel takes 2 seconds per revolution, what is its frequency?
3. A point near the edge of a spinning wheel: is it faster or slower than a point near the centre?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. 60 seconds.
2. 0.5 Hz (half a revolution per second).
3. Faster (same period but larger distance).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Mark one point on a spinning wheel. Time how long for one turn. Now mark a point farther out. Same time? Different speed?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners measure circular motion.
   - Mark point at radius r₁ on spinning object.
   - Time 10 revolutions. Divide by 10 for period T.
   - Mark point at radius r₂ > r₁.
   - Same T (same spinning object).
   - Calculate speed: v = 2πr/T.
   - Compare: v₂ > v₁ even though T same.
   - **Key discovery:** Same rotation rate means same period, but outer points travel faster.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Formulas:**
     - Circumference = 2πr.
     - One revolution: distance = 2πr, time = T.
     - Speed: v = 2πr/T.
     - Frequency: f = 1/T.
     - Also: v = 2πrf.
   - **Key insight:**
     - Same T for all points on rigid body.
     - v ∝ r: double radius → double speed.
     - Outer points move faster.
   - **Applications:**
     - Car wheels: outer tread wears faster.
    - CD players: inner tracks spin faster (constant linear speed).
     - Merry-go-round: outer riders feel more force.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why outer points are faster. Homework: calculate speed of bicycle wheel at 60 rpm.

## Hands-On Build: Speed Measurement

### Parts
- Spinning object — 1 — wheel/pot lid
- String/tape — 0.5 m — classroom
- Timer — 1 — phone/stopwatch
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Mark point at r = 5 cm from centre.
2. Spin object.
3. Time 10 revolutions.
4. Calculate T = time/10.
5. Calculate v = 2πr/T.
6. Repeat at r = 10 cm.
7. Compare speeds.
8. **Checkpoint:** Count revolutions carefully. Starting the timer at the exact same position each time reduces error.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong count:** Missed revolutions. Fix: mark clearly, count aloud.
- **Timer error:** Started/stopped wrong. Fix: practice timing.
- **Wrong radius:** Measured diameter. Fix: measure from centre to edge.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Period of second hand?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Frequency if T = 2 s?
3. Calculate: r = 0.3 m, T = 1.5 s. Speed?
4. If radius doubles but T stays same, what happens to speed?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **60 s**.
2. **0.5 Hz**.
3. v = 2π × 0.3 / 1.5 = **1.26 m/s**.
4. **Speed doubles**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A bicycle wheel of radius 0.35 m spins at 120 rpm. Calculate (a) the period, (b) the frequency, and (c) the linear speed of a point on the rim.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) T = 60/120 = **0.5 s**.

(b) f = 120/60 = **2 Hz**.

(c) v = 2πrf = 2π × 0.35 × 2 = **4.40 m/s**.

**Q:** A CD player must maintain constant linear speed (1.3 m/s) as the laser reads from inner to outer tracks. Explain why the disc must spin slower as the laser moves outward, and calculate the rotation speed at radii 2.5 cm and 5.8 cm.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why slower:**
- v = 2πrf (constant v).
- As r increases, f must decrease.
- Outer tracks: larger circumference → fewer rotations needed per second.

**Calculations:**
At r = 2.5 cm = 0.025 m:
  f = v/(2πr) = 1.3/(2π × 0.025) = **8.28 Hz** = **497 rpm**.

At r = 5.8 cm = 0.058 m:
  f = 1.3/(2π × 0.058) = **3.57 Hz** = **214 rpm**.

**Speed variation:**
- Inner: ~500 rpm.
- Outer: ~200 rpm.
- Player adjusts motor speed continuously.
