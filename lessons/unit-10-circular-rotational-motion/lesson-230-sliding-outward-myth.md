# Lesson 230: Why You Feel Flung Outward

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain why there is no "centrifugal force" — only inertia.
2. Describe what happens when centripetal force is removed.
3. Correct the common misconception of being "flung outward."

## Materials
- Photos or videos of circular motion situations
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Centrifugal force** *(nguvu ya kutoka)* — a fictitious force that appears to push outward in a rotating frame.
- **Inertia** *(vimbe)* — resistance to change in motion.
- **Fictitious force** *(nguvu ya dhana)* — a force that only appears in an accelerating reference frame.
- **Tangent** *(mstari wa kugusa)* — the straight-line path an object follows when released from circular motion.

## Pre-Test
1. When a car turns left, do you feel pushed left or right?
2. Is "centrifugal force" a real force?
3. What actually happens when a turning car skids?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Right (toward the outside of the turn).
2. No — it's a feeling from inertia, not a real force.
3. The car continues straight (tangent) because friction was insufficient.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "In a matatu turning sharply, you slide toward the door. Is something pushing you?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse the "flung outward" sensation.
   - Sit on a chair, spin slowly. Feel pressed against back.
   - Discuss: what is the chair doing? (Pushing you inward, toward centre.)
   - Your body wants to go straight (inertia).
   - Chair forces you to turn.
   - Remove force → you fly tangent, not outward.
   - **Key discovery:** There's no outward force. You feel the inward force from the seat. Your body resists turning due to inertia.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **The myth:**
     - People say "centrifugal force throws you outward."
     - This is false — no such force exists.
   - **What really happens:**
     - Inertia: your body wants to continue straight.
     - Seat/wall pushes you inward (centripetal).
     - You feel this inward push.
     - If seat disappears, you go straight (tangent), not outward.
   - **Rotating frame illusion:**
     - In a rotating car, the car seems still and the world spins.
     - In this non-inertial frame, fictitious forces appear.
     - "Centrifugal force" is a fictitious convenience, not real physics.
   - **Correct explanation:**
     - "I feel the seat pushing me inward."
     - "My body resists turning due to inertia."
     - "If released, I follow a tangent."

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner corrects the "flung outward" myth. Homework: explain to a family member.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Which way do you feel pushed in a left turn?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Is centrifugal force real?
3. What direction does an object fly when released from circular motion?
4. Why do you feel pressed against the door in a turning matatu?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Right** (toward outside).
2. **No** — it's inertia, not a real force.
3. **Tangent** (straight line touching the circle).
4. The door pushes you **inward**; you resist due to **inertia**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain the physics of a washing machine spin cycle using the concept of inertia and centripetal force. Why does water leave the clothes through holes in the drum?

**Worked Answer:**
**Spin cycle physics:**
- Drum rotates at high speed.
- Clothes pressed against drum wall by normal force (centripetal).
- Water in clothes has inertia — wants to go straight.
- Holes in drum let water escape tangent to rotation.
- Clothes stay because drum wall blocks them.

**Why water leaves:**
- Water not attached to drum.
- Inertia carries it straight through holes.
- Clothes are blocked by drum wall.
- Result: water exits, clothes stay.

**Not centrifugal force:**
- No force pushes water outward.
- Water simply continues its inertial path.
- Drum wall prevents clothes from doing the same.

**Q:** A child on a merry-go-round claims they are being pushed outward by centrifugal force. Explain why this is incorrect, and describe what would happen to the child if they let go while the merry-go-round is spinning.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why incorrect:**
- The child feels the merry-go-round floor pushing them inward (centripetal).
- Their body resists this inward push due to inertia.
- The "outward" feeling is actually the sensation of being forced inward.
- In an inertial frame, there is only inward force and straight-line inertia.

**If they let go:**
- No more inward force from floor.
- Child follows tangent (straight line at release point).
- Not outward — tangent.
- Relative to merry-go-round, child appears to spiral outward.
- But in ground frame: straight line.

**Teaching point:**
- Always analyse from inertial (non-rotating) frame.
- Fictitious forces are mathematical shortcuts, not physical reality.
