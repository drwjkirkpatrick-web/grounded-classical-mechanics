# Lesson 231: Leaning Bodas and Banked Corners

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.5;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain why motorbikes and bicycles lean into turns.
2. Describe how banked corners reduce the need for friction.
3. Calculate the ideal banking angle for a given speed and radius.

## Materials
- Photos of boda bodas, bicycles, banked roads, velodromes
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Banked corner** *(kona ya mwinuko)* — a road tilted inward on a curve.
- **Lean angle** *(pembe ya kuinama)* — the angle a vehicle tilts into a turn.
- **Normal force component** *(sehemu ya nguvu ya kawaida)* — the part of the surface force that points toward the centre.
- **Ideal banking** *(mwinuko bora)* — angle where no friction is needed.

## Pre-Test
1. Why does a boda lean when turning?
2. What is a banked corner?
3. Why are race tracks banked?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. To allow gravity and normal force to provide centripetal force.
2. A road tilted inward so gravity helps the turn.
3. So vehicles can turn faster without relying solely on friction.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Show photos.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Watch a boda take a corner. Why does the rider lean? What would happen if they didn't?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse leaning and banking.
   - Draw force diagrams for leaning boda.
   - Draw force diagrams for car on flat corner vs banked corner.
   - Compare: where does centripetal force come from?
   - Discuss: what if friction is low (wet road)?
   - **Key discovery:** Leaning shifts the normal force inward, providing centripetal force. Banking does the same with road tilt.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Leaning on flat ground:**
     - Normal force is vertical.
     - Friction provides centripetal force.
     - Lean to prevent tipping — not to create force.
     - Friction limit: F_friction ≤ μN = μmg.
     - Maximum speed: v_max = √(μrg).
   - **Banked corners:**
     - Road tilted at angle θ.
     - Normal force has horizontal component toward centre.
     - This component provides centripetal force.
     - Ideal banking: tan θ = v²/(rg) — no friction needed!
   - **Why lean:**
     - Torque balance: leaning shifts centre of gravity inward.
     - Prevents outward tipping.
     - Combined with handlebar steering for control.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains banked vs flat corners. Homework: find a banked road nearby.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Why lean?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What is banked corner?
3. Calculate ideal banking angle for v = 20 m/s, r = 100 m.
4. What provides centripetal force on a flat corner?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Shift normal force inward**, prevent tipping.
2. Road **tilted inward**.
3. tan θ = v²/(rg) = 400/(100 × 10) = 0.4 → θ = **21.8°**.
4. **Friction** between tyres and road.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A boda of mass 150 kg takes a corner of radius 30 m at 15 m/s on a flat road. Calculate (a) the centripetal force required, (b) the minimum coefficient of friction, and (c) the ideal banking angle.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) F = mv²/r = 150 × 15² / 30 = 150 × 225 / 30 = **1125 N**.

(b) μ = v²/(rg) = 225/(30 × 10) = **0.75**.

(c) tan θ = v²/(rg) = 0.75 → θ = **36.9°**.

**Q:** Explain why a boda rider must counter-steer (turn handlebars opposite to intended direction briefly) before leaning into a turn, using the concept of torque and centre of mass.

**Worked Answer:**
**Counter-steering physics:**
- Brief turn of handlebars opposite to intended direction.
- Front wheel moves laterally, changing contact patch.
- This creates a sideways force on the front wheel.
- Force creates torque about the centre of mass.
- Torque tips the bike toward intended direction.
- Once leaning, rider steers into the turn.

**Why necessary:**
- You can't lean a stationary bike by shifting weight alone (it just falls).
- Moving bike needs steering input to initiate lean.
- Counter-steering is fastest and most controlled method.

**Centre of mass:**
- Steering shifts front wheel track relative to centre of mass.
- Creates gravitational torque that initiates lean.
- Lean angle then creates the turning geometry.

**At speed:**
- Gyroscopic effects also contribute (spinning wheels resist tipping).
- Counter-steering overcomes gyroscopic stability.
