# Lesson 232: The Herder's Sling: Release at the Right Moment

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build and use a simple sling (herder's thrower).
2. Explain why release timing determines projectile direction.
3. Calculate the launch speed from circular motion parameters.

## Materials
- String or cord — 0.5 m
- Small pouch or cup
- Stone or weight
- Open space
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Sling** *(pete)* — a simple weapon using rotating motion to launch a projectile.
- **Release angle** *(pembe ya kutoa)* — the angle at which the projectile leaves the circular path.
- **Projectile motion** *(moto wa kutupa)* — motion under gravity after release.
- **Tangential velocity** *(kasi ya mstari wa kugusa)* — velocity at right angles to the radius at release.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens if you release a spinning stone too early?
2. What happens if you release too late?
3. In what direction does the stone fly when released?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It goes at wrong angle (too high or off target).
2. It wraps back or hits you.
3. Tangent to the circle at the release point.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "David vs Goliath — how did a small boy defeat a giant? Physics!"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build and test slings.
   - Tie pouch to string.
   - Place stone in pouch.
   - Swing in vertical circle.
   - Release at different points.
   - Observe flight direction.
   - **Key discovery:** Release timing determines launch angle. Tangent at release point = flight direction.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Sling physics:**
     - Swinging in circle builds up speed.
     - At release: stone has tangential velocity.
     - Direction = tangent at release point.
     - Speed = v = 2πr/T (or faster if whipped).
   - **Timing:**
     - Release at bottom of swing: stone flies horizontally.
     - Release at side: stone flies at angle.
     - Release at top: stone flies upward (risky!).
   - **Why slings work:**
     - Extended arm + rotation = high speed.
     - Stone launched at high velocity.
     - Centripetal force from arm until release.
   - **Historical context:**
     - Used by shepherds worldwide.
     - Biblical David defeated Goliath with sling.
     - Still used in some pastoral communities.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Best thrower wins. Homework: calculate your sling's launch speed.

## Hands-On Build: Herder's Sling

### Parts
- String — 0.5 m — home
- Pouch — 1 — cloth/leather
- Stone — 1 — ground
- Space — outdoors

### Workflow
1. Tie pouch securely to string.
2. Place stone in pouch.
3. Hold string end.
4. Swing in vertical circle (outdoor only!).
5. Release at bottom of swing for horizontal flight.
6. Observe direction.
7. **Checkpoint:** Release at exact bottom. Early = too high. Late = too low or backward.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong release point:** Stone goes wrong direction. Fix: practice timing.
- **Holding too tight:** String wraps on release. Fix: open hand completely.
- **Wrong weight:** Too heavy = slow. Too light = unstable. Fix: golf ball size.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Release too early?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Release too late?
3. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q3)* Direction of flight?
4. Calculate: r = 0.6 m, 2 rev/s. Launch speed?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Wrong angle** (off target).
2. **Wraps back** or hits you.
3. **Tangent** to circle.
4. v = 2πrf = 2π × 0.6 × 2 = **7.54 m/s**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A sling of radius 0.8 m rotates at 3 revolutions per second. Calculate (a) the tangential launch speed, (b) the centripetal acceleration, and (c) the horizontal range if launched from 1.5 m height.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) v = 2πrf = 2π × 0.8 × 3 = **15.08 m/s**.

(b) a = v²/r = 15.08² / 0.8 = **284 m/s²** (about 29g).

(c) Time to fall: t = √(2h/g) = √(3/10) = 0.548 s.
    Range = vt = 15.08 × 0.548 = **8.26 m**.

**Q:** Explain why a sling is more effective than simply throwing a stone by hand, even if the arm moves at the same speed in both cases.

**Worked Answer:**
**Sling advantage:**
- Arm moves at speed v.
- Sling stone is farther from shoulder (radius r).
- Sling adds rotation to translation.
- Stone speed = arm speed + rotational speed.
- Actually: if arm rotates at angular velocity ω, stone at distance r has speed v = ωr.
- Sling extends r → higher v for same ω.

**Comparison:**
- Throw by hand: stone at hand, speed ≈ arm speed.
- With sling: stone at 0.6 m from hand, speed = arm rotation × 0.6 m additional.
- Sling essentially extends arm length without extending actual arm.

**Energy:**
- Same muscle effort.
- Sling converts rotation to higher linear speed.
- Mechanical advantage of extended radius.
