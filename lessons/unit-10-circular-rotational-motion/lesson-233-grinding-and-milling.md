# Lesson 233: Grinding and Milling: Rotation at Work

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Describe how rotational motion is used in grinding and milling.
2. Explain why faster rotation means more grinding power.
3. Identify examples of rotational machines in Kenyan daily life.

## Materials
- Photos or videos of posho mills, grinding stones, flour mills
- Small hand grinder or mill (if available)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Grinding** *(kusaga)* — crushing material between two surfaces.
- **Milling** *(kusaga nafaka)* — processing grain into flour.
- **Rotor** *(kipiga mzunguko)* — the rotating part of a machine.
- **Posho mill** *(kiwanda cha posho)* — a local maize grinding machine.

## Pre-Test
1. What does a posho mill do?
2. Why do grinding stones need to spin?
3. What happens if a mill spins too slowly?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Grinds maize into flour (posho/ugali flour).
2. Rotating motion creates crushing and shearing forces.
3. Grinding is inefficient or doesn't happen.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Show photos of mills.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Visit a posho mill. What do you see spinning? Why?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners analyse rotational grinding.
   - Examine photos: hand grinding stone, posho mill, flour mill.
   - Compare: manual vs powered rotation.
   - Discuss: what does rotation do that pressing doesn't?
   - **Key discovery:** Rotation provides continuous crushing and shearing. Speed matters — faster = more material processed.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How grinding works:**
     - Two surfaces: one stationary, one rotating.
     - Grain caught between = crushed.
     - Gaps between stones determine particle size.
   - **Why rotation is key:**
     - Continuous process (not intermittent like pounding).
     - Shearing motion breaks particles.
     - Adjustable gap controls fineness.
   - **Speed effects:**
     - Faster rotation = more grain per second.
     - But too fast = heat, worn stones, broken machinery.
     - Optimal speed balances output and wear.
   - **Kenyan context:**
     - Posho mills: village maize grinding.
     - Grinding mills: spices, nuts.
     - Traditional: grinding stones (still used).
     - Industrial: large flour mills in Nairobi.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why posho mills need motors. Homework: visit a local mill.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A posho mill stone has diameter 0.4 m and rotates at 600 rpm. Calculate (a) the linear speed at the rim, (b) the energy delivered to 1 kg of grain per revolution if force is 50 N.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) r = 0.2 m, f = 600/60 = 10 Hz.
    v = 2πrf = 2π × 0.2 × 10 = **12.57 m/s**.

(b) Work per revolution = force × distance = F × 2πr = 50 × 2π × 0.2 = **62.8 J**.
    Per kg grain: **62.8 J/kg/rev**.

**Q:** Compare the energy efficiency of traditional hand grinding vs a motorised posho mill. Consider human power output, grinding rate, and overall energy conversion.

**Worked Answer:**
**Traditional hand grinding:**
- Human power: 50–100 W sustained.
- Grinding rate: ~1 kg/hour.
- Efficiency: muscle → motion ≈ 20%.
- Total energy per kg: 50 W × 3600 s / 1 kg = 180,000 J/kg.

**Motorised posho mill:**
- Motor power: 2–5 kW.
- Grinding rate: 50–100 kg/hour.
- Motor efficiency: 80%.
- Transmission efficiency: 70%.
- Total efficiency: 56%.
- Total energy per kg: 3000 W × 3600 s / 75 kg = 144,000 J/kg.

**Comparison:**
- Motorised: more efficient per kg (144 vs 180 kJ/kg).
- But requires fuel/electricity.
- Traditional: no fuel, but very slow.
- For small household: traditional may be adequate.
- For village/business: motorised essential.

**Factors:**
- Capital cost: motorised higher.
- Operating cost: motorised needs fuel.
- Speed: motorised 50–100× faster.
- Quality: motorised more consistent.
