# Lesson 234: Flywheels: Storing Rotation

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a flywheel stores rotational energy.
2. Demonstrate that a spinning wheel resists changes to its motion.
3. Identify applications of flywheels in machines and vehicles.

## Materials
- Bicycle wheel or pot lid
- String
- Weight
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Flywheel** *(gurudumu la kuhifadhi)* — a heavy rotating wheel that stores rotational energy.
- **Rotational inertia** *(vimbe vya mzunguko)* — resistance to changes in rotational speed.
- **Smoothing** *(kufanya laini)* — using a flywheel to even out speed variations.
- **Energy storage** *(hifadhi ya nishati)* — storing energy in a spinning mass.

## Pre-Test
1. What is a flywheel?
2. Why do engines have heavy spinning wheels?
3. What happens when you spin a heavy wheel and try to stop it?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A heavy wheel that stores rotational energy.
2. To keep rotation steady between power strokes.
3. It resists stopping (has rotational inertia).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q2 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Spin a heavy wheel. Try to stop it. What do you feel?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test flywheel effects.
   - Spin a wheel. Try to change its speed quickly.
   - Feel resistance.
   - Compare: heavy wheel vs light wheel.
   - Attach weight to string wrapped around axle. Drop weight.
   - Heavy wheel spins slower but longer.
   - **Key discovery:** Mass and speed contribute to rotational inertia. Heavy wheels resist speeding up and slowing down.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **How flywheels work:**
     - Rotational energy = ½Iω².
     - I (moment of inertia) depends on mass and distribution.
     - More mass at rim = more inertia.
   - **Smoothing:**
     - Engine power comes in pulses (piston strokes).
     - Flywheel stores energy during power stroke.
     - Releases energy between strokes.
     - Result: smooth rotation.
   - **Applications:**
     - Engines: car, motorbike, generator.
     - Pottery wheels: steady rotation.
     - Toys: spinning tops.
     - Modern: regenerative braking, grid energy storage.
   - **Kenyan context:**
     - Generators: flywheel keeps voltage steady.
     - Hand pumps: flywheel makes pumping easier.
     - Posho mills: heavy flywheel smooths grinding.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why generators need flywheels. Homework: find flywheels in your area.

## Hands-On Build: Flywheel Demonstration

### Parts
- Bicycle wheel — 1 — home
- String — 0.5 m — home
- Weight — 0.5 kg — stone
- Space to spin — classroom

### Workflow
1. Hold wheel by axle.
2. Spin it.
3. Try to stop quickly — feel resistance.
4. Wrap string around axle.
5. Attach weight.
6. Drop weight — wheel spins.
7. **Checkpoint:** Heavier rim = more inertia. Test with same weight on different wheels.

### Common Mistakes
- **Not spinning enough:** Effect too weak. Fix: spin faster.
- **Wrong axis:** Wheel wobbles. Fix: hold axle firmly.
- **Too heavy:** Dangerous. Fix: start with small weight.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A flywheel is a solid disc of mass 10 kg and radius 0.3 m, spinning at 300 rpm. Calculate (a) its moment of inertia, (b) its rotational kinetic energy, and (c) how long it can power a 50 W load before stopping.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) I = ½mr² = 0.5 × 10 × 0.3² = **0.45 kg·m²**.

(b) ω = 300 × 2π/60 = 31.4 rad/s.
    KE = ½Iω² = 0.5 × 0.45 × 31.4² = **222 J**.

(c) t = E/P = 222/50 = **4.44 s**.

**Q:** Explain why modern flywheel energy storage systems use carbon fibre composites spun in vacuum on magnetic bearings, and calculate the energy density advantage over lead-acid batteries.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why carbon fibre + vacuum + magnetic bearings:**
- Carbon fibre: high strength-to-weight ratio → can spin very fast without breaking.
- Vacuum: eliminates air resistance (drag), allowing high speeds.
- Magnetic bearings: no friction, no wear, minimal energy loss.

**Energy density:**
- Flywheel: KE = ½Iω². With ω = 60,000 rpm = 6283 rad/s, I = 0.1 kg·m²:
  KE = 0.5 × 0.1 × 6283² = **2 MJ** per kg (theoretical).
- Lead-acid battery: ~0.14 MJ/kg.
- Advantage: **~14× higher** theoretical energy density.

**Practical considerations:**
- Actual flywheel systems: 0.1–0.5 MJ/kg (still competitive).
- Batteries: constant output, flywheel: speed decreases.
- Flywheels: fast charge/discharge, long cycle life.
- Batteries: higher volumetric density, easier to transport.
