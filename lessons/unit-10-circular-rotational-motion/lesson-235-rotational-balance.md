# Lesson 235: Balancing a Wheel

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate the importance of rotational balance.
2. Identify symptoms of an unbalanced rotating object.
3. Build a simple balanced rotating structure.

## Materials
- Bicycle wheel or pot lid
- Clay or putty
- Nail or marker
- String for axle support
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Balance** *(uwiano)* — even distribution of mass around the axis.
- **Wobble** *(kutikisika)* — uneven rotation due to imbalance.
- **Counterweight** *(kipimo cha kinyume)* — added mass to offset imbalance.
- **Centre of mass** *(kitovu cha masi)* — the point where mass is evenly distributed.

## Pre-Test
1. Why do wheels need balancing?
2. What happens if a wheel is not balanced?
3. How do you balance a wobbly wheel?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. To prevent vibration and wobble.
2. It shakes, vibrates, wears bearings.
3. Add weight opposite the heavy side.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Spin a bicycle wheel. Now add clay to one side. What happens?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test balance.
   - Spin balanced wheel — smooth rotation.
   - Add clay to one side.
   - Spin — observe wobble.
   - Move clay to opposite side.
   - Spin — observe improvement.
   - Adjust until smooth.
   - **Key discovery:** Unbalanced mass pulls the axis. Balance means centre of mass on the axis.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why balance matters:**
     - Unbalanced mass creates centrifugal... wait, we don't use that term.
     - Unbalanced mass requires centripetal force that varies.
     - Creates vibration, noise, wear.
     - Can destroy bearings and axles.
   - **How to balance:**
     - Find heavy side (lowest point when freely suspended).
     - Add counterweight opposite.
     - Test and adjust.
   - **Applications:**
     - Car tyres: balanced with lead weights.
     - Washing machines: redistribute load.
     - Engines: balanced crankshafts.
     - Fans: blades must be equal.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why wobble is bad. Homework: check if your bicycle wheels are balanced.

## Hands-On Build: Wheel Balancing

### Parts
- Wheel or lid — 1 — bicycle/pot
- Clay — small amount — art supplies
- String — 0.3 m — home
- Marker — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Suspend wheel on string through axle.
2. Let it settle. Mark lowest point.
3. Spin wheel.
4. Add clay to mark. Spin again.
5. Observe wobble.
6. Adjust clay position.
7. **Checkpoint:** The heavy side always ends up at the bottom when freely suspended. This is how you find imbalance.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wrong side:** Added to heavy side instead of opposite. Fix: add opposite the mark.
- **Too much clay:** Overcompensates. Fix: small amounts.
- **Not spinning:** Can't see wobble. Fix: spin before and after.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A wheel has an imbalance of 50 g at radius 0.2 m, spinning at 600 rpm. Calculate (a) the centrifugal... no, the centripetal force required to keep this mass moving in a circle, and (b) the vibration frequency.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) ω = 600 × 2π/60 = 62.8 rad/s.
    F = mω²r = 0.05 × 62.8² × 0.2 = **39.4 N**.
    (This is the unbalanced force causing vibration.)

(b) Vibration frequency = rotation frequency = **600 rpm = 10 Hz**.
    (One pulse per revolution as heavy spot passes.)

**Q:** Explain why dynamic balancing (balancing while spinning) is more effective than static balancing (balancing while stationary), and describe a method for dynamic balancing of a car wheel.

**Worked Answer:**
**Static vs dynamic:**
- Static: centre of mass on axis. Good for thin discs.
- Dynamic: also corrects for uneven mass distribution along the axis.
- A wheel can be statically balanced but still wobble if heavy spots are on opposite sides (couple imbalance).

**Dynamic balancing method:**
1. Mount wheel on balancer spindle.
2. Spin at test speed.
3. Sensors detect vibration amplitude and phase.
4. Computer calculates required weights and positions.
5. Clip weights to inner and outer rim.
6. Retest until vibration acceptable.

**Why more effective:**
- Corrects both static and couple imbalance.
- Accounts for actual operating conditions.
- Essential for modern high-speed wheels.
