# Lesson 237: Rolling: Rotation Plus Translation

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain that rolling combines rotation and translation.
2. Mark and observe the motion of a point on a rolling wheel.
3. Describe why the contact point of a rolling wheel is momentarily at rest.

## Materials
- Cylinder or wheel
- Marker
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Rolling motion** *(mazunguko ya kuguruma)* — combined rotation and translation.
- **Cycloid** *(duara-kwenda)* — the path traced by a point on a rolling wheel.
- **Translation** *(kusonga)* — straight-line movement.
- **Instantaneous centre** *(kitovu cha papo hapo)* — the point about which a rolling object rotates at any instant.

## Pre-Test
1. Does a rolling wheel only rotate, or does it also move forward?
2. What shape does a point on a rolling wheel trace?
3. Is the bottom of a rolling wheel moving?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Both — rotation plus translation.
2. A cycloid (looping curve).
3. Momentarily at rest (instantaneous centre).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q3 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Mark a point on a rolling bottle. What path does it trace?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners trace rolling motion.
   - Mark a point on cylinder.
   - Roll along paper.
   - Trace the path.
   - Observe: loops and curves.
   - Mark point at top, side, bottom.
   - Compare paths.
   - **Key discovery:** The point traces a cycloid. Bottom point is instantaneously at rest relative to ground.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Rolling = rotation + translation:**
     - Centre moves forward at speed v.
     - Rim rotates at angular speed ω = v/r.
     - Combined: points trace cycloids.
   - **Contact point:**
     - Translation forward + rotation backward at same speed.
     - Net velocity = 0 (momentarily at rest).
     - This is why rolling friction is low.
   - **Top point:**
     - Translation forward + rotation forward.
     - Speed = 2v (twice centre speed).
   - **Applications:**
     - Wheels: efficient transport.
     - Gears: controlled rolling.
     - Ball bearings: reduce friction.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why rolling is efficient. Homework: trace a cycloid with a coin.

## Hands-On Build: Tracing Cycloids

### Parts
- Cylinder — 1 — bottle/can
- Marker — 1 — classroom
- Paper — 1 sheet — classroom

### Workflow
1. Mark point on cylinder.
2. Place on paper.
3. Roll while marking.
4. Trace path.
5. Mark different points (top, side, bottom).
6. Compare.
7. **Checkpoint:** Roll without slipping. If cylinder slides, path is wrong.

### Common Mistakes
- **Sliding:** Not pure rolling. Fix: roll carefully.
- **Wrong mark:** Marker slips. Fix: tape marker to cylinder.
- **Not enough space:** Paper too short. Fix: long paper or multiple sheets.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Rolling = only rotate or also translate?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Path traced?
3. What is the speed of the top point of a rolling wheel?
4. Why is the contact point momentarily at rest?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Both** rotation and translation.
2. **Cycloid**.
3. **Twice** the centre speed (2v).
4. Translation forward cancels rotation backward.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A wheel of radius 0.3 m rolls at 5 m/s. Calculate (a) the angular velocity, (b) the speed of the top point, and (c) the speed of a point on the rim at 45° from vertical.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) ω = v/r = 5/0.3 = **16.67 rad/s**.

(b) v_top = 2v = **10 m/s**.

(c) Components: v_x = v − v_rot sin 45° = 5 − 5 × 0.707 = 1.46 m/s.
    v_y = v_rot cos 45° = 5 × 0.707 = 3.54 m/s.
    Speed = √(1.46² + 3.54²) = **3.83 m/s**.

**Q:** Explain why a wheel rolling without slipping travels exactly its circumference in one revolution, and derive the relationship between linear and angular velocity.

**Worked Answer:**
**Rolling without slipping:**
- One revolution: wheel rotates 2π radians.
- Contact point on rim touches new ground each instant.
- Distance covered = arc length = 2πr = circumference.

**Derivation:**
- In time Δt, wheel rotates Δθ.
- Arc length on rim = rΔθ.
- This equals distance moved: Δx = rΔθ.
- Divide by Δt: v = rω.

**Key insight:**
- No-slip condition links rotation and translation.
- If slipping occurs: v ≠ rω.
- Braking wheel: v < rω (skidding).
- Spinning wheel: v > rω (burning rubber).
