# Lesson 238: Ball Bearings: Rolling Between Surfaces

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how ball bearings reduce friction.
2. Build a simple ball bearing from marbles or beads.
3. Compare sliding friction to rolling friction.

## Materials
- Marbles or small beads — 6 per group
- Two flat surfaces (cardboard, plates)
- Grease or oil (optional)
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Ball bearing** *(beari ya mpira)* — a device using rolling balls to reduce friction between surfaces.
- **Rolling friction** *(msuguano wa kuguruma)* — resistance when an object rolls over a surface.
- **Sliding friction** *(msuguano wa kuteleza)* — resistance when surfaces slide past each other.
- **Lubrication** *(kutia mafuta)* — adding oil or grease to reduce friction.

## Pre-Test
1. Is it easier to slide a heavy box or roll it on cylinders?
2. What do ball bearings do?
3. Why do machines need lubrication?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Roll on cylinders (less friction).
2. Reduce friction by replacing sliding with rolling.
3. Reduce friction further, prevent wear.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Try to slide two books past each other. Now put marbles between them. What happens?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test friction reduction.
   - Place heavy object on flat surface. Try to slide. Feel friction.
   - Place marbles between object and surface.
   - Try to move. Much easier!
   - Compare: with and without marbles.
   - Add oil. Compare again.
   - **Key discovery:** Rolling replaces sliding, reducing friction dramatically. Lubrication helps further.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why rolling reduces friction:**
     - Sliding: surfaces rub, creating heat and wear.
     - Rolling: only small contact points, minimal rubbing.
     - Rolling friction ≈ 1/10 to 1/100 of sliding friction.
   - **Ball bearing construction:**
     - Inner race (attached to shaft).
     - Outer race (attached to housing).
     - Balls between races.
     - Cage keeps balls spaced.
   - **Applications:**
     - Bicycle wheels, car wheels.
     - Motors, fans, machinery.
     - Anywhere something spins.
   - **Maintenance:**
     - Keep clean.
     - Lubricate regularly.
     - Replace worn bearings.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains why bearings matter. Homework: find bearings in your home.

## Hands-On Build: Simple Ball Bearing

### Parts
- Marbles — 6 — game/classroom
- Flat plates — 2 — cardboard/metal
- Heavy object — 1 — book/stone
- Oil — optional — kitchen

### Workflow
1. Place heavy object on one plate.
2. Try to slide. Note difficulty.
3. Place marbles between plates.
4. Place object on top.
5. Try to move. Much easier!
6. Add oil to marbles. Test again.
7. **Checkpoint:** Marbles must be free to roll. Trapped marbles don't work.

### Common Mistakes
- **Too few marbles:** Object tilts. Fix: use 6–8 marbles.
- **Trapped marbles:** Can't roll. Fix: ensure space for movement.
- **Wrong surface:** Too rough. Fix: smooth plates.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A sliding block experiences 50 N friction. When placed on rollers (cylinders), friction drops to 8 N. Calculate the percentage reduction, and explain why ball bearings reduce friction even more than cylinders.

**Worked Answer:**
**Percentage reduction:**
Reduction = 50 − 8 = 42 N.
Percentage = 42/50 × 100 = **84%**.

**Why ball bearings are better than cylinders:**
- Cylinders: line contact, some sliding at ends.
- Balls: point contact, pure rolling.
- Balls can rotate in any direction (omnidirectional).
- Cylinders only roll in one direction.
- Balls distribute load over many points.
- Cylinder alignment is critical; balls self-align.

**Q:** Calculate the power lost to friction in a motor with ball bearings if the shaft rotates at 1500 rpm, the bearing friction is 2 N, and the shaft radius is 0.025 m.

**Worked Answer:**
**Power lost to friction:**
Torque = friction force × radius = 2 × 0.025 = 0.05 N·m.

Angular velocity: ω = 1500 × 2π/60 = 157 rad/s.

Power = torque × ω = 0.05 × 157 = **7.85 W**.

**Comparison:**
If sliding bearing (μ = 0.3, normal force = 100 N):
Friction = 30 N.
Torque = 30 × 0.025 = 0.75 N·m.
Power = 0.75 × 157 = **118 W**.

Ball bearings save: 118 − 7.85 = **110 W** (93% reduction).
