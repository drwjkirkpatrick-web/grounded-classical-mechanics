# Lesson 239: Axles and Torque: The Long Spanner Rule

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.5;JS-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Explain how a longer spanner makes turning easier.
2. Calculate torque from force and lever arm.
3. Apply the torque concept to everyday tools.

## Materials
- Spanners or wrenches of different lengths
- Bolt or nut to turn
- String, weight
- Ruler
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Torque** *(nguvu ya mzunguko)* — turning force = force × perpendicular distance.
- **Lever arm** *(mkono wa ulinganishi)* — perpendicular distance from axis to force.
- **Spanner** *(spana)* — a tool for turning nuts and bolts.
- **Axle** *(mhimili wa gurudumu)* — the shaft on which a wheel rotates.

## Pre-Test
1. Is it easier to turn a nut with a short spanner or a long one?
2. What is torque?
3. How does a longer handle help?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Long one.
2. Turning force = force × distance.
3. Same force produces more torque.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Collect Q1 by show of hands.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Try to turn a bolt with your fingers. Now use a long spanner. Feel the difference?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test torque.
   - Try to turn bolt with short spanner. Measure force needed.
   - Try with long spanner. Measure force.
   - Compare: long spanner needs less force.
   - Try with string and weight at different distances.
   - **Key discovery:** Torque = force × distance. Longer distance = less force for same torque.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Torque formula:**
     - τ = F × d (force × perpendicular distance).
     - Units: N·m.
   - **Long spanner rule:**
     - Same torque with less force.
     - τ = F₁ × d₁ = F₂ × d₂.
     - Double distance → half force.
   - **Applications:**
     - Door handles: far from hinge.
     - Steering wheels: large diameter.
     - Bicycle pedals: long cranks.
     - Wheelbarrow: long handles.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains the long spanner rule. Homework: measure spanner lengths at home.

## Hands-On Build: Torque Test

### Parts
- Bolt/nut — 1 — hardware
- Spanners — 2 (short and long) — workshop
- String — 0.3 m — home
- Weight — 1 kg — stone
- Ruler — 1 — classroom

### Workflow
1. Tighten bolt with short spanner.
2. Measure force to loosen (use weight on string).
3. Record distance and force.
4. Repeat with long spanner.
5. Compare: F₁ × d₁ vs F₂ × d₂.
6. **Checkpoint:** Force must be perpendicular to spanner. Angled force reduces effective torque.

### Common Mistakes
- **Angled force:** Less torque. Fix: pull perpendicular.
- **Slipping spanner:** Wrong size. Fix: correct fit.
- **Wrong measurement:** Measure perpendicular distance. Fix: use ruler carefully.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Short or long spanner easier?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* What is torque?
3. Calculate: 20 N at 0.3 m. Torque?
4. Name one everyday use of torque.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Long**.
2. **Turning force** = force × distance.
3. τ = 20 × 0.3 = **6 N·m**.
4. Any: **door handle**, **steering wheel**, **spanner**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A wheel nut requires 120 N·m to loosen. A driver has a 0.3 m spanner. Calculate (a) the minimum force needed, and (b) the force if using a 0.5 m extension pipe.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) F = τ/d = 120/0.3 = **400 N** (about 40 kg weight).

(b) F = 120/0.5 = **240 N** (about 24 kg weight).

**Extension advantage:** 240/400 = **60%** of original force.

**Q:** Explain why a wheelbarrow is designed with long handles, and calculate the mechanical advantage if the load is 0.3 m from the wheel and the handles are 1.5 m from the wheel.

**Worked Answer:**
**Why long handles:**
- Load (weight) close to wheel (fulcrum).
- Handles far from wheel.
- Small lifting force at handles lifts large load.
- Torque balance: F × 1.5 = W × 0.3.

**Mechanical advantage:**
MA = effort arm / load arm = 1.5/0.3 = **5**.

**Force needed:**
If load = 200 N: F = 200/5 = **40 N**.

**Other examples:**
- Door handle: MA = handle distance / hinge distance.
- Bicycle crank: MA = crank length / pedal radius.
- Steering wheel: MA = wheel radius / pinion radius.
