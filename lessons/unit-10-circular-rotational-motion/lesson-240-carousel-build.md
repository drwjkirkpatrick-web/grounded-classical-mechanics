# Lesson 240: Build: A Bottle-Top Carousel

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** carousel

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a rotating carousel from bottle tops and string.
2. Demonstrate centripetal force keeping riders on a rotating platform.
3. Test how speed affects stability.

## Materials
- Bottle tops — 4–6
- String — 0.5 m
- Stick or pencil
- Tape
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Carousel** *(mzingo wa vitu)* — a rotating platform with seats.
- **Rider** *(mpanda)* — an object on the rotating platform.
- **Stability** *(uthabiti)* — resistance to tipping or falling.
- **Rotation speed** *(kasi ya mzunguko)* — how fast something spins.

## Pre-Test
1. What happens to objects on a spinning platform?
2. Why do carousel seats swing outward?
3. How can you make a carousel spin faster?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. They move in circles (or fly off if not held).
2. Centripetal force from chains holds them; they swing due to inertia.
3. Pull string faster, reduce friction, lighter load.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups plan carousels.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Build a miniature carousel. What keeps the riders on?"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners build bottle-top carousels.
   - Tie bottle tops to string.
   - Attach strings to stick at equal distances.
   - Spin stick.
   - Observe: tops swing outward.
   - Vary speed: slow vs fast.
   - **Key discovery:** Faster spin = greater swing. String tension provides centripetal force.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Centripetal force from string:**
     - Tension in string pulls tops inward.
     - This is the centripetal force.
     - Without string: tops fly tangent.
   - **Angle and speed:**
     - Faster spin → larger angle.
     - Tension has vertical (supports weight) and horizontal (centripetal) components.
   - **Real carousels:**
     - Motors provide rotation.
     - Seats on chains or poles.
     - Safety: speed limited, restraints.

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Best carousel demonstration. Homework: calculate string tension at different speeds.

## Hands-On Build: Bottle-Top Carousel

### Parts
- Bottle tops — 4–6 — home
- String — 0.5 m — home
- Stick/pencil — 1 — classroom
- Tape — 1 roll — classroom

### Workflow
1. Tie string to each bottle top.
2. Attach strings to stick at equal spacing.
3. Equal lengths for symmetry.
4. Hold stick, spin.
5. Observe tops swinging outward.
6. Vary speed. Record angles.
7. **Checkpoint:** Unequal lengths cause wobble. Adjust until balanced.

### Common Mistakes
- **Unequal lengths:** Wobble. Fix: measure and adjust.
- **Weak knots:** Tops fly off. Fix: secure knots.
- **Too fast:** Strings tangle. Fix: controlled speed.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* What happens to objects on spinning platform?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why seats swing outward?
3. Calculate: string at 30° from vertical. What provides centripetal force?
4. How does speed affect swing angle?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. They move in **circles**.
2. **Inertia** resists turning; **tension** pulls inward.
3. **Horizontal component of tension**.
4. **Faster = greater angle**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** A carousel seat of mass 0.5 kg hangs on a 2 m chain. When the carousel rotates, the chain makes 40° with vertical. Calculate (a) the tension in the chain, (b) the centripetal force, and (c) the rotation speed.

**Worked Answer:**
(a) Vertical: T cos 40° = mg → T = 0.5 × 10 / cos 40° = 5 / 0.766 = **6.53 N**.

(b) Horizontal: F_c = T sin 40° = 6.53 × 0.643 = **4.20 N**.

(c) Radius: r = 2 sin 40° = 2 × 0.643 = 1.286 m.
    F_c = mv²/r → v = √(F_c × r / m) = √(4.20 × 1.286 / 0.5) = √10.8 = **3.29 m/s**.

**Q:** Design a safe miniature carousel for primary school children using only recycled materials. Calculate the maximum safe speed if the platform is 1 m diameter and children must experience no more than 1.5g horizontal acceleration.

**Worked Answer:**
**Design:**
- Platform: plywood disc, 1 m diameter.
- Axis: vertical pole through centre.
- Seats: buckets or boxes at edge.
- Drive: hand crank or small motor.
- Safety: guard rail, non-slip surface.

**Maximum speed:**
a_max = 1.5g = 15 m/s².
r = 0.5 m.

a = v²/r → v = √(ar) = √(15 × 0.5) = √7.5 = **2.74 m/s**.

Period: T = 2πr/v = 2π × 0.5 / 2.74 = **1.15 s/rev**.

**Safety checks:**
- At 2.74 m/s: force = 1.5 × weight.
- Children must hold on or be strapped.
- Slower for very young children.

## Safety Notes
- Keep area clear when spinning.
- Don't spin too fast.
- Bottle tops can fly off.
- Standard classroom safety.
