# Lesson 241: Spinning Eggs: Raw vs Boiled

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** Yes | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Distinguish a raw egg from a boiled egg by spinning.
2. Explain how internal fluid motion affects rotational inertia.
3. Use the egg test as a simple diagnostic tool.

## Materials
- Raw eggs — 2
- Boiled eggs — 2
- Flat surface
- Marker
- Notebook for each learner

## Vocabulary
- **Rotational inertia** *(vimbe vya mzunguko)* — resistance to changes in rotational speed.
- **Internal friction** *(msuguano wa ndani)* — resistance between layers inside a fluid.
- **Fluid motion** *(mchakato wa kioevu)* — movement of liquid inside the egg.
- **Diagnostic test** *(kipimo cha utambuzi)* — a simple test to identify a condition.

## Pre-Test
1. Which spins longer: raw egg or boiled egg?
2. Why does a raw egg wobble when spun?
3. Can you tell if an egg is boiled without cracking it?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Boiled egg (solid inside = more rotational inertia).
2. Fluid inside sloshes, creating internal friction.
3. Yes — spin it.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Groups get eggs.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Can you tell if an egg is boiled without cracking it? Try spinning it!"

3. **(15 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** In groups of 4, learners test raw vs boiled eggs.
   - Spin raw egg. Observe: wobbles, stops quickly.
   - Spin boiled egg. Observe: spins smoothly, longer.
   - Try to stop each, then release.
   - Raw egg starts again (fluid keeps moving).
   - **Key discovery:** Boiled egg (solid) has consistent rotational inertia. Raw egg (fluid) has internal motion that dissipates energy.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Why boiled eggs spin better:**
     - Solid inside: all mass rotates together.
     - Consistent rotational inertia.
     - No internal friction.
   - **Why raw eggs wobble:**
     - Fluid inside doesn't spin immediately.
     - Shell spins, fluid lags behind.
     - Creates internal friction.
     - Energy lost to fluid motion.
   - **Stop-and-release test:**
     - Stop boiled egg: stays stopped (solid).
     - Stop raw egg: releases and starts again (fluid still moving).
   - **Applications:**
     - Quick test for boiled eggs.
     - Demonstrates rotational inertia and internal friction.
     - Similar to Earth (solid crust, liquid core).

5. **(10 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually, then mark in pairs.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: One learner explains the physics. Homework: test eggs at home.

## Hands-On Build: Egg Spin Test

### Parts
- Raw egg — 1 — kitchen
- Boiled egg — 1 — kitchen
- Flat surface — table
- Marker — optional

### Workflow
1. Label eggs (don't mix up!).
2. Spin raw egg. Count rotations/time.
3. Spin boiled egg. Count rotations/time.
4. Compare.
5. Stop each, then release.
6. Raw egg restarts; boiled stays stopped.
7. **Checkpoint:** Boiled egg spins smoothly. Raw egg wobbles and stops fast.

### Common Mistakes
- **Mixing up eggs:** Can't tell which is which. Fix: mark clearly.
- **Not spinning enough:** Weak test. Fix: spin firmly.
- **Wrong surface:** Too rough. Fix: smooth table.

## Post-Test
1. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q1)* Which spins longer?
2. *(Mirrors Pre-Test Q2)* Why wobble?
3. What happens when you stop a raw egg and release?
4. Name one thing this test demonstrates.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. **Boiled** egg.
2. **Fluid inside** sloshes.
3. **Restarts spinning** (fluid still moving).
4. Any: **rotational inertia**, **internal friction**, **diagnostic test**.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Explain the physics behind the "stop-and-release" test in terms of angular momentum conservation and internal friction, and relate this to how Earth's liquid core affects the planet's rotation.

**Worked Answer:**
**Stop-and-release physics:**
- Shell stops: angular momentum transferred to hand.
- Fluid inside still has angular momentum (less friction with shell).
- Release: fluid's angular momentum transfers back to shell.
- Result: egg restarts spinning (weakly).

**Earth comparison:**
- Earth: solid mantle + crust, liquid outer core.
- Core doesn't rotate exactly with mantle.
- Differential rotation creates Earth's magnetic field (dynamo effect).
- Very slow friction: core-mantle boundary changes over millions of years.
- Unlike egg: Earth's internal friction is tiny.

**Q:** A solid sphere and a hollow sphere of the same mass and radius roll down an incline. Which reaches the bottom first, and how does this relate to the raw vs boiled egg test?

**Worked Answer:**
**Solid sphere wins:**
- Solid sphere: I = (2/5)mr².
- Hollow sphere: I = (2/3)mr².
- Solid sphere has less rotational inertia → more energy goes to translation.
- Reaches bottom first.

**Relation to egg test:**
- Boiled egg ≈ solid sphere (solid inside).
- Raw egg ≈ hollow sphere with internal friction (fluid doesn't rotate with shell).
- Both: raw egg has more effective inertia → slower spin.
- Raw egg also loses energy to internal friction → stops faster.
