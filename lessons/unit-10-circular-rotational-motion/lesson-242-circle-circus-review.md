# Lesson 242: Unit Review: Circle Circus

**Unit:** 10 — Circular & Rotational Motion | **Duration:** 45 minutes | **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE;UP-FE | **Hands-On:** No | **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Summarise the key concepts of Unit 10.
2. Present a project or demonstration from the unit.
3. Evaluate peer presentations and provide constructive feedback.

## Materials
- Projects and demonstrations from Unit 10
- Evaluation rubric
- Notebook for each learner

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **(5 min)** Settle and administer the Pre-Test. Set up presentation stations.

2. **(5 min)** Engage: "Welcome to the Circle Circus! Show your best rotational physics."

3. **(20 min)** Explore:
   - **Activity:** Expo rotations.
   - Each group presents one project/demonstration.
   - Presentations: bucket of water, herder's sling, bottle-top carousel, spinning eggs, wheel balancing, ball bearings, gyroscope.
   - Audience asks questions.
   - Peers evaluate using rubric.
   - **Key discovery:** Teaching others reinforces learning. Circular motion principles apply everywhere.

4. **(8 min)** Explain:
   - **Unit 10 summary:**
     - Circular motion: rotation + revolution.
     - Centripetal force: inward, required for circular motion.
     - Sources: tension, friction, gravity, normal force.
     - Speed: v = 2πr/T.
     - "Centrifugal force" is a myth — it's inertia.
     - Leaning and banking: tilt to get inward force component.
     - Rolling: rotation + translation, contact point instantaneously at rest.
     - Ball bearings: replace sliding with rolling friction.
     - Torque: force × distance, longer spanner = less force.
     - Gyroscopic effect: spinning resists tipping.
     - Flywheels: store rotational energy.
     - Wheel balancing: prevent vibration.
   - **Connections:**
     - Unit 3 (motion) → Unit 10 (circular).
     - Unit 6 (machines) → axles, torque, bearings.
     - Unit 7 (pressure) → centripetal in fluids.

5. **(5 min)** Post-Test (see below). Learners work individually.

6. **(2 min)** Wrap: Best presentations awarded. Preview Unit 11 (Oscillations & Pendulums). Homework: complete reflection journal.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

**Q:** Design a concept map connecting circular motion, centripetal force, torque, rolling motion, and gyroscopic stability, showing how they relate to linear motion concepts from earlier units.

**Worked Answer:**
**Concept map structure:**

```
LINEAR MOTION (Unit 3)
├── v = d/t, a = Δv/Δt
├── F = ma
└── → CIRCULAR MOTION (Unit 10)
    ├── v = 2πr/T (circular speed)
    ├── a = v²/r (centripetal acceleration)
    ├── F = mv²/r (centripetal force)
    │   ├── Sources: tension, friction, gravity, normal
    │   ├── Vertical circles: bucket, loop
    │   └── Leaning/banking: bodas, velodromes
    ├── TORQUE (extends F = ma to rotation)
    │   ├── τ = rF
    │   ├── Long spanner rule
    │   └── Axles, wheelbarrows
    ├── ROLLING MOTION
    │   ├── v = rω
    │   ├── Contact point at rest
    │   └── Ball bearings: low friction
    └── GYROSCOPIC STABILITY
        ├── Angular momentum
        ├── Precession
        └── Applications: bikes, spacecraft, tops
```

**Key connections:**
- Circular speed extends linear speed to angular measure.
- Centripetal acceleration is perpendicular to velocity.
- Torque is rotational equivalent of force.
- Rolling connects linear and angular motion.
- Gyroscopes demonstrate angular momentum conservation.

**Q:** A student claims that a satellite in circular orbit is in equilibrium because the centripetal force balances the gravitational force. Evaluate this claim using Newton's laws.

**Worked Answer:**
**Claim is FALSE.**

**Why:**
- Equilibrium means net force = 0, acceleration = 0.
- Satellite has acceleration: a = v²/r toward Earth.
- Net force is NOT zero — gravity IS the centripetal force.
- There is no "balancing" force — gravity causes the acceleration.

**Correct description:**
- Satellite is in free fall.
- Gravity provides exactly the centripetal force needed.
- F_gravity = F_centripetal = mv²/r.
- Acceleration is toward Earth.
- Constant speed but changing velocity (direction).

**Newton's second law:**
- F_net = ma.
- Here: F_gravity = ma_centripetal.
- Not equilibrium — accelerated motion.
