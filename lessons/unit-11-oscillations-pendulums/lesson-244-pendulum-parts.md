# Lesson 244: Anatomy of a Pendulum

**Unit:** 11 — Oscillations & Pendulums  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a simple pendulum from local materials and identify its parts (bob, string, pivot).
2. Locate the equilibrium position and explain why the bob returns there.
3. Sketch force arrows at the extremes and at the bottom of a swing.
4. Explain where the pendulum is fastest and where it is slowest using the PE↔KE exchange.

## Materials
- String or sisal twine, ~1 m per pair
- A bob: a stone, a bunch of keys, a large nut, or a debe lid with a hole
- A stand: a retort stand, or a string tied to a door frame, or a stick clamped to a desk
- A ruler or protractor card (paper with a protractor traced on it)
- Notebook and pencil for force-arrow sketches

## Vocabulary
- **bob** (takwimu) — the heavy mass at the end of the pendulum
- **pivot** (kituo) — the fixed point the string swings from
- **equilibrium** (usawaziko) — the rest position where forces balance
- **restoring force** (nguvu ya kurudisha) — the component of weight pulling the bob back to centre
- **potential energy** (nguvu iliyoko) — stored energy at the highest points
- **kinetic energy** (nguvu ya mwendo) — motion energy, greatest at the bottom

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Draw a pendulum at rest (hanging straight down). Label the string and the bob.
2. When you pull a pendulum to one side and release, what brings it back toward the centre?
3. Where do you think a pendulum moves fastest — at the top of its swing or at the bottom?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A string hanging vertically with a mass at the bottom. String goes up to pivot; bob at lower end.
2. Gravity (the weight of the bob) pulls it back. The component of weight along the arc acts as the restoring force.
3. At the bottom. It accelerates all the way down and decelerates going back up.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips before the build.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — show the pendulum from Lesson 243. Pull it aside and hold. Ask: *what forces act on the bob right now?* Then release. Ask: *where is it fastest? Where does it stop?* Board the words **equilibrium** and **restoring force**.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs build their own pendulum (see Hands-On Build). Each pair sets the pendulum swinging gently and observes: (a) where the bob is fastest, (b) where it momentarily stops, (c) how many swings before it noticeably slows. Then learners sketch three positions — left extreme, bottom (equilibrium), right extreme — and draw force arrows: weight (always down), tension (along the string). At the extremes, identify the restoring component of weight that points toward centre.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate on the board with a large labelled diagram. At the extremes the bob stops momentarily — PE is maximum, KE is zero. At the bottom the bob is fastest — KE is maximum, PE is minimum (relative to the swing). Energy sloshes: PE → KE → PE → KE, each cycle. The restoring force is the component of gravity along the arc; the perpendicular component is balanced by tension. Confront the misconception that the string "pulls the bob back" — the string only pulls along its length (tension); it is gravity that restores.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *a pendulum is an energy seesaw*. Homework: sketch the pendulum at the left extreme and label PE as "max" and KE as "zero"; sketch at the bottom and label KE as "max" and PE as "min".

## Hands-On Build: Simple Pendulum
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; gather parts the day before.*

### Parts
- String or sisal twine — ~1 m — any hardware shop or market
- Bob — a stone (50–200 g), a bunch of keys, or a large nut — must be heavy enough to keep the string taut
- Stand — a retort stand, or tie the string to a door frame, a hook in the ceiling, or a stick clamped to a desk edge
- Protractor card (optional) — paper with a protractor traced on it, to measure the release angle

### Workflow
1. Tie one end of the string firmly to the bob. **Checkpoint:** lift the bob by the string — the knot must hold without slipping.
2. Tie or clamp the other end to a fixed pivot point so the bob hangs ~50 cm below. **Checkpoint:** the bob hangs straight down without touching anything.
3. Pull the bob ~10 cm to one side (about 15°). Hold steady.
4. Release without pushing. Observe: where is it fastest? Where does it pause?
5. Count 10 complete swings. Note how the amplitude shrinks.
6. Sketch the three key positions with force arrows.

### Common Mistakes
- **String too short or too long** — aim for 40–80 cm; too short and it whips, too long and it drags on the floor.
- **Pushing instead of releasing** — a push adds extra energy; just open the hand.
- **Pivot not rigid** — if the top wobbles, energy leaks and the period changes. Clamp firmly.
- **Bob too light** — a feather or paper ball is dominated by air resistance; use something with mass.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Name the three main parts of a pendulum. (mirrors pre-test)
2. What force brings the pendulum back toward equilibrium? (mirrors pre-test)
3. At the bottom of the swing, which is greater — PE or KE? At the extreme, which is greater?
4. Sketch a pendulum at the left extreme. Draw the weight arrow (down) and the tension arrow (along the string). Which component of weight acts as the restoring force?
5. Challenge: why does the pendulum eventually stop if no one pushes it?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Bob, string, pivot.
2. Gravity — specifically the component of the bob's weight that acts along the arc toward equilibrium.
3. Bottom: KE is greater (maximum); extreme: PE is greater (maximum, KE ≈ 0).
4. Weight arrow points straight down from the bob. Tension arrow points up along the string toward the pivot. The restoring force is the component of weight perpendicular to the string, pointing toward equilibrium (to the right, when the bob is at the left extreme).
5. Energy is gradually lost to air resistance and friction at the pivot. With no energy input, the oscillation dies away — this is called damping (covered in Lesson 255).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A 100 g pendulum bob swings to a maximum height of 5 cm above the bottom. Calculate its PE at the top and its speed at the bottom (ignore air resistance, g = 10 N/kg).
   **Worked Answer:** PE at top = mgh = 0.100 × 10 × 0.05 = 0.05 J. At the bottom, all PE converts to KE: KE = ½mv² → v = √(2KE/m) = √(2 × 0.05 / 0.100) = √1.0 = 1.0 m/s. Speed at bottom ≈ 1.0 m/s.
2. **Q:** Sketch force arrows at both extremes and at the bottom. Where is the net force greatest, and where is it zero?
   **Worked Answer:** At the extremes: weight (down) and tension (along string). The net force is the restoring component = mg sin θ, pointing toward centre — maximum at the extremes. At the bottom: weight (down) and tension (up) are colinear; the net force is the centripetal force (tension exceeds weight), directed upward. The restoring force (tangential component) is zero at the bottom.
3. **Q:** A learner says the pendulum is fastest at the extremes because "it has just been released." Explain why this is wrong.
   **Worked Answer:** At the release point the bob is momentarily stationary (v = 0) — it has maximum PE and zero KE. It accelerates from rest, gaining speed all the way to the bottom where KE and speed are maximum. The extremes are the slowest points, not the fastest.

## Safety Notes
- Secure the pivot firmly — a falling bob can injure feet or damage the floor.
- Keep amplitude moderate; large swings can hit desks or learners nearby.
- Do not stand directly in the path of the swing.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Lesson 243 introduced oscillation, cycle, period, frequency, amplitude. This lesson opens up the anatomy and the force/energy picture.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on naming parts and identifying fast/slow points. Ages 14–17 should resolve forces into components and compute PE/KE.
- Local resource tips: any heavy object on a string — a stone, keys, a large nut. A door frame or a stick clamped to a desk edge is a fine pivot.
- This lesson is the foundation for the measurement and investigation lessons that follow (245–248). Ensure every pair has a working pendulum before the Explain phase.
