# Lesson 246: What Affects the Period? Test: Mass

**Unit:** 11 — Oscillations & Pendulums  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Set up a fair test that varies only the bob mass while keeping length and amplitude fixed.
2. Measure and compare periods for different masses using the 10-swing technique.
3. State the surprising result: mass does not affect the period of a pendulum.
4. Explain mass-independence qualitatively using both F = ma and the PE↔KE exchange.

## Materials
- Two bobs of clearly different mass: a stone (~200 g) and a smaller stone or bunch of keys (~50 g)
- String (~50 cm) and a stand (from Lesson 244)
- Stopwatch (phone)
- A simple balance or comparison method (a ruler balance, or compare by hand — "which feels heavier")
- Notebook and pencil

## Vocabulary
- **fair test** (jaribio la haki) — changing only one variable while keeping all others fixed
- **variable** (kigeuzi) — the thing you deliberately change in an experiment
- **control** (udhibiti) — the things you keep the same to make the test fair
- **mass-independence** — the property that changing mass does not change the period

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. If you put a heavier stone on a pendulum, do you think it will swing faster, slower, or the same? Predict before testing.
2. Name one thing you must keep the same when comparing a heavy bob with a light one.
3. What is the "10-swing method" and why do we use it?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Most learners predict "faster" (heavier = more force) or "slower" (heavier = harder to move). Both are reasonable guesses — the surprise is that the answer is "the same."
2. The length of the string and the amplitude (release angle). Only the mass should change.
3. Time 10 complete swings and divide by 10. It reduces the effect of reaction-time error by a factor of 10.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips. Note how many predict "faster" vs "slower."
2. **Engage (5 min)** — tell the story of Galileo watching a swinging chandelier in the Cathedral of Pisa (or a Kenyan equivalent: a hanging lantern in a mosque). He noticed that whether the swing was large or small, the time for each swing was the same. Galileo used his own pulse to time it. Ask: *did the weight matter? Let's find out.* Board the prediction tally.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs set up the fair test (see Hands-On Build). Each pair uses the same string length (~50 cm) and the same release angle (~10°) but swaps the bob between a heavy stone and a light one. They time 10 swings for each, three trials each, and record periods. Then they swap bobs with another pair to test a third mass. Results go on a shared class table on the board: mass | period.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. The periods are the same (within uncertainty). Why? Two ways to see it: (1) F = ma — a heavier bob has more gravitational force (weight = mg), but also more inertia (mass m); the two m's cancel, so acceleration is the same (g for all). (2) PE↔KE — a heavier bob has more PE at the top (mgh) but also more KE at the bottom (½mv²); the m cancels in the exchange, so the speed profile and timing are identical. Confront the misconception that "heavier means faster" — true for falling (air resistance aside, which is the same reason) but not for pendulum period.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *mass doesn't matter for a pendulum*. Homework: write one sentence explaining to a younger sibling why a heavy stone and a light stone take the same time to swing.

## Hands-On Build: Mass Fair Test
*Test whether bob mass changes the period, keeping everything else fixed.*

### Parts
- Two bobs of different mass — a large stone (~200 g) and a small stone or bunch of keys (~50 g)
- String — ~50 cm (same for both)
- Stand — same pivot point
- Stopwatch — phone
- Protractor card — to ensure same release angle

### Workflow
1. Hang the heavy bob at 50 cm. Pull to 10° and release. **Checkpoint:** the string length and angle are now fixed — do not change them.
2. Time 10 swings, three trials. Record each total and the period.
3. Swap to the light bob. Re-measure the string length — it must be the same 50 cm. **Checkpoint:** use the ruler to confirm identical length before proceeding.
4. Pull to the same 10° and release. Time 10 swings, three trials.
5. Record both periods on the class table. Compare.
6. If time allows, swap bobs with another pair for a third mass.

### Common Mistakes
- **Different string lengths** — when you swap bobs, the knot position shifts. Always re-measure from pivot to centre of bob.
- **Different amplitudes** — use the protractor card for both. A bigger swing for one bob ruins the comparison.
- **Bobs of similar mass** — the effect (none) is the same, but learners won't see it's a test. Use masses that feel clearly different.
- **Timing a single swing** — use the 10-swing method from Lesson 245.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Does a heavier pendulum bob swing faster, slower, or the same as a lighter one? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Name two things you must keep the same in this fair test. (mirrors pre-test)
3. Explain in one sentence why mass doesn't affect the period, using F = ma.
4. A 50 g bob and a 200 g bob on the same length string are both released at 10°. Predict the periods.
5. Challenge: explain the mass-independence using the PE↔KE energy exchange.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The same — mass does not affect the period of a simple pendulum.
2. String length and amplitude (release angle). The pivot point must also be fixed.
3. Weight = mg (restoring force proportional to m) and inertia = m. In F = ma, the m cancels: a = g sin θ, independent of mass.
4. Both have the same period. The values will be within experimental uncertainty of each other.
5. A heavier bob has more PE at the top (mgh) but also more mass to move (½mv² at the bottom). The m appears in both expressions and cancels — the speed and timing are identical regardless of mass.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Explain mass-independence using both F = ma and the PE↔KE exchange. Which explanation is more general?
   **Worked Answer:** F = ma: the restoring force is mg sin θ, so ma = mg sin θ → a = g sin θ. The mass cancels — acceleration depends only on g and angle, not on mass. PE↔KE: at the top, PE = mgh; at the bottom, KE = ½mv². Equating: mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh), again m cancels. The energy argument is more general because it works even when the path is curved (not a straight line), while F = ma along the arc requires resolving components. Both agree: mass cancels.
2. **Q:** A learner argues that a heavier bob should swing slower because "it's harder to lift." Why is this reasoning flawed for a pendulum?
   **Worked Answer:** It is true that a heavier bob has more PE at a given height (mgh is larger). But it also has more KE at the bottom (½mv² with the same m). The extra gravitational force (mg) exactly provides the extra push needed for the extra mass. The ratio force/mass = g is unchanged, so the acceleration and timing are the same. "Harder to lift" refers to the energy cost, not the motion timing — the pendulum doesn't lift the bob to a new height; it swings through a fixed arc.
3. **Q:** Design a test to confirm mass-independence using three masses that span a 10:1 ratio. What controls do you need?
   **Worked Answer:** Use bobs of ~20 g, ~100 g, and ~200 g (e.g., a small key, a stone, a large stone). Controls: same string length (measured from pivot to centre of bob for each), same release angle (protractor card), same pivot. Time 20 swings (for better precision) × 3 trials per mass. If all three periods agree within ±0.02 s, mass-independence is confirmed. The 10:1 ratio makes any real mass effect obvious; if the period were proportional to √m, the 200 g bob would be √10 ≈ 3.2× slower — easily detectable.

## Safety Notes
- Secure the stand; heavier bobs can cause more injury if the pivot fails.
- Do not use fragile bobs (glass, ceramic) that could shatter on a hard floor.
- Keep the swing area clear of other learners.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Lesson 245 taught the 10-swing timing technique. This lesson uses it in the first of three "what affects the period?" investigations.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the fair-test discipline and the surprise result. Ages 14–17 should explain the cancellation of mass using both F = ma and PE↔KE.
- Local resource tips: stones of different sizes, a bunch of keys vs a single key, a debe lid vs a small nut. The masses don't need to be measured precisely — they just need to feel clearly different.
- Tell the Galileo story briefly — it hooks the lesson and connects to the history of science. The next lesson (247) tests amplitude; the lesson after (248) tests length — the one that matters.
