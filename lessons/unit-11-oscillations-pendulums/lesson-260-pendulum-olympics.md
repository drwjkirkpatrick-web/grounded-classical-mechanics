# Lesson 260: Unit Review: Pendulum Olympics

**Unit:** 11 — Oscillations & Pendulums  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; G10-PHY-2.1  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Predict the period of a pendulum from its length using T = 2π√(L/g).
2. Tune a pendulum to a target period by adjusting the length.
3. Demonstrate coupled-pendulum resonance and explain the energy transfer.
4. Compare damping rates of different oscillators.
5. Synthesize all Unit 11 concepts in a comprehensive unit quiz.

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Write the formula for the period of a simple pendulum.
2. Does the period of a mass on a spring depend on mass?
3. What frequency causes resonance in an oscillator?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. T = 2π√(L/g), where L is the length (m) and g is gravitational acceleration (m/s²).
2. Yes — T = 2π√(m/k), so the spring period depends on mass (unlike the pendulum).
3. The driving frequency must match the oscillator's natural frequency for resonance to occur.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips. Divide the class into pairs or small groups. Each pair rotates through four stations (6 min each). After the stations, there is a unit quiz and a post-test. Board the station names and the rotation order.

2. **Station Rotation (24 min)** — pairs rotate through four stations (6 min each):

   **Station 1: Predict the Period**
   - *Setup:* A pendulum stand with a string and bob at an unknown length (covered by the station master).
   - *Task:* The pair measures the length (uncover it), predicts T using T = 2π√(L/g), then times 20 swings and computes the actual T. Score: |predicted − actual| < 0.02 s = gold, < 0.05 s = silver, < 0.1 s = bronze.

   **Station 2: Tune to Target**
   - *Setup:* A pendulum stand, string, bob, stopwatch. Target: T = 1.500 s.
   - *Task:* The pair calculates the needed length (L = g(T/2π)²), sets the string, times 20 swings, and adjusts until T = 1.500 ± 0.03 s. Score: within 0.03 s = gold, 0.05 s = silver, 0.10 s = bronze.

   **Station 3: Coupled Pendulum Demo**
   - *Setup:* Two pendulums on a shared string, one at 50 cm and one at 50 cm (matched). Plus a detuned pair (50 cm and 80 cm).
   - *Task:* The pair demonstrates resonance (drive one matched pendulum, the other responds) and detuning (drive one, the mismatched one stays small). They explain to the station master *why* the matched pair resonates and the mismatched pair doesn't. Score: correct explanation of both = gold, one = silver, partial = bronze.

   **Station 4: Damping Race**
   - *Setup:* Two rubber-strip oscillators — one with a card damper, one without. Both loaded with the same mass.
   - *Task:* The pair sets both oscillating with the same initial amplitude and counts cycles to settle. They record: cycles_no_damper, cycles_with_damper. They explain *why* the damped one settles faster (energy dissipation by air resistance). Score: correct count and explanation = gold, correct count only = silver, partial = bronze.

3. **Unit Quiz (10 min)** — administer the printed quiz (see below). Collect or peer-mark.

4. **Post-Test and Awards (4 min)** — administer the post-test (see below), then tally scores. Gold/silver/bronze for each station and overall. Announce the "Pendulum Olympics" champion pair.

5. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — homework: complete any unfinished quiz questions.

## Unit Quiz
*10 minutes, individual, closed book.*

**Part A: Multiple Choice (3 marks)**

1. The period of a simple pendulum depends on:
   (a) mass and length  (b) length only  (c) mass and amplitude  (d) amplitude only
2. The period of a mass on a spring depends on:
   (a) mass and spring constant  (b) spring constant only  (c) mass and amplitude  (d) amplitude only
3. Resonance occurs when the driving frequency:
   (a) is much higher than the natural frequency  (b) matches the natural frequency  (c) is much lower  (d) is zero

**Part B: Short Answer (4 marks)**

4. Write the formula for the period of a simple pendulum and state what each symbol means.
5. Explain one difference between a pendulum and a spring oscillator.
6. Why do water trucks have internal baffles?
7. Name one way engineers protect tall buildings from earthquake resonance.

**Part C: Computation (3 marks)**

8. A pendulum has L = 0.40 m. Using g = 9.8 m/s², calculate T.
9. A mass of 0.2 kg on a spring (k = 5 N/m) oscillates. Calculate T.
10. Challenge: design a pendulum that keeps time to within 30 s/day. What length accuracy is needed?

**Answer Key (Unit Quiz):**
1. (b) length only. (T = 2π√(L/g) — no mass.)
2. (a) mass and spring constant. (T = 2π√(m/k).)
3. (b) matches the natural frequency.
4. T = 2π√(L/g). T = period (s), L = length (m), g = gravitational acceleration (m/s²).
5. Accept any valid difference: (a) Pendulum period is mass-independent; spring period depends on mass. (b) Pendulum restoring force is gravity; spring restoring force is elastic (Hooke's law). (c) Pendulum "inertia" is geometric (L); spring "inertia" is mass (m).
6. Baffles break up the sloshing by forcing water through narrow openings, creating turbulence that dissipates kinetic energy as heat. This prevents the water from shifting the truck's centre of mass and causing a roll-over.
7. Accept any: (a) tuned mass dampers (a giant pendulum inside the building that absorbs sway energy), (b) base isolation (flexible bearings that decouple the building from ground motion), (c) cross-bracing (stiffens the building, shifting its natural frequency away from earthquake frequencies), (d) damping devices (hydraulic or friction dampers in the frame).
8. T = 2π√(0.40/9.8) = 2π√0.04082 = 2π × 0.2020 = 1.269 s ≈ 1.27 s.
9. T = 2π√(0.2/5) = 2π√0.04 = 2π × 0.2 = 1.257 s ≈ 1.26 s.
10. 30 s/day = 30/86400 = 3.47×10⁻⁴ fractional error. Since T ∝ √L: ΔL/L = 2 × ΔT/T = 2 × 3.47×10⁻⁴ = 6.94×10⁻⁴. For L ≈ 25 cm (1-second pendulum): ΔL = 0.25 × 6.94×10⁻⁴ = 1.74×10⁻⁴ m = 0.17 mm. The length must be accurate to ~0.2 mm — a demanding but achievable precision with careful calibration.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Championship:** Specify a pendulum to keep Nairobi time within 30 s/day. Show the design math.
   **Worked Answer:** Target: fractional error < 30/86400 = 3.47×10⁻⁴. Since T = 2π√(L/g) → T ∝ √L → ΔT/T = ½ ΔL/L. So ΔL/L = 2ΔT/T = 2 × 3.47×10⁻⁴ = 6.94×10⁻⁴. For a 1-second pendulum (L ≈ 0.248 m at g = 9.78 for Nairobi altitude ~1800 m): L_Nairobi = 9.78 × (1.000/6.2832)² = 9.78 × 0.02533 = 0.2477 m. Required length accuracy: ΔL = 0.2477 × 6.94×10⁻⁴ = 1.72×10⁻⁴ m = 0.17 mm. This is very demanding — a classroom ruler (1 mm) can't achieve it. The solution: calibrate against a reference clock over a long run (Lesson 252). Run for 30 minutes, measure the drift, correct the length. Iterate until the drift is under 30 s/day. The design: L = 24.77 cm (Nairobi g), 1-second period, small amplitude (≤10°), non-stretchy string, rigid stand, smooth pivot, calibrate over 30+ minutes. Temperature correction: if the string expands by 0.1% on a hot day, the clock loses ~43 s/day — so use a low-thermal-expansion material (invar or steel, not sisal or cotton).
2. **Q:** A coupled pendulum system has two matched 50-cm pendulums. The driver is released from 10°. Describe the energy transfer and the beat period.
   **Worked Answer:** When the driver is released, it oscillates and transfers energy through the shared support to the responder. The responder's amplitude grows while the driver's shrinks. Eventually, all the energy is in the responder and the driver stops — then the process reverses. This "beat" pattern has a period determined by the coupling strength: T_beat = 2/f_difference, where f_difference is the frequency splitting between the two normal modes (symmetric and antisymmetric). For weak coupling (a flexible string), the beat period can be 30–60 seconds. For the 50-cm pendulum: T = 2π√(0.50/9.8) = 1.418 s, f = 0.705 Hz. The beat period depends on how much the shared support moves — typically ~30 s for a string support. The energy oscillates back and forth between the two pendulums indefinitely (in the ideal, undamped case). With damping, the total energy decays and the beat pattern fades.
3. **Q:** Design a damped oscillator that settles in exactly 2 cycles. What parameters do you choose?
   **Worked Answer:** "Settles in 2 cycles" means the amplitude drops to <5% of initial in 2 cycles. For exponential decay: A(n) = A₀ × e^(-nδ), where δ is the logarithmic decrement per cycle. A(2)/A₀ = e^(-2δ) = 0.05 → -2δ = ln(0.05) = -3.0 → δ = 1.5. So the amplitude must drop by a factor of e^1.5 = 4.48 per cycle. For a spring oscillator: the damping ratio ζ relates to δ by δ ≈ 2πζ (for small damping) or more precisely δ = πζ/√(1-ζ²). For δ = 1.5: ζ ≈ δ/(2π) = 1.5/6.28 = 0.239. This is underdamped (ζ < 1) — the system oscillates but with significant damping. To achieve ζ = 0.24: choose a mass m, spring constant k, and damping coefficient c such that ζ = c/(2√(mk)). For example: m = 0.1 kg, k = 10 N/m → √(mk) = √1 = 1 → c = 2 × 1 × 0.24 = 0.48 N·s/m. A damper with c = 0.48 N·s/m (e.g., a piston in oil with the right viscosity and piston area) will settle the oscillator in 2 cycles. This is the design methodology used for vehicle shock absorbers — though real shock absorbers target 1–1.5 cycles, corresponding to ζ ≈ 0.3–0.4 (slightly more damped).

## Safety Notes
- All stations use small, low-energy pendulums — minimal risk.
- Keep the swing areas clear at each station.
- The coupled pendulum station can produce surprising amplitudes — stop the responder before it swings too wide.
- The damping race station uses rubber strips — do not overstretch.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: This is the culminating lesson of Unit 11. It synthesises all concepts: period measurement, length dependence, the T² vs L graph, g measurement, the pendulum clock project, resonance, damping, springs, the metronome, sloshing, and building oscillations.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the stations (hands-on prediction, tuning, resonance, damping) and the quiz's Parts A and B. Ages 14–17 should complete all parts including the championship design question and the beat-period analysis.
- The stations are the heart of the lesson. Each station should have a "station master" (a learner or the teacher) who scores the pair. If the class is large, run two sets of stations in parallel or reduce to 3 stations.
- The unit quiz is the summative assessment. It covers the core formulas (T = 2π√(L/g) and T = 2π√(m/k)), key concepts (resonance, damping, baffles, earthquake mitigation), and a computation question.
- The "Pendulum Olympics" framing is designed to make review fun and competitive. Award gold/silver/bronze stickers or marks. Display the champions' names.
- After this unit, learners have a complete picture of oscillations: the simple pendulum, the spring oscillator, the physical pendulum, resonance, damping, and real-world applications (clocks, metronomes, shock absorbers, baffles, buildings). The next unit can build on this with waves (Unit 12) or rotational motion (Unit 13).
