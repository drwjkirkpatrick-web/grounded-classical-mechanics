# Lesson 261: Mass in Motion: Introducing Momentum

**Unit:** 12 — Momentum & Collisions  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define momentum as p = mv and state the units (kg·m/s).
2. Compare the momentum of a fast light object (football) with a slow heavy object (rolling debe).
3. Explain that momentum is the "quantity of motion" — it takes into account both mass and speed.
4. Rank objects by momentum given mass and speed data.

## Materials
- A football and a heavy rolling object (a debe, a large stone, or a jerrycan of water)
- A notebook and pencil
- A whiteboard or chalkboard for class data

## Vocabulary
- **momentum** (mumbo) — the quantity of motion; p = mv, the product of mass and velocity
- **mass (m)** (uzito) — the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg)
- **velocity (v)** (mwendo) — speed in a specific direction, measured in metres per second (m/s)
- **quantity of motion** (kiwango cha harakati) — Newton's phrase for momentum; it captures both how heavy and how fast
- **kg·m/s** — the unit of momentum; kilogram metres per second

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Which is harder to stop: a slow lorry or a fast bicycle?
2. What two things does momentum combine?
3. Write the formula for momentum.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The slow lorry — it has much more mass. Even at low speed, the large mass gives it more momentum than a light bicycle at high speed. (Accept either answer if the reasoning is about mass vs speed, but the lorry's mass typically dominates.)
2. Mass and velocity (speed with direction).
3. p = mv, where p is momentum (kg·m/s), m is mass (kg), and v is velocity (m/s).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — hold up a football and a debe. Ask: *if I roll the debe slowly and kick the football fast, which is harder to stop?* Most learners will say the debe — it's heavier. But the football is faster. Momentum combines both. Board: **p = mv** — the "quantity of motion."
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs work through stopping stories (see Hands-On Build — a thought experiment, not physical). They compute momentum for several scenarios: a football (0.4 kg, 20 m/s), a rolling debe (12 kg, 1 m/s), a cyclist (80 kg, 5 m/s), a lorry (5000 kg, 10 m/s). They rank by momentum and discover that the lorry dominates despite the football's speed. They also explore: what happens to p if you double m? Double v? Both?
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. Momentum is p = mv. It is a vector — direction matters (a car going north and the same car going south have opposite momenta). The unit is kg·m/s. Doubling mass doubles momentum; doubling speed doubles momentum; doubling both quadruples it. The "stopping" intuition is correct: more momentum means harder to stop, which is exactly what Newton captured — force changes momentum over time (F = Δp/Δt, the seed of Lesson 263's impulse). Confront the misconception that "heavier always means more momentum" — a stationary lorry (v = 0) has zero momentum regardless of its mass. Momentum requires motion. Also address: "faster always means more momentum" — a fast feather (0.001 kg, 50 m/s → p = 0.05 kg·m/s) has less momentum than a slow lorry (5000 kg, 1 m/s → p = 5000 kg·m/s). Both mass and speed matter; neither alone tells the whole story.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *momentum = mass × velocity — both matter*. Homework: estimate the momentum of a boda-boda with a rider and passenger (estimate m and v).

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Write the formula for momentum and state the units. (mirrors pre-test)
2. Which has more momentum: a 0.4 kg football at 20 m/s or a 12 kg debe at 1 m/s? Show your working. (mirrors pre-test)
3. If you double the mass and keep the speed the same, what happens to the momentum?
4. A stationary lorry has a mass of 5000 kg. What is its momentum? Why?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. p = mv. Units: kg·m/s (kilogram metres per second).
2. Football: p = 0.4 × 20 = 8 kg·m/s. Debe: p = 12 × 1 = 12 kg·m/s. The debe has more momentum (12 > 8) despite being much slower — its mass is 30× larger.
3. Momentum doubles. p = mv, so if m → 2m with v unchanged: p → 2mv = 2p.
4. p = mv = 5000 × 0 = 0 kg·m/s. The lorry is stationary (v = 0), so it has zero momentum regardless of its mass. Momentum requires motion.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A 1500 kg car at 20 m/s and a 5000 kg lorry at 6 m/s. Which has more momentum? By what factor?
   **Worked Answer:** Car: p = 1500 × 20 = 30,000 kg·m/s. Lorry: p = 5000 × 6 = 30,000 kg·m/s. They have exactly the same momentum! Both have p = 30,000 kg·m/s. The lorry is 3.33× heavier but the car is 3.33× faster — the factors cancel. This shows that momentum combines mass and speed in a way that a heavy-slow object can match a light-fast one.
2. **Q:** A boda-boda (150 kg bike + 70 kg rider + 70 kg passenger = 290 kg) travels at 15 m/s. Compute p. If it hits a wall and stops in 0.1 s, estimate the force.
   **Worked Answer:** p = 290 × 15 = 4350 kg·m/s. Force: F = Δp/Δt = 4350/0.1 = 43,500 N. This is about 43 kN — an enormous force, equivalent to ~4.4 tonnes of weight. This is why overloaded bodas are so dangerous in a crash: the momentum (and thus the stopping force) scales with total mass.

## Safety Notes
- No physical activity in this lesson — it is conceptual.
- The rolling debe demonstration should be done by the teacher only, on a flat surface, with a clear stopping distance.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Units 4 (forces, Newton's laws) and 11 (oscillations) established F = ma and energy concepts. This lesson introduces momentum as a new quantity that links mass and velocity.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the idea that "both mass and speed matter" and compute simple momenta. Ages 14–17 should compare momenta, explore the doubling relationships, and connect to F = Δp/Δt (the impulse preview).
- Local resource tips: a football and a debe (cooking oil container) are everyday objects. The boda-boda context is locally relevant for the homework.
- This is the foundation lesson. Lesson 262 applies momentum to road safety (boda braking distance, overloaded pickups). Lesson 263 introduces impulse (F = Δp/Δt) as the link between momentum and force.
