# Lesson 265: Collisions That Bounce

**Unit:** 12 — Momentum & Collisions  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Set up marbles or bottle tops on a smooth board and observe elastic collisions.
2. Discover that equal-mass objects "swap speeds" in a head-on collision.
3. Explain that both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved in an elastic collision.
4. Predict the outcome of a collision given the masses and speeds.

## Materials
- Marbles (at least 4, same size) or bottle tops (smooth, same size)
- A smooth, flat board (a tabletop, a piece of plywood, or a large book)
- A ruler — to measure distances and set up the collision
- A stopwatch (phone) — optional
- Chalk or tape — to mark positions
- Notebook and pencil

## Vocabulary
- **elastic collision** (mgongano wa kurudisha) — a collision where objects bounce apart and both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved
- **conservation** (uhifadhi) — a quantity that does not change before and after the collision
- **swap speeds** (kubadilishana mwendo) — when two equal-mass objects collide head-on, the moving one stops and the stationary one moves at the same speed
- **line of centres** (mstari wa vituo) — the line connecting the centres of the two colliding objects; head-on collisions happen along this line

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. A marble hits another marble of the same size. What happens to the first marble?
2. In a bounce collision, is momentum conserved?
3. What does "elastic" mean in the context of collisions?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The first marble stops, and the second marble moves at the same speed (swap speeds). This is the hallmark of an equal-mass elastic collision.
2. Yes — momentum is always conserved in collisions (elastic and inelastic).
3. "Elastic" means the objects bounce apart and kinetic energy is conserved (no energy is lost to heat or deformation).

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — demonstrate: roll one marble into a stationary marble of the same size on the board. The first stops, the second rolls. Ask: *where did the speed go?* Board: **elastic collision** — the speed transferred completely.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs set up and test marble collisions (see Hands-On Build). They roll one marble into a stationary one (same size) and observe the swap. They try: (a) one moving, one stationary; (b) both moving toward each other; (c) a heavy marble into a light one (if different sizes are available). They mark the positions before and after with chalk or tape. Results go on a class table: scenario | before (speeds) | after (speeds) | pattern.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. In an elastic collision, both momentum (p = mv) and kinetic energy (KE = ½mv²) are conserved. For equal masses in a head-on collision, the only solution is a "speed swap": the moving marble stops and the stationary one takes the speed. For unequal masses, the outcome is different: a heavy marble hitting a light one continues forward (slower) while the light one shoots ahead fast. A light marble hitting a heavy one bounces back (reverses direction) while the heavy one creeps forward. The **line of centres** determines the direction: a head-on collision (along the line connecting centres) transfers speed along that line. A glancing collision (off-centre) sends the marbles off at angles. Confront the misconception that "the moving marble always stops" — this is only true for equal masses head-on. For unequal masses, the moving marble continues (heavy into light) or bounces back (light into heavy). The swap is a special case, not a general rule.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *elastic collisions swap speeds for equal masses — both momentum and energy survive*. Homework: try the marble collision at home and describe what happens.

## Hands-On Build: Marble Collision Board
*Set up marbles on a smooth board and observe elastic collisions.*

### Parts
- 4+ marbles (same size) or smooth bottle tops
- A smooth, flat board (tabletop, plywood, or a large book)
- A ruler — to launch with consistent speed and measure distances
- Chalk or tape — to mark positions
- Notebook and pencil

### Workflow
1. Place the board on a flat table. Mark a centre line with chalk. **Checkpoint:** the board is smooth and level — marbles roll straight.
2. Place one marble stationary on the centre line. **Checkpoint:** it stays still.
3. Roll a second marble (same size) along the line toward the stationary one. **Checkpoint:** the moving marble stops, the stationary one rolls away at the same speed — the swap!
4. Mark the positions: where the first marble stopped, where the second started, and where the second ended up. **Checkpoint:** the first marble stops at the collision point; the second travels a similar distance to what the first would have.
5. Try two marbles moving toward each other at similar speeds. Observe: they bounce apart, swapping speeds (each reverses direction). **Checkpoint:** both reverse — the swap works in both directions.
6. If different-size marbles are available: roll a large marble into a small stationary one. Observe: the large one continues (slower), the small one shoots ahead fast. Then try a small into a large: the small bounces back, the large creeps forward. **Checkpoint:** unequal masses do NOT swap — the pattern changes.

### Common Mistakes
- **Board not smooth** — rough surfaces slow the marbles unevenly. Use a smooth tabletop or a piece of plywood.
- **Marbles not aligned** — if the collision is off-centre (glancing), the marbles go off at angles instead of swapping. Align them carefully on the centre line.
- **Rolling too fast** — at very high speeds, the marbles may bounce off the board or the collision becomes inelastic (they chip). Roll gently.
- **Different-size marbles** — the swap only works for equal masses. If using different sizes, expect different outcomes (not a swap).
- **Not marking positions** — without marks, you can't compare before and after. Use chalk or tape.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. A marble hits a same-size stationary marble. What happens? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Is momentum conserved in an elastic collision? (mirrors pre-test)
3. What does "elastic" mean? (mirrors pre-test)
4. A heavy marble hits a light stationary marble. Does the heavy one stop? What happens instead?
5. Challenge: both momentum and KE are conserved in an elastic collision. For equal masses, show that the only solution is a speed swap.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The moving marble stops, and the stationary one rolls away at the same speed (the swap). This is the hallmark of an equal-mass elastic collision.
2. Yes — momentum is always conserved in collisions.
3. "Elastic" means the objects bounce apart and kinetic energy is conserved — no energy is lost to heat, deformation, or sound.
4. No — the heavy marble continues forward (slower than before) while the light marble shoots ahead fast. The swap only happens for equal masses.
5. Conservation of momentum: mv₁ = mv₁' + mv₂' → v₁ = v₁' + v₂'. Conservation of KE: ½mv₁² = ½mv₁'² + ½mv₂'² → v₁² = v₁'² + v₂'². From the first equation: v₂' = v₁ − v₁'. Substituting into the second: v₁² = v₁'² + (v₁ − v₁')² = v₁'² + v₁² − 2v₁v₁' + v₁'² → 0 = 2v₁'² − 2v₁v₁' → 0 = 2v₁'(v₁' − v₁) → v₁' = 0 or v₁' = v₁. If v₁' = v₁, then v₂' = 0 (no collision — they missed). So v₁' = 0 and v₂' = v₁ — the swap! The only physical solution is the speed swap.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Equal masses, head-on: show that the only solution to momentum + KE conservation is the speed swap.
   **Worked Answer:** See Post-Test Q5. Conservation of momentum: v₁ = v₁' + v₂'. Conservation of KE: v₁² = v₁'² + v₂'². Substituting v₂' = v₁ − v₁' into the KE equation: v₁² = v₁'² + (v₁ − v₁')² → v₁² = v₁'² + v₁² − 2v₁v₁' + v₁'² → 0 = 2v₁'² − 2v₁v₁' → v₁'(v₁' − v₁) = 0. Solutions: v₁' = 0 (the swap: v₂' = v₁) or v₁' = v₁ (no collision: v₂' = 0). The only physical solution is the swap.
2. **Q:** A 0.2 kg marble at 3 m/s hits a 0.1 kg stationary marble elastically. Compute the final speeds.
   **Worked Answer:** Conservation of momentum: 0.2 × 3 = 0.2v₁' + 0.1v₂' → 0.6 = 0.2v₁' + 0.1v₂'. Conservation of KE: ½ × 0.2 × 9 = ½ × 0.2 × v₁'² + ½ × 0.1 × v₂'² → 0.9 = 0.1v₁'² + 0.05v₂'². From momentum: v₂' = (0.6 − 0.2v₁')/0.1 = 6 − 2v₁'. Substituting into KE: 0.9 = 0.1v₁'² + 0.05(6 − 2v₁')² = 0.1v₁'² + 0.05(36 − 24v₁' + 4v₁'²) = 0.1v₁'² + 1.8 − 1.2v₁' + 0.2v₁'² = 0.3v₁'² − 1.2v₁' + 1.8. So: 0.3v₁'² − 1.2v₁' + 0.9 = 0 → v₁'² − 4v₁' + 3 = 0 → (v₁' − 1)(v₁' − 3) = 0. Solutions: v₁' = 3 (no collision) or v₁' = 1 m/s. Then v₂' = 6 − 2(1) = 4 m/s. The heavy marble slows to 1 m/s, the light one shoots ahead at 4 m/s. Check: momentum 0.2(1) + 0.1(4) = 0.6 ✓. KE: 0.1(1) + 0.05(16) = 0.1 + 0.8 = 0.9 ✓.
3. **Q:** A 0.1 kg marble at 4 m/s hits a 0.3 kg stationary marble elastically. Compute the final speeds and describe the outcome.
   **Worked Answer:** Momentum: 0.1 × 4 = 0.1v₁' + 0.3v₂' → 0.4 = 0.1v₁' + 0.3v₂'. KE: ½(0.1)(16) = ½(0.1)v₁'² + ½(0.3)v₂'² → 0.8 = 0.05v₁'² + 0.15v₂'². From momentum: v₁' = (0.4 − 0.3v₂')/0.1 = 4 − 3v₂'. Substituting: 0.8 = 0.05(4 − 3v₂')² + 0.15v₂'² = 0.05(16 − 24v₂' + 9v₂'²) + 0.15v₂'² = 0.8 − 1.2v₂' + 0.45v₂'² + 0.15v₂'² = 0.8 − 1.2v₂' + 0.6v₂'². So: 0.6v₂'² − 1.2v₂' = 0 → v₂'(0.6v₂' − 1.2) = 0 → v₂' = 0 (no collision) or v₂' = 2 m/s. Then v₁' = 4 − 3(2) = −2 m/s. The light marble bounces back at 2 m/s (negative = reversed), the heavy marble creeps forward at 2 m/s. Check: momentum 0.1(−2) + 0.3(2) = −0.2 + 0.6 = 0.4 ✓. KE: 0.05(4) + 0.15(4) = 0.2 + 0.6 = 0.8 ✓. The light marble reverses — it couldn't push the heavy one forward without bouncing back itself.

## Safety Notes
- Marbles can roll off the table — keep the area clear and pick up stray marbles.
- Do not flick marbles hard — they can fly off the board and hit someone.
- Bottle tops are a safer alternative to marbles (less likely to fly off).
- Do not use glass marbles on a hard floor — they can chip or shatter.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Lessons 261–264 introduced momentum and impulse. This lesson is the first of two on collision types: elastic (bounce) and inelastic (stick, Lesson 266).
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the marble swap demo and the idea that bounce collisions conserve both momentum and energy. Ages 14–17 should solve the elastic collision equations for equal and unequal masses.
- Local resource tips: marbles are widely available. Bottle tops (from soda bottles) are a free alternative — flatten them slightly for smooth rolling. A tabletop or a piece of plywood is the board.
- The equal-mass swap is the most striking demo. The unequal-mass cases (heavy into light, light into heavy) are best shown with different-size marbles if available.
