# Lesson 267: Conservation of Momentum

**Unit:** 12 — Momentum & Collisions  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Set up two trolleys (or bottles on rollers) with a compressed spring or balloon between them.
2. Release the spring and measure the speeds of both trolleys.
3. Verify that the momenta are equal and opposite: m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ (from rest, total p = 0).
4. Explain that internal forces cancel (Newton's third law) so total momentum is conserved.

## Materials
- Two trolleys, bottles, or blocks on rollers (cylindrical pens or pencils)
- A spring or a balloon (compressed between the trolleys)
- A smooth board or tabletop
- A ruler — to measure distances
- A stopwatch (phone)
- A balance or scale (if available) — to measure masses
- Tape or chalk — to mark positions
- Notebook and pencil

## Vocabulary
- **conservation of momentum** (uhifadhi wa mumbo) — total momentum before = total momentum after (no external force)
- **internal forces** (nguvu za ndani) — forces between the objects in the system; they cancel in pairs (Newton's third law)
- **equal and opposite** (sawa na kinyume) — the two momenta are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction
- **system** (mfumo) — the collection of objects being considered; here, the two trolleys

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Two trolleys sit at rest with a spring between them. The spring is released. What is the total momentum before release?
2. After release, the trolleys move apart. Are their momenta equal or different?
3. Why is momentum conserved — what law guarantees it?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Zero — both trolleys are at rest, so p_total = m₁(0) + m₂(0) = 0.
2. Equal and opposite — m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ (in opposite directions). The total momentum is still zero: p_total = m₁v₁ + m₂(−v₂) = 0.
3. Newton's third law — the spring pushes on both trolleys with equal and opposite forces. The internal forces cancel, so the total momentum doesn't change. No external force acts on the system.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — demonstrate: two trolleys with a compressed spring between them, at rest on the board. Release the spring — they fly apart. Ask: *if they started at rest, what is the total momentum?* (Zero.) *After release, is it still zero?* (Yes — the momenta are equal and opposite.) Board: **conservation of momentum**.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs set up and test the spring-apart trolleys (see Hands-On Build). They place two trolleys (or bottles on rollers) with a spring or balloon between them. They release the spring and measure the distances each trolley travels in the same time. Since d = vt, the ratio of distances gives the ratio of speeds: d₁/d₂ = v₁/v₂. They check: m₁d₁ ≈ m₂d₂ (equivalent to m₁v₁ = m₂v₂). They try equal masses (distances should be equal) and unequal masses (the lighter one travels further). Results go on a class table: m₁ | m₂ | d₁ | d₂ | m₁d₁ | m₂d₂ | ratio.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. Conservation of momentum: total p before = total p after (when no external force acts). For two trolleys starting at rest: 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ → m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂. The momenta are equal and opposite. The ratio of speeds: v₁/v₂ = m₂/m₁ — the lighter trolley moves faster. This is guaranteed by Newton's third law: the spring pushes on both trolleys with equal and opposite forces (F₁ = −F₂). Since the forces act for the same time, the impulses are equal and opposite (J₁ = −J₂), so the momentum changes are equal and opposite (Δp₁ = −Δp₂). Starting from rest, the final momenta are equal and opposite. Confront the misconception that "the spring gives more momentum to the lighter trolley" — it gives the SAME momentum to both (equal and opposite). The lighter one just moves faster because p = mv and a smaller m requires a larger v for the same p. Also address: "if the forces are equal, why don't the trolleys move the same distance?" — the forces are equal, but the lighter trolley accelerates more (a = F/m), so it reaches a higher speed and travels further in the same time.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *internal forces cancel — momentum is conserved*. Homework: a cannon fires a 5 kg ball at 100 m/s. The cannon (1000 kg) recoils. Find the recoil speed.

## Hands-On Build: Spring-Apart Trolleys
*Two trolleys with a spring between them — verify equal and opposite momenta.*

### Parts
- Two trolleys, bottles, or blocks on rollers (pens or pencils under each)
- A spring or a balloon (partially inflated, tied, and compressed between the trolleys)
- A smooth board or tabletop
- A ruler — to measure distances
- A stopwatch (phone) — to time the travel
- A balance or scale (if available) — to measure masses
- Tape or chalk — to mark positions
- Notebook and pencil

### Workflow
1. Place the two trolleys on the board with the spring or balloon compressed between them. Both at rest. **Checkpoint:** the trolleys are stationary; the spring is held by a string or your hand (to be cut or released).
2. Mark the starting positions. Release the spring (cut the string or let go). **Checkpoint:** both trolleys move apart in opposite directions.
3. Let them travel for 1–2 seconds (or until they stop). Mark the final positions. **Checkpoint:** the lighter trolley travels further than the heavier one (if masses are unequal).
4. Measure d₁ and d₂ (distances from start to finish). Since both travel for the same time: v₁/v₂ = d₁/d₂. **Checkpoint:** if masses are equal, d₁ = d₂. If m₁ < m₂, then d₁ > d₂.
5. Check momentum conservation: m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → m₁d₁ = m₂d₂ (since t is the same). Compute m₁d₁ and m₂d₂. **Checkpoint:** they should be approximately equal (within experimental error from friction).
6. Try different mass ratios: add a weight to one trolley and repeat. The ratio d₁/d₂ should equal m₂/m₁.

### Common Mistakes
- **Friction** — rollers reduce friction but don't eliminate it. The trolleys slow down, so the measured distances are slightly less than the ideal. Accept ~10–20% error.
- **Spring not centred** — if the spring is off-centre, the trolleys may go at angles instead of straight back. Centre the spring.
- **Not releasing simultaneously** — if one trolley is held while the other is released, the spring only pushes one. Release both at the same time (cut a string).
- **Board not level** — if the board slopes, gravity adds an external force and momentum is not conserved. Level the board.
- **Measuring total distance, not displacement** — if the trolley wobbles, measure the straight-line distance from start to finish, not the path length.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Two trolleys at rest with a spring. Total momentum before release? (mirrors pre-test)
2. After release: are the momenta equal or different? (mirrors pre-test)
3. What law guarantees momentum conservation? (mirrors pre-test)
4. A 1 kg trolley and a 2 kg trolley spring apart from rest. The 1 kg trolley moves at 3 m/s. Find the speed of the 2 kg trolley.
5. Challenge: a 1000 kg cannon fires a 5 kg ball at 100 m/s. Find the cannon's recoil speed.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Zero — both at rest, so p_total = 0.
2. Equal and opposite — m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ in opposite directions. The total momentum is still zero.
3. Newton's third law — the spring pushes on both with equal and opposite forces. Internal forces cancel, so total momentum is conserved.
4. m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → 1 × 3 = 2 × v₂ → v₂ = 1.5 m/s. The 2 kg trolley moves at 1.5 m/s in the opposite direction.
5. 0 = m_ball × v_ball + m_cannon × v_cannon → 0 = 5 × 100 + 1000 × v_cannon → v_cannon = −500/1000 = −0.5 m/s. The cannon recoils at 0.5 m/s (opposite to the ball). Check: p_ball = 500 kg·m/s, p_cannon = −500 kg·m/s. Total = 0 ✓.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Two trolleys (1 kg and 2 kg) spring apart from rest. The 1 kg moves at 3 m/s. Find the 2 kg speed and verify momentum conservation.
   **Worked Answer:** From rest, total p = 0. So m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → 1 × 3 = 2 × v₂ → v₂ = 1.5 m/s. Check: p₁ = 1 × 3 = 3 kg·m/s (right). p₂ = 2 × 1.5 = 3 kg·m/s (left). Total: 3 − 3 = 0 ✓. The lighter trolley moves twice as fast (3 vs 1.5 m/s) because it has half the mass. The momenta are equal (3 kg·m/s each).
2. **Q:** A 1000 kg cannon fires a 5 kg ball at 100 m/s. Find the recoil speed. Then compute the KE of the ball and the cannon. Where does the energy come from?
   **Worked Answer:** Recoil: v_cannon = −(5 × 100)/1000 = −0.5 m/s. KE_ball = ½ × 5 × 100² = 25,000 J. KE_cannon = ½ × 1000 × 0.5² = 125 J. Total KE = 25,125 J. The energy comes from the chemical energy of the gunpowder (explosive → thermal → mechanical). Note: the ball gets 99.5% of the KE (25,000/25,125) and the cannon gets 0.5% (125/25,125). This is because KE = p²/(2m) — for equal and opposite momenta, the lighter object gets more KE (smaller m → larger p²/2m). The cannon's KE is tiny because its mass is large. This is why a rifle bullet is deadly but the rifle's recoil is just a push.
3. **Q:** Two ice skaters (50 kg and 70 kg) push apart from rest. The 50 kg skater moves at 3 m/s. Find the 70 kg skater's speed and the total KE. Where does the KE come from?
   **Worked Answer:** m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → 50 × 3 = 70 × v₂ → v₂ = 150/70 = 2.14 m/s. KE₁ = ½ × 50 × 9 = 225 J. KE₂ = ½ × 70 × 2.14² = ½ × 70 × 4.59 = 160.5 J. Total KE = 385.5 J. The energy comes from the skaters' muscles (chemical → mechanical). They did work pushing against each other. The 50 kg skater gets more KE (225 J vs 160.5 J) because, for equal momenta, the lighter object has more KE (KE = p²/2m, smaller m → larger KE). Ratio: 225/160.5 = 1.40 = 70/50 = m₂/m₁ — the KE ratio is the inverse of the mass ratio.

## Safety Notes
- Trolleys on rollers can move unexpectedly — keep the area clear.
- The spring or balloon can release suddenly — do not lean over it.
- Do not compress the balloon too hard — it may pop.
- Rollers (pens) can roll off the table — keep them centred.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Lessons 265–266 covered elastic and inelastic collisions. This lesson formalises the conservation law that underlies both: total momentum is conserved when no external force acts.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the spring-apart demo and the idea that momenta are equal and opposite. Ages 14–17 should derive the speed ratios, compute KE, and explain why the lighter object gets more KE.
- Local resource tips: two bottles on pens are a good substitute for trolleys. A balloon (partially inflated and tied) is a good spring. A string to hold the balloon compressed, then cut to release, ensures simultaneous release.
- The cannon-and-ball problem is a classic application. The KE distribution (light gets more) is a surprising result that deepens understanding of the p = mv and KE = p²/2m relationship.
