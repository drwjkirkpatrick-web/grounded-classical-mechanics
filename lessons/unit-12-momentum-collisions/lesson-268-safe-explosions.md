# Lesson 268: Safe 'Explosions': Springs and Balloons

**Unit:** 12 — Momentum & Collisions  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a balloon-powered bottle boat and observe it propel forward when air rushes out the back.
2. Explain that internal forces (the air pushing the balloon, the balloon pushing the air) cancel — only the escaping air's momentum matters.
3. Verify that the total momentum of the system remains zero: p_boat = −p_air.
4. Relate the balloon release to rocket propulsion (Lesson 270).

## Materials
- A plastic water bottle (500 ml or 1 L)
- A balloon
- A straw (to direct the airflow)
- Tape or rubber bands
- A shallow tray of water (or a smooth tabletop)
- A stopwatch (phone) — optional
- A ruler — to measure distance
- Notebook and pencil

## Vocabulary
- **internal forces** (nguvu za ndani) — forces between parts of the same system; they cancel in pairs and cannot change the total momentum
- **balloon propulsion** (mwendo wa baluni) — air rushes out the back, the boat moves forward; equal and opposite momentum
- **system momentum** (mumbo wa mfumo) — the total momentum of all parts; conserved when no external force acts
- **nozzle** (nusu ya hewa) — the straw or opening that directs the airflow backward

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. A balloon is inflated and released (no straw). Which way does it fly?
2. If a balloon is taped to a bottle boat and released, which way does the boat move?
3. Why does the boat move — what pushes it?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The balloon flies erratically — the air rushes out in all directions and the balloon has no controlled direction. The net momentum is still zero, but the path is chaotic.
2. The boat moves forward (opposite to the air rushing out the back through the straw).
3. The air rushing out the back has momentum. By conservation of momentum (total p = 0), the boat must have equal and opposite momentum. The boat is pushed forward by the reaction to the escaping air — not by the air pushing on the outside world, but by the internal pressure forces.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — inflate a balloon, hold the neck, and release it. It flies erratically. Then tape the balloon to a bottle boat with a straw nozzle, place it in the water tray, and release. The boat shoots forward. Ask: *what pushed the boat?* Board: **internal forces** — the air goes back, the boat goes forward. Total momentum stays zero.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs build and test the balloon bottle boat (see Hands-On Build). They inflate the balloon, release the boat in the water tray, and measure the distance travelled. They try: (a) different balloon sizes (more air = more momentum = more distance), (b) different straw diameters (narrow straw = faster air but less mass flow; wide straw = slower air but more mass flow), (c) adding weight to the boat (more mass = less speed for the same momentum). Results go on a class table: balloon size | straw diameter | boat mass | distance | speed estimate.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. The balloon release is a "safe explosion" — internal forces push the air out the back and the boat forward. The total momentum is zero before (everything at rest) and zero after: p_boat + p_air = 0 → p_boat = −p_air. The boat's momentum equals the air's momentum in magnitude but opposite in direction. The boat's speed depends on the ratio: v_boat = m_air × v_air / m_boat. More air (bigger balloon) or faster air (narrow straw) gives more momentum to the boat. A heavier boat moves slower for the same momentum. Confront the misconception that "the air pushes on the water or the atmosphere behind the boat" — it does not. The propulsion is entirely internal: the balloon pushes the air out, and the air (by Newton's third law) pushes the balloon (and thus the boat) forward. The water only provides low friction (so the boat can move). If you did the same experiment in space (no water, no air outside), the boat would still move forward — the escaping air provides the reaction. This is exactly how a rocket works (Lesson 270).
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *internal forces redistribute momentum — the total stays zero*. Homework: explain in one sentence why a balloon rocket works in space where there is no air to push against.

## Hands-On Build: Balloon Bottle Boat
*A bottle boat propelled by a balloon — demonstrate internal-force propulsion.*

### Parts
- A plastic water bottle (500 ml or 1 L)
- A balloon
- A straw (a rigid plastic straw is best)
- Tape or rubber bands — to attach the balloon to the straw and the straw to the boat
- A shallow tray of water (a basin or a large baking tray)
- A ruler — to measure distance
- A stopwatch (phone) — optional
- Notebook and pencil

### Workflow
1. Cut the bottle in half lengthwise (or use the whole bottle as a hull). The teacher should do the cutting. **Checkpoint:** the hull floats in the water tray.
2. Insert the straw into the balloon neck and seal with tape or a rubber band. **Checkpoint:** no air leaks around the joint.
3. Tape the straw to the top of the bottle hull, pointing horizontally out the back. **Checkpoint:** the straw points straight back — the boat will go straight.
4. Inflate the balloon through the straw. Pinch the straw to hold the air. **Checkpoint:** the balloon is fully inflated.
5. Place the boat in the water tray. Release the straw. **Checkpoint:** the boat shoots forward as air rushes out the back.
6. Measure the distance travelled. Try different balloon sizes and straw diameters. Record the results. **Checkpoint:** bigger balloon = more distance; heavier boat = less distance.

### Common Mistakes
- **Straw pointing sideways or upward** — the boat will spin or dive. The straw must point horizontally straight back.
- **Air leak at the balloon-straw joint** — if air escapes around the joint, the thrust is lost. Seal tightly with tape.
- **Boat too heavy** — a full 1 L bottle may be too heavy for a small balloon. Use a 500 ml bottle or remove excess material.
- **Water tray too small** — the boat needs room to travel. Use a large basin or tray.
- **Not pinching the straw before release** — the balloon deflates before you place the boat in the water. Pinch firmly, place the boat, then release.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. A balloon is released with no straw. Which way does it fly? (mirrors pre-test)
2. A balloon boat with a straw nozzle is released. Which way does the boat move? (mirrors pre-test)
3. What pushes the boat forward? (mirrors pre-test)
4. The boat (0.2 kg) moves at 0.5 m/s. The escaping air has a mass of 0.005 kg. What is the air's speed?
5. Challenge: explain why this propulsion works in space (no external air), using conservation of momentum.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Erratically — the air rushes out in uncontrolled directions, so the balloon has no stable direction. The net momentum is still zero, but the path is chaotic.
2. Forward — the air rushes out the back (through the straw), and the boat moves forward by conservation of momentum.
3. The air rushing out the back has momentum. By conservation (total p = 0), the boat has equal and opposite momentum. The reaction to the escaping air pushes the boat forward — it is an internal force, not a push on the outside world.
4. p_boat = p_air → m_boat × v_boat = m_air × v_air → 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.005 × v_air → v_air = 0.10/0.005 = 20 m/s. The air exits at 20 m/s.
5. In space, there is no external air to push against. The propulsion is entirely internal: the balloon pushes the air out the back, and by Newton's third law, the air pushes the balloon (and the rocket) forward. The total momentum of the system (rocket + expelled air) is conserved at zero. No external medium is needed — this is why rockets work in the vacuum of space.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A balloon boat (0.2 kg) moves at 0.5 m/s after releasing 0.005 kg of air. Find the air's exit speed and the total KE. Where does the KE come from?
   **Worked Answer:** v_air = m_boat × v_boat / m_air = 0.2 × 0.5 / 0.005 = 20 m/s. KE_boat = ½ × 0.2 × 0.25 = 0.025 J. KE_air = ½ × 0.005 × 400 = 1.0 J. Total KE = 1.025 J. The energy comes from the elastic potential energy stored in the stretched balloon rubber. As the balloon deflates, the rubber contracts, doing work on the air (pushing it out) and on the boat (pushing it forward). The total KE (1.025 J) is less than the stored elastic energy (some is lost to friction with the straw, turbulence in the air, and water drag).
2. **Q:** If you double the balloon size (double the air mass, same exit speed), what happens to the boat's speed?
   **Worked Answer:** Momentum conservation: m_boat × v_boat = m_air × v_air. If m_air doubles and v_air stays the same: m_boat × v_boat' = 2 × m_air × v_air = 2 × (m_boat × v_boat). So v_boat' = 2 × v_boat. The boat's speed doubles. More air at the same exit speed gives more momentum to the boat. However, in practice, a bigger balloon may have a lower exit speed (lower pressure), so the effect is less than a perfect doubling.
3. **Q:** A balloon boat (0.3 kg) releases 0.01 kg of air at 15 m/s. Find the boat's speed and compare the KE of the boat vs the air. Which gets more KE?
   **Worked Answer:** v_boat = m_air × v_air / m_boat = 0.01 × 15 / 0.3 = 0.5 m/s. KE_boat = ½ × 0.3 × 0.25 = 0.0375 J. KE_air = ½ × 0.01 × 225 = 1.125 J. The air gets 30× more KE than the boat (1.125/0.0375 = 30). This is the same pattern as the cannon and ball (Lesson 267): for equal and opposite momenta, the lighter object (air) gets more KE (KE = p²/2m, smaller m → larger KE). The boat is 30× heavier than the air, so it gets 1/30 the KE. The boat moves slowly but steadily; the air moves fast but carries little mass.

## Safety Notes
- Do not over-inflate the balloon — it may pop. Inflate by mouth to a comfortable size.
- The teacher should cut the bottle — sharp plastic edges can cut.
- Do not launch the boat toward people.
- Clean up water spills — wet floors are slippery.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: Lesson 267 introduced conservation of momentum with spring-apart trolleys. This lesson applies the same principle to a continuous "explosion" (the balloon release).
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the balloon boat demo and the idea that "air goes back, boat goes forward." Ages 14–17 should compute momenta, KE, and explain why the air gets more KE.
- Local resource tips: a plastic water bottle, a balloon, and a straw are all readily available. A basin or large tray is the water container. If no water tray is available, the boat can be tested on a smooth tabletop with bottle caps as rollers.
- This lesson is a direct preview of Lesson 270 (Rockets Are Momentum Machines). The balloon boat is a water-rocket analogue — the same physics, just with air instead of water as the propellant.
