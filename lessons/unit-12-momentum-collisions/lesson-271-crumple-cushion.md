# Lesson 271: Crumple Zones and Cushions

**Unit:** 12 — Momentum & Collisions  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; UP-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Demonstrate that an egg on foam survives a drop while an egg on a hard floor breaks.
2. Explain that the foam extends the collision time, reducing the force (F = Δp/t).
3. Describe how car crumple zones extend collision time to protect passengers.
4. Explain the anatomy of a helmet: hard shell + foam liner = extended collision time + spread force.

## Materials
- Two raw eggs (or ripe tomatoes)
- A piece of foam or a cushion (at least 5 cm thick)
- A hard surface (a desk or the floor)
- A ruler — to measure drop height
- A notebook and pencil
- A helmet (if available) — to inspect the foam liner

## Vocabulary
- **crumple zone** (eneo la kunyofya) — the front or rear section of a car designed to deform and extend collision time
- **collision time** (muda wa mgongano) — the duration of the impact; longer t means less F for the same Δp
- **foam liner** (fomu ya ndani) — the soft inner layer of a helmet that compresses on impact, extending the collision time
- **extended time** (kuongeza muda) — the key principle: more time, less force (F = Δp/t)

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. An egg drops on a hard floor and on foam. Which survives?
2. Why does the foam save the egg?
3. What is a crumple zone and what does it do?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The egg on the foam survives — the foam extends the collision time, reducing the force. The egg on the hard floor breaks — the collision time is very short, so the force is very large.
2. The foam compresses, extending the collision time (t). Since F = Δp/t, a longer t means a smaller F. The egg's momentum change (Δp) is the same in both cases, but the foam reduces the force enough that the shell doesn't break.
3. A crumple zone is the front or rear section of a car designed to deform (crumple) in a crash. By crumpling, it extends the collision time, reducing the force on the passengers (F = Δp/t). Without a crumple zone, the car stops almost instantly (very short t, very large F), which is lethal.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — the egg drop: drop an egg on the hard floor (it breaks) and an egg on the foam (it survives). Ask: *why does foam save the egg?* Board: **F = Δp/t** — the foam extends t, reducing F.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs test the egg-on-foam vs egg-on-floor experiment (see Hands-On Build). They drop eggs (or tomatoes) from the same height onto foam and hard surfaces. They estimate the collision times: hard floor ~0.001 s, foam ~0.05 s — a factor of 50. They compute the force ratio: F_hard/F_foam = t_foam/t_hard = 0.05/0.001 = 50. The foam reduces the force by 50×. They also inspect a helmet (if available): the hard shell spreads the force over a larger area, and the foam liner compresses to extend the collision time. Results go on a class table: surface | collision time (estimate) | force ratio | egg survives?
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. The crumple zone is the car's "foam." In a crash, the front of the car crumples (deforms) over a distance of ~0.5–1 m, extending the collision time from ~0.002 s (hitting a wall with a rigid car) to ~0.1 s (crumpling). This reduces the force on the passengers by a factor of ~50 (0.1/0.002 = 50). The helmet works the same way: the hard shell spreads the force over the whole head (reducing pressure = force/area), and the foam liner compresses (~1–2 cm), extending the collision time from ~0.001 s to ~0.01 s — a factor of 10 in force reduction. Confront the misconception that "the crumple zone absorbs energy" — it does absorb energy (the deformation converts KE to heat), but the PRIMARY purpose is to extend the collision time, which reduces the PEAK force. The energy is the same in both cases (the car still stops); the crumple zone makes the stop take longer, so the force is gentler. Also address: "a stiffer car is safer" — no, a stiffer car stops faster (shorter t), so the force is larger. A crumple zone is DESIGNED to be weak — it sacrifices the car's front to save the passengers. The car is written off; the passengers walk away.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *crumple zones and cushions extend time, reduce force*. Homework: look at a car and identify the crumple zone (the front section between the bumper and the cabin).

## Hands-On Build: Egg on Foam vs Egg on Floor
*Compare the force of a hard stop vs a cushioned stop.*

### Parts
- Two raw eggs (or ripe tomatoes)
- A piece of foam or a cushion (at least 5 cm thick)
- A hard surface (a desk or the floor)
- A ruler — to measure drop height
- A notebook and pencil
- A helmet (if available) — to inspect the foam liner

### Workflow
1. Hold an egg at a height of ~50 cm above the hard floor. Drop it. **Checkpoint:** the egg breaks — the collision time is very short (~0.001 s), so the force is very large.
2. Hold a second egg at the same height above the foam. Drop it. **Checkpoint:** the egg survives — the foam compresses, extending the collision time (~0.05 s), so the force is much smaller.
3. Estimate the collision times: hard floor ~0.001 s (1 ms), foam ~0.05 s (50 ms). Compute the force ratio: F_hard/F_foam = t_foam/t_hard = 0.05/0.001 = 50. **Checkpoint:** the foam reduces the force by ~50×.
4. Try different foam thicknesses: 1 cm, 5 cm, 10 cm. Thicker foam = longer collision time = more protection. **Checkpoint:** thicker foam = more survival.
5. If a helmet is available: remove the foam liner and inspect it. Squeeze it — it compresses. This is the same as the foam cushion: it extends the collision time. The hard shell spreads the force over the whole head. **Checkpoint:** the helmet has two safety features: hard shell (spread force) + foam liner (extend time).

### Common Mistakes
- **Using an unripe tomato** — a hard tomato survives both drops, giving a false result. Use a ripe tomato or an egg.
- **Foam too thin** — a 1 cm piece of foam may not be enough to save the egg. Use at least 5 cm.
- **Dropping from too high** — even with foam, a very high drop may break the egg (the momentum is too large). Start at 50 cm.
- **Not cleaning up** — egg on the floor is slippery. Clean up immediately.
- **Confusing energy and force** — the energy is the same in both cases (same drop height → same KE). The force differs because the time differs. The foam doesn't reduce the energy; it reduces the force by extending the time.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Egg on foam vs egg on floor: which survives? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Why does the foam save the egg? (mirrors pre-test)
3. What is a crumple zone? (mirrors pre-test)
4. A car crashes at 15 m/s. Without a crumple zone, the collision time is 0.002 s. With a crumple zone, it is 0.1 s. By what factor is the force reduced?
5. Challenge: a helmet's foam liner compresses 1.5 cm. The head hits the ground at 5 m/s. Estimate the collision time and the force on a 5 kg head.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The egg on the foam survives — the foam extends the collision time, reducing the force.
2. The foam compresses, extending the collision time (t). Since F = Δp/t, a longer t means a smaller F. The egg's momentum change is the same, but the foam reduces the force enough that the shell doesn't break.
3. A crumple zone is the front or rear section of a car designed to deform in a crash. By crumpling, it extends the collision time, reducing the force on the passengers (F = Δp/t).
4. F_without/F_with = t_with/t_without = 0.1/0.002 = 50. The crumple zone reduces the force by 50×.
5. The head decelerates over 1.5 cm (0.015 m). Using v² = 2ad: a = v²/(2d) = 25/(2 × 0.015) = 833 m/s². Collision time: t = v/a = 5/833 = 0.006 s. Force: F = ma = 5 × 833 = 4167 N. Without the helmet (collision over ~0.5 mm = 0.0005 m): a = 25/(2 × 0.0005) = 25,000 m/s². F = 5 × 25,000 = 125,000 N. The helmet reduces the force by 125,000/4167 = 30×. This is why helmets save lives — a 30× force reduction is the difference between a headache and a skull fracture.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A car (1500 kg) crashes at 15 m/s. Without a crumple zone: t = 0.002 s. With: t = 0.1 s. Compute the force in each case and the reduction factor.
   **Worked Answer:** Δp = mv = 1500 × 15 = 22,500 kg·m/s. Without: F = 22,500/0.002 = 11,250,000 N — lethal. With: F = 22,500/0.1 = 225,000 N. Reduction factor: 11,250,000/225,000 = 50. The crumple zone reduces the force by 50×. Even with the crumple zone, 225,000 N is still about 15× the car's weight — but the seatbelt and airbag spread this over the body and extend the time further, bringing the force on any single body part to a survivable level.
2. **Q:** A cyclist (5 kg head) hits the ground at 5 m/s. Without a helmet: the skull stops in 0.5 mm. With a helmet: the foam compresses 1.5 cm. Compute the force in each case.
   **Worked Answer:** Without: d = 0.0005 m. a = v²/(2d) = 25/0.001 = 25,000 m/s². F = 5 × 25,000 = 125,000 N. With: d = 0.015 m. a = 25/0.03 = 833 m/s². F = 5 × 833 = 4167 N. Reduction: 125,000/4167 = 30×. The helmet reduces the force from 125 kN (certain skull fracture) to 4.2 kN (survivable — about 85× the head's weight, which the neck and body can absorb). The foam liner is the critical component — without it, the hard shell alone would spread the force but not extend the time, giving only a small reduction.
3. **Q:** A boda-boda passenger (60 kg) crashes at 12 m/s. The boda has no crumple zone (t = 0.003 s). A car with a crumple zone (t = 0.1 s) crashes at the same speed. Compare the forces on the passenger in each case.
   **Worked Answer:** Boda: Δp = 60 × 12 = 720 kg·m/s. F = 720/0.003 = 240,000 N. Car: F = 720/0.1 = 7200 N. Ratio: 240,000/7200 = 33. The boda passenger experiences 33× more force than the car passenger at the same speed. This is why boda-boda crashes are so much more lethal than car crashes at the same speed — the boda has no crumple zone, no seatbelt, and no airbag. The passenger takes the full force of the sudden stop. This is the physics behind the boda safety campaign (Lesson 272).

## Safety Notes
- Use ripe tomatoes instead of eggs to avoid mess. Eggs are more dramatic but harder to clean.
- Do not drop from above head height — the splatter area is large.
- Clean up spills immediately — wet floors are slippery.
- Do not dismantle a helmet — inspect the foam liner through the vents or by removing a removable liner.
- The teacher should demonstrate the egg drop; learners can observe from a safe distance.
