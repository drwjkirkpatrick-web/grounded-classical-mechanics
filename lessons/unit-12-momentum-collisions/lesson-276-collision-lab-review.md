# Lesson 276: Unit Review: Collision Lab Day

**Unit:** 12 — Momentum & Collisions  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Rotate through four review stations: marble swaps, clay sticks, cradle demo, and impulse egg test.
2. Answer quiz questions covering all 16 lessons of Unit 12.
3. Self-assess their understanding of momentum, impulse, collisions, and safety applications.
4. Identify areas for further study before the end-of-term assessment.

## Materials
- Station 1 (Marble Swaps): marbles or bottle tops, a smooth board, a ruler
- Station 2 (Clay Sticks): two clay lumps, a smooth board, a ruler
- Station 3 (Cradle Demo): the bottle-top Newton's cradle from Lesson 274 (or a commercial one)
- Station 4 (Impulse Egg Test): a ripe tomato, a soft cloth, a hard surface, a ruler
- A printed unit quiz (10 questions — see below)
- A printed self-assessment checklist (see below)
- Pencils and paper
- A timer (the teacher's phone or a clock)

## Vocabulary
- **momentum** (mumbo) — p = mv; the quantity of motion
- **impulse** (msukumo) — J = Ft = Δp; force × time = change in momentum
- **elastic collision** (mgongano wa kurudisha) — bounce; both p and KE conserved
- **inelastic collision** (mgongano wa kushikamana) — stick; p conserved, KE lost
- **conservation of momentum** (uhifadhi wa mumbo) — total p before = total p after (no external force)
- **crumple zone** (eneo la kunyofya) — extends collision time, reduces force (F = Δp/t)

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Write the formula for momentum.
2. Write the impulse formula.
3. State the conservation of momentum law.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. p = mv, where p is momentum (kg·m/s), m is mass (kg), and v is velocity (m/s).
2. J = Ft = Δp, where J is impulse (N·s), F is force (N), t is time (s), and Δp is the change in momentum (kg·m/s).
3. The total momentum of a system is conserved (constant) when no external force acts on it: p_before = p_after.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Organise (3 min)** — divide the class into four groups. Each group starts at one station and rotates every 6 minutes — 24 minutes total for four stations. After the stations, there is a unit quiz and a post-test. Board the station rotation order.
3. **Station Rotation (24 min)** — four stations, 6 min each:

   **Station 1: Marble Swaps (Elastic Collisions)**
   - Roll a marble into a stationary marble of the same size. Observe the swap (the first stops, the second moves).
   - Try a glancing hit. Observe the 90° rule (equal masses, glancing elastic → final directions at 90°).
   - Record: what happens in a head-on hit? What happens in a glancing hit?

   **Station 2: Clay Sticks (Inelastic Collisions)**
   - Roll a clay lump into a stationary clay lump. They stick and move together.
   - Measure the masses and estimate v_final from momentum: v_final = m₁v₁/(m₁ + m₂).
   - Record: is momentum conserved? Is KE conserved? Where does the KE go?

   **Station 3: Cradle Demo (Newton's Cradle)**
   - Pull one ball and release — one swings out. Pull two — two swing out.
   - Observe the pattern: N in → N out (equal masses, elastic).
   - Record: why does the pattern preserve the count? (Both p and KE are conserved.)

   **Station 4: Impulse Egg Test (Force = Δp/t)**
   - Drop a tomato on a hard surface (it breaks) and on a soft cloth (it survives).
   - Estimate the collision times: hard ~0.001 s, soft ~0.05 s. Force ratio: 50×.
   - Record: why does the soft cloth save the tomato? (Extended time → reduced force.)

4. **Unit Quiz (10 min)** — administer the printed quiz (see below). Collect or peer-mark.
5. **Post-Test and Self-Assessment (3 min)** — administer the post-test and the self-assessment checklist. Learners rate their confidence on each topic (1–5) and note areas for further study.

## Unit Quiz
*10 minutes, individual. 10 questions, 1 mark each.*

1. Write the formula for momentum and state the units. (Lesson 261)
2. A loaded boda (290 kg) at 15 m/s: compute its momentum. (Lesson 262)
3. Write the impulse formula. (Lesson 263)
4. In an elastic collision, what two quantities are conserved? (Lesson 265)
5. In an inelastic collision, what is conserved and what is lost? (Lesson 266)
6. Two trolleys (1 kg and 2 kg) spring apart from rest. The 1 kg moves at 3 m/s. Find the 2 kg speed. (Lesson 267)
7. A 1000 kg cannon fires a 5 kg ball at 100 m/s. Find the recoil speed. (Lesson 267)
8. A car at 15 m/s crashes. Without a crumple zone: t = 0.002 s. With: t = 0.1 s. By what factor is the force reduced? (Lesson 271)
9. In a Newton's cradle, two balls swing in. How many swing out? (Lesson 274)
10. A water jet (ρ = 1000 kg/m³) exits a 1 cm² nozzle at 5 m/s. Find the reaction force (F = ρAv²). (Lesson 275)

**Quiz Answer Key:**
1. p = mv. Units: kg·m/s.
2. p = 290 × 15 = 4350 kg·m/s.
3. J = Ft = Δp. Impulse = force × time = change in momentum.
4. Momentum (p = mv) and kinetic energy (KE = ½mv²).
5. Momentum is conserved. Kinetic energy is lost (converted to heat, sound, and deformation).
6. m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → 1 × 3 = 2 × v₂ → v₂ = 1.5 m/s.
7. 0 = 5 × 100 + 1000 × v → v = −500/1000 = −0.5 m/s. The cannon recoils at 0.5 m/s.
8. F_without/F_with = t_with/t_without = 0.1/0.002 = 50. The force is reduced by 50×.
9. Two balls swing out — both momentum and KE are conserved (N in → N out for equal masses).
10. A = 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m². F = ρAv² = 1000 × 10⁻⁴ × 25 = 2.5 N.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A 0.2 kg marble at 3 m/s hits a 0.1 kg stationary marble elastically. Find both final speeds.
   **Worked Answer:** Momentum: 0.2 × 3 = 0.2v₁' + 0.1v₂' → 0.6 = 0.2v₁' + 0.1v₂'. KE: ½(0.2)(9) = ½(0.2)v₁'² + ½(0.1)v₂'² → 0.9 = 0.1v₁'² + 0.05v₂'². From momentum: v₂' = 6 − 2v₁'. Substituting: 0.9 = 0.1v₁'² + 0.05(6 − 2v₁')² = 0.1v₁'² + 0.05(36 − 24v₁' + 4v₁'²) = 0.3v₁'² − 1.2v₁' + 1.8. So: 0.3v₁'² − 1.2v₁' + 0.9 = 0 → v₁'² − 4v₁' + 3 = 0 → (v₁' − 1)(v₁' − 3) = 0. v₁' = 1 m/s (physical) or 3 (no collision). v₂' = 6 − 2(1) = 4 m/s. Check: p = 0.2(1) + 0.1(4) = 0.6 ✓. KE = 0.1(1) + 0.05(16) = 0.9 ✓.
2. **Q:** A 1000 kg car at 15 m/s rear-ends a 2000 kg stationary lorry. They lock bumpers. Find v_final and the KE lost.
   **Worked Answer:** v_final = m₁v₁/(m₁ + m₂) = 1000 × 15/3000 = 5 m/s. KE_before = ½ × 1000 × 225 = 112,500 J. KE_after = ½ × 3000 × 25 = 37,500 J. KE_lost = 75,000 J (67%). The energy goes into crumpling the car's front end, heat, and sound.
3. **Q:** A boda-boda passenger (60 kg) crashes at 12 m/s without a helmet (t = 0.001 s). Compare with a car passenger (same mass, same speed) with a crumple zone (t = 0.1 s). By what factor is the boda force larger?
   **Worked Answer:** Boda: F = 60 × 12/0.001 = 720,000 N. Car: F = 60 × 12/0.1 = 7200 N. Ratio: 720,000/7200 = 100. The boda passenger experiences 100× more force than the car passenger at the same speed. (Note: in Lesson 271, the ratio was 33× with t = 0.003 s for the boda. The ratio depends on the collision time assumption — without a helmet, t is even shorter, so the ratio is even larger.) This is why boda-boda crashes are so lethal — the passenger takes the full force with no crumple zone, no seatbelt, and no airbag.

## Safety Notes
- Station 4 (Impulse Egg Test): use ripe tomatoes, not eggs, to avoid mess. Clean up immediately.
- Station 1 (Marble Swaps): do not flick marbles hard — they can fly off the board.
- Station 3 (Cradle Demo): do not pull the balls too far — they may tangle.
- All stations: keep the area clear and clean up between rotations.
- The teacher should supervise all stations and manage the timer.
