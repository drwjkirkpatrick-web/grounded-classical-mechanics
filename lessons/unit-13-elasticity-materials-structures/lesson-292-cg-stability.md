# Lesson 292: Centre of Gravity and Stability

**Unit:** 13 — Elasticity, Materials & Structures  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.5  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Define centre of gravity (CG) as the point where all the weight of an object acts.
2. Demonstrate that a wide base and a low CG make an object stable, while a narrow base and a high CG make it unstable.
3. Explain the topple rule: an object topples when the line of action of its weight falls outside its base.
4. Compute the tilt angle needed to topple a rectangular object given its width and CG height.

## Materials
- An empty plastic bottle and a full plastic bottle (same size)
- A sufuria (or a wide, low pot)
- A wooden plank or a textbook (as a tilting ramp)
- A ruler or protractor (to measure tilt angle)
- A rectangular box (e.g., a shoe box or a tea box) with a small weight that can be moved inside (to change the CG)
- A piece of string with a small weight (a plumb line)
- Notebook and pencil

## Vocabulary
- **centre of gravity (CG)** (kituo cha uzito) — the point where all the weight of an object acts; the balance point
- **base** (msingi) — the area of contact between an object and the ground; a wider base is more stable
- **line of action** (mstari wa nguvu) — the vertical line from the CG downward; if it falls inside the base, the object is stable; if outside, it topples
- **topple** (kuanguka) — when the line of action of the weight falls outside the base and the object tips over
- **plumb line** (uzito wa kamba) — a string with a weight, used to find the vertical line from the CG

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Which is more stable: an empty bottle (light, same height) or a full bottle (heavy, same height)?
2. Which is more stable: a tall narrow bottle or a short wide sufuria?
3. What makes an object topple?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The full bottle is more stable — it has a lower CG (the water fills the bottom, so the weight is concentrated lower) and more mass at the base. The empty bottle has a higher CG (the plastic is uniformly distributed, so the CG is at the geometric centre, which is higher relative to the base).
2. The short wide sufuria is more stable — it has a wide base (large contact area) and a low CG (the food/weight is near the ground). The tall narrow bottle has a small base and a high CG.
3. An object topples when the line of action of its weight (the vertical line from the CG) falls outside its base. When the CG is no longer above the base, gravity pulls the object over.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — stand an empty bottle and a full bottle side by side on a plank. Slowly tilt the plank. The empty bottle topples first (high CG, less mass at the bottom). The full bottle topples at a steeper angle (low CG, more mass at the bottom). Ask: *why does the full bottle survive a steeper tilt?* Board: **CG + base = stability. Topple when the line of action falls outside the base.**
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs test stability (see Hands-On Build). They tilt bottles, sufurias, and boxes on a plank. They move a weight inside a box (high vs low) and measure the topple angle. They use a plumb line to visualise the line of action. They compute the topple angle from the box dimensions and CG height. Results go on a class table: object | base width | CG height | topple angle (measured) | topple angle (computed).
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate. The centre of gravity (CG) is the point where all the weight acts — the balance point. An object is stable when the line of action (the vertical line from the CG) falls inside the base. It topples when the line of action falls outside the base. A wide base and a low CG make the object more stable: the wide base means the line of action has to move farther to fall outside, and the low CG means the line of action is shorter and moves less for a given tilt. The topple angle for a rectangular object is θ = arctan(base/2 ÷ CG_height) — the angle at which the line of action passes through the edge of the base. Confront the misconception that "heavier means more stable" — a heavy object with a high CG (e.g., a tall stack of bricks) is less stable than a light object with a low CG (e.g., a flat tray). It's the CG height and base width, not the total weight, that determine stability. Also address: "a wider base always helps" — yes, but only if the CG stays low. A wide base with a very high CG (e.g., a wide table with a tall stack on top) is still unstable. Both factors matter: wide base AND low CG.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *wide base, low CG = stable; topple when the line of action falls outside the base*. Homework: find the most stable and the least stable object in your home and explain why.

## Hands-On Build: Tilt Test and Topple Angle
*Test the stability of bottles, sufurias, and boxes on a tilting plank.*

### Parts
- An empty plastic bottle and a full plastic bottle (same size)
- A sufuria (or a wide, low pot)
- A wooden plank or a textbook (as a tilting ramp)
- A ruler or protractor (to measure tilt angle)
- A rectangular box (e.g., a shoe box) with a small weight (a stone or a lump of clay) that can be moved inside
- A piece of string with a small weight (a plumb line)
- Notebook and pencil

### Workflow
1. Place the empty bottle on the plank. Slowly tilt the plank until the bottle topples. Measure the tilt angle with a protractor or by measuring the rise of one end (tan θ = rise/length). **Checkpoint:** the topple angle is recorded.
2. Repeat with the full bottle. Measure the topple angle. **Checkpoint:** the full bottle topples at a steeper angle — lower CG, more mass at the base.
3. Place the sufuria on the plank. Tilt until it topples. Measure the angle. **Checkpoint:** the sufuria topples at a much steeper angle — wide base, low CG.
4. Place the weight at the bottom of the box. Tilt the plank until the box topples. Measure the angle. **Checkpoint:** the box with the weight at the bottom (low CG) topples at a steeper angle.
5. Move the weight to the top of the box (tape it inside the lid). Tilt again. Measure the topple angle. **Checkpoint:** the box with the weight at the top (high CG) topples at a much shallower angle.
6. Use the plumb line to visualise the line of action. Hold the plumb line from the CG (the middle of the box, or higher if the weight is at the top). Tilt the box until the plumb line falls outside the base — that's the topple point. **Checkpoint:** the plumb line confirms the topple rule — the object falls when the line of action exits the base.
7. Compute the topple angle: θ = arctan(half_base_width ÷ CG_height). For the box with the weight at the bottom: half_base = 5 cm, CG_height = 3 cm → θ = arctan(5/3) = 59°. With the weight at the top: CG_height = 12 cm → θ = arctan(5/12) = 23°. Compare with the measured angles. **Checkpoint:** the computed and measured angles should be close (within a few degrees).

### Common Mistakes
- **Tilting too fast** — the object may slide before it topples. Tilt slowly and use a non-slip surface (rubber or cloth) on the plank.
- **Not measuring the angle** — estimate by measuring the rise of one end of the plank: tan θ = rise/length. Use a ruler.
- **CG in the wrong place** — for a uniform box, the CG is at the geometric centre. But if a weight is placed inside, the CG shifts toward the weight. Estimate the CG by balancing the box on a finger (the balance point is the CG).
- **Confusing mass with stability** — a heavy object with a high CG is less stable than a light object with a low CG. It's the CG height and base width, not the mass, that determine stability.
- **Not using the plumb line** — the plumb line makes the line of action visible. Without it, the topple rule is abstract. Always use the plumb line at least once.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Which is more stable: an empty bottle or a full bottle? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Which is more stable: a tall narrow bottle or a short wide sufuria? (mirrors pre-test)
3. What makes an object topple? (mirrors pre-test)
4. A box is 40 cm wide with a CG 50 cm above the ground. At what tilt angle does it topple?
5. Challenge: how would you make a tall bookshelf more stable without bolting it to the wall?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The full bottle — it has a lower CG (water fills the bottom) and more mass at the base.
2. The short wide sufuria — wide base, low CG.
3. The object topples when the line of action of its weight (the vertical line from the CG) falls outside its base.
4. θ = arctan(half_base ÷ CG_height) = arctan(20/50) = arctan(0.4) = 21.8°. The box topples at about 22°.
5. Lower the CG: put the heaviest books on the bottom shelves and the lightest on top. Widen the base: extend the feet outward (add brackets or a wider base plate). Both changes increase the topple angle: lowering the CG reduces the CG_height in the formula, and widening the base increases the half_base. Even without bolting, a bookshelf with heavy books at the bottom and a wider base is much more stable.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A box is 40 cm wide and 60 cm tall. The CG is at the geometric centre (30 cm above the ground). Find the topple angle. Then a 5 kg weight is placed on top of the box (shifting the CG to 40 cm above the ground). Find the new topple angle.
   **Worked Answer:** Without the weight: half_base = 20 cm, CG_height = 30 cm. θ = arctan(20/30) = arctan(0.667) = 33.7°. With the weight on top: the CG shifts upward. If the box is 2 kg and the weight is 5 kg, the new CG is: CG = (2 × 30 + 5 × 60)/(2 + 5) = (60 + 300)/7 = 360/7 = 51.4 cm. New topple angle: θ = arctan(20/51.4) = arctan(0.389) = 21.3°. The topple angle drops from 33.7° to 21.3° — the box is much less stable with the weight on top. This is why you should never put heavy items on top of a tall stack.
2. **Q:** A boda-boda carries a passenger. The combined CG of bike + rider is 80 cm above the ground, and the tyre contact width is 30 cm. Find the topple angle. Then the passenger loads a sack of maize on the carrier (raising the CG to 100 cm). Find the new topple angle.
   **Worked Answer:** Without the sack: half_base = 15 cm, CG_height = 80 cm. θ = arctan(15/80) = arctan(0.1875) = 10.6°. With the sack: CG_height = 100 cm. θ = arctan(15/100) = arctan(0.15) = 8.5°. The topple angle drops from 10.6° to 8.5° — a 20% reduction. The boda is significantly less stable with the high load. This is why loading heavy items on the carrier (high CG) is dangerous — the topple angle is small, and a sharp turn or a bump can exceed it. Keeping the load low (between the rider's feet, on the tank) keeps the CG down and the topple angle larger.
3. **Q:** A water tank stand is 3 m tall with a 1 m square base. The tank (full) weighs 8000 N and the CG of the tank + stand is 2.5 m above the ground. What wind force on the tank would topple the stand?
   **Worked Answer:** The topple condition: the overturning moment (wind force × height) equals the restoring moment (weight × half_base). F_wind × CG_height = W × half_base. F_wind × 2.5 = 8000 × 0.5. F_wind = 4000/2.5 = 1600 N. A wind force of 1600 N on the tank would topple the stand. The wind pressure on the tank (assume the tank face is 2 m × 1 m = 2 m²): P = F/A = 1600/2 = 800 Pa. A wind of 800 Pa corresponds to a wind speed of about v = √(2P/ρ) = √(2 × 800/1.2) = √1333 = 36.5 m/s (about 130 km/h — a severe storm). A typical storm wind (20 m/s) would exert about 1.2 × 20²/2 = 240 Pa, giving a force of 240 × 2 = 480 N — well below the 1600 N topple threshold. So the stand is stable in normal storms but would topple in a severe storm. This is why tall tank stands need guy ropes or a wider base.

## Safety Notes
- Do not use glass bottles — they can break when they topple. Use plastic bottles.
- Tilt the plank slowly — sudden tilting can cause the object to slide or bounce.
- Keep feet away from the tilting plank — objects can roll off.
- Do not stand on the plank — it is a tilting surface, not a platform.
- The plumb line weight is small — do not swing it near people's eyes.
