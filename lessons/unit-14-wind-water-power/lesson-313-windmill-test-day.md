# Lesson 313: Windmill Project V: Test Day

**Unit:** 14 — Wind & Water Power Projects  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6; G10-PHY-1.1  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** windmill

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Run a completed windmill outdoors and measure the volume of water lifted per minute at a 1 m head.
2. Record test data (wind speed, time, water volume) in a log sheet with clear units.
3. Identify at least one failure mode observed during the test and propose a first fix.
4. Calculate the hydraulic power output using P = ρgQh and compare it with the estimated wind power input.

## Materials
- Completed windmill with pump from Lesson 312
- Measuring jar or graduated sufuria (marked in millilitres)
- Stopwatch (phone or wristwatch)
- Cup anemometer from Lesson 303 (or a Beaufort estimate card)
- Log sheets (pre-printed table: trial | wind speed | time | water lifted | notes)
- Camera or phone for photographs
- Chalk or string to mark a 2 m safety perimeter on the ground
- A bucket of water as the source supply
- Tape measure (to confirm 1 m head from pump to discharge)

## Vocabulary
- **head** (kichwa cha maji) — the vertical height the water is lifted, in metres
- **flow rate** (kiwango cha mtiririko) — volume of water moved per unit time, in litres per second
- **hydraulic power** (nguvu ya maji) — useful power delivered to the water, P = ρgQh
- **wind power** (nguvu ya upepo) — power available in the wind passing through the rotor, ½ρAv³
- **cut-in speed** (kasi ya kuanza) — the lowest wind speed at which the rotor begins to turn and pump
- **safety perimeter** (mpaka wa usalama) — a marked zone around the windmill that no one enters while blades spin

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Your windmill lifts 3 litres of water in 60 seconds to a height of 1 m. What is the flow rate in litres per second?
2. Why must no one stand in front of or beside the spinning blades during the test?
3. Name two things you should record on the log sheet for each trial besides the water volume.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Flow rate = 3 litres ÷ 60 s = 0.05 L/s.
2. Spinning blades can cause serious injury if a person is struck; a broken blade or detached hub can fly outward. The safety perimeter keeps everyone clear.
3. Wind speed (from the anemometer or Beaufort estimate) and the time taken (from the stopwatch). Also acceptable: head height, number of blade rotations, notes on failures.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)
1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — collect slips.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — show the completed windmill and ask: *today we find out if it actually works. What do we need to measure to know?* Board the learners' suggestions and group them into: wind speed, water lifted, time. Introduce the 2 m safety perimeter — mark it with chalk or string before going outside.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — teams carry the windmill to the test site. They set the pump at a 1 m head, fill the source bucket, and mark the safety perimeter. The teacher signals the start. One learner holds the anemometer upwind, another runs the stopwatch, a third reads the measuring jar. They run at least two trials of 60 seconds each, logging wind speed, time, and water volume. Photographs are taken of the running windmill. Any failure (stall, slip, leak) is noted on the log.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — back in class, teams write their best result on the board: wind speed, water lifted in 60 s, flow rate. Compare across teams. Confront the misconception that *more water always means a better windmill* — if one team had a higher wind speed, the comparison is unfair; wind speed must be stated alongside the output. Introduce the hydraulic power formula P = ρgQh and work one example on the board using a team's data.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *measure wind and water together; the windmill does work by lifting water*. Homework: write one sentence describing the biggest failure you saw today and one fix you would try next lesson.

## Hands-On Build: Windmill Test Run
*Build time fits inside the Explore block.*

### Parts
- Completed windmill with pump assembly
- Source bucket of water
- Measuring jar or graduated sufuria
- Stopwatch
- Cup anemometer or Beaufort card
- Chalk or string for the safety perimeter
- Tape measure
- Log sheets and pencil
- Camera or phone

### Workflow
1. Set the windmill on level ground with the pump intake in the source bucket and the discharge at a measured 1 m head. **Checkpoint:** the tower is vertical and the pump rod moves freely through its full stroke.
2. Mark a 2 m radius perimeter around the rotor with chalk or string. All learners and observers stand outside this line. **Checkpoint:** no one is within the perimeter; the upwind approach is clear for wind.
3. Measure the wind speed with the cup anemometer held at rotor height, upwind of the windmill. Record the value. **Checkpoint:** if wind is below the cut-in speed, wait for a gust or note "no rotation" and retry.
4. On the teacher's signal, start the stopwatch and let the windmill run for 60 seconds. One learner reads the measuring jar at the discharge. **Checkpoint:** the stopwatch and the jar reading start and stop together.
5. Record on the log sheet: trial number, wind speed, time, water volume, and any notes (stall, slip, leak, vibration). **Checkpoint:** every field is filled in with units before leaving the site.
6. Run a second trial. If the wind changes between trials, record the new wind speed. **Checkpoint:** both trials are logged with their own wind speed.
7. Photograph the running windmill from outside the perimeter. Pack up the source water and tools.

### Common Mistakes
- **Standing inside the perimeter** — even the builder must step out once the rotor is spinning. A detached blade can travel several metres.
- **Reading the jar at an angle** — read the water level at eye level to avoid parallax error in the volume.
- **Not recording wind speed** — a result without wind speed is meaningless for comparison. Always measure wind before each trial.
- **Head not actually 1 m** — measure from the water surface in the source bucket to the discharge point, not from the ground.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Your windmill lifts 4 litres of water in 60 seconds at a 1 m head. What is the flow rate in L/s? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Why is the safety perimeter marked at 2 m before the test begins?
3. Name two things you must record on the log sheet for each trial besides the water volume.
4. A team reports 6 L in 60 s but did not record the wind speed. Why is this result hard to interpret?
5. Challenge: the wind speed dropped between trial 1 and trial 2, and the water output fell. Is the windmill worse, or was the wind weaker? How would you find out?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Flow rate = 4 L ÷ 60 s = 0.067 L/s (≈ 0.07 L/s).
2. To keep all people clear of the spinning blades in case a blade detaches or the hub slips; 2 m gives a safety margin beyond the rotor radius.
3. Wind speed (anemometer or Beaufort estimate) and time (stopwatch). Also acceptable: head height, trial number, notes on failures.
4. Without wind speed you cannot tell whether the output is good or poor for the available wind — a low output in strong wind indicates a problem, while the same output in weak wind may be the best the windmill can do.
5. The wind was likely weaker, not the windmill worse. To find out, compare the output-to-wind-speed ratio for both trials (water per minute ÷ wind speed). If the ratio is similar, the windmill performed consistently and the wind caused the difference.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Your windmill lifts 4.2 L of water in 60 s at a 1 m head. The rotor has a swept area of 0.8 m² and the wind speed is 4 m/s. Take ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², ρ_air = 1.2 kg/m³. Calculate the hydraulic power output and the wind power input. What is the efficiency?
   **Worked Answer:** Flow rate Q = 4.2 L / 60 s = 0.07 L/s = 7 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s. Hydraulic power P_out = ρgQh = 1000 × 9.81 × 7 × 10⁻⁵ × 1 = 0.69 W. Wind power P_in = ½ρAv³ = 0.5 × 1.2 × 0.8 × 4³ = 0.5 × 1.2 × 0.8 × 64 = 30.7 W. Efficiency = P_out / P_in = 0.69 / 30.7 = 0.022 = 2.2%.
2. **Q:** The theoretical maximum efficiency of a wind turbine (the Betz limit) is 59.3%. Your windmill achieved 2.2%. List three reasons your real efficiency is so far below the Betz limit.
   **Worked Answer:** (a) The rotor is a simple drag-type or poorly shaped rotor, not an aerodynamic lift-type blade, so it extracts far less than the Betz fraction. (b) Mechanical losses: bearing friction, shaft misalignment, and the pump rod sliding in a poorly lubricated guide. (c) The pump itself is inefficient — leakage past the piston, air in the cylinder, and dead volume reduce the effective flow. (d) The swept area may be less than measured if blades stall or feather at the test wind speed. The honest efficiency includes all of these losses, while the Betz limit applies only to an ideal rotor.
3. **Q:** If you doubled the head to 2 m (keeping the same windmill and wind speed), the flow rate would drop because the pump must do more work per stroke. Explain why, and predict whether the hydraulic power output would rise, fall, or stay the same.
   **Worked Answer:** Doubling the head doubles the work per litre lifted, so the pump must push harder each stroke. At the same wind power, the rotor slows down and the flow rate Q drops — roughly by half if the pump is the limiting factor, because P_out = ρgQh and the available mechanical power is roughly constant. So if h doubles and Q halves, P_out = ρg(½Q)(2h) = ρgQh — the hydraulic power stays about the same. In practice it falls slightly because the extra load pushes the rotor further from its optimal tip-speed ratio, reducing efficiency. The takeaway: for a given windmill, there is a trade-off between head and flow rate, and the useful power is capped by the wind.

## Safety Notes
- Spinning blades can cause serious cuts or eye injury. Enforce the 2 m perimeter — no one enters while the rotor is turning.
- A detached blade or slipped hub can fly several metres. Inspect the hub and blade fastenings before the test; stand upwind or to the side, never directly in line with the rotor plane.
- The pump rod moves up and down rapidly — keep fingers clear of the crank and linkage while the windmill is running.
- Water on the ground is a slip hazard. Wipe up spills and keep the measuring area clear.
- If the wind gusts strongly (Beaufort 6 or above), stop the test and wait for calmer conditions — the windmill may overspeed and shed a blade.

- This lesson produces the first real performance data for the windmill. Save the log sheets — they are the baseline for Lesson 314 (iteration) and Lesson 315 (data and graphs). Photograph the windmill running for the engineering report in Lesson 316.
- Celebrate the result. Even a windmill that lifts 1 litre per minute is a working machine. The point of test day is honest measurement, not perfection.
