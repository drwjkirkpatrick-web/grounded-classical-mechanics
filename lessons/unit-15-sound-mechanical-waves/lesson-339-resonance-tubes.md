# Lesson 339: Singing Tubes and Bottles: Air Columns Resonate

**Unit:** 15 — Sound & Mechanical Waves  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-2.1  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Make a bottle or straw sound by blowing across the opening and describe the vibrating air column inside.
2. Show that a longer air column gives a lower pitch and a shorter column gives a higher pitch.
3. Explain resonance: the air column sounds loudest when its length fits the wavelength of the standing wave.
4. Predict whether a tube open at one end will sound higher or lower when shortened.

## Materials
- Three or four glass or plastic bottles of different sizes (water bottles, soda bottles, beer bottles washed out)
- Water to pour into bottles (to change the air-column length)
- Drinking straws — several per pair
- Scissors
- Ruler
- A small dish of water (to find the water level by eye)
- Phone tuner app or the bottle xylophone from Lesson 335 for comparison

## Vocabulary
- **air column** (safu ya hewa) — the column of air inside a bottle or tube that vibrates as a unit
- **resonance** (mnysisitizo) — when a tube length fits the wave so the sound becomes loud and clear
- **standing wave** (wimbi lisilotembea) — a wave pattern that fits exactly in the tube, bouncing end to end
- **open end / closed end** (ncha wazi / ncha iliyofungwa) — whether the tube end is open to air or blocked
- **wavelength** (urefu wa wimbi) — the distance one full wave cycle occupies, written λ

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. When you blow across the top of an empty bottle, it makes a note. What is vibrating inside the bottle to make the sound?
2. If you pour water into the bottle so the air space becomes smaller, will the note go higher or lower?
3. A long didgeridoo sounds low and a short whistle sounds high. What does this tell you about air-column length and pitch?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The air column inside the bottle vibrates (the glass also hums a little, but the air is the main source).
2. Higher — the air column is shorter.
3. Longer air column = lower pitch; shorter air column = higher pitch.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect before blowing.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — the teacher blows across an empty bottle and gets a clear note, then pours in water halfway and blows again — the pitch jumps up. Ask: *what changed inside?*
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs (a) blow across three different bottles and rank them high to low; (b) take one bottle, pour water in stages, and note the pitch each time; (c) make a straw oboe — flatten one end of a straw, cut two slits to form a reed, and blow; then cut the straw shorter in steps and note the pitch rising. They record length vs. pitch for each.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — board: the air column is the vibrating thing, like a string but made of air. Resonance happens when the column length matches part of the sound wavelength. For a tube closed at one end, the fundamental fits a quarter-wavelength: **λ ≈ 4L**. Shorter L → shorter λ → higher frequency f = v/λ. Confront the misconception that "the bottle glass makes the note" — show that adding water (which only changes the air column) changes the pitch, so the air, not the glass, is the source.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *air columns resonate; shorter = higher*. Homework: blow across three different bottles or jugs at home and write down which is highest and which is lowest, with the reason.

## Hands-On Build: Bottle and Straw Resonators
*Build fits inside the Explore block; wash bottles and straws beforehand.*

### Parts
- Bottles — 3–4 per group, different sizes — glass soda bottles or plastic water bottles
- Water — in a jug — to adjust air columns
- Straws — several per pair — for the straw oboe
- Scissors — 1 per pair — to cut the reed and shorten the straw
- Ruler — 30 cm
- Phone tuner or bottle xylophone — for comparison

### Workflow
1. Blow across the mouth of an empty bottle (not into it — across the lip) until a clear note sounds. **Checkpoint:** you hear a steady tone, not just wind noise; adjust the angle of your breath.
2. Rank the three bottles from lowest to highest note; record which has the longest air column.
3. Pour water into one bottle in three stages; blow after each stage and record "higher / lower". **Checkpoint:** each added pour should raise the pitch; if not, you are blowing too hard or at the wrong angle.
4. Make a straw oboe: flatten 1 cm at one end, cut two small V-slits to form a reed, put the reed in your mouth and blow gently.
5. Cut 2 cm off the open end of the straw and blow again; repeat twice, recording the pitch each time. **Checkpoint:** pitch rises each cut.
6. Measure the final straw length and compare its pitch to the tuner.

### Common Mistakes
- **Blowing into, not across** — produces wind noise, not a note; aim the breath across the lip at an angle.
- **Reed too big or too small** — cut the V-slits about 5 mm; too deep and the reed closes, too shallow and it won't vibrate.
- **Wet straw blocking reed** — shake out water; keep the reed end dry.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test; 3–5 probe new ideas.*

1. What vibrates inside a bottle to make the note when you blow across the top? (mirrors pre-test)
2. You add water to a bottle so the air space is shorter. Does the pitch go higher or lower? (mirrors pre-test)
3. Two straws are identical except one is cut in half. Which makes the higher note, and why?
4. For a tube closed at one end, the fundamental wavelength is about λ ≈ 4L. If a tube is 25 cm long, what is the approximate wavelength of its note?
5. Challenge: a learner says the note comes from the glass of the bottle vibrating. Describe one observation from today that shows this is mostly wrong.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The air column inside the bottle.
2. Higher — the air column is shorter, so the resonant wavelength is shorter and the frequency rises.
3. The half-length straw — shorter air column gives a higher pitch (f ∝ 1/L for the same wave speed).
4. λ ≈ 4 × 25 cm = 100 cm = 1.0 m.
5. Adding water only changes the air column (the glass is unchanged), yet the pitch changes — so the air column, not the glass, is the source. (Accept: the straw oboe has no "bottle glass" and still sounds.)

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A tube closed at one end has length L = 25 cm. Using λ ≈ 4L and the speed of sound v ≈ 340 m/s, find the fundamental frequency and name the nearest musical note (A4 = 440 Hz; each octave halves/doubles the frequency).
   **Worked Answer:** λ ≈ 4 × 0.25 m = 1.0 m. f = v/λ = 340 / 1.0 = 340 Hz. The nearest common note is E4 ≈ 329.6 Hz (or F4 ≈ 349 Hz); 340 Hz is between them, closest to about E4–F4. The tube would sound a low, slightly flat F.
2. **Q:** You have a straw oboe 30 cm long sounding a note at about 283 Hz (λ ≈ 4L). You cut it to 20 cm. Predict the new frequency and explain the change.
   **Worked Answer:** Original λ ≈ 4 × 0.30 = 1.2 m, f = 340/1.2 ≈ 283 Hz. New λ ≈ 4 × 0.20 = 0.8 m, f = 340/0.8 = 425 Hz. The frequency rises by the ratio 30/20 = 1.5, so 283 × 1.5 ≈ 425 Hz — close to A4 (440 Hz).
3. **Q:** A bottle is blown across and sounds at 170 Hz. Estimate the length of the air column assuming the closed-end resonance λ ≈ 4L.
   **Worked Answer:** λ = v/f = 340/170 = 2.0 m. L ≈ λ/4 = 2.0/4 = 0.50 m = 50 cm. The air column is about half a metre — a tall bottle or jug.

## Safety Notes
- Do not share straws or blow into a straw another learner has used; give each learner their own.
- Scissors slips cut fingers — cut on a flat surface, blade away from the body; younger learners use safety scissors.
- Glass bottles can break if knocked together — set them on a stable surface, not the floor where they can be kicked.
- Do not blow so hard you feel dizzy; take turns and breathe normally between attempts.
