# Lesson 340: Build: Pan Pipes from Straws

**Unit:** 15 — Sound & Mechanical Waves  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-2.1  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** pan-pipes

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a set of pan pipes by cutting straws to calculated lengths, plugging one end, and binding them into a raft.
2. Play an ascending and descending scale on the pan pipes and compare each note to predicted pitches.
3. Apply the relationship f ∝ 1/L (longer pipe = lower note, shorter pipe = higher note) to choose the straw lengths.
4. Predict whether a lengthened or shortened pipe will sound higher or lower and test it.

## Materials
- Drinking straws or thin bamboo sections — 8 per pair (bamboo holds tuning better; straws are cheaper)
- Clay, plasticine, or chewed maize-meal dough — to plug one end of each pipe (airtight)
- Sticky tape or string — to bind the pipes into a raft side by side
- Ruler — 30 cm
- Scissors or a sharp knife (younger learners use scissors)
- Phone tuner app or the bottle xylophone from Lesson 335 for pitch comparison
- Pencil and notebook

## Vocabulary
- **pan pipes** (mabomba ya pepo) — a set of tubes of different lengths, each closed at one end, blown to make a scale
- **closed end** (ncha iliyofungwa) — the plugged end that reflects the sound wave back
- **scale** (mizizi) — a sequence of notes ordered low to high (e.g. do re mi fa sol la ti do)
- **resonance** (mnysisitizo) — the pipe sounds clearly when its length fits the standing wave
- **frequency** (masikio mara ngapi) — how fast the air vibrates; higher frequency = higher pitch, and f ∝ 1/L

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. In a pan pipe, what makes the sound when you blow across the open end?
2. Two pipes are identical except one is longer. Which one sounds higher?
3. Why must one end of each pan pipe be plugged (closed)?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. The air column inside the pipe vibrates.
2. The shorter pipe sounds higher (f ∝ 1/L).
3. The closed end reflects the wave back, setting up a standing wave at a definite pitch; an open-open pipe would need a different length for the same note.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect before handling tools.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — the teacher plays three pre-made pan pipes (long, medium, short) and the class ranks them low to high. Then the teacher shows the open end is blown *across*, not into, like a bottle.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs (a) decide a longest pipe length (e.g. 20 cm) and compute the eight lengths for a scale using f ∝ 1/L (advanced learners do the full diatonic ratios; younger learners use the guide table on the board); (b) cut and plug each straw, checking the plug is airtight; (c) bind the eight pipes in order from longest to shortest with tape; (d) play the scale up and down and compare to the tuner or bottle xylophone.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — board: each pipe is an air column closed at one end; λ ≈ 4L and f ∝ 1/L. To go up a scale, the frequency rises, so the lengths get shorter. The diatonic ratios give the exact lengths. Confront the misconception that "blowing harder makes the note higher" — blowing harder only makes it louder; the pitch is fixed by the length.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *each pipe is a tuned air column*. Homework: play your pan-pipe scale for someone at home and tell them why the long pipe is the low note.

## Hands-On Build: Straw Pan Pipes
*Build fits inside the Explore block; cut the longest straws the day before if bamboo is used.*

### Parts
- Straws or thin bamboo — 8 per pair — ~25 cm long to start
- Clay or plasticine — a small ball per pipe — to plug one end airtight
- Sticky tape or string — to bind the raft
- Ruler — 30 cm
- Scissors — 1 per pair
- Tuner app or bottle xylophone — for comparison

### Workflow
1. Decide the longest pipe length L₀ (use 20 cm). Write the eight target lengths from the board table (advanced: compute them from the diatonic ratios and f ∝ 1/L).
2. Cut each straw to its target length; keep the cuts square, not slanted.
3. Plug one end of each pipe with clay, pressing firmly so it is airtight. **Checkpoint:** blow across the open end — if air leaks from the plug, re-seal it; a leaky plug gives a weak or no note.
4. Lay the eight pipes side by side, longest to shortest, open ends aligned.
5. Bind them with tape near the closed ends and again near the middle. **Checkpoint:** the raft holds together when you lift it; no pipe slides out.
6. Blow across the open end of each pipe in turn and play the scale up (long→short) and down. Compare the lowest and highest to the tuner.

### Common Mistakes
- **Leaky plug** — the note is faint or absent; press the clay in firmly and test each pipe before binding.
- **Slanted cut** — changes the effective length; cut square across the straw.
- **Blowing into the pipe, not across** — produces hissing, not a note; aim the breath across the open lip at an angle.
- **Pipes out of order** — the scale sounds wrong; re-check longest = lowest before binding.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror the pre-test; 3–5 probe new ideas.*

1. What vibrates inside a pan pipe to make the sound? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Two pan pipes differ only in length. Which sounds higher, and why? (mirrors pre-test)
3. You lengthen a pipe by adding a straw extension. Will its pitch go up or down?
4. For a closed-end pipe, λ ≈ 4L. If your longest pipe is 20 cm, what is the approximate wavelength of its lowest note?
5. Challenge: a learner says "blow harder to reach the high note on the long pipe." Is this correct? Explain in terms of what sets the pitch.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. The air column inside the pipe.
2. The shorter pipe — because f ∝ 1/L, a shorter column gives a higher frequency.
3. Down — a longer pipe gives a lower pitch.
4. λ ≈ 4 × 20 cm = 80 cm = 0.8 m.
5. Incorrect — blowing harder changes loudness, not pitch. The pitch is set by the pipe length (f ∝ 1/L); to reach a high note you must use a shorter pipe, not blow harder.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Using f ∝ 1/L, compute the eight pipe lengths for a diatonic scale (do re mi fa sol la ti do) with the lowest note "do" at L₀ = 20 cm. The frequency ratios relative to "do" are 1, 9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3, 15/8, 2. Give each length in cm.
   **Worked Answer:** Since L ∝ 1/f, each length = L₀ × (1/ratio). do: 20 × 1 = 20.0 cm. re: 20 × 8/9 = 17.78 cm. mi: 20 × 4/5 = 16.00 cm. fa: 20 × 3/4 = 15.00 cm. sol: 20 × 2/3 = 13.33 cm. la: 20 × 3/5 = 12.00 cm. ti: 20 × 8/15 = 10.67 cm. do(high): 20 × 1/2 = 10.00 cm.
2. **Q:** The lowest pipe is 20 cm and the speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find the frequency of the lowest note and the highest note of the eight-pipe scale.
   **Worked Answer:** Lowest: λ ≈ 4 × 0.20 = 0.80 m; f = 340/0.80 = 425 Hz. Highest (do high, L = 10 cm): λ ≈ 4 × 0.10 = 0.40 m; f = 340/0.40 = 850 Hz. The highest is exactly double the lowest — one octave up, as expected.
3. **Q:** A learner's fifth pipe (sol) sounds flat compared to the tuner. Give two likely causes and a fix for each.
   **Worked Answer:** (a) The plug is leaky — air escapes so the effective length is shorter and the note drifts; re-seal the clay plug and re-test. (b) The cut was slanted, making the effective length longer than marked, which lowers the pitch; re-cut square at exactly 13.33 cm. (Also accept: the straw was stretched or bent, changing the length.)

## Safety Notes
- Scissors or knives cut fingers — cut on a flat surface, blade away from the body; younger learners use safety scissors, the teacher cuts bamboo.
- Do not share straws or blow into a pipe another learner has used; each learner uses their own set or washes between uses.
- Clay plugs can choke if small pieces break off and are inhaled — keep plugs larger than the straw bore so they cannot be sucked in.
- Bamboo splits sharply — smooth cut ends with a stone or file before blowing.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lesson 339 (Resonance Tubes) introduced the closed-end air column and λ ≈ 4L; this lesson applies it to a tuned instrument. The bottle xylophone (Lesson 334–335) is the pitch reference — set it out for comparison.
- Younger learners (10–13): use the pre-computed length table on the board and focus on plugging, binding, and playing the scale; skip the diatonic-ratio derivation. Older learners: compute the eight lengths themselves (advanced Q1) and check each against the tuner.
- Local tip: bamboo of ~6–8 mm bore (from a homestead fence or a fundi) holds tuning far better than plastic straws, which bend and detune; if only straws are available, keep them straight and dry. Clay from the school compound or plasticine from a duka makes good plugs — the plug must be airtight, so test each pipe by blowing before binding.
- CBE assessment note: the built pan pipes and a played scale are evidence for the "design and make" competency; record whether each pair's scale matched the tuner (at least directionally — lowest to highest).
