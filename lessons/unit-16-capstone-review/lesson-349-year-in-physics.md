# Lesson 349: The Year in Physics: The Big Map

**Unit:** 16 — Capstone, Review & Community Science  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Name the six big threads of the year — forces, motion, energy, machines, fluids, waves — and place each on a single class concept map.
2. Draw at least one correct arrow (link) between two different threads and say in one sentence why they connect.
3. Explain the year's "spine" chain — a force changes motion, work transfers energy, machines trade force for distance, fluids move loads, waves carry energy through matter — in their own words.
4. Defend one cross-link that nobody in the class has drawn yet, using a real Kenyan example.

## Materials
- A long, clear wall space or a corridor wall for the big map (about 2 m of width)
- About 60 blank card squares cut from manila card (or torn exercise-book pages), roughly 10 cm × 8 cm
- Six large heading cards, one per thread, written before the lesson: **FORCES**, **MOTION**, **ENERGY**, **MACHINES**, **FLUIDS**, **WAVES**
- A ball of sisal string or sewing thread and a roll of cellotape to pin cards and connect them
- Thick marker pens, two or three, for writing on cards
- A printed copy of the six-thread spine for the teacher only (the teacher keeps this hidden until the Explain stage)
- Plain paper and a pencil per learner for the homework sketch

## Vocabulary
- **concept map** (ramani ya dhana) — a diagram of ideas as boxes joined by labelled arrows showing how the ideas link
- **thread** (nyuzi) — one big line of ideas running through the year, e.g. forces or energy
- **cross-link** (kiungo cha pembeni) — an arrow joining two ideas that sit in different threads
- **spine** (mgongo) — the main chain of the year: force → motion → energy → machines → fluids → waves
- **review** (mapitio) — going back over the whole year to fix the big ideas firmly

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. Name two of the big physics threads we have studied this year.
2. In one sentence, how does a force connect to motion?
3. Draw one arrow between any two ideas you remember from the year, and label the arrow with one word.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Accept any two of: forces, motion (describing motion / gravity), energy (work and power), machines (simple machines), fluids (pressure, buoyancy, pumps), waves (sound and mechanical waves). Spelling not marked — the idea must be identifiable.
2. A force changes the motion of a body — it can start, stop, speed up, slow down, or change the direction of a moving object (Newton's first and second law). Accept "a push or pull that changes how something moves" or equivalent.
3. Award the mark for any two ideas joined by an arrow with a one-word label, e.g. FORCE →(causes)→ MOTION, or ENERGY →(runs)→ MACHINE. The label must be one word; the arrow must join two real ideas from the year.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect after five minutes. Tell learners this lesson is not a new topic — it is the year's review, built as one big map on the wall that the whole class makes together.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — stand at the blank wall. Ask: *if you had to put the whole year of physics into six boxes, what would they be?* Take six answers and stick the six prepared heading cards on the wall in a row as they are named: FORCES, MOTION, ENERGY, MACHINES, FLUIDS, WAVES. The hook: today we do not just list these threads — we link them, and the links are the real physics.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — build the map in three waves. **Wave 1 (about five minutes):** each learner writes one small card with one concrete idea from the year (e.g. "friction on a panga", "PE of a jerrican on a shelf", "resonance of a bottle") and tapes it under the thread it belongs to. Aim for at least eight cards under each heading. **Wave 2 (about five minutes):** in pairs, learners draw sisal-string arrows between cards in the *same* thread (e.g. inside FORCES: weight → friction) and label each arrow in one word. **Wave 3 (about five minutes):** pairs draw cross-links between *different* threads (e.g. ENERGY → MACHINES labelled "runs", FORCES → FLUIDS labelled "pressure") and label them. The teacher walks the room and asks each pair to justify one arrow aloud. **Checkpoint:** every thread has at least six cards and at least two cross-links leaving it.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — reveal the spine on the board: **force → motion → energy → machines → fluids → waves**. Walk it in one breath: a force changes motion (Unit 2–3); work transfers energy (Unit 5); machines trade force for distance using that energy (Unit 6); fluids carry loads by pressure and buoyancy (Units 7–9); waves carry energy through matter without carrying the matter (Units 11, 15). Now confront the one misconception head-on: *the threads are separate chapters.* They are not — the same idea (energy) flows through all of them, and the cross-links on the wall prove it. Point to three learner cross-links and read the label; ask the class whether the link is fair. Finish by naming the cross-link that surprised the class most.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *physics is one map, not six boxes — the links between the threads are where the real understanding lives.* Homework: on plain paper, redraw the wall map from memory, then add three cross-links nobody in the class found, each with a real Kenyan example and a one-word label. Bring it next lesson.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror pre-test; 3–5 probe new ideas.*

1. Name two of the big physics threads we studied this year. (mirrors pre-test)
2. In one sentence, how does a force connect to motion? (mirrors pre-test)
3. Look at the wall map. Write down one cross-link between two different threads and give the one-word label on its arrow.
4. Why is it wrong to think of the year as six separate, unconnected chapters? Answer in one sentence using the idea of the spine.
5. Challenge: draw a small concept map (four boxes, three arrows) that links a force, a machine, and a fluid. Label every arrow in one word.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Accept any two of: forces, motion, energy, machines, fluids, waves. The idea must be identifiable; spelling not marked.
2. A force changes the motion of a body — it can start, stop, speed up, slow down, or change direction. Accept any equivalent one-sentence statement linking force to a change in motion.
3. Accept any cross-link actually drawn on the class wall map between two different threads, with its one-word label correctly copied. Example: ENERGY → MACHINES, label "runs"; or FORCES → FLUIDS, label "pressure".
4. The six threads form one spine — force leads to motion, motion involves work and energy, energy runs machines, machines move fluids, and waves carry energy — so the ideas flow into each other rather than standing alone.
5. Award full marks for four boxes (at least one force, one machine, one fluid) joined by three arrows each labelled in one word. Example: FORCE →(pushes)→ MACHINE →(moves)→ WATER, with WATER being the fluid box. The arrows must be labelled and the chain must be physically sensible.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Add three cross-links to the class wall map that nobody else has drawn, and defend each one with a real Kenyan example and a one-word label. At least one cross-link must join the WAVES thread to another thread.
   **Worked Answer:** Three valid cross-links with labels and Kenyan examples: (a) WAVES ↔ FORCES, label "pressure" — a loud speaker cone pushes air, making a sound wave; the wave is a pressure disturbance started by a force (a matatu speaker). (b) MACHINES ↔ FLUIDS, label "pumps" — a jua kali water pump is a machine whose only job is to move a fluid; the bicycle pump from Unit 8 links the two threads directly (a hand pump at a well). (c) ENERGY ↔ WAVES, label "carries" — a wave carries energy from source to receiver without carrying matter, so the energy thread feeds the waves thread (drum sound reaching a listener across a field). Each link names two different threads, a one-word label, and a real Kenyan object. Other valid pairs are accepted if the label and example are correct.
2. **Q:** The year's spine is force → motion → energy → machines → fluids → waves. Give one concrete Kenyan object that travels the *entire* spine from force to wave, and trace the chain in four sentences naming the physics at each step.
   **Worked Answer:** Object: a bicycle bell on a boda boda. (1) Force: the rider's finger pushes the bell lever — a force is applied (Unit 2). (2) Motion: the lever moves and rotates the clapper arm — a force changes motion (Unit 3). (3) Energy: the moving clapper has kinetic energy, and on impact it does work on the bell dome, transferring energy (Unit 5). (4) Machines: the bell lever is a small second-class lever that trades a small finger push for a larger clapper strike — a machine (Unit 6). (5) Waves: the struck dome vibrates and sets the air vibrating, sending a sound wave that carries the energy to a listener's ear (Unit 15). The whole spine is traced from force to wave in one everyday object.
3. **Q:** A learner draws a cross-link from FORCES to WAVES labelled "resonance", with the note "a tuned bottle resonates when a force shakes the table". Defend or correct this cross-link using the physics of resonance from Unit 15, and say what the correct label should be if it is wrong.
   **Worked Answer:** The cross-link is partly right but the label is misleading. Resonance is a wave phenomenon — a body vibrates at its natural frequency because energy is fed to it at that frequency (Unit 15, Lesson 339). The force that shakes the table is the *driver*, but the link is not "resonance = force"; it is that a driving force at the right frequency transfers energy into a standing wave. The cleaner label is "drives" (the force drives the wave) or "feeds" (the force feeds energy into the resonator). The Kenyan example is sound: a bottle xylophone bottle resonates when struck because the strike is a force impulse containing many frequencies, and the bottle picks out and amplifies its natural one. So the link is valid, but the one-word label should be "drives", not "resonance", because resonance is the wave result, not the force itself.

## Safety Notes
- The wall map work happens at a wall — keep learners from standing on chairs or desks to reach high cards; the teacher sticks any card above head height.
- Sisal string under tension can flick into an eye if it snaps; do not pull string tight between cards, a gentle loop taped at both ends is enough.
- Marker pens can stain uniform and skin; keep caps on when not in use and keep them off the floor where they can be stepped on.
- A crowded class at the wall can cause bumping; send pairs up to the wall two at a time while others draft cards at their desks.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: this lesson assumes the whole year (Units 1–15). Learners should recall at least the names of the threads and one concrete example from each. No new content is introduced — this is consolidation.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on naming the six threads, placing cards under the right heading, and drawing simple same-thread arrows (e.g. weight → friction inside FORCES). Ages 14–17 draw the cross-links between different threads, defend arrows aloud, and tackle the three-new-cross-links challenge in the Advanced Questions.
- The wall map is the unit's anchor artifact — leave it up for the whole of Unit 16 if the wall space allows, and add to it each lesson. Photograph it for the portfolio.
- The biggest pitfall is learners drawing arrows that are just "both are physics". Push them: *what is the one word on the arrow? what moves from one box to the other?* A cross-link must name a transfer or a cause, not a category.
- If wall space is unavailable, the map can be built on a long table or the chalkboard with chalk arrows instead of string; the physics is identical.
- CBE assessment note: the post-test and the homework sketch measure the learner's ability to connect ideas across the JS-FE strand — exactly the integrative competence the strand is designed to build. A learner who can draw a four-box, three-arrow mini-map with correct one-word labels is at mastery level for year-end integration.
