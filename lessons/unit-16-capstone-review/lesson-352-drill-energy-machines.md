# Lesson 352: Core Drill III: Energy and Machines

**Unit:** 16 — Capstone, Review & Community Science  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.6; G10-PHY-1.5  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Calculate work (W = Fd), power (P = W/t), and efficiency (η = useful output ÷ input × 100%) in a chain and state the units for each.
2. Determine the mechanical advantage (MA = load ÷ effort) and velocity ratio (VR = distance effort ÷ distance load) for a lever and a pulley system.
3. Look at a photo of a real Kenyan machine (wheelbarrow, jua kali pulley, posho mill) and name the machine, the load, the effort, and the pivot or strand count.
4. Diagnose why a machine's efficiency is below 100% and name the energy loss (friction, heat, sound, lifting the moving parts).

## Materials
- A set of about 12 machine photo cards: printed or drawn pictures of real Kenyan machines — a wheelbarrow loaded with stones, a jua kali pulley lifting a sufuria, a posho mill, a panga as a lever, a screw jack, a block-and-tackle with 4 strands
- A set of about 16 problem cards: four rounds of four cards covering work, power, efficiency, and MA/VR
- A printed mark scheme per pair for peer marking at the end of each round
- A mini-whiteboard or the chalkboard for the teacher's worked examples
- Blank paper and a pencil per learner for working
- A stopwatch or the classroom clock for the round timing
- A simple score sheet per learner (four rounds, a mark per round)

## Vocabulary
- **work** (kazi) — force multiplied by the distance moved in the direction of the force, in joules (J)
- **power** (nguvu) — the rate of doing work, work per unit time, in watts (W)
- **efficiency** (ufanisi) — the ratio of useful output energy to input energy, as a percentage
- **mechanical advantage** (ufaida wa mitambo) — load divided by effort (MA = L/E); how many times the machine multiplies the force
- **velocity ratio** (uwiano wa kasi) — distance moved by effort divided by distance moved by load (VR = d_E/d_L); set by the geometry of the machine
- **block and tackle** (gudu la kurudia) — a pulley system with several strands sharing the load

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. A force of 50 N moves a load 4 m in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done.
2. Define mechanical advantage and write its formula.
3. Why is the efficiency of a real machine always less than 100%?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. Work = force × distance = 50 × 4 = 200 J.
2. Mechanical advantage is the ratio of the load to the effort. MA = load ÷ effort (MA = L/E).
3. Some input energy is lost — mainly to friction (heat), and to lifting the moving parts of the machine; the lost energy does not reach the load, so useful output is less than input.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out slips; collect after five minutes. Tell learners this is a drill lesson: four speed rounds on energy and machines, each with a photo-identification step and a short problem, peer-marked at the end of each round.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — hold up the wheelbarrow photo card. Ask: *what is the load, what is the effort, and where is the pivot?* Take one or two answers; the wheel is the pivot (a second-class lever), the load is the stones, the effort is the handler's lift on the handles. The hook: today we drill the four skills of Units 5 and 6 — work, power, efficiency, and the MA/VR of real machines — at speed, using photos of machines you see every day.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — run four rounds, about three to four minutes each. **Round 1 — Work:** learners compute W = Fd for a named case (a jua kali artisan lifting a sufuria, a panga cutting). **Round 2 — Power:** learners compute P = W/t (a pump lifting water, a boy climbing stairs). **Round 3 — Efficiency:** learners compute η = useful output ÷ input × 100% for a machine given input work and useful output (a posho mill). **Round 4 — MA and VR:** learners identify the machine from a photo card and compute MA = L/E and VR = d_E/d_L for a lever and a pulley. In each round, learners work the answer on paper in 90 seconds, pairs swap and peer-mark against the printed scheme in 60 seconds, and the teacher posts the worked answer on the board. **Checkpoint:** every learner has a score for all four rounds on their score sheet.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate the four skills on the board, one line each. **Work:** W = Fd, in joules, but only the force in the direction of motion counts — lifting a jerrican straight up does work against gravity (W = mgh), carrying it level does no work against gravity. **Power:** P = W/t, in watts; the same work done faster is more power — a fast runner and a slow climber do the same work climbing a hill, but the runner has more power. **Efficiency:** η = useful output ÷ input × 100%; always below 100% for a real machine because friction and the lifting of moving parts waste energy. **MA and VR:** MA = load ÷ effort (what you actually get), VR = distance effort ÷ distance load (what the geometry gives); for an ideal machine MA = VR, but in a real machine MA < VR because of friction. Confront the misconception that *a machine multiplies energy* — it does not. A machine multiplies force (MA) by trading force for distance (VR), but the work you put in is at least the work you get out; energy is conserved. Point to the block-and-tackle photo: four strands share the load, so the effort is about a quarter of the load, but you pull four times the distance.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark using the printed scheme.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *machines trade force for distance — they never create energy; efficiency measures how much of your work actually reaches the load.* Homework: a block and tackle lifts a 600 N load with a 200 N effort over 4 strands. Calculate the MA, the VR, and the efficiency, and say what is the most likely reason the efficiency is below 100%. Bring the worked solution next lesson.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror pre-test; 3–5 probe new ideas.*

1. A force of 80 N moves a load 5 m in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done. (mirrors pre-test)
2. Define mechanical advantage and write its formula. (mirrors pre-test)
3. A pump does 3000 J of work in 10 s. Calculate the power output.
4. A machine is supplied with 500 J of energy and delivers 400 J of useful work to the load. Calculate the efficiency and state where the missing energy most likely went.
5. Challenge: a block and tackle has 4 strands sharing a 600 N load and is worked with an effort of 200 N. Calculate the MA, the VR, and the efficiency, and give the most likely diagnosis for the loss.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Work = force × distance = 80 × 5 = 400 J.
2. Mechanical advantage is the ratio of the load to the effort. MA = load ÷ effort (MA = L/E).
3. Power = work ÷ time = 3000 ÷ 10 = 300 W.
4. Efficiency = useful output ÷ input × 100% = 400 ÷ 500 × 100% = 80%. The missing 100 J is mostly lost to friction (heat) in the moving parts, with some lost to lifting the moving parts of the machine itself.
5. MA = load ÷ effort = 600 ÷ 200 = 3. VR for a block and tackle = number of strands = 4. Efficiency = MA ÷ VR × 100% = 3 ÷ 4 × 100% = 75%. The diagnosis: the actual MA (3) is below the ideal (4), so 25% of the effort is being wasted — most likely to friction in the pulley bearings and the weight of the moving pulley block; the rope rubbing on the pulleys also turns some work into heat.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A block and tackle has 4 strands sharing the load. It lifts a 600 N load using an effort of 200 N, and the load rises 0.5 m. (a) Calculate the MA. (b) Calculate the VR and the distance the effort must be pulled. (c) Calculate the efficiency. (d) Give the most likely diagnosis for the loss, and one practical way to raise the efficiency.
   **Worked Answer:** (a) MA = load ÷ effort = 600 ÷ 200 = 3. (b) VR = number of strands = 4. The effort distance = VR × load distance = 4 × 0.5 = 2.0 m. (c) Input work = effort × effort distance = 200 × 2.0 = 400 J. Useful output work = load × load distance = 600 × 0.5 = 300 J. Efficiency = useful output ÷ input × 100% = 300 ÷ 400 × 100% = 75%. (Equivalently, efficiency = MA ÷ VR × 100% = 3 ÷ 4 × 100% = 75%.) (d) The actual MA (3) is below the ideal (4), so 25% of the input is wasted — most likely to friction in the pulley bearings and the weight of the moving pulley block. A practical fix is to oil or grease the pulley bearings to reduce friction, and to use a lighter moving pulley block so less effort goes to lifting the block itself.
2. **Q:** A wheelbarrow is loaded with stones of total weight 600 N. The centre of mass of the load is 0.5 m from the wheel (the pivot); the handler lifts the handles 1.5 m from the wheel. (a) Use the principle of moments to find the effort needed to just lift the load. (b) Hence find the MA. (c) The handler lifts the handles 0.3 m; how high does the load rise, and what is the VR?
   **Worked Answer:** (a) By the principle of moments, at the point of just lifting: effort × its distance from pivot = load × its distance from pivot. E × 1.5 = 600 × 0.5 = 300. E = 300 ÷ 1.5 = 200 N. (b) MA = load ÷ effort = 600 ÷ 200 = 3. (c) The load rises by the lever ratio: load distance / effort distance = 0.5 / 1.5 = 1/3. So if the handles move 0.3 m down, the load rises 0.3 ÷ 3 = 0.1 m. VR = effort distance ÷ load distance = 0.3 ÷ 0.1 = 3 (which equals the moment arm ratio 1.5/0.5 = 3, as expected for a lever).
3. **Q:** A posho mill motor delivers 1500 W to the mill. Of this, 1200 W goes into grinding maize and 300 W is lost as heat and sound in the bearings and belt. (a) Calculate the efficiency of the mill. (b) Calculate the useful work done in 2 minutes. (c) State two specific reasons the efficiency is below 100%, and for each say whether it could be reduced.
   **Worked Answer:** (a) Efficiency = useful power ÷ input power × 100% = 1200 ÷ 1500 × 100% = 80%. (b) Useful power = 1200 W. Useful work in 2 minutes (120 s) = power × time = 1200 × 120 = 144 000 J (144 kJ). (c) Two losses: (i) friction in the bearings turns some input power into heat — this can be reduced by oiling or replacing worn bearings; (ii) the drive belt slips and flexes, losing power as heat and sound — this can be reduced by tightening or replacing a worn belt. Both are real, fixable losses, which is why the efficiency is 80% rather than 100%.

## Safety Notes
- This is a paper-and-photo drill with no machines handled by learners — the main risk is paper cuts from photo cards; handle cards by the edges.
- The photo cards show real machines but no machine is operated in class; do not allow learners to imitate lifting heavy loads during the round.
- Peer marking can cause arguments; the printed mark scheme is final, and the teacher adjudicates any dispute.
- Keep the pace brisk but clear — efficiency calculations have several steps, so allow the full 90 seconds per problem before swapping to mark.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: this drill revises Unit 5 (Work, Energy and Power) and Unit 6 (Simple Machines). Learners should already know W = Fd, P = W/t, efficiency, and MA/VR for levers and pulleys. The drill is for speed and confidence, not first teaching — if a round exposes a widespread gap, slow the round and re-teach at the board.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on the work and power one-step problems and on identifying the load, effort, and pivot in the photo cards (wheelbarrow, panga). Ages 14–17 take the moment-based MA of the wheelbarrow, the block-and-tackle efficiency diagnosis, and the posho mill power chain in the Advanced Questions.
- The biggest pitfall in Round 4 is learners computing MA from the geometry (the VR) instead of from the actual load and effort. Insist on the rule: MA comes from measured forces (load ÷ effort), VR comes from measured distances or strand count; efficiency compares the two.
- If photo cards run short, the teacher can sketch a machine on the board each round; the format does not depend on printed cards.
- Peer marking with the printed scheme doubles as revision — learners see the expected working for every round. Collect the score sheets to find which skill was weakest across the class.
- CBE assessment note: the four rounds and post-test map to G10-PHY-1.6 (energy, work, power and machines) and G10-PHY-1.5 (moments and equilibrium, via the wheelbarrow lever). A learner who can compute work, power, and efficiency, and who can diagnose why MA < VR in a real machine, is at mastery level for these two strands.
