# Lesson 364: Reflection and Portfolio

**Unit:** 16 — Capstone, Review & Community Science  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** G10-PHY-1.1; JS-FE  |  **Hands-On:** No  |  **Project:** —

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Compile a personal portfolio that gathers their best pre-test, post-test, graphs, and build photos from the year into one place.
2. Write a "what I can now do" statement that names a CBE outcome and cites a piece of evidence from the portfolio.
3. Identify three areas of growth and two areas that still need work, using their own pre-test and post-test scores.
4. Present one portfolio item to a partner and explain the physics it shows in their own words.

## Materials
- Each learner's folder of the year's work: pre-tests, post-tests, graph sheets, lab notebooks, and build photos (the "year files")
- A clean portfolio cover sheet or manila folder per learner (one to keep, one to hand in)
- Glue, scissors, and pens for mounting items
- A printed list of the year's ten key CBE outcomes, one copy per learner
- A blank "what I can now do" reflection sheet (one per learner)
- A sample portfolio page on the wall showing the expected layout — evidence on the left, written reflection on the right

## Vocabulary
- **portfolio** (mkusanyiko) — a folder that gathers your best work to show what you have learned
- **evidence** (uthibitisho) — a real piece of your work (a test, a graph, a photo) that proves you can do something
- **outcome** (lengo) — a skill or idea the curriculum says you should master by the end of the year
- **growth** (maendeleo) — the difference between what you could do at the start and what you can do now
- **self-assessment** (kujipima) — a learner judging their own learning against the outcomes
- **citation** (nukuu) — naming the exact page, test, or photo where the evidence is found

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. In one sentence, what is a learning portfolio for?
2. Name one piece of evidence from your own year's work that shows you can do physics.
3. What is one CBE outcome (a skill) you think you have improved on this year?

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. A portfolio is a folder that gathers your best work — tests, graphs, photos — to show what you have learned and prove your growth. Accept any answer naming a collection of evidence showing learning.
2. Accept any concrete item from the learner's own year: a pre-test, a post-test, a graph they drew, a build photo (bottle xylophone, windmill, etc.), or a lab result. The item must be real and theirs.
3. Accept any named skill that maps to a real unit outcome — measuring, drawing force diagrams, using v = fλ, calculating work or efficiency, reading a graph, building a machine, etc. The outcome must be stated, not just "I improved in physics."

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out the pre-test slips; collect after 5 minutes. Tell learners this is the last working lesson of the year — today they gather their evidence and judge their own growth, not learn a new topic.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — hold up one learner's folder (with permission) and ask: "If a stranger wanted to know what this learner can do after a year of physics, what three things in here would prove it?" Take 2–3 answers. The hook: a portfolio is the story of your year told in your own evidence — and today every learner writes that story.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — each learner builds the portfolio. Step 1: lay out the year's files and sort into four piles — best pre-test, best post-test, best graph or data table, and best build photo or diagram. Step 2: mount each item on a portfolio page with the unit and lesson number written beside it. Step 3: on the reflection sheet, write "what I can now do" against each of the ten key outcomes, citing the page or photo that proves it. Step 4: pair up and show one item to a partner, explaining the physics in your own words. **Checkpoint:** every learner has at least four evidence items mounted and at least five of the ten outcomes addressed on the reflection sheet.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — regroup and consolidate the year on the board: the big arc was forces → motion → energy → machines → fluids → waves, and every learner now holds evidence for parts of that arc. Confront the misconception that "the portfolio is just a folder of old papers." It is not — it is an argument: each item is evidence, and the reflection sheet is the claim that "I can now do X," supported by that evidence. A claim without a citation is weak; show the class one strong and one weak example from the sample page. Remind them that naming where the evidence is (the lesson number, the page) is what makes it a citation, not a guess.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; collect or peer-mark.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *your portfolio is the proof of your year — keep it, it is your record*. Homework: finish the reflection sheet for any outcomes not yet addressed, and bring the portfolio to the final celebration lesson — certificates are given against the portfolio.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual. Questions 1–2 mirror pre-test; 3–5 probe new ideas.*

1. In one sentence, what is a learning portfolio for? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Name one piece of evidence from your own year's work that shows you can do physics. (mirrors pre-test)
3. Write one "what I can now do" statement for a CBE outcome. Name the outcome and cite the evidence (lesson number or page) that proves it.
4. Why is a claim like "I can read a motion graph" stronger when it cites a specific graph from your portfolio than when it stands alone?
5. Challenge: name two outcomes you have strong evidence for, and one outcome you still need to work on. For the weak one, say what you would do next to improve.

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. A portfolio is a folder that gathers your best work — tests, graphs, photos — to show what you have learned and prove your growth. Accept any answer naming a collection of evidence showing learning.
2. Accept any concrete item from the learner's own year: a pre-test, a post-test, a graph, a build photo, or a lab result. The item must be real and theirs.
3. Accept any statement of the form "I can now [skill], as shown by [evidence]." Example: "I can now read a speed-time graph, as shown by my Unit 3 post-test, question 4, where I found the gradient." Award full marks for a named outcome plus a cited piece of evidence (lesson number or page).
4. A cited claim is stronger because the evidence is checkable — a reader can turn to that page and verify the skill. A bare claim could be true or false and gives no proof; the citation turns a claim into demonstrated learning. Accept any answer making the point that evidence supports the claim.
5. Two strong outcomes with evidence named, plus one weak outcome with a concrete next step. Example: strong — "I can calculate efficiency (Unit 5 windmill log, 38%)" and "I can draw a force diagram (Unit 2 post-test Q2)"; weak — "I still struggle with v = fλ; next I would redo the pan-pipe calculation and check my answer against the wave equation." Award marks for evidence on the two strong items and a realistic plan on the weak one.

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** Self-assess against ten key outcomes from the year, citing a piece of evidence from your portfolio for each. For any outcome where your evidence is weak, state a concrete next step.
   **Worked Answer:** A learner's ten key outcomes and evidence: (1) Measurement and units — Unit 1 post-test, all conversions correct. (2) Force diagrams — Unit 2 windmill diagram, three forces labelled. (3) Motion graphs — Unit 3 post-test Q4, gradient found from v-t graph. (4) Gravity and fall — Unit 4 drop log, g ≈ 9.8 m/s² from timing. (5) Work and energy — Unit 5 energy chain, efficiency 38% calculated. (6) Machines MA/VR — Unit 6 pulley photo, MA = 3 stated. (7) Pressure P = ρgh — Unit 7 water-column graph, depth vs pressure linear. (8) Buoyancy — Unit 9 float/sink table, three materials predicted correctly. (9) Circular motion — Unit 10 pendulum data, T = 2π√(L/g) verified. (10) Waves v = fλ — Unit 15 pan-pipe build, f = v/2L applied. Weak area: outcome 3 (motion graphs) — evidence is thin; next step is to redo the gradient problem and check the intercept meaning. Full marks for ten outcomes each with a cited piece of evidence and at least one weak area with a next step.
2. **Q:** Your Unit 5 windmill log shows 38% efficiency. Using your portfolio, trace the full energy chain from wind to pumped water and identify the two biggest losses. State which loss you would attack first and why.
   **Worked Answer:** The chain: wind → blades (kinetic energy of wind captured, some spills past blades) → shaft rotation (gear friction loss) → pump (mechanical linkage loss, some water leaks back) → water lifted (gravitational PE gained). The two biggest losses are usually blade spillage (the wind not fully captured, a theoretical limit of about 59% — the Betz limit — plus real-blade losses) and mechanical friction in the shaft, gears, and pump. Attack the blade loss first: it is the upstream loss, so every step after it also benefits. A better blade profile or angle captures more of the wind, raising the 38% before any downstream fix. Fixing a downstream friction loss helps only that step. The principle: fix the biggest loss first, and prefer upstream losses because they compound. Accept any defensible answer naming two losses and a justified priority.
3. **Q:** A reviewer says your portfolio "shows activity but not mastery." Using two specific items from your portfolio, argue the opposite — show how each item demonstrates a transferable skill, not just a completed task.
   **Worked Answer:** Item 1 — the Unit 3 speed-time graph. It is not just a drawn line; the learner found the gradient to get acceleration and used the area under the line to get distance. That skill transfers to any graph with a rate (velocity-time, force-extension, pressure-depth) — gradient always means a rate, area always means a total. Item 2 — the Unit 6 pulley photo with MA = 3. It is not just a built machine; the learner verified MA = load/effort by measurement and linked it to VR (the rope geometry), then predicted the efficiency from the two. That skill transfers to any machine — measure MA, compare to the ideal VR, and the gap is the friction loss. Both items show a transferable method (read a rate from a graph; compare real to ideal for any machine), not a one-off task. Full marks for two items each tied to a general skill the learner could apply elsewhere.

## Safety Notes
- This is a paper-and-folder lesson — the main risk is paper cuts from old test sheets and manila folders; remind learners to slide items apart, not tear.
- Scissors for mounting items should be blunt-nosed school scissors; collect them back before the post-test to avoid distractions and poking.
- Build photos may show old apparatus (bottles, string, weights) — do not recreate or handle those items today; this lesson works only with paper.
- If portfolios are stored in the classroom, keep them off the floor and away from water or open windows to protect the year's evidence.

## Teacher Notes
- Prior knowledge: this lesson draws on the whole year (Units 1–15). Learners should have kept their pre-tests, post-tests, graph sheets, and build photos in a folder. No new physics content is introduced — this is synthesis and self-assessment.
- Differentiation: ages 10–13 focus on mounting four clear evidence items and writing "what I can now do" for five outcomes in plain words, with the partner show-and-tell as the main oral step. Ages 14–17 do the full ten-outcome self-assessment with citations, the windmill energy-chain loss analysis, and the "activity vs mastery" argument, treating the portfolio as evidence for transferable skills.
- If a learner's year files are incomplete, do not penalize them — let them use whatever evidence they have and write "no evidence yet" for missing outcomes, with a next step. The honest gap is itself useful self-assessment.
- The portfolio is the learner's record for the year and the basis for the certificate in Lesson 365. Photograph each portfolio page before the celebration so there is a backup if a folder is lost.
- CBE assessment note: the reflection sheet maps directly to G10-PHY-1.1 (scientific skills and evidence) and JS-FE (foundational enquiry). The strength of each "what I can now do" statement, judged by whether it cites evidence, is the assessment — a claim with a citation is mastery-level; a bare claim is emerging.
