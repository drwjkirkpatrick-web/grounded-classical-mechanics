# Lesson 051: Build — Balloon Rocket Line

**Unit:** 2 — Forces in Everyday Life  |  **Duration:** 45 minutes  |  **Ages:** 10–17 (differentiated)
**CBE Alignment:** JS-FE; UP-FE  |  **Hands-On:** Yes  |  **Project:** balloon-rocket

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners can:
1. Build a balloon rocket that travels along a string line.
2. Explain the motion using Newton's third law (action–reaction).
3. Measure travel distance and test one variable fairly (balloon size or payload).
4. Predict how payload mass affects acceleration using F = ma.

## Materials
- Round balloons (one per pair; a few spares — they burst)
- Plastic drinking straws, cut in half
- Strong string or fishing line, 6–8 m per line (sisal string works if smooth)
- Cellotape
- Paperclips (payload)
- Tape measure or metre stick
- Two chairs or posts to tension each line

## Vocabulary
- **thrust** (msukumo) — the forward push from the escaping air
- **payload** (mzigo) — the load carried
- **friction** (msuguano) — rubbing force that slows the straw on the string
- **variable** (kigeuzi) — the one thing you change in a fair test

## Pre-Test
*5 minutes, individual, closed book.*

1. A balloon is released with its neck open. Predict and describe its motion.
2. Which way does the air escape, and which way does the balloon go?
3. What could make a balloon rocket travel further? Name one thing.

**Answer Key (Pre-Test):**
1. It shoots forward, zigzagging if free, slowing as air runs out. (Accept any sensible prediction.)
2. Air escapes backwards; the balloon moves forwards.
3. Accept any one: more air, smoother string, lighter payload, straight line, less tape weight.

## Lesson Flow (45 Minutes)

1. **Settle and Pre-Test (5 min)** — hand out pre-test slips; collect before the demo.
2. **Engage (5 min)** — inflate a balloon and release it untied. Laughter, then the question: *what pushed it?* Board the words "action" and "reaction" without explaining yet.
3. **Explore (15 min)** — pairs build the rocket line (see Hands-On Build). Once a line works, teams run three trials: big balloon vs small balloon, then add two paperclips as payload. They record distance each time on the board table.
4. **Explain (8 min)** — consolidate: the balloon pushes air backwards (action); the air pushes the balloon forwards (reaction). Compare class results: more air → more thrust time; more payload → less acceleration (F = ma in words). Confront the misconception that "the air pushing on the room" moves it — ask why the rocket still works along a string in a straight line better than in open air.
5. **Post-Test (10 min)** — administer; peer-mark in class if time allows.
6. **Wrap and Homework (2 min)** — takeaway: *forces come in pairs*. Homework: find one action–reaction pair at home (e.g., stepping off a dugout canoe) and write one sentence on it.

## Hands-On Build: Balloon Rocket Line
*Build time fits inside the Explore block; cut straws and string the day before.*

### Parts
- Balloon — 1 per pair — market or shop; any round balloon
- Straw — 1, cut in half — or rolled paper tube as substitute
- String — 6–8 m — fishing line or smooth sisal; avoid rough rope (friction)
- Cellotape — two small pieces — to join balloon to straw
- Paperclips — for payload trials

### Workflow
1. Thread the string through the straw half.
2. Tie the string tightly between two chairs, as level as possible. **Checkpoint:** pluck it — it should twang, not sag.
3. Inflate the balloon, pinch the neck shut with fingers (do not tie).
4. Tape the balloon to the straw so the neck points backwards along the line.
5. Pull the straw to one end. **Checkpoint:** balloon neck points away from the direction of travel.
6. Release the neck and the straw together. Measure where the straw stops.
7. Repeat with a bigger balloon, then add paperclips one at a time; record each distance.

### Common Mistakes
- **Sagging string** — rocket stalls halfway; re-tension the line.
- **Tape over the neck** — blocks the airflow; tape the balloon body only.
- **Neck pointing forward** — rocket flies backwards; flip the balloon.
- **Measuring from the wrong end** — measure how far the *straw* travelled from its start mark.

## Post-Test
*10 minutes, individual.*

1. Which way does air leave the balloon, and which way does the balloon move? (mirrors pre-test)
2. Name the force pair in this experiment. (mirrors pre-test)
3. Our class found: big balloon 5.2 m, small balloon 3.1 m, same line. Give one physics reason.
4. A rocket carries two paperclips, then four. Predict and explain the difference using F = ma in words.
5. Challenge: why does the rocket move better along a tight string than when thrown freely?

**Answer Key (Post-Test):**
1. Air leaves backwards; balloon moves forwards.
2. Balloon pushes air backwards (action); air pushes balloon forwards (reaction).
3. More air = longer/greater thrust, so more impulse and more distance (accept: overcomes friction for longer).
4. Four paperclips = more mass = less acceleration for the same thrust, so shorter distance.
5. The string removes zigzagging and wasted sideways motion; all thrust goes into forward motion, and the straw keeps direction steady (plus less energy wasted tumbling).

## Advanced Questions (Ages 14–17)

1. **Q:** A 60 g balloon-plus-straw rocket accelerates from rest to 4 m/s in 0.5 s along the line. Estimate the average thrust. (Ignore friction.)
   **Worked Answer:** a = Δv/Δt = 4/0.5 = 8 m/s². F = ma = 0.060 kg × 8 m/s² = 0.48 N ≈ 0.5 N. That is roughly the weight of 48 g of water — a small but measurable push.
2. **Q:** The same rocket with a 40 g payload (total 100 g) gets the same 0.48 N thrust. Find its new acceleration and explain the lower distance travelled.
   **Worked Answer:** a = F/m = 0.48/0.100 = 4.8 m/s², down from 8 m/s². Less acceleration over a similar thrust-time means lower final speed and shorter coasting distance against friction.
3. **Q:** A student claims the rocket moves because the escaping air pushes against the air in the room. Design a test or argument to show this is wrong.
   **Worked Answer:** Fire the rocket inside a large plastic bag that traps the exhausted air, or reason from the string experiment: the rocket still accelerates in a straight line regardless of nearby walls. Momentum conservation explains it — air carries backward momentum, the rocket gains equal forward momentum; no external "wall of air" is needed. (A vacuum would work even better, not worse.)

## Safety Notes
- Balloon fragments are a choking hazard — sweep up burst pieces immediately.
- Do not over-inflate balloons near faces; eye protection is sensible for the "giant balloon" trials.
- Tensioned string can snap back if it breaks — check knots, keep faces away from the line ends.
- Clear the flight path; a straw at speed can poke.

## Teacher Notes
- Assumes Lesson 049 (Newton's third law) has run; this is the applied build that makes it stick.
- Younger learners (10–13): focus on fair testing and the pair language ("pushes back / pushes forward"). Older learners: take real measurements and run the advanced thrust calculation with their own data.
- If wind is strong, run the lines indoors along desks — wind adds a third force and confuses the fair test.
- Balloons perish in heat; buy fresh the same week. Fishing line from the market outperforms sisal.
- CBE assessment note: record the pre/post growth per learner; the build itself is evidence for the "scientific investigation" competency.
